🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1987 · 1987 — 3 matches

Wed 30 Sep 1987 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDLDWD

Wednesday 30th September saw us make the trip to Penshurst Park, and having called correctly for once in my captaincy career, there was never much doubt about what I'd do with it — up we went to bat.

What followed was as handsome a display as this scribe has had the pleasure of witnessing. The openers set the tone with 95 for the first wicket before either fell, and then David Castle simply took over, compiling a majestic 100 not out while Andrew Hales chipped in with a classy 64 and the pair added 110 for the second wicket. Paul Bowles' 35 rounded things off nicely as we closed on 206 for 2 from our 43.3 overs — the kind of total that makes the tea interval taste that bit sweeter.

Defending it was another matter entirely. Penshurst's Milns played a fine hand of 52, well supported by Darby (34) and Boyden (26), and a stand of 66 for the third wicket had us shifting in our boots. But cricket has a way of turning, and turn it did — they slid from 182 for 6 to 192 for 9, with G Cooper and John Lambert both back in the hutch without so much as a run to their name. Ian Brattle was magnificent, snaring 4 for 53, with Bowles (3-23) and Thirkell (1-10) offering fine support. My own contribution behind the stumps was rather less distinguished — 11 byes is not a figure I'll be framing.

A tense finish, but a fine win by 10 runs. Onwards indeed.

Sun 27 Sep 1987 🧢 1st XIv LX Lord Cornwallis XI · HomeformDWDDD

The lads gathered on Sunday, all set for the visit of Dummy Site — and true to form, I lost the toss again, watching them elect to bat while I tried to look unbothered about it.

They ground their way to 158 all out in 57.4 overs, with Titchener-Barrett unbeaten on 34 and Allen (27) and Oldridge (26) chipping in respectably enough. For a while it looked ominous — 56-2 sliding to 70-5 in a hurry — before Jon Harris got among them properly, 4-46 with six miserly maidens thrown in for good measure, ably assisted by Alan Boyns (3-22) and Malcolm Pay (2-5). Poor Irwin and Jefferson trudged back without so much as troubling the scorers, which is never a pleasant walk.

Our reply was a rather more nervous affair. Two down for one run and I confess I did wonder what I'd say in this very report. But Keith Hatcher, calm as you like, played a lovely knock of 58, with David Castle (36) and Bob Lines (23) lending sturdy support — the third-wicket pair adding a priceless 73 before the middle order rather lost its head, 119-3 becoming 128-6 in the blink of an eye. Whyte, Simmonds and Large all made their way back for ducks, bless them, and we finished on 139-8. Castle also had a busy afternoon behind the stumps, 20 byes the price of an entertaining contest — though he can console himself as our leading run-scorer this season, just as Harris tops the wicket charts, and reached 1500 career victims in the process.

A draw, then — honours even, and on the balance of the day's fortunes, a fair result all round.

Sat 26 Sep 1987 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformLDWDD

Here is the match report.

Saturday last saw Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC roll up the drive, and my luck deserted me at the toss — they elected to bat, and I resigned myself to a long afternoon in the field. Jonathon Coles gave us the full tour with a polished 51, Ian Gillespie stuck around unbeaten on 46, and J Withers chipped in with 32, the openers laying down a maddeningly solid platform of 75 before the first wicket finally fell. They closed on 184/6 from their 40 overs, a total that felt heavier than it should have thanks to some excellent death bowling. Jon Harris was quite magnificent, prising out five for 54 — a spell to make the tea urn proud — while Matt Large toiled away for 1-85, his two maidens the only thing keeping the scoreboard honest. Terry Wright, for his part, departed without so much as a glance at the scorers.

In reply we were steady rather than spectacular, closing on 141/6 from 52 overs. Nigel Thirkell played the anchor role beautifully for 43, with Dave Todd offering fine support alongside on 23. I contributed a workmanlike 19 of my own, more graft than glory. Spare a thought for poor Peter Whyte, whose duck will be inscribed in the club's ledger of shame for weeks to come.

With David Castle still purring along nicely in the season's run charts and Jon Harris now boasting 94 wickets, the engine room of this side remains in rude health. As for Saturday — honours shared, a draw that satisfied nobody and offended nobody, which in village cricket is very nearly a result.

Sun 20 Sep 1987 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLLDWD

Sunday 20 September saw us play host to Anchorians CC, and losing the toss meant we were straight into the field – never the ideal way to spend an English afternoon, but there we are. The visitors made themselves thoroughly at home, racing to 217/2 from their 42 overs on the back of a monumental 179-run second-wicket stand. Graham Siveyer batted with the serene indifference of a man who knows the bowling isn't troubling him, finishing unbeaten on 96, while Keith Wooding chipped in a handsome 80 alongside him. Credit where due: Matt Large kept plugging away for 2-42, and Alan Boyns bowled with the meanness of a man guarding his allotment, three maidens and all.

Chasing 218, we set off promisingly enough – 93 for the first wicket suggested a platform worth building on. Nigel Thirkell was the mainstay, striking a fine 89, well supported by David Castle's 40. But cricket has a cruel sense of comedy, and ours arrived between 141 and 146, three wickets tumbling in the blink of an eye. I contributed my own small footnote to that collapse with a duck – the sort of dismissal best filed under "least said." We limped to 149/9 from our 43, honours shared.

One shaft of sunshine amid the wreckage: David Castle brought up his 100th appearance in Linton colours, a landmark worth more than any scoreline. A draw, then – no losers, but with that middle-order wobble, no winners either. We'll take the point and the cap doff to Castle, and move swiftly on.

Sat 19 Sep 1987 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · AwayformWLLDW

Nigel Thirkell here, filing from another sun-dappled Saturday in the good county of Kent, where West Farleigh had the good manners to win the toss and the poor judgement to bat first anyway.

We toiled honestly in the field, and it would have been a long afternoon indeed had Jon Harris not decided this was his day — 5-51 of the highest quality, the sort of spell that has opposition batsmen suddenly remembering appointments elsewhere. Mick Holness (42), Mick Beeney (29) and Tim Baker (28) all got in and got amongst it for the home side, but between Jon's five-for, a tidy 1-22 I managed to scrape together, David Castle's 1-24, and three maidens from the ever-parsimonious Matt Large, we dragged them back to 156 for 9 off their 48.3 overs — a score that felt, by teatime, distinctly gettable.

Alas, gettable is not the same as got. By the time we'd crept to 6 without loss off three overs, both captains cast a weather eye at the clock, shrugged in the manner of men who have seen enough village cricket to know a lost cause when the light's fading, and shook hands on a draw.

Elsewhere, the season ticks pleasingly along — David Castle now sits second in our 1987 run charts with 1246, and Jon Harris leads the wickets column with 89, proof if any were needed that some players are simply having a year of it.

A draw, then — honours shared, pride intact, and the tea urn, as ever, the only clear winner.

Wed 09 Sep 1987 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · HomeformDDLDW

Called it right at the toss on a fine September Wednesday and, with The Mote CC standing between us and a bit of tea-time glory, there was never really a question of doing anything but batting first. I managed to find some rhythm and worked through for 3 for 52, with Nigel and Trevor Back chipping in a wicket apiece — Trevor's solitary strike enough to leapfrog Tony Snell into 15th on the wicket-takers' list — while Alan Boyns bowled with miserly discipline, seven maidens testament to a line the batsmen simply couldn't budge.

In the end, honours even. A draw, then — but a thoroughly good-natured one, and no complaints from this captain's chair.

Mon 31 Aug 1987 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformDWWWL

Beaten by 3 wickets, but I'll say this for the lads: we made Bearsted work for it.

Monday 31 August 1987, and we had Bearsted CC in at Linton Park on what felt, at the toss, like a home fixture we ought to win. I called it right and elected to bat — or so I thought at tea. We were bowled out for 138 off 58.5 overs, which tells its own story of application rather than fireworks. Sean Charlton was the mainstay with 40, and a sixth-wicket stand of 56 dragged us to something defendable. Poor Matt Large will want this one struck from the record — a duck to open his afternoon, though he'd have his say later.

Defending 138 was always going to ask questions, and Bearsted answered most of them. An early wicket with just 1 on the board had us dreaming, but Rupert Humphrey (49) and Richard Coward (47) put together a stand of 76 for the fifth wicket that took the game away from us, N Bateman seeing them home unbeaten on 29. Credit where it's due: Matt Large atoned in style with 4-33, Sean Charlton and Allan Cooper chipped in, and Jon Harris bowled six overs as tight as his club-leading wicket tally suggests.

In the end, 144/7 in 54 overs edged us out by three wickets. Not our finest hour, but there was enough good cricket in there to suggest better days are coming — and coming soon, I hope.
