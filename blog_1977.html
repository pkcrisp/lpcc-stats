🦆 Duck Report — 1st & 2nd October 1977 🦆

10 ducks
Week 24 · 1 Oct 1977 – 2 Oct 1977
Ten ducks in two days to close out the season in style — the exclusive zero club has had a busy weekend, headlined by a genuine middle-order stampede at Lordswood. Details below. Saturday — 1st XI v Lordswood CC A proper procession. Ian Webb went first, caught off Kevin Masters for his 2nd career duck (8 innings, 188th all-time), drawing level with A Mitchell (1964-65) — and a fitting nod to kin Ken Webb, who carries 3 of his own. Then the honours board reunion arrived in full: Tim Thirkell (3rd this season, 36th career, 7th all-time) fell to join Richard Harris (1940-70) on the same tally — no small feat for a Full House member with a duck at every position and kin stretching from John Thirkell's mighty 90 down to Stuart's solitary 1. Brian Woodcock followed for his 2nd of the season and 3rd overall, level with 1893's A Kent. And then, magnificently, Jim Hall and Martin Mears both departed in the same innings as Tim — all three sharing "Quacking Together" honours with one another, so this was less a collapse than a scheduled get-together. Jim Hall's was his staggering 11th duck of the season (34th career, 10th all-time) and keeps him on course to defend his Duck of the Year 1977 crown. Martin Mears made it 9 career ducks, level with B Westover (1940-45) — small mercy, he'd be back for more the very next day. Sunday — Married v Linton Park CC Colin Dicker fell early for his 1st duck of the season, 23rd career, a two-time Duck of the Year alumnus (1973, 1975) making a fresh case for a third. Martin Mears returned to complete a grim pair — his 6th of the season, 10th career, level with Andrew Moss (1968-77), having failed to trouble the scorer in successive fixtures. Keith Miller made it 4 ducks in just 7 innings, a fearsome 57% strike rate, level with Brian Mills (1959-60). Sunday — Singles v Linton Park CC William Buck, 29 career ducks from a marathon 328 innings, drew level with Ron Hickman (1963-77) — proof that longevity and the zero club are not mutually exclusive. Allan Cooper edged past for his 2nd of the season, caught by none other than the previous day's victim Ian Webb, and moves to 16 career, level with Percy Brattle (1934-73). Week in summary 10 ducks, 2 matches, one genuinely historic innings at Lordswood where three Quacking Together club-mates fell together. Martin Mears's weekend pair the individual highlight — two matches, no runs, full commitment. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 39 inns, 11 ducks (28%) 2. Martin Mears — 17 inns, 6 ducks (35%) 3. Keith Miller — 7 inns, 4 ducks (57%) 4. Paul Brattle — 43 inns, 4 ducks (9%) 5. Trevor Back — 16 inns, 3 ducks (19%) Jim Hall holds a healthy lead in the race for Duck of the Year 1977 — but with Martin Mears breathing down his neck at 35%, the title is far from safe. Next fixture beckons. The zero club never rests — see you at the crease. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 24 to 28 September 1977

2 ducks
Week 23 · 24 Sep 1977 – 28 Sep 1977
A quiet week for the executioner's blade, but what it lacked in volume it made up for in company kept — two ducks, one hungry attack, and a rare shared credit on the honours board. 🏏 Saturday 24th — 1st XI v West Farleigh CC (home) The only fixture to trouble the scorers this week, and trouble them it did. Batting at seven, Jim Hall was bowled by Norman Underdown for his 10th duck of the summer — a landmark that ties neatly into his Duck of the Year 1977 credentials, matching the crown he first lifted back in 1970. That's 33 career ducks now, good enough for 12th on the all-time list out of 942 names, level company with Basil Sears (1947–1975) and Stan Wratten (1943–1976), both also parked on exactly 33. Jim remains a Full House nearly-man too, with ducks now spread across ten of the eleven possible batting positions — one berth still to conquer for the complete set. Long since a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own first-ever knock, he shows no sign of slowing his collecting in year 1977 either. Three wickets later, at number ten, Martin Mears joined him in the pavilion, bowled by Trevor Back for his 4th duck of the season and 8th of his career — 63rd on the all-time table from 48 innings. Fittingly, the pair already share a "Quacking Together" entry on the honours board, and this week they've earned it properly — falling in the very same innings of the very same match. One scorebook, two blobs, twice the ignominy. Spookily, both men share their historical echo too: on this very day back in 1960, Frank Brattle — 1937 to 1971, 38 career ducks, 5th all-time — also strolled back for nought, against A. Trembeths. History, it seems, is fond of the 24th of September. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both from the same fixture — the rest of the round proved disappointingly fowl-free. West Farleigh CC can consider themselves honorary sponsors of the Duck Club this week. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 37 inns, 10 ducks, 27.03% 2. Martin Mears — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% 3. Paul Brattle — 43 inns, 4 ducks, 9.30% 4. Keith Miller — 6 inns, 3 ducks, 50.00% 5. Trevor Back — 15 inns, 3 ducks, 20.00% Jim Hall sits well clear at the summit, but Martin Mears is closing fast on percentage. With the season winding down, there's still time for one more name to blot its copybook before the last bell. Same time next week — bring your bat, and preferably use it.

🦆 Duck News — 17 to 22 September 1977

3 ducks
Week 22 · 17 Sep 1977 – 22 Sep 1977
Three matches, three ducks — a lean week for the Duck Club, but each one arrived with pedigree, and by the time Thursday's Midweek fixture rolled round the honours boards were groaning under the weight of history disturbed. 🏏 Saturday 17th — 1st XI v Southborough CC Paul Bowles opened the innings and, with the ink barely dry on the scorebook, was back in the pavilion — caught Sam Martin, bowled Ron Kingsmill, for a duck that is his 2nd of the season and 28th of an increasingly distinguished career. That tally lifts him to 17th in the all-time standings out of 942 hopefuls, alongside two Duck of the Year crowns (1966 and 1968) and membership of the Ducks on the Road top ten. On this very day back in 1972, none other than John Thirkell — the club's undisputed monarch of the nought, on 90 career ducks and still climbing — was also dismissed for nought, against Mersham Hatch. Keep that name in mind. We'll be seeing it again by Thursday. 🌱 Sunday 18th — 1st XI at Mersham Hatch Batting at number ten, Jon Harris departed caught J- Copeland, bowled D- Bromley, for his 3rd duck of the season and 35th of his career — good enough for 7th on the all-time list. Jon arrives with a positively baronial collection of honours: Duck of the Year 1976, a place in both the Homebody and Lone Duck top tens, Tourist Duck top ten membership, the rare Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11, a feat of comprehensive incompetence), and Quacking Together partnerships with both Allan Cooper and current season pace-setter Jim Hall. His relative Richard Harris is no stranger to this company either, with 36 career ducks of his own — clearly a family that takes its cricket, and its ducks, seriously. On this day in 1960, Cliff Anscombe made an identical journey back to the pavilion against Tilling Steven. 🌾 Thursday 22nd — Midweek XI v West Kent Wanderers CC And so to the name promised above. Batting at nine, John Thirkell was bowled by M- Brown for his 2nd duck of the season — his 90th in a career spanning 786 innings, a figure that remains utterly untouchable at the top of the all-time table. Five Duck of the Year titles (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967 and 1971) and top-ten residency in both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody categories confirm what the numbers already show: this is a man who has made an art of the nought. Fittingly, the family trade continues — son Tim Thirkell sits on 35 career ducks of his own, level with Jon Harris above, and Nigel Thirkell isn't far behind on 26. On this day in 1951, John --'s Taylor also fell for a duck, against Hunton Wanderers CC — small consolation for a man who has now outducked practically the entire club roster. 📋 The Week in Brief Three ducks, three different days, not a single repeat offender — a rare show of restraint from the Duck Club this week. No pairs, no family doubles this time, just three individually excellent contributions to the art of not scoring. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 9 ducks in 36 innings (25.0%) 2. Paul Brattle — 4 ducks in 42 innings (9.5%) 3. Keith Miller — 3 ducks in 6 innings (50.0%) 4. Martin Mears — 3 ducks in 14 innings (21.4%) 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks in 15 innings (20.0%) Keith Miller's 50% strike rate remains the stuff of nightmares for any bowler hoping for a fair contest. Next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, further additions to these ranks. The Duck Club never rests. 🦆

🦆 Duck Club Tour Bulletin — 10 to 14 September 1977

1 duck
Week 21 · 10 Sep 1977 – 14 Sep 1977
Three matches out on tour this week and the ducks, mostly, kept their heads down. Only one name earned its place in the ledger — but he made it count. Tour XI At Beacon CC in Cornwall on the 11th, David Buck came in at number two and departed lbw to Michael Bryant without registering — a plain, unadorned nought. It's his 2nd duck of the season and his 4th in a 19-innings career, keeping him handily inside the all-time top 101 (of 940 he'd rather not be counted among). Fittingly, on this very day back in 1954, Len Conway also fell for a duck against Old Roffensians, on his way to 12 career ducks and a place in the club's Debutante Duck club — proof the calendar has form for this sort of thing. David isn't the only Buck on the books either — kinsman William Buck carries 28 career ducks of his own, a proper family tradition in the making. Week in Summary Three matches, one duck — a stingy return by Duck Club standards, but quality over quantity. The Cornish leg of the tour otherwise passed off disappointingly duck-free. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Jim Hall — 9 ducks / 34 innings (26.47%) 2. Paul Brattle — 4 ducks / 39 innings (10.26%) 3. Keith Miller — 3 ducks / 5 innings (60.0%) 4. Martin Mears — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks / 15 innings (20.0%) Keith Miller's 60% strike rate remains a masterclass in the art of not scoring — three ducks from five innings, no signs of slowing. Next fixture looms, and with it, fresh opportunity for the zero club to welcome new — or returning — members. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 3rd to 9th September 1977 🦆

8 ducks
Week 20 · 3 Sep 1977 – 9 Sep 1977
A proper touring week for the Duck Club, with the lads down in Cornwall for a mid-week jaunt while the 1st XI held the fort at home against Staplehurst. Six fixtures, eight ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomes plenty of fresh — and returning — blood. Saturday 3rd — 1st XI v Staplehurst CC A trio for Linton in this one, and history nodded along: on this very day back in 1949, P- Martin also made a duck, albeit against East Peckham & Nettlestead CC. - Gary Marshall opened his LPCC account in the cruellest way possible, bowled by B- Russell for a duck on debut — straight onto the Debutante Duck board at the first time of asking. 1 career duck from 4 innings, 311th on the all-time list. - Jim Hall was bowled by C Rogers for his 8th of the season and 31st career duck from 177 innings — a lofty 13th all-time. His Quacking Together honours with fellow victim Martin Mears were very much fulfilled this week, and with 9 already banked for the season he's cruising towards Duck of the Year 1977. - Martin Mears fell to a- Davies, his 3rd of the season and 7th career duck from 45 innings, 69th all-time — the Quacking Together partnership with Jim Hall alive and well. Tour — Paul CC, Cornwall (Tues 6th) Two down in the same innings, and on this same day in 1967 Roger Craddock was doing likewise against Birchington CC. - Jim Hall completed the pair — caught B Lawry, bowled R Stephens — his 9th of the season and 32nd career duck, pulling further clear at the top of the Duck of the Year 1977 race. - Keith Miller ran himself out for nought, his 3rd duck of the season and, remarkably, his 3rd from just 5 career innings — a duck rate to make the accountants wince, 138th all-time. Tour — St Just CC (Wed 7th) Tim Thirkell, lbw to P Pearce, notched his 2nd of the season and 35th of a long career spanning 262 innings — 7th all-time and a proud member of the Full House board, having now ducked from every position 1 to 11. On this day in 1963, Frank Brattle also fell for nought. Tim keeps it in the family too — kin John Thirkell tops the entire club's all-time duck standings with a barely believable 89. Tour — Grampound Road CC (Thurs 8th) Trevor Back was bowled by T Maddern, his 3rd of the season and 6th career duck from 38 innings, 82nd all-time, adding to his Quacking Together ledger with Jon Harris. On this day in 1945, Stan Wratten was similarly dismissed. Tour — Cornwall Crusaders (Fri 9th) Andrew Moss, bowled by P Johns, opened his season's account and reached double figures for his career — 10 ducks from 131 innings, 47th all-time, and a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck board from a past farewell. On this day in 1973, none other than John Ames — 2nd on the all-time chart — made a duck of his own. One fixture on the tour stayed resolutely duck-free — a rare mercy for someone. Week in summary Eight ducks from six matches — the curse followed the tour bus the length of Cornwall. Jim Hall's pair was the standout feat of the week and puts him well clear at the top of the season standings. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 9 ducks, 34 innings, 26.47% 2. Paul Brattle — 4 ducks, 37 innings, 10.81% 3. Keith Miller — 3 ducks, 5 innings, 60.0% 4. Martin Mears — 3 ducks, 12 innings, 25.0% 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks, 14 innings, 21.43% The season presses on and the Duck of the Year race is Jim Hall's to lose. Roll on next weekend — the zero club always has room for one more. 🍩🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 28th to 31st August 1977

2 ducks
Week 19 · 28 Aug 1977 – 31 Aug 1977
Three fixtures out on the road and in the field this week, and the ducks kept a remarkably low profile — only two brave souls answered the call of the exclusive zero club. Quality, evidently, over quantity. 🏏 Saturday 28th — 1st XI v Hempstead CC Keith Miller, at five, departed bowled by L Worrall without so much as troubling the scorer. It's his second duck of the campaign and, tellingly, his second in a career that stretches to just four innings — a 50% strike rate for the art of not scoring that would make any bowler blush. He sits 196th of 939 in the all-time reckoning, and on this very day back in 1974 a young Roland Parr was doing exactly the same thing against Gravesend CC, en route to a solitary career duck of his own. Keith, you're in fine if fleeting company. 🚌 Sunday 31st — Midweek XI away at Gravesend CC Opening the innings and setting the tone rather differently than intended, Paul Brattle nicked off, caught Simon Hinks off the bowling of C Biddlecombe. It's Paul's 4th of the season and a chunky 21st for his career from 218 innings — good enough for 27th on the all-time list, and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from a debut long since consigned to history. Fittingly, on this same date in 1974 Mike Iliffe was also walking back for nought against Whitstable CC, on his way to 34 career ducks and 9th spot overall. Paul, incidentally, bats in rather feathered company off the field too — with a parent, a sibling and wider family all carrying double-figure duck tallies between them, this is very much the family trade. 📋 Week in Summary Three matches, two ducks — a quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, with the Saturday and Sunday sides mostly finding their way to the middle without incident. No pairs, no kings, no family gatherings on the honours board this time round. Sometimes the ducks simply migrate elsewhere. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Jim Hall — 28 innings, 7 ducks, 25.0% - Paul Brattle — 32 innings, 4 ducks, 12.5% - Keith Miller — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.0% - Alan Cradduck — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.0% - Tony Gauna — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.0% Keith Miller's blistering 50% duck rate keeps him lurking dangerously close to the summit despite the shortest innings count in the top five — one to watch as the season enters its closing stretch. 🍩 Next fixture, next chance. The ducks never truly rest — see you all out there.

Duck Report — 20–21 August 1977 🦆

9 ducks
Week 18 · 20 Aug 1977 – 21 Aug 1977
Two matches, nine ducks, and the club's own correspondent bureau is pleased to report the season's harvest is coming along nicely. 🏏 1st XI v Bobbing Court CC, Saturday 20th August (home) Tim Thirkell fell at four, caught by J Gorf off Roger Mills, for his 1st duck of the season and 34th of a distinguished career — 10th in the all-time table of 939. A proud member of the Full House board (a duck at every position, 1 to 11 — a completionist's curse if ever there was one), Tim now sits level on 34 with Jon Harris himself, who ducked twice in this very match. Neat symmetry: on this day in 1944, W Bunting also made a duck, against KCC Maidstone. Jim Hall (7) was caught behind off D Hookway for his 6th of the season, 29th career, 14th all-time — a name that appears on more honours boards than the clubhouse noticeboard, including back-to-back Duck of the Year in 1970 and 1977. He wasn't done, either — more below. Keith Miller (9), caught off D Hookway, notched his maiden career duck from just 3 innings — an inauspicious start to a short ledger, 351st of 939. And Jon Harris (10), bowled by P Bedelle, added his 2nd of the weekend, 34th of his career — the Full House man himself, level with Tim Thirkell, Gordon Fenner and Mike Iliffe on that same total. 🏏 1st XI v Lordswood CC, Sunday 21st August (home) David Buck opened and fell lbw to W Wells for his 1st of the season, 3rd career, from just 12 innings. On this day in 1955, John Ames — the club's own duck-royalty, 49 career ducks, 2nd all-time — also fell, against Halfway House. Trevor Back (5), caught off Wells, took his 2nd of the season and 5th career, joining a well-populated table at 94th all-time. Then Jim Hall returned to the crease and promptly departed again, caught off Wells for his 7th of the season and 30th career — a pair for the weekend, out in both innings this period. Not quite regal enough for a king pair, but a grim double all the same, and it vaults him to the top of the current Duck Hunting Season standings. Martin Mears (7), also off Wells, moved to 2 for the season and 6 career. And Tony Gauna, last man in, fell to N Scudder for his 2nd of the weekend, 19th career — level with Fred Greenfield and just ahead of Tony Snell on the all-time list. 📋 Week in Summary Nine ducks across two matches — Wells the Lordswood bowler alone accounted for four of them, a proper poultry harvest for the visitors. Jim Hall's weekend pair was the headline event, though the whole 1st XI seemed determined to make a weekend of it. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 27 inns, 7 ducks (25.9%) 2. Paul Brattle — 29 inns, 3 ducks (10.3%) 3. Alan Cradduck — 4 inns, 2 ducks (50.0%) 4. Tony Gauna — 5 inns, 2 ducks (40.0%) 5. Trevor Back — 9 inns, 2 ducks (22.2%) 🍩 The zero club never sleeps, and neither do the bowlers. Next fixture, someone else's name goes up on the board — see you there.

🦆 Duck News — 13-14 August 1977

3 ducks
Week 17 · 13 Aug 1977 – 14 Aug 1977
Two fixtures graced the fixture list this week, but the Duck Club's entire harvest came from one killing field: Saturday's home date with Sibton Park CC, where three Linton batsmen were sent packing without troubling the scorer. Sunday, by contrast, passed off suspiciously clean. 🏏 1st XI v Sibton Park CC (home) Jim Hall departed at first wicket down for nought, bowled by E. Prince — his 5th duck of the season and 28th of an ever-more-decorated career, good enough for 16th on the all-time list of 939. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club since his very first innings, Hall already carries the 1977 Duck of the Year board to his name, and this latest failure only tightens his grip on that crown as the season stands. He also remains just one batting position shy of the fabled Full House — a duck from ten of eleven slots — a piece of dubious history he seems determined to complete. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1893, A Kent made a duck of his own against Royal Engineers CC, going on to claim that year's Duck of the Year honours — kindred spirits separated by eighty-four years. Trevor Back followed at five, bowled by Nick Guy, for his first duck of the season and the 4th of his career from 32 innings, 112th of 939 all time. Back keeps distinguished company in the Duck Club's travel section, sitting inside the top ten for both Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck — proof that ducks find you whether you stay or go. He too shares his calendar square with 1893's A Kent, who was busy doing precisely the same thing against Royal Engineers CC on this very date. At number ten, Tony Gauna fell to the very same E. Prince who had already claimed Hall, moving to 18 career ducks from 76 innings — 32nd of 939 — and his first of the season. Prince can rightly claim a poultry brace of his own for the afternoon's work. Gauna joins the growing 1893 reunion, A Kent having made his own duck against Royal Engineers CC on this same date, proof the calendar has a long memory for failure. 📊 Week in Brief Three ducks from two matches, every one of them served up by Sibton Park CC's bowling attack, with E. Prince doing the double almost single-handedly. Sunday's fixture, meanwhile, kept its powder dry. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 5 ducks / 25 innings (20%) 2. Paul Brattle — 3 ducks / 27 innings (11.11%) 3. Alan Cradduck — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50%) 4. Malcolm Pay — 2 ducks / 16 innings (12.5%) 5. Giles Daubeny — 1 duck / 1 innings (100%) Hall leads the chase for now, but Cradduck's fearsome 50% strike rate and Daubeny's flawless record lurk menacingly behind him. The next fixture awaits — bat raised, expectations low, as ever. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 30–31 July 1977

1 duck
Week 15 · 30 Jul 1977 – 31 Jul 1977
A quiet weekend by the exclusive zero club's usual standards — just the one duck laid across two fixtures — but what it lacked in volume it made up for in pedigree. 🏏 Saturday 30 July — Away at Sibton Park CC The 1st XI's trip to Sibton Park brought the weekend's sole casualty. In at six, Jim Hall departed courtesy of Gordon Webb's gloves off Nick Guy's bowling — a tidy little dismissal that adds a fourth duck to his season and a 27th to a career now 165 innings deep. That's rank 18 of 939 in the all-time table, comfortably inside the sport's most committed circle of underachievement. Hall barely needs introducing to these pages. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from whenever his first innings went the same way, he's also a two-time Duck of the Year — 1970, and, rather pointedly, 1977 itself, a crown this latest effort does nothing to loosen his grip on. He sits in the "Full House nearly-man" bracket too, having managed the indignity of a duck from 10 of 11 batting positions — leaving only one spot on the fixture list still needing a visit.
