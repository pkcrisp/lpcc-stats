🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 1966 · 1966 — 1 match

Sat 24 Sep 1966 🧢 1st XIv HC Hartley Country Club CC · HomeformLWLWW

Saturday 24 September 1966 saw Hartley Country Club CC visit Linton Park, and winning the toss I had no hesitation taking first use of what looked a fair batting strip — the right call, I still maintain. We reached 168 for 9 from our 44 overs, built on a fine unbeaten 46 from Dick Langridge, with Terry Whittaker's 32 and Derry Evans's unbeaten 27 lending sturdy support. In between came one of those spells that make cricket the cruel mistress she is: 11 for 1 became 12 for 4 almost before anyone had settled into their deckchair. Credit to the last pair, whose defiant stand of 59 restored some dignity to the card. Less creditable were the ducks — Benny Bell, Mike Iliffe and I all contributed one apiece, and the less said about mine the better.

With ball in hand we set about defending it, and defend it we did. E Cherry's 48 was the backbone of Hartley's reply, with A Lush chipping in 24, but C Cooper departed for nought at our hands. Gordon Fenner was pick of the attack with 3 for 20, well backed by Dick Langridge's 3 for 34 — five maidens thrown in for good measure — and Paul Bowles took 1 for 40 as Hartley closed on 128 for 7.

A draw, then, honours shared, neither side able to land the decisive blow. With Terry Whittaker now 4th in the season's run charts and Dick Langridge 3rd among the wickets, the engine room of this side hums along nicely even when the top order — captain included — has an afternoon best forgotten.

🗓️ 17 Sep – 23 Sep 1966 · 1966 — 2 matches

Sun 18 Sep 1966 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDLWLW

Sunday 18 September 1966, and Anchorians CC arrived as our visitors with the sun doing its level best and the toss doing its level worst — down we went, and out we were sent to bowl.

Not that any of us minded overmuch once Terry Whittaker got his fingers round the ball. Turning it square all afternoon, he finished with a scarcely believable 7 for 29, and by the time he'd finished tormenting the Anchorians middle order there wasn't much of an innings left to torment. From 3 for 1 we had them reeling to 16 for 4, A Hart and M- Brown both back in the pavilion without so much as a squint at the scoreboard, and Gordon Fenner strangling the other end with nine maidens and 3 for 37 for good measure. Only J- Lock, unbeaten on 29, kept the innings from complete collapse, and 79 all out in 37.4 overs felt, frankly, generous of us to allow.

Chasing so modest a total ought to be simple, and for once it very nearly was. Ron Wratten batted with the calm of a man who knew the hard work was already done, seeing us home unbeaten on 35, with Robert Chance (21 not out) and Stan Wratten (20) doing the early legwork. We reached 80 for 1 in 32.5 overs without ever looking troubled.

A fine win by 9 wickets, then, and no false modesty required this week — Whittaker was magnificent, Fenner was miserly, and the rest of us simply had to turn up. Onwards.

Sat 17 Sep 1966 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · AwayformDDLWL

John Thirkell's Captain's Log — Bowaters CC, Saturday 17 September 1966

Lost the toss, asked to bowl. There are worse fates on a fine September afternoon, and so it proved. Brian Relf steamed in like a man with a train to catch, snaring 3-12, while Dick Langridge (2-16) and Benny Bell (2-38) chipped away at the other end. Terry Whittaker, meanwhile, bowled with the sort of miserly discipline that would make a Yorkshireman weep with pride — five maidens and barely a run to be had. Bowaters limped to 129/9 off their fifty-two overs, and we trooped off rather pleased with ourselves.

Chasing 130 should have been a formality, and true to club tradition we made it look anything but, stumbling to 1-1 before we'd barely settled into our deckchairs. Basil Sears steadied things with a fine 29, and I was fortunate enough to find some form myself, contributing 38 as the pair of us put on 61 for the second wicket to drag the game back onto an even keel. Poor Benny Bell endured the walk of shame with a golden duck — not his day with the bat, though nobody begrudges a man his 2-38 earlier on. We eventually got home by five wickets in 37.1 overs, with the minimum of fuss and the maximum of relief.

A tidy team performance all round — bowlers doing the hard yards, batters doing just enough. Five wickets, five o'clock finish, and a pint well earned. Onwards to the next one.

🗓️ 10 Sep – 16 Sep 1966 · 1966 — 2 matches

Sun 11 Sep 1966 🧢 1st XIv SC Staplehurst CC · AwayformDDDLW

Down at Staplehurst on a fine September Sunday, I won the toss and elected to bat — the right call, I felt, and I'll stand by that verdict even after what followed. Robert Chance played the innings of the day, a fine 72, while I chipped in with 35 and Basil Sears added a brisk 27; the two of us put on 79 for the second wicket to give the innings some backbone. Poor Frank Brattle will want to forget his golden duck, though the extras column did rather more for us than he did, 26 of them swelling the total to a respectable 181 for 8 off 48.5 overs.

Defending that, we made a decent fist of it. Gordon Fenner bowled beautifully for 2 for 31, two maidens among them, with Dick Langridge (2-46) and Paul Bowles (1-39) chipping away at the other end. But L. Murrell had other ideas, seeing his side home with an unbeaten 71, well supported by G. Murrell's 39 and W Tipples' 29, the pair adding 61 for the fourth wicket to put the game beyond us. Benny Bell had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, ten byes going astray, and Staplehurst got home by five wickets.

Small consolations amid the defeat: Dick Langridge's two wickets took him past Herbert Rayner into 14th on the club's all-time list, and I remain top of our 1966 run charts with 886 for the season. We'll be better for it next week.

Sat 10 Sep 1966 🧢 1st XIv RC Reeds CC · HomeformLDDDL

Saturday 10 September 1966 brought Reeds CC to Linton Park, and I lost the toss for the umpteenth time — they had no hesitation in batting first, and who can blame them on a decent wicket. Jepson gave us a proper working over with a classy 51, well supported by Dampier's 37, and when they'd added a crucial 57 for the third wicket I confess my heart sank a little in the covers. But cricket, like the English weather, turns quickly: from 101-4 they collapsed to 108-7 in the blink of an eye, Medhurst, Holmes and Franks all trudging back for ducks. Dick Langridge was the chief tormentor, ripping through them for 5-18 with two miserly maidens thrown in for good measure, while Paul Bowles (2-45) and Terry Whittaker (1-27) tidied up the rest. All out for 122 — a target we felt we could chase down with tea still warm in the pot.

Chasing it, poor Benny Bell departed without troubling the scorers, which was a blot on an otherwise tidy card. Thankfully a second-wicket stand of 62 set us on our way, and I was fortunate enough to find some form myself, seeing the innings through to an unbeaten 79 as we reached 123-4 to win by six wickets.

A thoroughly good day for the club, then — Dick's five-for the difference between a tense finish and a comfortable stroll, and not a bad way to keep the runs ticking over for the season.

Mon 29 Aug 1966 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · AwayformDLLDD

From the captain's chair — Monday 29 August 1966. We were away at Bearsted CC, and I confess I fancied our chances enough to win the toss and stick us in, a decision I'm rather pleased to report vindicated itself in the writing of this report if nothing else.

We batted first and made a solid fist of it, 240 all out from 71.2 overs, built on a splendid opening stand of 85 that set the tone nicely. John Ames played the anchor role beautifully for 59, with Don Clayton (49) and Terry Whittaker (33) chipping in handsomely. I found some form myself with the bat, contributing 25 down the order. Less gloriously, Fred Greenfield and Derry Evans both trudged back for ducks — the sort of walk of shame every clubman knows and none of us enjoy watching.

In the field, Dick Langridge was quite magnificent, spinning his way to 5-90 and thoroughly deserving every one of them, while I managed to get among the wickets too with 3-42. It wasn't quite enough to dislodge David Andrews, who made a superb, chanceless 109 and was well supported by Tony Snook's unbeaten 35 and Colin Fleetwood's 20 as Bearsted closed on 224/8. Nine byes told their own tale of a testing day behind the stumps for Fred.

A word of real cheer amid it all: William Buck brought up 6,000 career runs for the club, a landmark worth raising a glass to. In the end honours were shared — a draw, honestly earned by both sides, and no disgrace in that whatsoever.

Aylesford Paper Mills CC at home, and having called correctly at the toss I saw no earthly reason not to bat — a decision I stood by rather more stoutly than our top order did, as we lost a wicket with just 4 on the board and wobbled about wondering where the next run might come from.
