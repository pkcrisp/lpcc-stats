Sat 17 Aug 1940 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · AwayformWWWLL

Skipper's Log — Saturday 17 August 1940. The road took us to Charles Arkcoll, a pleasant enough journey for a game of cricket, and I confess I was not sorry to lose the toss for once, for their captain elected to bat and gave our bowlers first crack at a wicket that looked, to my eye, to hold a little for the seamer.

And hold it did. K Heathfield played the best innings of the day for the home side, a watchful 45 that alone kept them respectable, but around him the innings crumbled handsomely, from 24 for 2 to 40 for 5 as our attack found its rhythm. D Jolliffe, G Wratten and X Heard all departed without troubling the scorer, and J Short was the chief tormentor with a fine 3 for 12. I was glad to chip in with 3 for 33 myself, and Don Hartridge kept up the pressure with 2 for 20, so that Charles Arkcoll were dismissed for 110 in 23 overs, a total we felt we could chase down with sensible batting.

So it proved, though not without the usual alarms, our eleven getting home for 112 in 27.5 overs, a target reached with more nerve than grace but reached all the same. I made a useful 17 down the order. Worth recording too that J Short's 19 runs carried him past Leslie Wilson into 20th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, while Bert Buckoke's solitary wicket lifted him past J N Tonge into 15th among our wicket-takers, quiet milestones in a season Percy Brattle continues to lead with the bat and I am fortunate enough to lead with the ball. A fine, sporting contest, and a thoroughly satisfying win to bring home to the pavilion.

🗓️ 10 Aug – 16 Aug 1940 · 1940 — 1 match

Sat 10 Aug 1940 🧢 1st XIv T Tovil · HomeformDWWWL

We had first use of the ball against Tovil on the tenth of August, and for a good while it seemed a sound enough decision, though the visitors made us pay handsomely for it in the end. Day gave us a well-made fifty, batting with real assurance for his sixty-eight, and found a willing ally in White, who compiled a fifty of his own in equally fine style. Their third-wicket pair put on a crucial ninety-three, and though Epsley finished unbeaten on thirty-six, it was Short who kept us in the contest, bowling with real miserliness through two maiden overs to finish with two for thirty-three, well supported by Westover's one for thirty-four. Even so, one hundred and ninety-five for three off thirty-two overs was a formidable total to chase.

Our reply never gained a footing. The first wicket fell at six, and though Short played the innings of the day with a defiant twenty, wickets tumbled from sixty-nine for five to seventy-two for eight in a rush that undid all his good work. I must confess my own part in the affair was a sorry one, a golden blank that proved a captain does not always lead by example with the bat, and Smith and Westover kept me company in that unhappy company of ducks. Behind the stumps I fared little better, fifteen byes slipping past for the visitors' benefit.

There was, at least, cause for quiet pride: Short's twenty runs carried him past G J V Weigall into twenty-second on the club's all-time run-scorers list, while Brattle continues to lead our season's run-getters with four hundred and sixty-five, and Short himself stands fourth among the wicket-takers with fourteen. Beaten by one hundred and seventeen runs, we shall doubtless be the better for it next week.
