🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 2014 · 2014 — 1 match

Sat 27 Sep 2014 🧢 1st XIv KK Kennington CC, Kent · HomeformWWWWW

Saturday 27 September saw Kennington CC visit us on a warm afternoon that couldn't decide whether it wanted to be sunny or soggy, and I won the toss and elected to field — a decision I'm rather pleased to take credit for, since it's not every week the skipper gets to look clever. Kennington laboured to 153/9 from their forty overs, propped up by Chris Drakeley's fine unbeaten 58 and David Finch's 25, while at the other end John Cunningham and Oliver Matthews departed for ducks, poor souls. Martin Harris was the pick of the attack with 2-17, well backed by Joshua Knowler's 2-18, and I chipped in with 1-15 and a couple of miserly maidens of my own — enough to feel I'd earned my tea.

With 154 to chase, James Piper simply took the game away from Kennington, striking an unbeaten 73 that made light of the target, ably supported by Richard Piper's 28 and Will Thirkell's unbeaten 27, with a generous 26 extras thrown in for good measure. We cantered home by nine wickets in just 26.4 overs. There was a lovely moment of history too, as James brought up 6,000 career runs for the club — a milestone that deserved rather more than the polite ripple of applause it got from a rain-flecked boundary.

A thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round. Onwards.

Sun 21 Sep 2014 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · AwayformLWWWW

As we won the toss and put Tenterden in to bat, I'd best get straight to it.

Sunday 21st September, and off we trundled to Tenterden on a pleasant, grey afternoon perfectly suited to village cricket. The coin obliged and I had no hesitation in putting the opposition in — a decision that, with hindsight, we'd have done well to reconsider. Glen Rawson anchored the innings superbly with an unbeaten 56, well supported by Daniel Thirkell (28) and myself, also 28. Extras chipped in a hearty 29, which tells its own story about the discipline on show, while poor Richard Piper's golden duck was the one blemish on an otherwise respectable 181/7 from our 45.2 overs.

Defending it proved a bridge too far. James Allen played the innings of the day with a chanceless 58 not out, ushered along by Tim Russell's polished fifty and Mark Kokkinos's 42, and Tenterden strolled home on 182/3. We did have our moments — Darrell Houghton-Judge picked up 2-26, Martin Harris chipped in with 1-34, Will Thirkell bowled five miserly maidens, and Guy Day departed for a duck at our hands — but 20 byes past Glen Rawson's gloves rather summed up an afternoon where nothing quite clicked.

There was a crumb of comfort: my 28 nudged me past Jim Hall into 25th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and I remain top of our 2014 Sunday first-team run charts, with Martin Harris third among the wicket-takers. Small mercies. Beaten by seven wickets in the end — we'll be better for it next week.

Skipper's Log — Sunday 14 September 2014. Cobham CC, Kent, at home.

Two points, thoroughly earned, and a win by six wickets. With 231 runs for the season I find myself atop the run charts, and Martin Harris sits third among the wicket-takers with thirteen — proof, if any were needed, that this club still knows how to win ugly and smile about it afterwards.

Sun 07 Sep 2014 🧢 Sunday 2nd XIv HC Herne CC · HomeformLLWWW

Skipper's Log, Sunday 7th September 2014. Herne CC visited us on a pleasant, drizzly afternoon — the kind of weather that makes a captain's toss decision feel weightier than it has any right to. I called it right and elected to bat, and for a while it looked positively cunning: Tim Wright and Jack Gwynn put on 61 for the first wicket before the breakthrough came, Wright going on to a fine 45 with Gwynn's 22 in support. But Anwer had other ideas, ploughing on past his fifty to 76 in fine company with Richardson's 32, the pair adding a match-turning 105 for the second wicket. Poor Mark Gibson trudged off without troubling the scorers, but by then the damage was done, and Herne cantered home with five wickets and a few overs to spare.

Not our day, then. Wright's runs and wickets keep the season ticking along nicely on his personal ledger, but the rest of us must simply dust ourselves down, head back to the nets, and hope for drier conditions and firmer resolve next time out.
