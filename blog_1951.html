The Duck Club Gazette — Weekend of 29th and 30th September, 1951

5 ducks
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1951 – 30 Sep 1951
A brief lament opens this week's chronicle, for five of the Eleven's number found their way back to the pavilion without so much as a run to show for the outing. Two fixtures were played against Linton Park CC over the weekend, and the Zero Club receives its latest inductees with the solemnity the occasion demands. The Married Don Hartridge, at number five, was bowled by Ray Tippen for his second duck of the season and the seventh of a distinguished career, placing him ninth of 614 in the club's long roll of futility. He now stands level with the venerable A Startup, whose own seven ducks between 1868 and 1882 earned three Ducks of the Year and a place among the Ducks in a Row — kindred spirits separated by the better part of a century. Dennis Smith, at seven, was taken by Taylor off the bowling of Ray Tippen — his first duck of the season, but the fourth of a career now level with Bob Arnold's own tally. Mr Smith is, we note for the record, a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck fraternity from a past and entirely separate farewell. At number eleven, H Newport was bowled by Taylor for his third duck of the campaign, good enough to place him third on the season's own table of dismay, and his fourth career duck, joining the same gathering around Bob Arnold. Like Mr Smith, he too carries the Retirement Duck honour from an earlier innings altogether. The Singles Stan Wratten, at five, was bowled by Frank Taylor for his maiden duck of the season and the fifth of a career now standing twenty-second of 614, level with Allan Peach of the Edwardian era. The honours board also credits Wratten with a Quacking Together pairing alongside John Ames, struck in a season gone by. Richard Harris, at nine, fell to a smart piece of self-service from Dennis Smith, caught and bowled, for his fourth duck of the season and ninth of his career — a tally that places him a mere sixth of 614 all time, level with B Westover of the war years. His season, as the table below shows, is shaping into one of real distinction. Week in Summary Two matches, five ducks, and a sticky wicket for the home batting on both occasions. Tippen and Taylor between them accounted for four of the five dismissals, a fine afternoon's work for the visiting attack. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Arthur Cradduck — 19 innings, 5 ducks, 26.32% 2. Richard Harris — 13 innings, 4 ducks, 30.77% 3. H Newport — 9 innings, 3 ducks, 33.33% 4. John Thirkell — 27 innings, 3 ducks, 11.11% 5. J Enfield — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% The Club looks forward, as ever, to the next fixture and whatever fresh additions to the roll of nought it may bring. A straight bat, members are reminded, is no guarantee against these things — the pavilion door remains ever open.

The Duck Club Gazette — Saturday, 22nd September 1951

1 duck
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1951
A single fixture, a single wicket down for nought, but the Club's honours board never rests for long. Away at Hunton Wanderers CC, the eleven surrendered one entry to the exclusive zero club, and duty compels us to record it in full. 1st XI Coming in at first wicket down, John --'s Taylor essayed no stroke of note before being bowled by R Venner, and turned for the long walk back to the pavilion with the innings ball still gleaming. It is his first duck of the season and the third of a career now standing at forty-one innings, a tally that plants him a respectable fiftieth of six hundred and thirteen in the Club's long roll of the dismissed. He arrives in company: sitting on precisely the same career figure of three is A Kent, who in his brief single season of 1893 gathered his three ducks in only five innings and was crowned Duck of the Year for his trouble — proof, if proof were needed, that the art of not scoring has always rewarded efficiency over endurance. Week in Summary One match, one duck, and Hunton Wanderers CC may reflect that R Venner did his afternoon's work with admirable economy. A quiet week for the Club by its usual standards, though the season's grander campaigners continue their work below. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Arthur Cradduck — 18 innings, 5 ducks, 27.78% - Richard Harris — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.00% - John Thirkell — 26 innings, 3 ducks, 11.54% - J Enfield — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% - E Lockyer — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% Arthur Cradduck holds his customary place at the summit, though J Enfield's fifty per cent strike rate in nought-scoring is a sobering thing to contemplate over the winter. The season is not yet done, and neither, we may safely wager, is the Club's membership roll. Members are cordially invited to the next fixture, where the eleven will no doubt continue their diligent pursuit of the duck's egg with all the plucky application for which they are renowned.

The LPCC Duck Club Gazette — Sunday, 16th September, 1951

0 ducks
Week 21 · 16 Sep 1951
🦆🏏 Well now, members. A curious thing has happened, and it happened by not happening at all. Not a single batsman troubled the exclusive zero club this Sunday. Not one plucky soul walked out, faced his ball or balls, and walked straight back without so much as a single to show for the journey. The eleven, on this occasion, kept their bats and their dignity intact from top to tail — a state of affairs the Committee finds almost unsporting in its rarity. Whether this speaks to a sound wicket, a merciful bowling attack, or simply an afternoon when fortune smiled on Linton Park's willow-wielders, the Gazette cannot say. What we can say is that the season's duck hunters will have watched from the boundary with folded arms and nothing whatsoever to add to their ledgers this week. The nought, it seems, took Sunday off. Let it not be said, however, that the record book has gone hungry this season. Far from it. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - 🥇 Arthur Cradduck — 18 innings, 5 ducks, 27.78% — the season's most reliable early return, and no mistake - 🥈 Richard Harris — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.00% — a sticky wicket seems to follow the man about - 🥉 John Thirkell — 26 innings, 3 ducks, 11.54% — plenty of innings between his duck's eggs, which rather counts as restraint - J Enfield — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% — a coin-toss of a batting record if ever there was one - E Lockyer — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% — quietly building a case for the honours board yet A fine collection of gentlemen who know the art of not scoring better than most know the art of scoring, and to whom the Committee extends its warmest and most solemn congratulations, in absentia this week. So there it is, members — a blank page in the club's book of ignominy, though we harbour no illusions it shall stay that way for long. The next fixture will surely furnish the zero club with fresh recruits, and the Gazette will be waiting, pen in hand, to record every last one of them. Until then — mind the sticky wickets, and good batting to you all. 🍩0️⃣

The Duck Club Gazette — 8th and 9th September, 1951

2 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1951 – 9 Sep 1951
A modest instalment this week, but not without its charm: two eggs laid in the match against Gillingham CC of Kent, both by the home eleven's lower order, both accounted for by sharp work in the field. 1st XI - Tony Levick, sent in at number seven, was caught by F Saunders off the bowling of R Beckenham without troubling the scorers. It is his first duck of the season, and stands as his solitary entry in the Club's ledger of failure to date — one duck now sits against ten innings, placing him 234th of 613 in the club's long and honourable roll. He joins - Gaskin, a name from the 1949 season, on exactly one career duck apiece — kindred spirits separated by two years and joined in nothing. - Richard Harris, one place further down the order at eight, edged behind to R Davies off A Fairbrass and walked back for the third time this season without scoring — a sequence that speaks less of misfortune than of a settled and particular consistency. It is his eighth career duck from thirty-six innings, and it lifts him to a lofty seventh of 613 on the all-time list, a position most members can only dream of and rather fewer would wish for. In doing so he draws level with George Champion, who plied his trade between 1886 and 1912 and whose own eighth duck, taken in his 74th innings, earned him the Duck of the Year honour in 1897. The Week in Brief Two matches, two ducks, both claimed in the same fixture against Gillingham — the lower order once again shouldering the burden while their colleagues, presumably, looked on from the safety of the pavilion. A quiet week for the Club, but an instructive one: consistency, as ever, comes in many forms. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Arthur Cradduck — 5 ducks in 18 innings (27.78%) 2. Richard Harris — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.00%) 3. John Thirkell — 3 ducks in 26 innings (11.54%) 4. J Enfield — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.00%) 5. E Lockyer — 2 ducks in 7 innings (28.57%) The season's honours board remains keenly contested, and with fixtures yet to come there is every prospect of fresh names being added to the register before the year is out. Members are reminded that the eleven's next outing offers ample opportunity for further contributions to the Club's proud tradition of not troubling the scorers. The pavilion awaits.

Week 18 · 25 Aug 1951 – 26 Aug 1951
Recording the week of the 25th and 26th of August, 1951 Two fixtures were played this week, and between them the bowling sides could manage but a single duck's egg between them — a lean week by the Club's usual generous standards, yet the honours board is not so proud as to refuse a single addition. 1st XI - B Lloyd, sent in at number nine to shore up the tail, was bowled by Randolph Thompson in the away fixture against Charles Arkcoll on the 25th, and returned to the pavilion without a run to his name. It is his first duck of this season and the second of a career now three innings old, placing him a modest 58th of 613 upon the Club's long and honourable roll of the dismissed. Old members will recall that Lloyd is already a paid-up subscriber to the Debutante Duck club, his cricketing life having begun, some seasons since, in precisely this unpromising fashion — a man true to his origins. It is worth remarking, too, that on this very date in 1904 one T Woollett also strolled back for nought, against the Leeds Castle house eleven — the calendar, it would seem, has long borne a fondness for the twenty-fifth of August. A quiet week, then, for the connoisseur of the collapsed stump — two matches yielding but one sacrifice to the great cause, and the tail, on this occasion, wagging rather less than it fell. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard, 1951 - Arthur Cradduck — 17 innings, 5 ducks (29.41 per cent) - John Thirkell — 24 innings, 3 ducks (12.5 per cent) - J Enfield — 4 innings, 2 ducks (50.0 per cent) - E Lockyer — 7 innings, 2 ducks (28.57 per cent) - H Newport — 7 innings, 2 ducks (28.57 per cent) The season's leading exponents remain in fine and fruitless form, and members are reminded that Cradduck wants but a modest run of bad luck to extend his lead further still. The next fixture will doubtless furnish fresh candidates for these pages, and the Club awaits, as ever, with quiet and ungentlemanly anticipation.

Week 17 · 18 Aug 1951 – 19 Aug 1951
A record of the week's visits to the exclusive zero club, 18th–19th August, 1951 Two fixtures at home this week, and the eleven contributed three names to the ledger of the scoreless — the tail, as so often, doing its own quiet wagging. 1st XI Saturday, 18th August, host to F R Sutton's XI: Peter Weedon, promoted to number eight, played around one from J Toyer and was given lbw for nought. Not a first-ball dismissal by the record, merely a straight bat that found no runs behind it. It is his second duck of the summer and the fourth of a career now 22 innings deep, holding him at 34th of 613 on the club's roll of dubious distinction. Mr Weedon remains, and has long been, a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club — that solemn honour reserved for a career's presumed final innings ending on a duck's egg — though nothing this week suggests his innings with the club are done, only that the board awaits its moment. A pleasing coincidence attends him: on this very date in 1893, one Jack Fryer, twice Duck of the Year and twelve times a visitor to nought, was likewise trudging back to the pavilion, his against Eton Ramblers CC. Sunday, 19th August, host to Mote A, proved a sticky wicket for the lower order twice over. Richard Harris, at number eight, was trapped lbw by C. Horton without troubling the scorers — his second duck of the season and seventh of a career spanning 35 innings, a tally lofty enough to rank him eighth of 613, rarefied company indeed. At number eleven, E Lockyer followed him back, bowled by H. Hawkins for nought — also his second duck of the season, and the fourth in a brisk career of fifteen innings, 28th on the club's roll. Both gentlemen share good company across the years: on this same date in 1944, Herbert Rayner made his own duck against the R.A. Training Wing, Wrotham, a fellow traveller separated from them by seven summers exactly. The Week in Brief Two matches, three ducks, and the home dressing room rather better acquainted with nought than it might have wished — Sunday's Mote A fixture alone accounting for two of the week's three, a plucky lower order undone twice in an afternoon. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Arthur Cradduck — 5 ducks in 16 innings (31.25%) - John Thirkell — 3 ducks in 22 innings (13.64%) - J Enfield — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.00%) - E Lockyer — 2 ducks in 6 innings (33.33%) - H Newport — 2 ducks in 7 innings (28.57%) The season's leading names hold their ground, though the club extends as ever its warmest welcome to any member of the eleven minded to add his name to the list come the next fixture. The art of not scoring, after all, is never wholly mastered — there is always another sticky wicket waiting.

The Duck Report — 28th to 29th July, 1951

3 ducks
Week 14 · 28 Jul 1951 – 29 Jul 1951
🏏 Good evening, members. The 1st XI took the long road to Old Williamsonians on the 28th and came home with three names for the register — a sound reminder that a straight bat is no guarantee against a straighter ball. Away form continues to suit the collectors rather better than the collected. 1st XI John Thirkell strolled to the crease at four and departed as he arrived, undone by Bowring for his 2nd duck of the season and the 5th of a career now 52 innings deep — good enough for 21st on the all-time roll of dishonour, and a man who already keeps company on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, besides sharing "Quacking Together" honours with both Bob Arnold and William Buck. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1897, one Ernest Cruttenden also trudged back for nought against Brenchley CC — the calendar, it seems, has long memory for a duck's egg. Arthur Cradduck, sent in at eight, fell to the same Bowring for his 4th duck of a rampaging season and his 5th career duck in a mere 19 innings — form that has already earned him the Duck of the Year 1951 board, and at this rate he may retain it by Michaelmas. He too shares the curious distinction of a duck on the 28th of July matching Cruttenden's 1897 vintage — a date, it would seem, with a certain reputation. Bert Buckoke, last man in at eleven, was cleaned up by Trower for the 1st duck of his season, but his career column tells the graver tale — an 11th career duck from 43 innings, good for a lofty 3rd on the all-time list, with Duck of the Year honours banked in both 1928 and 1950. A true elder statesman of the exclusive zero club, for whom the walk back to the pavilion has become almost a matter of routine. The Week in Summary Two matches, three ducks, and Old Williamsonians' bowlers Bowring and Trower doing the club's collecting for it — a plucky Saturday undone twice by the same hand. No pairs, no king pairs, and no family affairs this week, though with Cradduck's rate of return one wonders how long that can last. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Arthur Cradduck — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% - J Enfield — 3 innings, 2 ducks, 66.67% - H Newport — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.00% - Gordon Bugge — 10 innings, 2 ducks, 20.00% - Bob Arnold — 11 innings, 2 ducks, 18.18% Until the next fixture, when no doubt further candidates shall present themselves for admission to this most dubious of distinctions — the eleven plays on, and the ducks, one suspects, are already limbering up.

The LPCC Duck Gazette — for the week of 21st and 22nd July, 1951

2 ducks
Week 13 · 21 Jul 1951 – 22 Jul 1951
A modest week for the exclusive zero club, but a week nonetheless, and the eleven's opening pair supplied both entries between them. The First XI Against Town Malling CC on the 21st, the innings began in the poorest of fashions. Basil Sears, opening the batting, and William Buck, sent in first wicket down beside him, were both undone by the identical hand — b M- Wheatley to each — the pair back at the pavilion gate almost before the scoreboard had properly turned over. A sticky start to any innings, and Wheatley may fairly claim the morning's honours, though it is not a wicket the club enters in its own book. For Sears this is the season's first duck's egg, and the second of a career now standing at thirty-three innings, a tally that lodges him sixtieth of six hundred and thirteen in the club's long and undistinguished roll of nought-getters. On this very day in 1897, one H G Smith performed the identical feat against Maidstone CC, so Sears keeps rather venerable company down the years. For Buck the story is briefer but no less final, his very first career duck in twenty-one innings, placing him two hundred and forty-second of six hundred and thirteen upon the all-time list. He is besides a paid-up member of the Quacking Together honours board, sharing that distinction with John Thirkell from an earlier occasion, and needs no repeat performance this week to keep his place there. Buck too finds himself in Smith's 1897 anniversary company, the same duck against Maidstone recalled once more, fifty-four years on almost to the day. The Week in Brief Two matches were played in the period, and the zero club received exactly two new subscriptions, both from the same Saturday afternoon at Town Malling and both from the same bowler's hand. A sticky wicket for the top of the order, though the remainder of the innings, mercifully, falls outside this correspondent's remit. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard The season's most dedicated collectors, ranked in the art of not scoring: - Arthur Cradduck — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% - J Enfield — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100.0% - H Newport — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.0% - Gordon Bugge — 9 innings, 2 ducks, 22.22% - Bob Arnold — 10 innings, 2 ducks, 20.0% J Enfield's record remains the season's most complete, a full and unbroken century of failure against every innings played thus far. Until the next fixture, when the eleven will doubtless furnish the book with fresh material. The duck's egg, as ever, waits for no man.

0 ducks
Week 12 · 15 Jul 1951
A quiet week at Linton Park, and one must report it plainly: not a single duck was laid across the fixture played in this period. The eleven turned out, the overs were bowled, and by whatever conspiracy of application or plain good fortune, every batsman contrived to trouble the scorers before departing. The exclusive zero club held no induction ceremony this week — its members may rest easy, its waiting list unchanged. It happens, now and then, that the ducks evade the net entirely, and a club built on the celebration of the nought must simply note the drought and look to next Saturday with quiet confidence that form, in this particular art, is rarely absent for long. The pitch remains sticky in patches, and reputations built on adversity are not undone by a single week of runs. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard The season's honours remain undisturbed, and the standings are a reminder that the art of not scoring is pursued with real dedication in some quarters: - Arthur Cradduck — 11 innings, 3 ducks, a rate of 27.27 per cent, and a fine consistency of a very particular kind - J Enfield — 2 innings, 2 ducks, a perfect 100 per cent, a beginning as pure as it is unfortunate - H Newport — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40 per cent, keeping honest company near the top - Gordon Bugge — 8 innings, 2 ducks, 25 per cent, a steady if unspectacular contribution to the cause - Bob Arnold — 10 innings, 2 ducks, 20 per cent, proof that the nought comes for gentlemen of experience as readily as for the newcomer Members are warmly reminded that the season is young yet, and the eleven takes the field again next Saturday. History suggests the ducks are merely gathering their strength — do come along, for the next fixture is unlikely to pass so serenely, and the honours board waits for no man.

Let it be recorded plainly: a week without a duck is a rare thing indeed, and the Committee trusts the Eleven will not make a habit of it.
