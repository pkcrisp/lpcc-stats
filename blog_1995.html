🦆 Duck News — 30 September 1995

0 ducks
Week 23 · 30 Sep 1995
A single fixture took to the field this week, and for once — against all tradition, against the very spirit of the club — not a single duck was laid. Eleven batsmen went out, and eleven batsmen, infuriatingly, got off the mark. The zero club stands unrecruited. The exclusive membership rolls remain unchanged. Somewhere, a scorer is disappointed. We shan't pretend this isn't a little awkward to report. The Duck Club exists to celebrate triumph through adversity, the art of not scoring, and this week adversity simply declined to show up. Consider it a rain check on ignominy — the ducks are out there, and they will return. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 27 innings, 6 ducks, 22.22%. The season's clear pace-setter, quietly assembling a collection most would rather not admit to. - Michael Beeney — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67%. Efficient work — over a quarter of his innings end in the traditional fashion. - Ian Brattle — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60.0%. A frankly staggering strike rate in the wrong direction — three ducks from five knocks is the kind of consistency the honours board was built for. - Daniel Thirkell — 14 innings, 3 ducks, 21.43%. Keeping pace nicely in the chasing pack. - Stuart Cheesman — 22 innings, 3 ducks, 13.64%. The volume player of the group — patient accumulation of noughts across a long campaign. Ian Brattle's 60% deserves a special mention — at that rate he's arguably more reliable failing than most of the club is succeeding. 📅 Looking ahead A quiet week for the collection, but the season isn't done with us yet. Bat carefully, gentlemen — or don't, and give the Duck Club correspondent something to write about. Next fixture, normal service will very likely resume. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 23 September 1995 🦆

0 ducks
Week 22 · 23 Sep 1995
A single fixture on the books this week, and I bring news that will unsettle the purists: not a single duck to report. Eleven batsmen went to the crease and eleven batsmen, by some conspiracy of application or plain good fortune, got off the mark. The zero club held no induction ceremony this week. Make no mistake — this is not good news. The art of not scoring is a proud Linton tradition, and a blank weekend is a stain on the collective average, not a badge of honour. Somewhere out there a bowler will be feeling very hard done by. Still, the season standings roll on regardless, and the race for Duck of the Year remains very much alive. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏆 1. Matt Large — 6 ducks from 27 innings (22.22%) 2. Michael Beeney — 4 ducks from 15 innings (26.67%) 3. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks from 5 innings (60.00%) 🍩 4. Daniel Thirkell — 3 ducks from 13 innings (23.08%) 5. Stuart Cheesman — 3 ducks from 22 innings (13.64%) Special mention to Ian Brattle, whose 60% strike rate in the failure-to-score department puts the rest of the top five to shame — three ducks from just five innings is consistency of a very particular kind, and he leads the field by some distance on pure efficiency. Matt Large sits comfortably atop the pile for now, but with Michael Beeney breathing down his neck and a fixture list still to come, this one is far from settled. So — evaded this week, but not forever. The Duck Club committee watches, waits, and fully expects the next scorecard to make amends. Get out there, middle stump, and give us something to celebrate. 0️⃣🏏🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 9-10 September 1995

4 ducks
Week 20 · 9 Sep 1995 – 10 Sep 1995
Four fresh entries for the exclusive zero club this weekend, spread across three fixtures as the 1995 season runs down to the wire. The art of not scoring remains alive and well at Linton Park. Saturday 9 September 1st XI away to Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC Michael Beeney went first, caught Jim Chaplin off the bowling of Michael Glazier at number nine — his 4th duck of the campaign and 4th of a growing career from 15 innings, good enough for 153rd on the all-time list. A long-serving member of the Only Two Quacks honours reel alongside Ian Brattle, and no stranger to the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell innings either. On this very day in 1977, Andrew Moss (1968–1987, 10 ducks, 73rd all-time) was doing exactly the same thing against Cornwall Crusaders. 2nd XI at home to Borden CC Ollie Priestly edged Roy King through to Martin Noakes after eight balls of defiance — his 2nd of the season and 3rd career duck from just five innings, propelling him to 201st overall. A Debutante Duck alumnus from way back. Andrew Moss, again, provides the "on this day" company from 1977. Then came the real fireworks. B Powell, on debut, was run out without troubling the scorers — a proper golden duck, gone before he'd had time to take guard mentally. First-ever senior innings, first-ever duck: an instant Debutante Duck, and with career ducks and career innings both sitting at a tidy one apiece, he walks straight into the One and Done club for 1995 too. Rank 333 of 1208 and rising fast, presumably. Andrew Moss's 1977 vintage strikes a third time as the day's constant companion. Sunday 10 September 1st XI at home to Crowborough CC Richard Ellis was bowled by A Ball for nought at number five — his 2nd duck of the season and 4th career, from 17 innings, 159th on the all-time roll. Long ago inducted into both the Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack honours boards from a debut-and-farewell double in a previous life at the club. On this day in 1955, John --'s Taylor (1949–1960, 15 ducks, 51st all-time) also fell for a duck, against Old Roffensians. Week in summary Four ducks from three matches, one of them the purest kind — a golden duck on debut for B Powell, who has managed the rare trick of joining three honours boards in a single innings. Beeney's fourth of the season keeps him snapping at the heels of the season leader. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 27 inns, 6 ducks (22.2%) 2. Michael Beeney — 15 inns, 4 ducks (26.7%) 3. Ian Brattle — 5 inns, 3 ducks (60.0%) 4. Daniel Thirkell — 12 inns, 3 ducks (25.0%) 5. Stuart Cheesman — 21 inns, 3 ducks (14.3%) Matt Large holds firm at the top, but Michael Beeney is closing. Roll on next weekend — the ducks never take a rest, and neither do we. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 3rd to 6th September 1995

2 ducks
Week 19 · 3 Sep 1995 – 6 Sep 1995
A quiet corner of the calendar, but two fixtures still found a way to add to the club's ledger of noughts. One at home, one on tour in Cornwall — the Duck Club never takes a fixture off. Midweek XI v The Mote CC (away), 6th September Richard Piper strode out at number seven and departed for a duck, bowled by Nigel Mullarkey. It's his 1st of the season and takes him to 9 career ducks in 24 innings — a healthy 86th of 1207 in the all-time reckoning. Richard is a paid-up member of some of the club's grander standing honours: Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11, no stone left unturned), plus top-10 residency in both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck charts — a man equally committed to failure home and away. He's also in good company down the family tree, with parent Brian Piper sitting on 20 career ducks of his own — clearly a trait, not an accident. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1967, Roger Craddock (1966–1968) also trudged off for nought against Birchington CC — same calendar date, 28 years apart, same result. Tour XI v Beacon CC, Cornwall (away), 3rd September Daniel Thirkell fell for a duck too, caught Larry Martin, bowled D Wheatley, at number seven. It's his 3rd of the season and 3rd of his career from just 15 innings — 176th of 1207 all-time, and enough to make an early case in this season's race. He carries the Tourist Duck top-10 badge, entirely appropriate given the circumstances, and arrives with serious pedigree — the Thirkell name is practically a duck dynasty at this club, with John (90), Tim (66), father Nigel (49) and Simon (33) all decorated members, plus Stuart just getting started on 1. The zero club clearly runs in the blood. On this same day in 1972, Benny Bell (1966–1975) also made a duck, against Staplehurst CC — another entry in the club's long tradition of scoring nothing on schedule. Week in summary Two matches, two ducks, one at home and one abroad — a modest harvest, but a tidy one. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this week, just two solid, honest contributions to the art of not scoring. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 27 innings, 6 ducks, 22.22% - Ian Brattle — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60.00% - Daniel Thirkell — 10 innings, 3 ducks, 30.00% - Michael Beeney — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% - Stuart Cheesman — 19 innings, 3 ducks, 15.79% Daniel Thirkell's Cornish contribution keeps him firmly in the season's top five — worth watching as the race for Duck of the Year enters its business end. Fixtures keep coming, and so, inevitably, will the ducks. Bats at the ready, gentlemen — the exclusive zero club is always recruiting. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 26 August

3 ducks
Week 18 · 26 Aug 1995 – 1 Sep 1995
A quieter week in the hunting grounds — just three across seven fixtures — but each one earns its place in the ledger, and one of our regulars manages to appear on the honours page twice in the same report without actually doing anything twice. Let's get into it. Saturday 26 August 1st XI away at Marden CC — Rob Doick departed for nought at number five, stumped by Nigel Bardsley off the bowling of T Rudd. It's his 1st duck of the season and 14th of an increasingly distinguished career, from 103 innings, sitting 56th of 1206 in the all-time list. Neat bit of calendar symmetry here too — on this very day back in 1979, club legend Tim Thirkell (1967–1995, 66 ducks, 4th all-time, Duck of the Year 1972 among a shelf of other honours) also trudged off against Old Anchorians CC. Different ground, different bowler, same date, same result. Sunday 27 August 1st XI at home to Old Anchorians CC — Richard Ellis fell for 0 at first drop, bowled by B Carter. His 1st of the season, 3rd for his career from 15 innings, ranked 203 of 1206. Richard is already a fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club — his very first senior innings, years back, was also a blob — and carries the Retirement Duck honour in waiting on the shelf too, though nobody's calling time on anything today. Fittingly, his on-this-day parallel is none other than Mike Elworthy, who ducked against British Rail Ashford on this date back in 1983 — and who, as it happens, adds fresh ink to this very report just three days later. Keep reading. Wednesday 30 August — Tour Match Away at Truro CC, the touring party found Mike Elworthy bowled by Les Driver for a duck. His 1st of the season and 17th of a well-travelled career from 195 innings, 47th of 1206 all-time, and a proud resident of the Tourist Duck top 10 — entirely appropriate, given where he was standing when it happened. On this day in 1965, Frank Brattle (1937–1971, 38 ducks, 14th all-time) was doing much the same thing against Bearsted CC. The art of not scoring, it seems, travels well. Week in Summary Three ducks, seven matches, no pairs and no family affairs this time round — the bloodlines behaved themselves for once. Two came at Linton, one on tour, and Old Anchorians CC turn up twice in the parallels despite not fielding a single duck of their own this week. Make of that what you will. Next round of fixtures awaits — the exclusive zero club never closes its books for long. 🦆🍩0️⃣

4 ducks
Week 17 · 19 Aug 1995 – 20 Aug 1995
Three matches, four ducks, and one brand new member sworn into the exclusive zero club before he'd even faced a second ball. A tidy little haul to close out the season's business end. 1st XI Saturday at Harrietsham CC, and David Castle departed at first drop for nought, caught Micky Tong bowled Kevin Grebbell without so much as troubling the scoreboard operator. His 2nd duck of the season, 22nd of a long and distinguished career, and enough to hold station at 36th of 1205 in the all-time list — a proper stalwart of the Tourist Duck top 10, doing his best work strictly on the road. On this very day back in 1956, John --'s Taylor was up to the same trick, one of 15 ducks in a career that ran 1949 to 1960. David wasn't done. Sunday at Whitstable CC he opened the innings and fell again, bowled M Wells for a duck to match the first — a genuine pair across the weekend, the art of not scoring practised on consecutive days against consecutive attacks. Season tally moves to 3, career to 23, still 36th all-time. On this day in 1944, W Bunting was doing precisely the same against KCC Maidstone. Also at Whitstable, Daniel Thirkell edged Lorne Hart's bowling into safe hands for nought at number 8 — his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from just 11 innings, a healthy start to a family tradition. Daniel joins a Thirkell household already heaving with silverware of this particular kind: John (90 career ducks), Tim (66), Nigel (49), and Simon (33) all keep the family name well represented on the honours board — Daniel has some catching up to do, but he's off the mark early. Another Tourist Duck top 10 entry, and another 1944-vintage companion in W Bunting. 2nd XI And now, ceremony. At home to Harrietsham CC, Gavin Saunders walked out at number 10, was run out for nought, and thereby claimed the rarest of openings — a duck on debut. First innings, first duck, immediate and automatic induction into the Debutante Duck club, no probationary period required. Whether this marks the beginning of a long and fruitful membership or a one-and-done cameo, only time will tell. On this day in 1956, John --'s Taylor also made nought, for whatever comfort that's worth. The Week in Brief Four ducks from three matches, headlined by a debut blob and a weekend pair — consistency of a very particular kind from the top of the Castle order. No king pairs, no golden ducks, and the family-duck ledger stays quiet this week, though the Thirkells remain very much in business for another day. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 3 ducks, 27 innings, 11.11% Ian Brattle's 60% strike rate remains the standard nobody wants to chase. Next fixture, the ducks will no doubt be looking for fresh recruits — bring your bat, and try to use it. 🦆🏏0️⃣

A rare and troubling weekend at Linton Park. Three matches played, three matches came and went, and not a single soul was sent packing for nought. 3 ducks
Week 14 · 29 Jul 1995 – 31 Jul 1995
A modest harvest this week — just three ducks netted from four matches — but two of them fell in exactly the same game, to exactly the pair the honours board already had earmarked for each other. The zero club does love a bit of symmetry. 🏏 1st XI — away to Clarion CC Michael Beeney, at number 8, was snapped up by Steve Harris off P Elsden's bowling for his 3rd duck of the season and, handily, his 3rd career duck from 11 innings — placing him 195th of 1,203 in the all-time reckoning. Beeney is a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, a distinction that says nothing whatsoever about today. On this very day back in 1989, Nigel Thirkell also trudged off for nought, against The Mote CC, en route to a career haul of 49 and 8th place all-time. Two batsmen later, Ian Brattle joined him in the queue, bowled by the same P Elsden for his own 3rd duck of the season — and his 35th career duck from 103 innings, a lofty 16th of 1,203 all-time. Brattle carries serious pedigree here: a member of the "50 Before a 50" club (51 ducks, high score 33), a Debutante Duck from way back, and the proud holder of 7 Ducks in a Row. His sibling Paul isn't far behind either, sitting on 26 career ducks of his own — clearly a family gift. And here's the pleasing bit: Beeney and Brattle share the exclusive "Only Two Quacks" honour with one another — and today, for once, they actually earned it together, both back in the pavilion inside the same Clarion CC innings. A proper reunion of the club's most devoted double act. 🏏 Midweek XI — Cleethorpes CC (home) At number 3, David Castle was caught by Will Abe off J Davies for his 1st duck of the season, a rare blot on an otherwise remarkably durable record — 21 career ducks from 379 innings is the kind of restraint most of the club can only dream of. That puts him 40th of 1,203 all-time, and a fixture of the Tourist Duck top 10. On this same date in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden made his own duck against Headcorn CC, finishing his career on 10 and 76th all-time — proof the art of not scoring has been practised at Linton Park for well over a century. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks, four matches, and a tidy little coincidence at Clarion CC to show for it. No pairs, no golden ducks, no family affairs this week — just honest toil in the nought column. The zero club remains open for business. ⭐ Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 21 inns, 6 ducks (28.6%) 2. Ian Brattle — 5 inns, 3 ducks (60.0%) 3. Stuart Cheesman — 9 inns, 3 ducks (33.3%) 4. Michael Beeney — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.3%) 5. Piers McDonald — 24 inns, 3 ducks (12.5%) Ian Brattle's 60% strike rate is one to watch as the season enters its final stretch — can anyone catch him before the last ball is bowled? Roll on the next fixture, and with it, the next crop of noughts. 🦆🏏0️⃣

Five matches, five ducks, and one thumping great milestone at Linton Park this week. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees, and one very old member reaches a summit few will ever see. Read on. 1st XI Clive Newman got as far as 7 deliveries away at Tenterden CC before Bill Lacey pouched him off Graham Richards — a duck of some resistance, if such a thing exists. His first of the season, fourth of a career now 102 innings deep, and good enough for 143rd of 1,203 in the all-time reckoning. On this very day in 1960, Brian Relf also departed scoreless against Tovil — a man who managed the rare Quack-to-Quack of duck on debut and duck on farewell, with a Retirement Duck to match. Two days later at Hartley Country Club, Piers McDonald was bowled by Justin Crouch for his third of the season and 28th of a career that now sits proudly at 27th all-time — a fully paid-up member of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. On this same date in 1982, none other than Trevor Back made his own duck against The Mote CC. Keep that name in mind — he's not done with us yet this week. 2nd XI At home to Linden Park CC, Chris Tymms was caught behind by Mark Wilson off Mash Patel — his first duck of the summer, sixth of a career still only 26 innings young, slotting him in at 107th all-time. On this day in 1973, Cliff Anscombe — Debutante Duck and Duck of the Year 1957 — fell the same way against Sevenoaks Vine CC. Midweek XI — a Hall of Fame afternoon And so to the big one. Facing Ipswich Greyhounds CC on the Kent Tour, Matt Large was caught and bowled by K Greenway — a routine-looking dismissal that in fact delivered his 50th career duck. Full Membership of the Duck Club, secured in style, and now the club's 5th all-time practitioner of the art of not scoring. This is his sixth of the season already, leading the pack on a startling 30% duck rate from 20 innings, and it adds a fresh Duck of the Year 1995 to a mantelpiece already groaning under 1987, 1988, 1989, 1992 and 1993. On this day in 1940, J Short made his own lone duck against Boughton Monchelsea CC. Batting one place behind him, Trevor Back — yes, the very same from Piers's parallel above — edged the very next over from K Greenway, caught by I Croton. His second duck of the season and 42nd of a career spanning 343 innings, 11th on the all-time list, and a stalwart of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. K Greenway can rightly claim a Linton brace for the week. Week in summary Five ducks from five fixtures — a modest harvest by volume, but one that carried Linton's most decorated modern name past the half-century. Two of the five fell in the same over at the hands of the same bowler, proof that misery, like tea, is best shared. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 20 innings, 6 ducks, 30.0% 2. The exclusive zero club never closes its books — see you at the crease. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report: 15–21 July 1995

1 duck
Week 12 · 15 Jul 1995 – 21 Jul 1995
Five matches played across the week and the Duck Club almost went hungry — but almost is the operative word. Just the one nought fell, and it fell with proper craftsmanship.
