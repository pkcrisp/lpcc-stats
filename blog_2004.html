🦆 Duck News — 26 September 2004

2 ducks
Week 24 · 26 Sep 2004
The end-of-season card threw up a curious pair of fixtures on the 26th — Russell Baldwin's XI facing off against Matt Large's XI, and vice versa — and the zero club, ever reliable, made sure neither side left empty-handed. 🏏 1st XI Russell Baldwin, walking in at seven against Matt Large's side, departed caught off an unsure bowler to an unsure fielder — cricket's great mystery genre. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 14th for his LPCC career from 162 innings, moving him to 67th of 1305 in the all-time standings. On this very day back in 1965, Roy Clarke was doing exactly the same thing against Linton Park CC — a 40-duck career (1957–1966) that also yielded a Duck of the Year gong in 1959 and a place in the Quacking Together honours alongside John Ames. Fine company to keep on a Sunday afternoon of mutual dismissal. Opening for the other side against Russell Baldwin's XI, Matthew Carter went early again — same mysterious "c Unsure b Unsure" verdict, evidently the umpire's handwriting was having an off day too. It's his 6th duck of the season already, and his 11th career nought from just 39 innings, vaulting him to 83rd of 1305 all-time. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club — his very first LPCC innings was, fittingly, a duck — Matthew is also flying the flag for Duck of the Year 2004, and on this evidence the field will need to find another gear to catch him. He too joins Roy Clarke in the on-this-day ledger — same date, same opponent, same glorious nothing, 39 years apart. 📊 Week in Summary A tidy little haul: 2 ducks from 2 matches, both from the 1st XI, both undone by the enigmatic "Unsure" — surely due an honorary LPCC cap of his own by now. Matthew Carter's effort was the standout, a second duck of the campaign inside what looks like a very busy final few weeks of 2004. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matthew Carter — 28 inns, 6 ducks (21.43%) - Richard Piper — 32 inns, 3 ducks (9.38%) - Clive Newman — 12 inns, 2 ducks (16.67%) - Simon Thirkell — 12 inns, 2 ducks (16.67%) - Jack Jenner — 13 inns, 2 ducks (15.38%) Matthew Carter sits well clear at the top with the season drawing in — can anyone mount a late charge for the 2004 crown? 🥇0️⃣ Next fixture, same invitation as ever: pads on, guard taken, and here's hoping — for entirely dubious reasons — that the zero club welcomes a few more members. See you out there. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — Week of 18–22 September 2004

1 duck
Week 23 · 18 Sep 2004 – 22 Sep 2004
A quiet week in the paddock — three matches on the road, and the ducks found exactly one taker. Sometimes scarcity is its own kind of story. Midweek XI — Wednesday, away to Penshurst Park CC Jim Hall came to the crease at six and fell lbw to Richie Rudden, his 1st duck of the season and a startling 76th of an already storied career across 479 innings. That tally holds him at a rarefied #3 of 1304 in the all-time standings — a spot most club cricketers will only ever admire from a respectful distance. Jim's honours cabinet is not short of silver either — three separate Duck of the Year crowns (1970, 1977 and 1985), permanent residency in the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck, Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and — perhaps cruellest of all — "Full House nearly-man" status, having ducked at ten of the eleven batting positions and still chasing that elusive last one. He's shared quacks down the years too, in Quacking Together company with Jon Harris, Martin Mears and Tim Thirkell, and Only Two Quacks pairings with Allan Cooper and Nigel Thirkell. Neatly, on this very day back in 1977, LPCC legend John Thirkell — the club's all-time #1 with 90 career ducks, playing from 1943 to 1977 — was also walking back for nought, against West Kent Wanderers CC. Some habits echo down the decades. The other two fixtures this week were, disappointingly, a fowl-free zone — every bat in both line-ups found at least one run. The art of not scoring took the week off. 📊 Week in Summary 1 duck from 3 matches — thin pickings for the flock, but Jim Hall made sure the club's noble tradition wasn't allowed to lapse entirely. 🦆 Duck Report — 11-12 September 2004

3 ducks
Week 22 · 11 Sep 2004 – 12 Sep 2004
Slim pickings this weekend from the Duck Hunting fields — just three additions to the ledger across three fixtures — but each one is worth its place in the register. 🏏 Saturday 11 September — 2nd XI away at Teston CC A rough afternoon at the top of the order for the seconds. Christopher Thirkell, opening up, fell to Geoff King without so much as a squeak — his 2nd duck of the season and the 13th of an increasingly decorated Linton career, now 91 innings deep and sitting 68th of 1,304 in the all-time table. Christopher remains that most tantalising of things, a "Full House nearly-man" — ducks logged in 10 of the 11 batting positions, today's number 1 doing nothing whatsoever to complete the set. He keeps fine company on 13: level with 1940s stalwart Derek Pantony and modern-era regular Russell Baldwin. And by happy calendar coincidence, on this very date back in 1991, twice Duck of the Year Fred Cheesman was also strolling back against The Mote CC. Moments later Chris Newman followed him through the gate, bowled by Dan Tyler at number 2 — his 1st duck of the season and just his 2nd in a Linton career still finding its feet (14 innings, rank 248 of 1,304). He shares that same 11 September 1991 date with Christopher above, so Fred Cheesman gets a second mention for the price of one. Worth noting Chris carries decorated ducking blood too — kin Clive Newman already has 16 career noughts to his name, and pops up in this week's season top five below. 🌱 Sunday 12 September — Sunday 1st XI at home to Northend Sunday Nigel Thirkell, in at four, was bowled by K Stapley for his 2nd duck of the season and the 57th of a genuinely epic career — 1,104 innings and counting, good enough for 7th on the all-time Linton roll of dishonour. A Duck of the Year winner back in 1986 and a permanent fixture of both the Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens, Nigel continues to make not scoring look like a full-time occupation. On this same 12 September, all the way back in 1959, S Lusher was doing precisely the same thing against Bowaters CC — a duck echoing 45 years down the calendar. It rather looks like the Thirkell name was doing brisk business this weekend — Christopher on Saturday, Nigel on Sunday — though with the ducks falling in separate matches, the shared honours board will have to wait for another day. 📊 The Weekend in Brief Three ducks, three matches, no pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs to report — just three honest, solitary noughts. Teston CC did the early damage taking two in one innings before Northend Sunday completed the hat-trick of misery. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1.
