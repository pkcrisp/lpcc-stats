🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1978 · 1978 — 2 matches

Sun 01 Oct 1978 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWLWW

There are days when the toss feels like a formality, and Sunday was not one of them. I called correctly, took first knock without a second thought, and for once the decision looked good from the very first over.

We batted our way to 148/9 from the allotted 32, a total built on the sort of patient, old-fashioned club cricket that makes a captain proud. John Ames set the tone with a lovely 56, and Brian Woodcock matched him stride for stride with 55 of his own — two half-centuries in the same innings is not something we manage every week, and I shan't pretend otherwise. Terry Donovan finished unbeaten on 34, shepherding the tail with the calm of a man who had seen it all before. I chipped in with the bat myself, and with 316 runs now to my name this season I find myself sitting third in the 1st XI charts, which I mention once and shall not mention again. Young Jon Harris's 22 deserves its own line too — enough to leapfrog Brian Piper into 23rd on the club's all-time run list, a quiet piece of history made on an otherwise ordinary Sunday.

In the end neither side could find a way through, and honours were shared. A draw is a funny old result — not quite victory, not quite defeat, rather like coming home from the pub having neither won nor lost at darts. But with knocks like these on the board, I'll happily take it.

Sat 30 Sep 1978 🧢 1st XIv Lordswood CC · HomeformDDWLW

Saturday 30 September 1978, and Lordswood CC rolled up as our final visitors of the season. I lost the toss — no great surprise, my coin-calling has never troubled the record books — and we were put in the field first.

The lads did us proud with the ball. Lordswood ground their way to 142/6 off 49 overs, Colin Gardener the chief architect with a well-made 57, nursed along by v- Corbin's unbeaten 24, the pair adding 69 for the third wicket while the rest of us stood about admiring their application. John Bloomfield was the pick of the attack by some distance — 2 for 23 with four maidens, not a run given away lightly, the sort of spell that makes a captain's job look easy. Trevor Back (2-39) and Paul Bowles (1-21) chipped in nicely too.

Set 143 to win, we made rather light work of it. Tim Thirkell — who now leads our 1978 run charts with 644 for the season, and looks in no mood to be caught — carried the innings with a chanceless 74 not out, while Paul Bowles, clearly not content with just bowling well, added a fine 59 of his own. The pair put on 123 for the third wicket and left the rest of the order to watch from the pavilion with cups of tea.

Home by seven wickets with overs in hand — a thoroughly good day for the club, and not a bad note to end September on.

Wed 27 Sep 1978 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformWWLDD

Here's the match report:

I'll admit the toss going against us on that Wednesday at The Mote set the tone for a long, patient afternoon in the field. We stuck to our task well enough, though — The Mote posted 198/5 from their 46.4 overs, with John Munn's 45 the backbone of the innings, nicely backed up by Malcolm Bristow's 31 and an unbeaten 31 from Ken Emmett. I managed to find a bit of form with the ball myself, picking up 3-59, while Trevor Back and Allan Cooper chipped in a wicket apiece — Cooper bowling especially tightly, with three maidens to show for his miserly spell.

Chasing 199, we never quite got the wheels turning. Losing our first wicket for just 1 run is not the platform any captain dreams of, and when both Paul Bowles and Malcolm Pay departed without troubling the scorers, it looked grim. Brian Piper, though, played a lovely knock — 60 in all, the innings of the day — with Trevor Back's 23 and an unbeaten 20 from Allan Cooper lending real support. As for my own contribution with the bat, the less said the better: nought, and we shall draw a veil over it. We closed on 155/8 from our 44, short of the target.

So honours shared, and perhaps rightly so — neither side did quite enough to prise the game away from the other. A draw that felt, on reflection, a fair reward for two sides who gave as good as they got.

Sun 24 Sep 1978 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDDDWL

Sunday, 24 September 1978, and Anchorians came to Linton Park with the sun still hanging on for one last innings of summer. I lost the toss — I generally do — and duly found myself leading the lads out into the field rather than putting my pads on and my feet up.

Two footnotes worth framing: Trevor brought up 1,000 runs for the club, and Brian's innings pushed him past Tony Levick into 24th on our all-time run charts. A thoroughly deserved nine-wicket win, and two points banked without ever really looking in doubt.

Lost the toss again — I'm beginning to think the West Farleigh coin has a grudge against me — and so we spent our Saturday chasing leather rather than making it fly. On the brighter side, I find myself atop the 1978 run charts with 570 for the season, and Martin Thompson sits a creditable fifth in the wickets列 with 18 — small mercies in a chastening week. Beaten by 134 runs, fair and square. We'll dust ourselves off and be better for it next Saturday.

Sun 17 Sep 1978 🧢 1st XIv Mersham Le Hatch CC · HomeformDDDDD

It isn't often the toss goes against you and you end up thanking your lucky stars, but that was rather the shape of Sunday's affair against Mersham Le Hatch. I called wrong, they had a bat, and for once I didn't mind one bit — our attack has been in irresistible form all summer and I fancied our chances of making early inroads.

How right that proved. Allan Cooper, spinning it past bat, pad and occasionally common sense, ran through their order for a scarcely believable 5-28, and in doing so quietly slipped past Ron Hickman into 13th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — no mean feat, that. I chipped in with 2-20 and, if I may say so just the once, kept things tight enough with four maidens to strangle any hope of momentum. Tony Gauna weighed in with 2-31 for good measure. Bruce Campbell's 24 and A Gosling's 22 were the only real resistance, while poor Greig and Moorhead won't be dining out on their golden ducks. All out for 99 in 41.3 overs — a total that never felt like trouble.

Chasing it should have been the easy part, and in truth it never quite was — 101 for 6 took us 43.4 overs of the sort of nervy, low-scoring nudging that keeps a captain's tea lukewarm. Tim Thirkell's 24 led the way, well supported by Malcolm Pay's unbeaten 20, and we scrambled home by four wickets.

Two points, and thoroughly deserved, even if we did our best to make hard work of a modest target. Thirkell continues to top our season's run charts with 558, and with 73 wickets to my name I lead the bowling — though on this evidence, Cooper is closing fast. A fine team effort, if a slightly untidy one.

We fell to Paul CC by 8 runs at their place, chasing 159 after being put in.

Alas, Keith Miller's duck will not trouble the highlights reel. We closed on 150 for 6 from our 45, agonisingly short.

There was cause for a quiet smile amid the gloom: Thirkell brought up 4,000 career runs for the club, Piper's fifty vaulted him past Frank Taylor into 25th on the all-time run list, and Hickman's brace lifted him past Allan Cooper into 13th among the wicket-takers. Bowles and Gauna, meanwhile, continue to lead the Tour XI's run and wicket charts respectively.

Eight runs short, then — not our day, but no disgrace in it either. Heads up, and back to the nets.

Allan Cooper set about them with real zip, finishing with 3-9, ably supported by Tony Gauna (2-18) and Nigel Thirkell (1-10), and between them they reduced the visitors from a shaky 11-1 to a positively wobbly 19-4. John Adams, Rod Warboys and D. Silvers all trooped back for ducks, and I confess I allowed myself a quiet smile at the scorer's book. K. Beckell, to his credit, held firm for an unbeaten 24 and, with the last pair adding a thoroughly unwelcome 38, dragged them up to 68/6 from their sixteen overs – a few more than we'd have liked to chase, if not many.

Our reply was never elegant. We stumbled to 69/7 in 15.3 overs, scraping home by three wickets with rather more alarm than the target warranted. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, shipping ten byes for his trouble, and no doubt spent last orders explaining himself to all who'd listen.

Two points banked, Tim Thirkell still tops our run charts with 15, and Cooper leads the wickets with three. A scrappy, unconvincing win – but a win nonetheless, and I'll take that every Thursday going.

Sun 07 May 1978 🧢 Sunday NVKCv BC Biddenden CC · HomeformLWLLL

Lost the toss on a grey Sunday at home to Biddenden — never mind, I told the lads, we'll have them in anyway, and for once fortune smiled on the sulk that follows losing the coin. Biddenden could only cobble together 92 all out in 29.3 overs, undone early when their opener fell for 3-1 and further rattled when J Robinson trudged off for a duck. A Williamson (21) and D Packham (20) tried to rebuild, but Allan Cooper was in irresistible mood, scything through with 4-21, while Trevor Back bowled as if the boundary rope were an insult to his craft — 3-12 off three parsimonious maidens — and Nigel Thirkell chipped in with 2-20 for good measure.

Chasing 93 was never going to be a stroll for this side, and so it proved. We wobbled from 74-3 to 85-6 in a flurry that had me reaching for the scorebook and a stiff tea, with Malcolm Pay and Jim Hall both walking back for ducks of their own. But Nigel Thirkell, calm as you like amid the carnage, carried us home with a splendid 51, seeing us over the line at 93/6 from 28.4 overs.

A four-wicket win, then, and a tidy one at that. Thirkell now tops our Sunday run charts, Cooper the wickets — leaders both, and thoroughly deserving. Onwards we go.
