🦆 Duck News — 19–20 September 2015

2 ducks
Week 22 · 19 Sep 2015 – 20 Sep 2015
Two matches, two ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees as the 2015 season edges towards its close. 🏏 Saturday — 1st XI v Stowting CC Richard Piper, promoted to number two, departed caught and bowled by Troy Harding without so much as troubling the scoreboard operator. It's duck number four of the campaign and the 72nd of a decorated career, which now stands at a mighty 6th in the club's all-time list of 1435 duck-getters. Richard remains a permanent fixture of the honours board furniture — a Full House man (a duck registered at every position 1 to 11, no small feat of dedication), a fixture of the Homebody Duck, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, and Duck of the Year back in 2003. The name runs in the blood too, with relatives Brian (23 career ducks) and James (15) both keeping the family average respectably low elsewhere. On this very day in 1982, David Iliffe also fell for nought against Anchorians CC — that year's Duck of the Year, and himself Quacked by Kin courtesy of Mike Iliffe. History, as ever, rhymes. 🌱 Sunday — 1st XI v Tenterden CC Nat Presneill was bowled by Angus Shepherd at number six, his fourth duck of the season and 16th of a career now spanning 43 innings — good enough for 79th on the all-time list. Nat is already a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his senior account with a nought many seasons back, and carries Duck of the Year honours from 2013. His name also sits, presumptively and hopefully prematurely, on the Retirement Duck and Quack-to-Quack boards — standing honours only, and we expect to see him at the crease for a good while yet. Kinsman Kevin Presneill already carries 23 career ducks of his own, so the gene pool remains undefeated. Nat draws level on exactly 16 career ducks with Iain Rawson, who played 2006–2013 and took 135 innings to reach the same total — Nat getting there in a rather more efficient 43. Efficiency, as always, cuts both ways in this club. 📋 Week in Summary A modest but perfectly formed haul — two ducks from two matches, both at 1st XI level, both bowled or caught in the first half of the order. No pairs, no families, no fireworks — just the quiet, steady business of the art of not scoring. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Luke Welsman — 7 ducks, 24 innings (29.17%) 2. Chris Newman — 5 ducks, 13 innings (38.46%) 3. Jordan Austin — 5 ducks, 18 innings (27.78%) 4. Martin Harris — 5 ducks, 23 innings (21.74%) 5. Tim Wright — 5 ducks, 31 innings (16.13%) Chris Newman still boasts the division's most alarming strike rate, but Luke Welsman clings to top spot on volume alone. The race for 2015 Duck of the Year remains very much alive. 🦆 See you next weekend, when the club's bowlers will no doubt be doing their level best to keep the tally climbing.

🦆 Duck News — 12 to 13 September 2015

0 ducks

A quiet weekend on the LPCC oche, ducker-hunters. Two fixtures went into the book across Saturday and Sunday and, remarkably, every single Linton bat that walked to the crease found a way to trouble the scorers. Not one nought recorded. Not a sniff. The zero club's membership secretary has nothing to file this week. It happens. The ducks, it seems, took the weekend off to roost elsewhere. Small mercies for the club's top order, then, though the regulars in the Duck Hunting Season standings will hardly be resting easy. The chasing pack is fierce and the percentages don't forget. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇🍩 1. Luke Welsman — 7 ducks in 23 innings (30.43%) 2. 🦆 Duck News — 5-6 September 2015

7 ducks
Week 20 · 5 Sep 2015 – 6 Sep 2015
Only one of the weekend's three fixtures troubled the Duck Club scorers, but Stockbury CC made absolutely certain of it — a Sunday 2nd XI collapse yielded a full seven ducks in one innings, the kind of haul that fills an honours board in an afternoon. 🏏 Sunday 2nd XI at Stockbury CC — an innings for the ages Jack Gwynn got the rot started at three, bowled by Nick Emery for his 2nd duck of the season and 8th of a career now sitting 134th all-time. He draws level with Carl Davis, who played 1994-2009 and also settled on 8 before eventually bowing out on a Retirement Duck — decent company for a Sunday stroll back to the pavilion. In at four came Christopher Thirkell, run out by Luke Collins for his 2nd of the campaign and 28th for the club, good enough for 40th on the all-time list and membership of both Duck of the Year 2005 and the tantalisingly unfinished Full House nearly-man board, ten of eleven positions conquered. On this very day back in 1998, Richard Piper was also trudging off for nought, en route to 71 career ducks and 6th place all-time. Ray Dixon followed, caught Emery bowled James Newell for his 3rd of the season, taking him to 25 career ducks and 50th all-time — level pegging with Dave Charlton, who needed 219 innings across 1988-2009 to reach the same figure Ray has managed rather more briskly. Luke Welsman, this season's runaway Duck of the Year frontrunner, chalked up a remarkable 7th of the campaign, lbw to Newell, and moves to 11 career ducks, 107th all-time — joining Andrew Moss on the same tally, who took 135 innings across 1968-2008 before himself eventually signing off with a Retirement Duck. Luke already carries the Debutante Duck badge from his very first innings, so today was simply business as usual. Tobey Castle was stumped off Newell for his maiden duck of the season, a tidy 3rd for his career and 270th all-time, drawing level with Daniel McCarthy (2006-2007, 19 innings). Tobey's father David carries 46 career ducks of his own — evidently the family tree bears fruit, or rather, doesn't. At nine, club legend Tim Thirkell added his 2nd duck of the season and a staggering 91st of his career, bowled by Newell, cementing an almost unbelievable 2nd place on the all-time list. Tim's cabinet groans with silverware for scoring nothing — Duck of the Year 1972, Full House, and top-ten places in both Lone Duck and Homebody Duck — and, like Christopher earlier in the order, he too echoes Richard Piper's duck of this day in 1998. Which brings us to the day's rarest ceremony: Christopher and Tim Thirkell falling in the same innings makes this an official Family of Ducks occasion for the wider Thirkell dynasty, whose collective nought-count runs into the hundreds. A shared bloodline, a shared fate, a proud family tradition of walking back early. Rounding things off, Darrell Houghton-Judge was run out by Jack Herklots for his 4th of the season and 7th career duck, 146th all-time, level with Mike Caddick (1993-2009, 127 innings). Darrell too sits on the Full House nearly-man board — so close, yet so agonisingly far. 📊 Week in Summary Seven ducks from a single innings, one thoroughly chastened batting order, and Saturday's two fixtures apparently too well-mannered to join in. James Newell's figures against Linton will make pleasant reading in Stockbury's clubhouse tonight. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Luke Welsman — 22 inns, 7 ducks (31.82%) - Chris Newman — 12 inns, 5 ducks (41.67%) - Jordan Austin — 16 inns, 5 ducks (31.25%) - Martin Harris — 22 inns, 5 ducks (22.73%) - Tim Wright — 30 inns, 5 ducks (16.67%) Luke leads the chase, but Chris Newman's 41.67% strike rate for nought is the number that should worry him most. Next fixture, same risk, same reward — see you at the crease. 🏏0️⃣

3 ducks

Jack Gwynn went first, bowled by Marsh at number two for his 1st duck of the season and 7th of an accomplished career from 47 innings — sitting him 148th of 1435 in the all-time reckoning. He now draws level on 7 career ducks with Mike Caddick, who plied his trade for Linton from 1993 to 2006 across 127 innings. Quality, not consistency, clearly the watchword there. One place later, Trevor Back followed him back to the pavilion, also bowled D Marsh, for his own 1st duck of the season — though at Trevor's level that barely moves the needle. It's his 79th career duck from 715 innings, keeping him a mighty 5th in the all-time standings, and a proud shelf-holder of Ducks on the Road top 10, Homebody Duck top 10, Lone Duck top 10, Only Two Quacks with Allan Cooper, and Quacking Together with Jon Harris. Trevor now sits level on 79 with club legend Jim Hall, who racked up the same tally between 1967 and 2008 in 503 innings. Two great eras, one shared number. 🏏 Midweek XI — v The Mote CC (2 September, home) Tim Wright chipped in at number seven, lbw to James Waterson, for his 5th duck of the season and 18th of his career from 266 innings — good enough for 71st all-time. He now stands shoulder to shoulder on 18 with Russell Baldwin, a name from the 1988 to 2006 vintage who reached the same total in 208 innings. Worth noting Tim also has a duck-collecting relative in the family tree, with kin Adam Wright already carrying 6 career noughts of his own — the acorn, evidently, not falling far. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from four matches, and a genuine "matching pair" moment at Knockers CC where one bowler claimed two Linton scalps for nought in successive balls of the order — the sort of thing that ought to earn D Marsh an honorary Duck Club tie. Elsewhere, business as usual: the season's serial offenders continue to circle the honours board. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Luke Welsman — 6 ducks from 21 innings, 28.57% - Chris Newman — 5 ducks from 12 innings, 41.67% - Jordan Austin — 5 ducks from 14 innings, 35.71% - Martin Harris — 5 ducks from 21 innings, 23.81% - Tim Wright — 5 ducks from 29 innings, 17.24% Tim Wright's Midweek effort above lands him right on that leaderboard, so keep an eye on the top of the table over the coming weeks. 🍩 That's the lot for this period — quiet by the club's own high standards, but never fear. The next fixture list awaits, and the ducks are, as always, only ever one good ball away. See you at the crease.

His 2nd of the season and 23rd of an increasingly distinguished career from 257 innings, good enough for 57th on the all-time list. He now sits level with Brian Piper, who reached the same 23 across a marathon 1973–2013 career (444 innings — Kevin's getting there quicker). Thomas Beeney fell caught off Chris Knight after four balls of defiance, his 1st duck of the season and 33rd career, edging him to 30th all-time. A paid-up Debutante Duck club member and 2014's reigning Duck of the Year, Thomas is also a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10 — fittingly, given this one came away from home. His relative Michael Beeney watches on with 38 career ducks of his own. Two batsmen later Chris Newman followed him back, lbw for 2 balls faced — his 5th duck of the season already, and a hefty 36th of his career from 207 innings, 25th all-time. Both Beeney and Newman join the exclusive club on this very calendar date — on this day in 1981, LPCC legend Brian Piper also made a duck, against Old Anchorians CC, the same fixture claiming three ducks in this data set alone. Popular ground for nought-hunters, evidently. 🌱 Sunday — Pett CC (2nd XI, home) And then there's Tim Thirkell. The man is a walking honours board — Duck of the Year 1972, Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11, a feat of genuinely diabolical range), plus entries in the Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top 10s, and a Quacking Together partnership with Jim Hall for good measure. This one, caught behind, was merely his 1st of the season but his 90th career duck — 3rd on the all-time list, from a staggering 903 innings. The Thirkell name is basically a duck dynasty in its own right: father John also sits on exactly 90, siblings Nigel (60) and Simon (59) aren't far behind, and cousins Daniel, Christopher, Will, Tom and Stuart round out a family tree groaning with noughts. Like the others, Tim's fall echoes Brian Piper's 1981 duck against Old Anchorians CC — quite the popular date in the archives. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from four matches — a tidy, unspectacular haul, but Chris Newman's contribution keeps him firmly in the season's title race, while Kevin's golden effort was the weekend's purest work of art. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1.
