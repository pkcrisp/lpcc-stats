Wed 28 Sep 1994 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDWDDD

Wednesday saw us on the road to Penshurst Park, and with the coin kind to me for once, I had no hesitation in sending us in — a decision that looked rather clever by close of play, though I'll admit to a nervous hour or two along the way.

Mike Caddick set about the bowling with real intent, cruising to 71, and I was fortunate enough to find some form alongside him — a personal 54 that I'm quietly rather proud of, the two of us putting on 104 for the second wicket to give the innings its backbone. Peter Whyte chipped in with a tidy 27 as we closed on 213/5 from our 47 overs, a total that felt competitive without being conclusive.

Defending it proved a nerve-shredder. Penshurst stumbled to 4-1 early and we allowed ourselves a smile, but John Boyden (71 not out) and R Hunter (51) rebuilt with a stand of 69 for the third wicket, and Quasar's 46 kept the required rate within reach. Webb, Richard Banks and Scott Lock all departed without troubling the scorers, thanks largely to Ian Brattle, whose 5-66 with three tidy maidens was the spell of the day, well backed by Alan Boyns (2-19) and Jon Harris (1-62). Poor Peter Whyte had a torrid time behind the stumps, nine byes going begging, but it mattered not in the end.

Five runs. That was the margin, and every one of them earned. Two points banked, and honourable mention to Trevor Back, whose 16 runs edged him past Jim Hall into 13th on the all-time run list — a night to remember all round.

Sat 24 Sep 1994 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformWWLWL

Losing the toss on a fine late-September Saturday was never going to help our cause, and duly Hastings & St Leonards Priory elected to bat, leaving us to toil in the field. They made a solid fist of it too, their openers putting on 59 before Jim Chaplin (49) and N Phillips (39) built steadily towards 187 all out in the 38th over, with S Tibble chipping in 24. Matt Large was the pick of our attack with 3-26, Don Dansie chimed in with 2-20, and I managed to prise out a couple myself for 2-24 — modest reward for a long afternoon's graft, but wickets are wickets.

Chasing 188, we stumbled early — 3 for 1 had the tea urn rattling with nerves — before Piers McDonald and Mike Caddick set about repairing things with real purpose, adding 111 for the second wicket. McDonald's 79 was a innings of real class, Caddick's 54 no less handsome, and for a while 188 looked eminently gettable. Then, as so often at Linton Park, the wheels came clean off: from a commanding 155 for 3 we subsided to 170 for 6 in what felt like the blink of an eye. Don Dansie's unbeaten 28 held the tail together, but it wasn't quite enough, and we finished on 175 for 6 from our 40 overs.

Twelve runs short in the end — the sort of margin that makes for a quiet drive home and a long look in the mirror. Heads up, though: back to the nets, and on to the next one.

Sat 24 Sep 1994 🧢 2nd XIv YC Yalding CC · AwayformWDWLL

Here's the match report:

---

Saturday 24 September, and off we went to Yalding CC with autumn very much in the air. We lost the toss, were put in to field, and I can't say the day ever quite recovered from there.

Yalding built steadily to 181 for 8 from their 48 overs, with Kevin Shrimpton unbeaten on 37 and useful runs from Graham Walker (35) and Iain Cardew (31). The one bright spot amid the graft was Trevor Back, who was quite magnificent — five wickets for 29, three of them maidens, and not a run given away without a fight. I chipped in with a couple myself (2-27), Adrian Caddick took one for 27, and at least we sent K Brady back for a duck to remind ourselves we weren't entirely toothless.

Set 182, we never got going. Two on the board and we'd already lost a wicket, and from there it was a familiar tale of tea-time introspection. Trevor Back, having done the business with the ball, then top-scored with the bat too — 34 of grit and application while wickets fell around him. As for the skipper, the least said the better: a golden blob, out first ball. They do say a captain should lead from the front. On this occasion I led rather too enthusiastically back to the pavilion.

There was a silver lining of sorts — Trevor's five-for nudges him up to 12th on the club's all-time wicket list, past Ian Brattle, which is no mean feat. Bowled out for 82 in 45.2 overs, though, we were always going to come up well short, and a 99-run defeat is exactly what the scorebook suggests: not our day. Heads up, and back to the nets.

Sat 17 Sep 1994 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWWWLW

Saturday 17 September, and West Farleigh rolled up to Linton Park under skies that looked more sceptical than the scorebook eventually proved. The coin bounced my way and, in a rare fit of decisiveness, I put us in to bat first.

Clive Newman's innings lasted precisely as long as it took him to walk out and back — a golden duck to open proceedings, and 0-1 looked distinctly ominous. But Paul Bowles and I dug in, added a priceless 115 for the second wicket, and by the time I trudged off for 36, the platform was well and truly laid. Bowles simply kept going, unbeaten on a magnificent hundred, with Don Dansie chipping in an unbeaten 20 as we closed on a handsome 163/2 from 44.2 overs — a total that felt, over the tea urn, entirely defensible.

It wasn't. West Farleigh fumbled their own start, 4-1, before R Peterson and Dave Latter shared 84 for the second wicket and simply declined to leave. Darren Styles bowled tidily for his one wicket, Chris Gray toiled manfully for 1-53 with two miserly maidens, but Peterson finished unbeaten on 71, Latter cruised past his fifty, and Glen Aukett saw them home unbeaten. 166/2 in 32.2 overs, and the visitors had it by eight wickets.

Not our finest afternoon in the field, but with 1039 runs now to my name in the 1994 charts, the bat is doing its bit — the ball simply needs to catch up. We'll be better for it next week.

Beaten by two wickets, then, with the game there for the taking and not quite taken. We'll be better for it next week.

Sun 11 Sep 1994 🧢 1st XIv NS Northend Sunday · HomeformWWWWL

Sunday 11 September saw us take on Northend, and winning the toss on a decent-looking morning, there was really only one decision to make – we batted. David Castle set about proving the wicket's benevolence with a majestic 93 not out, and together with Clive Newman's brisk 48 he put on 111 for the first wicket before either of them so much as looked troubled. Mike Caddick chipped in a tidy unbeaten 20 as we closed on a formidable 179 for 3 from our 46 overs – the sort of total that lets a captain enjoy his tea rather than gnaw at the biscuits.

If our batting was a stroll, the bowling that followed was closer to a massacre. Northend's top three – Taylor, Nightingale and Parkins – all departed without registering a run, which I am reliably informed is the sort of thing that gets you a duck emoji in the modern scorebook. Matt Large was the chief destroyer, 3 for 14 with five wickets' worth of miserliness thrown in for good measure, while I was pleased to get among the wickets myself with figures of 3 for 19, and Adrian Caddick mopped up with 2 for 4. Northend were bowled out for a paltry 56, and the match was over almost before the urn had boiled.

A word too for Jon Harris, who passed 2,000 career wickets in the process – a landmark that puts the rest of us rather in the shade. Won by 123 runs, two points banked, and about as comprehensive a Sunday as this club has enjoyed in some time.

There was a silver lining amid the wreckage — Ian Brattle's wicket nudged him past Trevor Back into 12th on the club's all-time list, a nice moment on an otherwise chastening afternoon. Beaten by 73 runs, then, fair and square. We'll dust ourselves down and be better for it next week.

Sun 04 Sep 1994 🧢 Tour XIv BC Beacon CC, Cornwall · AwayformWWLLW

Sunday 4th September brought us to Beacon CC in Cornwall, a fixture that always promises sea air, cream teas, and the possibility of losing all sense of the scoreboard. I won the toss and had no hesitation in batting first — a decision I felt rather smug about at the time, and one the lads didn't let me forget either way, whatever happened.

We posted 152 for 7 from our forty overs, a total built almost single-handedly by Nigel Thirkell, whose 69 was the innings' backbone — a proper well-made fifty and then some. A useful 59-run stand for the fourth wicket gave the innings some spine, though I'll confess it never quite felt like enough as we walked off.

It nearly wasn't. Beacon replied with real intent, Chris Bennett top-scoring with a brisk 46, but our attack found its rhythm: David Castle was superb with 2 for 21, Nigel Thirkell chipped in again with 2 for 26 — a fine all-round day's work — and Richard Ashby snaffled one for the princely cost of a single run. They were bowled out for exactly 152 in 23.3 overs.

Level. Dead level. Twenty-two men, a whole day's toil, and not a run between us. You couldn't script it, and frankly I wouldn't want to — some results are better left as they fell. A tie is nobody's triumph and everybody's story, and that's precisely how it should stay down at Beacon.
