🦆 Duck News — 26 September 2020

3 ducks
Week 12 · 26 Sep 2020
🏏 1st XI v The Mote CC (Home) Only one fixture on the books this week, but Linton's top and tail order made sure the Duck Club had plenty to file. Richard Piper opened the innings and closed his afternoon almost as fast — ct James Waterson b Nathan Hinkley — his 1st duck of the season. It's the 86th of an extraordinary career spanning 811 innings, holding him at a lofty 5th in the all-time standings, a shelf that also carries Duck of the Year 2003, membership of the Full House club (a duck at every position, 1 to 11), and top-ten billing in both Homebody and Ducks on the Road. Fittingly, on this very date back in 1965, Roy Clarke (1957–1966, 40 career ducks, 21st all-time) also strolled back for nought against Linton Park — some anniversaries insist on repeating themselves. Then the tail belonged entirely to the Karassellos family. Anthony Karassellos departed at number nine — ct Theeshan Satkunaseelan b Lewis Bone — his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from just 32 innings, drawing level with Archie Fairfax-Ross (2018–2020, 24 innings). Anthony is already a Debutante Duck man, having ducked on his very first outing, and has since gone one better by completing his own Full House. Two wickets later his father John Karassellos rounded things off at number eleven, run out for his 4th duck of the season and 27th career nought from 79 innings, level with Jordan Austin (2008–2020, 202 innings) and sitting 51st all-time. John is a three-time reigning Duck of the Year — 2018, 2019 and, this innings confirms, 2020 again — a man riding a run of 5 Ducks in a Row and agonisingly one position shy of his own Full House. 🎖️ Duck and Ducklings A parent and child both back in the pavilion for nought in the same innings is one of the rarest ceremonies the club can offer, and Linton delivered it with style: John Karassellos and son Anthony Karassellos, both undone by The Mote CC on the same afternoon. Somewhere a family WhatsApp group has gone very quiet. 📊 The Week in Numbers Three ducks from one match, and the Karassellos household accounted for two of them. A tidy strike rate for a single fixture, and proof the art of not scoring runs in the family. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Karassellos — 4 ducks in 6 innings (66.67%) - Alex Wade — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) - Henry Bourdillon — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) - James West — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) - Alex Keeler — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) John Karassellos leads by a distance and has already banked the 2020 crown — the only question left is how far clear he can pull. Bring your bats, gentlemen, and your bad luck. The Duck Club will be watching next weekend. 🦆🍩

🦆 LPCC Duck Club Bulletin — 5th to 9th September 2020 🦆

7 ducks
Week 9 · 5 Sep 2020 – 9 Sep 2020
Season's dying embers, but the ducks weren't done with us yet. Seven noughts across five matches as the club limped towards the finish line. 🏏 Saturday 5th September 1st XI away at Bidborough saw Toby Hughes bowled by Ed Shepherdson for his first duck of the season and 3rd of a 23-innings career, ranking him 290th of 1499 all-time. He now sits shoulder to shoulder with Archie Fairfax-Ross, who plied his trade 2018–2020 and parked himself on exactly the same tally of 3. Moments later Alex Keeler edged behind for his 2nd of the season, his 6th career duck from 59 innings, 165th on the all-time roll. On this very day back in 1970, David T Lowen was doing much the same thing against A.Trembeths — history repeating itself, quietly, in the classifieds of failure. Down at home, the 2nd XI entertained Bearsted and obliged them twice over. Martin Rivers fell for his 1st career duck in just 12 innings — bowled off 3 balls, so at least he had a look at it — landing him alongside the briefest of careers, the mysterious "- Howard", whose entire Linton legacy was a single duck on debut back in 2006, complete with Retirement Duck honours to match. Not the company Martin will be hoping to keep for long. Then Alex Wade departed caught behind for his 3rd of the season and 4th career duck — a genuinely brisk campaign already, 4 ducks from just 12 career innings. He joins -Matthew Ward (2011–2012, 10 innings) on the same tally, proof that some players simply skip the apprenticeship and go straight for consistency of a very particular kind. 🌱 Sunday 6th September — the Karassellos Occasion Sunday 2nd XI travelled to Loose CC and delivered the rarest ceremony the Duck Club offers — a genuine Duck and Ducklings. Fred Taylor opened the misery, bowled for 11 balls' worth of defiance before succumbing — his 1st duck of the season, 6th career, and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first innings. On this day in 1998, Richard Piper — LPCC's own Full House laureate — was doing the same against Beacon CC, Cornwall. Then came the family business. John Karassellos, three-time reigning Duck of the Year (2018, 2019, 2020, no less) and owner of 5 Ducks in a Row, ran himself out first ball for a golden duck — his 2nd of the season and landmark 25th career, drawing level with club stalwart Dave Charlton (223 innings, 1988–2018) on the very same total. And moments later his son Anthony followed him back to the pavilion, caught off the very first ball he faced — another golden duck, his 1st of the season and 2nd career, level with Abishek Pushpavinothan on the same tally. Father and son, ducks in the same innings, same match, same wretched afternoon — a bona fide Duck and Ducklings for the honours board, the sort of shared ignominy the club exists to celebrate. Take a bow, the Karassellos family. 📊 Week in Summary Seven ducks, five matches, one glorious family double act. The golden duck count stood at two, both claimed by the same household — a hereditary talent for departing without troubling the scorers, it seems. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Alex Wade — 6 innings, 3 ducks, 50.0% 2. Henry Bourdillon — 7 innings, 3 ducks, 42.86% 3. John Karassellos — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100.0% 4. Alex Keeler — 9 innings, 2 ducks, 22.22% 5. Darrell Houghton-Judge — 13 innings, 2 ducks, 15.38% With John Karassellos sat on a perfect 100% conversion rate this season, the race for Duck of the Year remains very much alive. The season may be winding down, but the ducks rarely read the calendar — see you at the next fixture, where someone, somewhere, will surely add to the tally. 🦆 0️⃣ 🏏

Four fixtures. Four full scorecards. Zero ducks. Let the record show that on this particular weekend, the LPCC top order took one look at the exclusive zero club, its membership rolls and its solemn honours boards, and declined the invitation entirely. Not a single batsman anywhere in the club could summon the special blend of misfortune and horror required for admission. Was it good batting? Generous bowling? A collective pact to deny the correspondent his copy? We may never know. Ducks are, after all, notoriously camouflaged — they evaded capture this time, but rest assured the traps remain set for next weekend. No pairs, no king pairs, and certainly no family affairs to report — the honours boards stay exactly as they were on Friday evening. Week 1 · 11 Jul 2020 – 15 Jul 2020
Six fixtures, seven ducks, and a spot of family ignominy — the Club's least wanted membership had a busy week. 🏏 Saturday 11 July — v Linton Park CC 1st XI Henry Bourdillon fell early at 2, run out by Sam Boyns for his maiden season duck and 3rd of his career from 14 innings — 261st of 1497 all time. He joins Archie Fairfax-Ross, who reached the same tally across 2018–2020, level on three apiece. Ryan Silk was caught and bowled by David T Lowen, moving to 5 career ducks from 17 innings, 196th all time — a fully paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, though nobody's hanging up the boots on this one. He draws level with Engarshal Sinnaraja (2017–2018), also on five. 2nd XI Family honours were the story here. Anton Bourdillon, batting at 7, was caught off the bowling of his own son Henry — Quacked by Kin, and being 2020, this one's fresh off the press rather than the archives. Anton's 10th career duck from 26 innings, 118th all time, puts him level with Jack Jenner (2003–2008, 53 innings). Combined with Henry's effort up in the 1s, the Bourdillons complete a proper Duck and Ducklings — parent and child both back in the hutch on the same afternoon. A rare ceremony, and the Club salutes it in full. Benjamin Austin also departed, caught Welsman bowled Harris, his 18th career duck from 128 innings, 77th all time — a Debutante Duck alumnus of old, now level with James Piper (1998–2020, 349 innings). He arrives with pedigree too: sibling Jordan Austin already sits on 27 career noughts. 3rd XI Darrell Houghton-Judge was bowled by Toby Hughes for his 28th career duck from 167 innings, 47th all time — a Full House nearly-man, agonisingly missing just the one batting position for the complete set — drawing level with Christopher Thirkell (1998–2015), 2005's Duck of the Year, on the same tally. Mark Christmas was bowled by Anthony Karassellos, his 38th career duck from a mighty 412 innings, 25th all time — 2008's Duck of the Year and another Full House nearly-man — now sharing his number with Michael Beeney (1995–2015). Will Thew was run out for his 33rd career duck from 240 innings, 33rd all time, a Debutante Duck and 2012's Duck of the Year in his own right. On this very day in 1967, Mike Stephens also strolled back for nought against West Farleigh CC — the calendar has a long memory. 📊 The Week in Numbers Seven ducks across six matches, evenly spread over all three XIs, with the Bourdillons providing the week's headline act — a father undone by his own flesh and blood. No pairs, no king pairs this time, just honest failure all round.
