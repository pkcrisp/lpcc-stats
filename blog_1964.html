🦆 LPCC Duck Report — 26-27 September 1964

3 ducks
Week 21 · 26 Sep 1964 – 27 Sep 1964
A quiet closing weekend of the season saw the 1st XI shoulder the entire burden of ignominy, with three noughts recorded across two fixtures. The 2nds, 3rds and Sunday sides, for once, kept their heads down and their bats straight. 1st XI — Saturday 26th vs Anchorians CC In at nine, Len Conway fell b Alan Dixon for his 2nd duck of the season and 12th of a fine career spanning 110 innings — good enough for 24th on the all-time list of 792. Len remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first LPCC innings, though this latest effort was merely business as usual. Curiously, on this very day back in 1959, Robert Wood was also dismissed without score against A.Trembeths — Robert went on to 21 career ducks and 9th all-time, so Len has some catching up to do. Last man in, Dick Langridge departed b J- White for the 3rd duck of his season, moving to 9 for his career from just 46 innings — 32nd of 792, and closing in fast. A chip off the old block, no less — his relative Barry Langridge carries 6 career ducks of his own. And wouldn't you know it, that same 26th September in 1959 also claimed Robert Wood — two Langridge-and-Conway noughts, six decades apart, on the identical date. 1st XI — Sunday 27th vs Linton Park CC At four, Robert Murray was bowled by David Gale for his first duck of the season, but his 16th career effort from 172 innings sees him sat handsomely at 15th of 792 all-time — rarefied company indeed. On this same day in 1958, Terry Whittaker also made a duck against A.Trembeths, going on to bow out on a nought himself and finish with 13 career ducks — a reminder that the calendar has long memories where the art of not scoring is concerned. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, all falling to the 1st XI's middle and lower order — Anchorians CC and Linton Park CC both walked away with a wicket-maiden's worth of satisfaction. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this week — just honest, unglamorous failure, exactly as the club likes it. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Terry Donovan — 6 ducks / 17 inns (35.29%) 2. Richard Ashby — 6 ducks / 28 inns (21.43%) 3. Roy Clarke — 6 ducks / 29 inns (20.69%) 4. Ron Hickman — 5 ducks / 8 inns (62.50%) 🍩 5. John Ames — 3 ducks / 13 inns (23.08%) Ron Hickman's strike rate for failure remains the stuff of legend this season — over three in five trips to the crease ending in nought. 🥇 The season draws to a close but the zero club never rests. Roll on next weekend's fixtures — someone's average is always due a reminder that not every innings ends in runs. 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 19-20 September 1964

0 ducks
Week 20 · 19 Sep 1964 – 20 Sep 1964
Two fixtures out on the paddock this weekend, and for once the exclusive zero club had absolutely nothing to show for it. Not a single batsman offered himself up for ignominy — eleven-plus players a side, twice over, and every last one of them found the middle of the bat at least once. A vegan weekend if ever there was one, fowl-free from first ball to last. Whether this is a triumph of application or simply a conspiracy of good weather and generous fielding, the Duck Club cannot say. What we can say is that somewhere out there, a bowler went home most aggrieved. Rest assured, normal service will resume. The art of not scoring is not so easily retired — it merely waits. Meanwhile Ron Hickman continues to defy the laws of probability, finding nought more often than not. A quiet weekend for the club, but the season isn't done with anyone yet. Next fixture, someone somewhere will nick one behind first ball and restore order to the universe. 🏏0️⃣ Until then — keep watching the crease.

🦆 Duck News — 12–13 September 1964

2 ducks
Week 19 · 12 Sep 1964 – 13 Sep 1964
A quiet weekend for the naughty nought by Linton standards — just two matches, but each handed over its own name to the ledger, both courtesy of the 1st XI. 🏏 1st XI Saturday 12th September, home to Bowaters CC: Frank Brattle, opening the innings, was trapped lbw by Edward Chick without scoring. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 22nd of a career now spanning 213 innings, holding him at 6th in the all-time standings out of 792 — rarefied company indeed. Kin of long-serving fellow quacker Percy Brattle, who carries 5 career ducks of his own — the Brattle name clearly has an eye for the leave-it-that-turns-out-plumb. And in a delightful quirk of the calendar, on this very day back in 1959 S Lusher also fell to Bowaters CC for a duck — a five-year echo across the same fixture. Sunday 13th September, away to Staplehurst CC: Basil Sears departed at number two, bowled by R. Newick for nought. Also his 3rd duck of the season, and his 20th career duck from 276 innings, sitting 11th of 792 all-time. On this same day in 1959, the legendary John Ames — a man with 31 career ducks, a place on the Ducks in a Row honours board and two separate Quacking Together memberships — was himself trudging back against Tunbridge Wells CC. Good company to keep, even in ignominy. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one team responsible for the lot — the 1st XI cornered the market this week. Curiously, both duckers arrive at an identical season tally of three, though their career ledgers tell rather different stories. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs this time round — just honest, workmanlike failure, delivered on consecutive days. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Terry Donovan: 6 ducks from 17 innings (35.29%) - Richard Ashby: 6 ducks from 26 innings (23.08%) - Roy Clarke: 6 ducks from 27 innings (22.22%) - Ron Hickman: 5 ducks from 8 innings (62.5%) 🍩 - John Ames: 3 ducks from 11 innings (27.27%) Ron Hickman's strike rate for nought remains the stuff of legend — over three in every five innings ending in a blob. Someone ought to check in on him. 📅 Looking Ahead The season rolls on and the leaderboard remains wide open. Who joins Frank and Basil on the honours board next weekend? We watch. We wait. We celebrate the art of not scoring. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 22–23 August 1964

4 ducks
Week 16 · 22 Aug 1964 – 23 Aug 1964
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the 1st XI doing all the honours this week as Linton's exclusive zero club welcomes fresh entries to its books. 🏏 Saturday 22 August — v Aylesford Paper Mills CC First to fall was Terry Donovan, bowled by M Spry at number four for his 6th duck of the season and 8th of an illustrious career, edging him to 41st in the all-time standings. Terry remains the man to catch in this year's Duck of the Year race, and with the "Full House nearly-man" board already carrying his name, one wonders which of the eleven batting positions he has left to conquer in pursuit of the full set. Next in was Barry Langridge, caught by P Pollock off the bowling of Eric Churchyard for his first duck of the season and 6th of his career, moving to 47th all-time. Barry already has a page in the "Quacked by Kin" book, courtesy of his relative Dick Langridge back in 1963 — proof, if any were needed, that the family name and the number 0 travel together at Linton. Both keep fine historical company: on this very day in 1953, J Barclay (7 ducks, 1953-54) also trudged off for nought against Charles Arkcoll. Some things never change. 🏏 Sunday 23 August — v Sevenoaks Vine CC David Gale departed bowled by Derek Preston, his 1st duck of the season and 6th career effort, sitting 49th on the all-time list. Then the headline turn: Alan Wilkes, bowled by Alan Hurd for his 2nd duck of the season, climbs to a mighty 19 career ducks — 11th in the all-time table, one of the loftiest perches in club history. Alan is already a two-time laureate of the exclusive "Only Two Quacks" club, alongside both Bob Arnold and John Ames, and 1964 has been unkind to him in the extreme — already enshrined this season as a "Runaway Duck", a campaign yet to yield him a single run. David and Alan share a duck-day ancestor too: on this same date in 1952, D Simmons (4 ducks, 1949-63) also fell against Charles Arkcoll. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, both entirely the 1st XI's own work — the 2nd, 3rd and 4th XIs escaped unscathed this week. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Terry Donovan — 15 inns, 6 ducks (40.0%) 2. Richard Ashby — 22 inns, 6 ducks (27.3%) 3. Roy Clarke — 24 inns, 6 ducks (25.0%) 4. Ron Hickman — 8 inns, 5 ducks (62.5%) 5. John Ames — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.3%) Terry leads outright for now, but with Richard and Roy snapping close behind, 1964's Duck of the Year remains anyone's to lose. Next fixture, the zero club opens its doors once more — who's ready to make an entrance? 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 15-16 August 1964

4 ducks
Week 15 · 15 Aug 1964 – 16 Aug 1964
Two Linton fixtures this weekend, and one of them decided to give the exclusive zero club a proper working over. Bowaters CC hosted what can only be described as a small massacre of the 1st XI top and middle order — four ducks in a single innings, a genuine triumph through adversity. The other fixture, mercifully or dully depending on your allegiance, passed without a single nought to report. 🏏 1st XI at Bowaters CC — a procession of nothing First to go, at three, was John Thirkell — caught Jack Day, bowled Roy Ecclestone, for the second time this season. That takes him to a staggering 54 career ducks, which remains, and has long remained, top of the all-time pile at 792-deep Linton — the undisputed king of the art of not scoring. A three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955) and a fixture of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens alike, John ducks with equal distinction whether he's stayed local or travelled. Rather neatly, on this very day in 1936, Percy Brattle (1934-1963) was doing much the same thing against Bearsted CC. At six, Terry Donovan nicked off, caught and bowled Edward Chick, for his 5th of the season and 7th of his career — a tally that sits him 44th all time and keeps him firmly in this year's Duck of the Year conversation, a title he already holds for 1964. He's also a Full House nearly-man, having ducked in ten of the eleven batting positions — number six now ticked off, one slot still cruelly unconquered. Twenty-eight years back to the day, Percy Brattle was completing the very same ritual at Bearsted. Number seven, Richard Ashby, fell bowled to Chick's second victim of the innings, his 6th duck of the season — enough to lead the current Duck Hunting Season standings outright — and his 9th career, good for 33rd all time. A veteran of Ducks in a Row (five consecutive, no less) and a Tourist Duck top-ten regular, an away ground like Bowaters is precisely his natural habitat. Percy Brattle, unusually persistent this week, was there again in 1936. And completing Chick's hat-trick of noughts, at nine, Bob Arnold went caught John Evans bowled Chick — his 2nd of the season but a mighty 47th of his career, second only to Thirkell in the entire 792-strong club history. A Duck of the Year in 1963 and holder of his own Ducks in a Row (four), Bob shares an "Only Two Quacks" honour with Alan Wilkes and a "Quacking Together" board with none other than John Thirkell — and rather fittingly, the two of them renewed that acquaintance in the very same Bowaters innings. Percy Brattle, appropriately, made it four for four in the 1936 parallel stakes. 📊 The week in numbers Four ducks, two matches, one bowler — Edward Chick claimed three of the four victims in a single spell, and Linton's top two all-time duck aristocrats, Thirkell and Arnold, both fell in the same innings. A quiet weekend everywhere else, but Bowaters will do. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Richard Ashby — 20 inns, 6 ducks (30%) - Roy Clarke — 22 inns, 6 ducks (27.3%) - Ron Hickman — 7 inns, 5 ducks (71.4%) - Terry Donovan — 14 inns, 5 ducks (35.7%) - John Ames — 10 inns, 3 ducks (30%) Ashby leads the pack, but with Chick apparently bowling for the opposition on a weekly basis, nobody's tally is safe. 🍩 Roll on the next fixture — the noughts are watching, 0️⃣🦆

Two fixtures, two days, four visits to the exclusive zero club this week — the 1st XI doing all the heavy lifting in the art of not scoring. Saturday 18 July — 1st XI v Rumwood CC (h) Bowler J- Nolan enjoyed himself enormously, claiming a brace for the album. First to go was Frank Brattle, undone at five in the order, his bat troubling the scorers for precisely nought runs.
