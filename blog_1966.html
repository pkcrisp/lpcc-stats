🦆 Duck News — 24 September 1966

3 ducks
Week 22 · 24 Sep 1966
Hartley Country Club came to call, and while the top order mostly held firm, three Linton batsmen decided the art of not scoring deserved a fresh outing. A quiet afternoon by the club's standards, but the exclusive zero club never turns anyone away. 🏏 1st XI v Hartley Country Club CC First to the crease and first to depart was John Thirkell, bowled by I Gibbons at first wicket down for his 2nd duck of the season and 57th of an extraordinary career — still Linton's undisputed number one on the all-time list, a full 550 innings deep into a reign nobody is threatening any time soon. A veteran of three separate Duck of the Year titles (1952, 1954 and 1955) and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, John does this the way other men breathe. Neatly enough, on this very day back in 1961 John Ames also fell for nought against Linton Park — some anniversaries just keep quacking. Next in was Benny Bell, caught behind off C Cooper for his 5th duck of the season, which is also his 5th in career — a young account still very much under construction after just 16 innings, but already sat at a tidy 63rd on the all-time chart. Benny now shares his exact tally with a rather crowded table of history, from W Goad (1864–1872) through to Duck of the Year winners Jimmy Jameson (1945) and R Davison (1962) — good company for a first proper introduction to double figures territory. And completing the hat-trick of ignominy, Mike Iliffe followed at seven, caught by C Young off the same I Gibbons, his 5th duck of the season and 5th of his career too — both figures identical to Benny's, the pair now level on the ladder at 68th all time. Mike's fresh to this game, having only debuted this year, and he's already found the numbers to match players who took decades to get there. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks, one match, and Hartley's bowlers doing rather too efficient a job of it. Gibbons alone accounted for two of the three — a spell the Duck Club committee has taken careful note of. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Paul Bowles — 7 ducks, 23 innings (30.4%) 2. Mike Iliffe — 5 ducks, 13 innings (38.5%) 🍩 3. Benny Bell — 5 ducks, 16 innings (31.3%) 4. Alan Tucker — 4 ducks, 9 innings (44.4%) 5. Richard Ashby — 4 ducks, 19 innings (21.1%) Mike Iliffe's strike rate for nothing is the standout of the lot — nearly two in every five visits to the crease ending in triumphant failure. Same time next fixture, ducks. The nought never sleeps. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 17–18 September 1966

1 duck
Week 21 · 17 Sep 1966 – 18 Sep 1966
1st XI at Bowaters CC (away), Saturday 17th Openers are supposed to set the tone, and Benny Bell did exactly that, marching out first wicket down and marching straight back in again, bowled by Roy Ecclestone before he'd troubled the scorers. A tidy, unfussy nought — no fuss, no fireworks, just the quiet efficiency of a man who knows the way back to the pavilion better than the way to the crease. It's Benny's 4th duck of the summer and his 4th career quack from 15 innings, which lands him a very respectable 73rd of 820 in Linton's all-time roll of dishonour. 🥇0️⃣ And in a delightful quirk of the calendar — on this very day back in 1960, Basil Sears was going through precisely the same ritual, also nought not out against Bearsted CC. Sears (1947–1966) went on to retire with 20 ducks to his name, good enough for 11th on the all-time list, so Benny keeps rather grand company on the 17th of September. Elsewhere The second fixture of the weekend passed without incident — batsmen behaving themselves, ducks staying firmly in their pond. A rare outbreak of competence. Week in summary One duck from two matches this weekend — the Duck Club's hunting grounds ran unusually quiet, with only Benny answering the call. Consistency of a very particular kind continues elsewhere in the club, even if this week it mostly kept its head down. 🍩 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 1. 🦆 Duck News — 10-11 September 1966

2 ducks
Week 20 · 10 Sep 1966 – 11 Sep 1966
Two matches, two visits to the crease, two long walks back. A quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club, but quality over quantity, as ever. 🏏 1st XI — Home to Reeds CC, Saturday 10th Benny Bell, in at four, departed bowled by J Doe without troubling the scorers. His third duck of the season and, remarkably, his third for his LPCC career too — a tidy symmetry not many manage. From 14 innings that puts him 96th of 820 in the all-time roll, a position of dubious distinction he shares with rather good company: on this very day in 1955, John "--'s" Taylor was also walking back against Old Roffensians, en route to 15 career ducks and a lofty 19th on the honours board. Some things, it seems, are written in the stars — or at least in the scorebook, on the same date, eleven years apart. 🏏 1st XI — Away to Staplehurst CC, Sunday 11th Frank Brattle, batting at six, fell bowled by A Thirkell — his third duck of the season, but a rather more imposing 28th of an illustrious career spanning 254 innings. That's good enough for 6th of 820 all-time, the sort of consistency of a very particular kind that takes decades to cultivate. And he's not flying solo in the family stakes either — his parent-child relation Percy Brattle already carries 5 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring evidently runs in the blood. Frank's nought also has a ghost in the machine: on this same day in 1954, Len Conway made his own duck against Old Roffensians, one of twelve on his way to a Debutante Duck badge and 28th in the all-time standings. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one each for the 1st XI home and away — a modest but perfectly formed haul. Don Clayton — 4 ducks / 28 innings (14.29%) Alan Tucker still holds the grimmest strike rate of the lot at a barnstorming 44%, though Paul Bowles leads on raw volume and shows no sign of slowing. 🥇 The season rolls on, and with it the ever-present promise that next weekend's fixtures will surely add a name or two to the list. Until then — bat well, but not too well. 🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 3 to 7 September 1966

3 ducks
Week 19 · 3 Sep 1966 – 7 Sep 1966
A modest but perfectly formed haul this week: three ducks harvested across two fixtures, the exclusive zero club welcoming its usual mix of the seasoned and the merely unlucky. Saturday 3 September — 1st XI v St Lukes Maidstone CC (h) Terry Donovan, at number 7, was caught W Hunt bowled C Clark without troubling the scorers — his 3rd duck of the campaign and 13th of an increasingly decorated career, now 24th on the all-time list from 820 names. A former Duck of the Year (1964) and a nearly-man on the Full House board with ducks at 10 of 11 positions, Terry remains one short of true positional immortality. Neatly, on this very day back in 1944 Jimmy Jameson also walked back for nought, against the Home Guard's Royal West Kent 11th Battalion no less — some anniversaries just won't let go. At 11, Jon Harris departed caught J Beesley bowled John Francis, also without scoring. His 2nd duck of the season and, remarkably, his 2nd in only 2 career innings — a start to remember, if not fondly. Jon already holds a startling spread of honours: a Full House man himself (duck at every position 1-11), a fixture on the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck, Tourist Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first knock. He also shares Quacking Together billing with Jim Hall and Trevor Back, and Only Two Quacks company with Allan Cooper — company he is in no hurry to leave. Fittingly, like Terry, Jon's duck also falls on the same 1944 anniversary as Jimmy Jameson's. A chip off an unusual block too — his relative Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own. Wednesday 7 September — Midweek XI v Birchington CC (a) Mike Iliffe, at 7, was caught M Yates bowled C Chetwynd for nought — his 4th duck of the season, and 4th of his career from just 12 innings, a fearsome 33% strike rate that plants him 84th on the all-time chart. On this day in 1963, Frank Brattle — a Linton stalwart of 1937 to 1966 with 27 career ducks to his name — also made a duck, against St Lukes Maidstone CC. The week in brief Two matches, three ducks, no pairs and no family affairs this time round — the ducks kept themselves to themselves. Fitting that both 1st XI victims fell on the same day in cricketing history as a wartime forebear 22 years their senior. Richard Ashby — 4 ducks, 19 innings, 21.05% 5. Don Clayton — 4 ducks, 28 innings, 14.29% Mike Iliffe's Wednesday nought vaults him straight into podium contention — one to watch as the season enters its closing stretch. Roll on the next fixture, LPCC — the zero club never sleeps. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 27th to 29th August 1966

2 ducks
Week 18 · 27 Aug 1966 – 29 Aug 1966
A quiet week for the exclusive zero club, but quality over quantity — both of this week's ducks arrived in the same innings, off the same bowler, at the same ground. Bryan Elswood clearly fancied his chances against Linton Park at Bearsted on the 29th, and history duly obliged him twice. 🏏 1st XI — Bearsted CC (away) First to fall was Fred Greenfield, cleaned up by Bryan Elswood at number five. It's duck number two of the season for Fred and the 10th of a career now standing at 69 innings — good enough for 34th on the all-time list, a position he shares in spirit with Don Hartridge, Ernest Cruttenden and William Blight, all three also parked on 10. Fred is, of course, a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck honours board from a past farewell innings, so no fresh alarm bells there — merely a reminder that the club never forgets. And on this very day back in 1954, the great Stan Wratten was doing much the same thing against D Hambrook XI, en route to 27 career ducks and 8th place on the all-time roll of dishonour. Four down the order, Derry Evans joined him in the walk of shame, also bowled by the relentless Elswood — a tidy brace of scalps for the bowler and a shared miserable afternoon for Linton Park. It's Derry's second duck of the season and, fittingly, his second for the club overall, from just 12 innings — a career very much in its infancy on the honours board, currently sat at 137th of 819. Derry too finds himself keeping Stan Wratten company from 1954, proof that some Augusts simply repeat themselves. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks — both from the same fixture, both off the same bowler. The other match came and went without troubling the scorers of nought, a rare mercy. Bryan Elswood, if he's reading this, has our reluctant admiration. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Colin Eldred — 3 ducks from 4 innings (75.00%) 2. Alan Tucker — 4 ducks from 9 innings (44.44%) 3. Paul Bowles — 7 ducks from 19 innings (36.84%) 4. Richard Ashby — 4 ducks from 18 innings (22.22%) 5. Don Clayton — 4 ducks from 27 innings (14.81%) Colin Eldred continues to make not scoring look like a full-time occupation. Roll on the next fixture — the ducks, as ever, will be waiting.

🦆 Duck News — 20–21 August 1966 🦆

0 ducks
Week 17 · 20 Aug 1966 – 21 Aug 1966
A quiet weekend for the connoisseurs of the nought. Two matches were played across the 20th and 21st and, remarkably, not a single duck was laid at either ground. Every batsman who walked to the crease at least troubled the scorers before departing — a shocking outbreak of competence that the Duck Club can only view with suspicion. Some might call this "good batting". We call it a wasted opportunity. The zero club's membership secretary reports no new inductions, no pairs, no golden ducks, and certainly no families quietly uniting in shared ignominy. The art of not scoring, it seems, took the weekend off. Still, the season standings remain fiercely contested, and nobody at the top of this table got there by accident. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 - Paul Bowles — 7 ducks from 17 innings (41.2%). The clear pacesetter, and a man seemingly allergic to the middle of the bat. - Alan Tucker — 4 ducks from just 9 innings (44.4%). The highest strike rate in the top five, in the very worst sense. - Richard Ashby — 4 ducks from 17 innings (23.5%). Consistency of a very particular kind. - Don Clayton — 4 ducks from 25 innings (16.0%). Getting there the scenic way. - Colin Eldred — 3 ducks from just 4 innings (75.0%) 🍩. The pitch, the bowlers, and history itself all suggest this drought cannot last. Get your ducks in a row, Linton — or better yet, don't. 🦆 0️⃣ 🏏

Elsewhere the fixture list stayed refreshingly fowl-free. 1st XI — Charles Arkcoll (away, 2 July) - Don Clayton fell for 0 at first drop, bowled by Vic Wellard. His third career duck lifts him to 99th of 815 on the all-time list — and, delightfully, on this very day back in 1955, one G Hill also made a duck at this same ground against these same opponents. Some fixtures simply demand tribute. - John Thirkell followed, caught Wilson bowled Aiston, and simply extended his own reign. Career duck number 56 keeps him unchallenged at #1 of 815 in Linton's all-time records — a three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955), a permanent fixture of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, and a veteran of two separate Quacking Together pairings down the years.
