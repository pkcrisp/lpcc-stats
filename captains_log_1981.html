🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1981 · 1981 — 1 match

Sun 27 Sep 1981 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWLWD

Sunday last, up at Linton Park, and yours truly won the toss and elected to bat — the right call, I still maintain, whatever the lads mutter about jinxing it in the tea queue. We batted through our twenty-four overs for 159 for 7, which felt about par on a day when nothing quite raced away but nothing embarrassed us either. Malcolm Pay set the tone with a fine 32, and he had good company: Jon Harris chipped in 29 and Martin Mears 27, while I managed to potter along to 22 myself before doing something needlessly rash, as is tradition.

The proper story of the day, though, belonged to Jon Harris, who brought up 3,000 career runs for the club in the same innings — a landmark worth far more than a paragraph, and one the whole side was delighted to see him reach. Elsewhere in the ledger, I find myself leading the 1981 1st XI run charts with 1,661 for the season, while over in the 2nds Gareth Thomas tops their table with 35 — early days there yet, but every column counts.

As for the match itself, we simply couldn't defend what we'd made, and the visitors saw us off by 7 wickets with something to spare. Not our day, then, and no use pretending otherwise. Heads up, chins forward, and back to the nets — Linton Park will be sharper for it next time out.

🗓️ 19 Sep – 25 Sep 1981 · 1981 — 1 match

Wed 23 Sep 1981 🧢 Midweek XIv HC Hawkenbury CC · HomeformLLDWD

I'm the honorary match reporter here — no security or file tools needed, just the report.

Another cracker at Linton Park, this one, though it took me until the toss to feel like a genius. I put us in — the right call, I felt — and the lads duly obliged. Adrian Smith played the innings of the day, 71 well past his fifty, with Paul Bowles giving him fine support, unbeaten on 59. Not everyone got among the runs, mind — C- Wilkins and Steve Petty both walked back for golden ducks, but 163/7 from our 51.3 overs felt like a total with enough about it to make Hawkenbury work.

And work they did. R Kemp gave us a proper fright with a fine half-century of his own, 54, ably assisted by S Gower's 21, but the rest of the order rather melted away — R Jones went for nought, and Paul Bowles was simply magnificent with the ball, 3-17 and unplayable for stretches. I found some rhythm myself, 3-37 with three maidens to show for the effort, and Steve Petty chipped in with one. Adrian Smith had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 19 byes leaking through, but nobody's perfect, least of all with the gloves after a long day's toil.

In the end Hawkenbury crawled to 127/7 and neither side could land the killer blow. A draw, then, honours shared — and on the balance of a match with more character than the scoreline lets on, I'll call that a fair result.

Tue 15 Sep 1981 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformDLLDW

Tuesday last took us down the road to The Mote, where I lost the toss for what felt like the ninth week running and found myself leading the lads into the field regardless. No great hardship, as it turned out — bowling first suits us better than we sometimes admit.

The Mote ground their way to 145 for 9 from 49.4 overs, William Hickling top-scoring with a watchful 45, well supported by B. Snow's 25 and Nigel Mullarkey's 21. J. Williams departed without troubling the scorers. I found some rare rhythm with the ball, finishing with 5 for 29 and three maidens — the sort of spell a captain dreams of and seldom gets — while Trevor Back (1-4) and Laurie Turner (1-19) kept things admirably tight at the other end.

Chasing 146, we never quite matched our bowling for fluency. Nigel Thirkell's 31 anchored the innings nicely, with Paul Bowles and Andy Woollett both chipping in 29 apiece, and I added a watchful 17 that, pleasingly, edged me past Terry Whittaker into 16th on the club's all-time run-scorers list. We stalled at 136 for 7 from our 44, short of the target but far from disgraced.

Elsewhere, Jim Hall continues to lead our 1981 Midweek run charts with 234, while I find myself, rather nicely, out front on wickets with 20 for the summer.

A draw, then — honours shared, and a fair one at that: we bowled like champions and batted like it was still only April.

Wednesday, 09 September saw us take the road to Penryn St Gluvias, and winning the toss I did not hesitate to bat first — the pitch had "runs" written all over it, and I like to think I can still read a wicket even if the knees no longer read the singles quite so eagerly. Michael Chambers was in irresistible form, spinning through Penryn's order for 5-13, well backed by Fred Cheesman's 3-18 and a tidy 1-2 from Tim Thirkell, with Allan Cooper positively miserly, sending down five maidens without so much as a run to show for it. Jaymin Patel's 21 was the only real resistance as the home side folded for 88 in 31 overs, Bettison and Hocking both departing without troubling the scorer.

Paul Bowles had a busier afternoon behind the stumps than his 19 byes suggest he'd have liked, but nobody will remember that once they hear Michael Chambers passed 100 career wickets in the same match — a landmark thoroughly deserved and warmly toasted in the bar. A 122-run victory, two points banked, and, dare I say it, rather a good day to be captain.

There was a footnote of history too — Tim Thirkell's 42 lifted him past Frank Brattle into 9th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, a genuinely proud moment amid the chaos.

We got home by four runs. Ragged, nervy, thoroughly English — and an absolutely smashing day for the club.
