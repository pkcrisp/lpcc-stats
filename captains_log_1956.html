🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1956 · 1956 — 2 matches

Sun 30 Sep 1956 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDDDW

Sunday 30th September, and by rights we should still be out there padded up somewhere, given how little cricket we actually witnessed. We hosted Linton Park CC, I won the toss, and — in a decision I shall be defending at the bar for some weeks yet — elected to bat first.

It did not go well. Forty-two all out in 34.1 overs is the sort of total that makes the tea urn seem the highlight of the afternoon. Basil Sears, Peter Weedon and Monty Deakes all contrived to depart without troubling the scorer, a hat-trick of ducks that had the wives in the pavilion reaching for their knitting rather than their scorecards. Quite how three good men can queue up to be bowled for nought in the same innings I shall leave to greater cricketing minds than mine.

And yet — cricket, glorious and infuriating, found a way to spare our blushes. Neither side could force home the advantage, and honours were shared in a draw that flattered us rather more than our batting deserved.

Elsewhere, the season's ledger continues to make cheerful reading regardless of Sunday's efforts: John Thirkell sits proudly atop the 1st XI run charts with 683 runs, John Taylor leads the Married men with 7, and Clive Smith heads the Singles with 33.

Verdict: a match we were fortunate to escape with a draw, and one best filed under "character-building."

Sat 29 Sep 1956 🧢 1st XIv AX A. Snell XI · HomeformDWWWW

John Thirkell put us in — brave call, and for a while it looked like the sort of decision that gets discussed ruefully in the bar for weeks. Seventy-nine for five off our twenty-six overs was hardly a declaration of intent, and the Duck Club took two new members in Frank Brattle and Tony Tipples, both trudging back without troubling the scorer. Not our finest hour with the willow, and I'll admit I sat pad-less on the boundary wondering whether I'd made the coin toss look like an act of self-harm.

But cricket, bless it, has a way of making a fool of you twice in one afternoon. Alan Wilkes turned the game on its head with an absolutely magnificent spell of 6 for 10, spinning through the A. Snell XI order like a man possessed, five of his overs going for nothing at all. John Taylor backed him up beautifully with 4 for 23, and between them they sent S Capon, J Nicholson and J Knowles packing for ducks of their own — the Duck Club doing very well out of both innings. The visitors were bowled out for a paltry 51 in 23.3 overs, and just like that our shaky total looked positively handsome. I chipped in with 15 with the bat for good measure, and it was pleasing to see the runs keep coming up the season charts too.

We got over the line by 28 runs in the end — a scruffy, nervy, thoroughly entertaining way to win, and on reflection, exactly the kind of afternoon that makes this club what it is. A good day for the club indeed.

Sun 23 Sep 1956 🧢 1st XIv BW Birchington Wanderers · HomeformDDWWW

Called it correctly at the toss on Sunday, and with a green-looking pitch and no wind to speak of, batting first was hardly a decision at all. The lads settled in nicely: Ray Howard was the man of the morning, powering to 61, while William Buck anchored the tail end of the innings with an unbeaten 51 to see us home at 158 for 5 from 47.4 overs. Not a flashy total, perhaps, but a substantial one, and enough runs on the board to make Birchington Wanderers think rather than swing.

And think they did not, as it turned out. A Walker battled to 27 and E Webb chipped in with 21, but around them the innings simply came apart. D Edwards, B Edwards and J Shaylor all trooped back without so much as a single to their names, three ducks in a heap, and the whole thing was over inside 30.4 overs for a paltry 78. I had one of those afternoons a captain dreams about and rarely gets, five for 21 with four maidens thrown in for good measure, while Alan Wilkes was hunting from the other end with 5 for 32 of his own. Between us there was precious little left for Birchington to work with.

Eighty runs the margin, and a thoroughly satisfying one. Stan Wratten continues to churn out the runs and sits third in the season's charts, and I'll confess I'm rather enjoying life at the top of the wicket-takers for now. Onwards indeed.

Sat 22 Sep 1956 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformWDDWW

Called it right at the toss on a bright Saturday against Bearsted, and with barely a thought I stuck us in to bat — a decision I spent much of the afternoon quietly reconsidering. Ninety-seven all out in 38.1 overs is not a total to frighten anyone, and only Stan Wratten, with a battling 29, seemed to have read the script; the rest of us rather fell over ourselves, Bob Arnold and C Sandford both departing for golden ducks that will live longer in the memory than either of them would wish.

Tea was a subdued affair, but the lads turned to the task of defending our modest total with a spirit that did the club proud. Bearsted never got going, subsiding to 56 all out in 32.1 overs, with F Bayley and Peter Longley both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. Ray Howard was the architect of it all — 5 for 15, six of his overs maidens, bowling with a miserliness bordering on the parsimonious — well supported by John Ames's 4 for 21 and a tidy 1 for 4 from Tony Snell. Thirteen byes rather gave the game away about the state of things behind the stumps, and poor Bob Arnold will not thank me for dwelling on it.

As it stands I lead the season's run charts with 628, while John Ames tops the wicket-takers on 63 — form that showed handsomely today. We got home by 41 runs: a scrappy, unlikely, thoroughly satisfying win, and a good day for the club.

🗓️ 15 Sep – 21 Sep 1956 · 1956 — 2 matches

Sun 16 Sep 1956 🧢 1st XIv TC Tunbridge Wells CC · HomeformWWDDW

Tuesday, and I'll admit I slept rather well after Sunday's exertions. Winning the toss and choosing to bat felt like the obvious call, though I confess I say that every week whether we bat first or not — this time, mercifully, the pitch and Tom Osborn agreed with me. What followed was as fine an opening stand as this club has produced in some while: 176 for the first wicket, an innings of such serenity that several of us on the boundary had time to finish our tea before a wicket fell. Then, as cricket so loves to remind us, the game turned briefly comic — 176 for one became 187 for four in what felt like the time it takes to pour another cup, poor Ray Howard departing without troubling the scorers. Osborn, though, was unmoved by the chaos around him, sailing serenely to 103 not out, well supported by Stan Wratten's 69. We closed on 195 for 4.

If our batting had its wobble, our bowling had none. Tunbridge Wells were bowled out for a meagre 76, undone almost entirely by John Ames, who span a web few of them escaped — 9 for 33, six of those overs maidens, figures that verge on the unsporting. Tony Snell chipped in for good measure, and three of their number trooped back without scoring a run between them.

A word for the history books: Osborn's century lifts him to 11th on the club's all-time run list, past Maurice Fenner, while Wratten and Ames continue to lead our season charts with real distinction.

A fine win by 119 runs, then, and thoroughly deserved. Onwards.

All in all, a thoroughly good day for Linton Park — the sort of afternoon that makes the teas taste that little bit sweeter.

So a draw it was, honours shared, neither side quite able to land the decisive blow. Frustrating, perhaps, but with Bob Brigden bowling like that and Basten batting like that, there's plenty for this Linton Park side to be cheerful about.

Sun 02 Sep 1956 🧢 1st XIv HH Halfway House · HomeformWDLWW

Called correctly for once — small mercies — and with the sun doing its best impression of summer, there was never much doubt I'd have us in. Ninety-three for seven from our thirty-nine overs was scarcely a declaration of intent, more a modest request for consideration, but Tom Osborn batted with real substance for his unbeaten 40, and Len Conway chipped in a tidy 22 to keep the scoreboard honest. Stan Wratten and Gordon Fenner both contrived to depart without troubling the scorers — a matching pair of ducks that did nothing for the tea-interval conversation.

Eighty on board looked eminently gettable when Halfway House came out to chase, but cricket has a wonderful way of humbling expectation. Robin Bush played the innings of the day with 22, and Roger Mills went first ball without scoring — cruel game. I found some turn that I scarcely deserved and finished with 5 for 33, four of my seven overs going for nothing at all, while Len Conway backed up his batting with 3 for 26 and Stan Wratten snaffled one for just 3 runs. They limped to 80 for 9 from their thirty-seven, and honours were shared.

A draw, then — honest toil rewarded with neither triumph nor disaster, which is really what club cricket is all about. William Buck continues to knock along nicely in third in the season's run charts, and I'm rather chuffed to find myself topping the wicket pile with fifty for the year. Not a bad afternoon's work, all told.

Sun 26 Aug 1956 🧢 1st XIv TX Tom Osborn's XI · HomeformDWDLW

Sunday last, 26 August 1956, and we welcomed Tom Osborn's XI to the ground on a day that would end very much in our favour. I called correctly at the toss and there was never any real debate in my mind — we would bat.

The lads made handsome use of the decision, posting 162 for 6 from our 48 overs. John Larking played beautifully for a well-deserved 52, with Clive Smith chipping in a fine 38 and Tony Levick a useful 26 to give the total real substance.

If batting had been a pleasure, bowling was pure theatre. Tom Osborn's XI simply had no answer, folding for 43 in only 17.5 overs, with Fleetwood, Reader and Haylor all departing without so much as a run to their names. Alan Wilkes was magnificent, taking 4 for 19, while Ray Howard picked up 1 for 3. I had some joy myself with the ball, finding a bit of rhythm to take 3 for 21 including three maidens, which I mention only the once and am rather pleased about.

There was extra reason to raise a glass afterwards — those three wickets took me past 200 for my career and lifted me above George Champion into 4th on the club's all-time list, while I currently lead the 1956 wicket charts with 45 and sit 5th among the run-scorers with 229.

We ran out winners by 119 runs. A thoroughly good day for the club, and one to savour.
