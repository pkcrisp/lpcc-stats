🦆 Duck Report — 26–27 September 1998

3 ducks
Week 22 · 26 Sep 1998 – 27 Sep 1998
A quiet weekend for the counting of runs but a fruitful one for the counting of noughts. Three ducks fell across two fixtures, and — fittingly for a club that treats failure as heritage — the Thirkell name managed to touch every single one of them. Saturday 26 September — 1st XI v Bidborough CC Nigel Thirkell departed at number six, caught P Wood bowled Doug Moore, for his 2nd duck of the season and 53rd of an extraordinary career spanning 969 innings — good enough for outright 7th place of 1238 in the all-time table. Nigel already sits comfortably among his own honours: Duck of the Year 1986, a fixture of both the Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens, and one half of Only Two Quacks with Jim Hall. The surname runs deep in the duck business too — siblings Tim (67) and Simon (34), children John (90) and Daniel (8), and cousin-in-arms Stuart (1) are all paid-up members of the family firm. And on this very day back in 1965, Roy Clarke also made a duck against Linton Park CC, one of 40 across a 1957–66 career that leaves him 14th all-time. Last man in Jon Harris kept him company, bowled Mark Murphy, for his 1st duck of the season and a staggering 75th career nought from 563 innings — the silver medal position, 2nd of 1238 all time. Jon's honours board reads like a museum wing: Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, the Debutante Duck club, and three separate Duck of the Year titles (1976, 1980, 1981), alongside Quacking Together links to both Jim Hall and Trevor Back. Son Richard carries on the tradition with 36 career ducks of his own. And remarkably, the same 1965 date claimed Roy Clarke too — Jon and Nigel keeping identical company across the decades. Sunday 27 September — 1st XI v Linton Park CC Peter Whyte fell at number five, bowled by none other than Tim Thirkell — evidently the family trade extends to dismissing others as well as suffering the duck themselves. It was Peter's 1st duck of the season and 18th of a 213-innings career, 51st of 1238 all-time. On this same day in 1964, Robert Murray also went without troubling the scorer, one of 17 ducks in a 1958–65 career that leaves him just two rungs further up the ladder at 53rd. A tidy little weekend, then: three ducks, two matches, one XI, and a Thirkell involved in every single dismissal — batting or bowling. The art of not scoring remains a proud family tradition at Linton Park. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 30 innings, 20.0% 2. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks, 10 innings, 40.0% 3. Adam Pooley — 4 ducks, 21 innings, 19.05% 4. John Taylor — 3 ducks, 11 innings, 27.27% 5. Richard Piper — 3 ducks, 19 innings, 15.79% Ian Brattle's 40% strike rate keeps him firmly in the shake-up, but Piers McDonald's volume still has him out front. Plenty of season left for the standings to shuffle further. 🏏 Next fixture, next chance — the zero club never sleeps. See you all out there, and may your bat find the middle 🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Club Bulletin — 19–20 September 1998

6 ducks
Week 21 · 19 Sep 1998 – 20 Sep 1998
Six ducks across three fixtures as the season winds toward its close, plenty of last-gasp candidates padding out the tallies before the scorebooks go away for winter. Nobody troubled the golden duck lists this week — every one of the six got bat on ball before the long walk back. 🏏 Saturday 19th — Elliotts CC (away) The 2nd XI did the heavy lifting away at Elliotts, four ducks in the one innings — a genuine cluster event for the club's dubious distinction. John Taylor edged behind off Alan Ricketts for 0️⃣, his 3rd duck of the summer and, remarkably, his 3rd in an 11-innings career too — a man building his entire batting record on noughts. On this very day in 1982, David Iliffe was about the same business against Anchorians CC, en route to Duck of the Year and a spot on the Quacked by Kin roll (courtesy of Mike Iliffe). Dave Todd ran himself out for his 1st of the season, moving to 13 career ducks in 181 innings — an occasional lapse rather than a habit. Same 1982 Iliffe parallel. Paul Croucher went lbw to Steve Swindell, his 1st of the campaign and 9th overall, and does so as a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from some past farewell innings — no danger of that repeating today. Another entry on the 1982 Iliffe roll call. Closing the innings, Ian Brattle was bowled by Steve Swindell for his 4th of the season, stretching to 44 career ducks — 11th all-time — with standing membership of 50 Before a 50 (51 ducks, high score 33), Ducks in a Row (7 consecutive) and Only Two Quacks (with Michael Beeney), plus a Debutante Duck credential from his very first innings. His sibling Paul Brattle carries 26 career ducks of his own, so the family trade is in good hands. Same 1982 parallel once more — David Iliffe really got about that day. 🏏 Sunday 20th — Anchorians CC (home) The 1st XI kept the tradition ticking over at home. Richard Piper was caught and bowled by Eddie Stanford for his 3rd duck of the season, a 15th career entry that sits him 57th all-time and keeps his name on the Ducks on the Road, Full House, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck boards — a proper collector's set. His parent Brian Piper carries 20 career ducks of his own. On this day in 1997, Daniel Thirkell made a duck against Borden CC. Trevor Back was run out for his 2nd of the season, moving to a formidable 48 career ducks — 9th all-time — already decorating Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck, Lone Duck, Only Two Quacks (with Allan Cooper) and Quacking Together (with Jon Harris). Same day, same year, same Daniel Thirkell parallel. 📊 Week in summary Six ducks, three matches, one four-wicket cluster at Elliotts doing most of the damage. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks in 29 innings (20.69%) 2. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks in 9 innings (44.44%) 3. Adam Pooley — 4 ducks in 20 innings (20.0%) 4. John Taylor — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) 5. Richard Piper — 3 ducks in 19 innings (15.79%) With three of this week's names already propping up the season table, next weekend offers fresh opportunity for the exclusive zero club to welcome new candidates. We'll be watching. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Midweek Special, 13–16 September 1998

2 ducks
Week 20 · 13 Sep 1998 – 16 Sep 1998
Two matches in the diary this week, and the club's ever-reliable purveyors of nought found exactly one fixture worth troubling — but oh, how they troubled it. The Midweek XI's trip against Tunbridge Wells Wednesday CC on the 16th produced a tidy brace of ducks, top and bottom of the order, as if to prove the art of not scoring respects no batting position. 🏏 Midweek XI v Tunbridge Wells Wednesday CC (home) Opening the innings and, evidently, opening the account for the day, Piers McDonald was bowled by Tedder without troubling the scorers — his 6th duck of the summer and 41st for the club, a tally that vaults him to 13th in Linton's all-time roll of dishonour, out of 1,236 names on the list. Piers already wears the Duck of the Year sash for both 1997 and 1998, and with 6 already banked this season he shows no sign of relinquishing it, a familiar face too in the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. On this very day back in 1962, Richard Ashby (1962–1995, 28 career ducks, 30th all-time) was performing the identical ceremony against Staplehurst CC — some fixtures, it seems, are written into the calendar itself. Down at number nine, Fred Cheesman kept the tradition alive, bowled by M. Radford for his 1st duck of the season and 33rd of an illustrious career, good enough for 22nd on the all-time list. Fred is a Full House member, a duck logged in every position from 1 through 11 across his career, and a two-time Duck of the Year from 1990 and 1991, proof that former glories age rather well. He now sits level on 33 with Basil Sears, who took 420 innings between 1947 and 1975 to reach the same number — Fred needed a mere 189. Efficiency, of a sort. It runs in the family too: Stuart and Steve Cheesman are already established club members on 27 and 13 career ducks respectively, so the surname clearly carries a duck gene. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both from the same Midweek outing — top and tail of the order doing their bit while the rest of the XI, rather selfishly, got on with scoring runs. Elsewhere the ducks evaded the net entirely. A quiet week by Linton's exacting standards, but quality over quantity, as ever, in the exclusive zero club. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season 1998 — Leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks / 27 innings (22.2%) 2. Adam Pooley — 4 ducks / 19 innings (21.1%) 3. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks / 8 innings (37.5%) 4. Mike Elworthy — 3 ducks / 19 innings (15.8%) 5. David Castle — 3 ducks / 20 innings (15.0%) Piers extends his lead at the top, though Ian Brattle's frankly alarming 37.5% strike rate from just 8 innings suggests a late charge could yet be on the cards. The season rolls on — who's next through the turnstile of the exclusive zero club? Get out there, get in, and get out for nought. We'll be watching. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — The Cornish Quartet, 6 September 1998

4 ducks
Week 19 · 6 Sep 1998
Tour week brought the Tour XI down to Cornwall to face Beacon CC, and while the scenery was glorious the batting card was not. Four ducks fell in the one innings, a proper cluster that turned an away day into open season for the club's most dubious honour. 🏏 Tour XI at Beacon CC, Cornwall First to go was Richard Piper, caught by a fielder the scorer clearly couldn't name and bowled Steve Eddy at number two. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 14th of a career now 70 innings deep, good enough for 59th on the all-time list. A card-carrying member of the Ducks on the Road, Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens, and a Full House man who has now ducked from every position 1 to 11 — Richard really does specialise. He keeps it in the family too, with kin Brian Piper sitting on 20 career noughts of his own. Neatly, on this very day back in 1967, Roger Craddock (1966–68) also trooped off for nought, against Birchington CC — proof the calendar has form for this sort of thing. Next in was Stuart Cheesman, caught David Proctor bowled Chris Terrill at five, his 3rd of the season and 27th career duck, moving him to 32nd all-time from 157 innings. Stuart ducked on debut all those years ago and has twice been crowned Duck of the Year, in 1994 and 1996, and owns his own Full House besides. The Cheesman name runs thick with noughts — brother Steve carries 13, and kin Fred Cheesman a full 32. And who else should share Stuart's spot on the 6th of September, 1967? Roger Craddock again, out for nought at Birchington. At six, Simon Thirkell was bowled by Chris Terrill for his 1st duck of the season, but a hefty 34th of a career that puts him 20th all-time from 152 innings — a paid-up member of 50 Before a 50 (59 ducks, high score a mere 48), a former Duck of the Year in 1983, and doubly fitting today, a fixture of the Tourist Duck top ten. The Thirkell name is a duck dynasty in itself: kin John Thirkell tops the entire club charts with 90, siblings Tim and Nigel sit on 67 and 52, and the line runs on through Daniel and Stuart Thirkell besides. And yes — Roger Craddock was there in 1967 too. Rounding it off, Adam Pooley was bowled David Proctor at seven for his 4th duck of the season, taking him to 13 career and 60th all-time from just 52 innings — a rapid rise. He shares Only Two Quacks honours with both Fred Cheesman (Stuart's own kin, no less) and Paul Bowles, and is a Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell innings. Fittingly, Roger Craddock's 1967 nought completes the set for the fourth time today. 📅 Week in Summary One match, four ducks, zero survivors past the top seven — Beacon CC's bowlers clearly enjoyed the Cornish air more than the Tour XI's batting did. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 25 inns, 5 ducks (20.0%) 2. Adam Pooley — 18 inns, 4 ducks (22.22%) 3. Ian Brattle — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.86%) 4. Mike Elworthy — 18 inns, 3 ducks (16.67%) 5. David Castle — 20 inns, 3 ducks (15.0%) Ian Brattle's 42.86% remains the number to fear. The tour rolls on — who fancies adding their name to this exclusive zero club next time out? 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 29 August to 3 September 1998 🦆

3 ducks
Week 18 · 29 Aug 1998 – 3 Sep 1998
Four fixtures, three ducks, and the club's Cornwall tour proving that the art of not scoring travels rather well. A tidy little haul to close out the summer. Saturday 29 August — 1st XI v Herne Bay CC Stuart Cheesman got the ball rolling at number two, bowled by Paul Crow for his 1st duck of the season and 25th of an increasingly distinguished career, holding at 34th in the all-time table. A fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, a two-time Duck of the Year (1994 and 1996), and a Full House honoree (a duck at every position 1 to 11) — Stuart barely needs the introduction anymore. On this very day in 1954, Stan Wratten also strolled back for nought, against D Hambrook XI, on his way to 35 career ducks and 18th on the all-time list. On Tour in Cornwall — Tour XI The tour party found the Cornish air agreed with the club's oldest tradition. At Helston on 1 September, Stewart Morrison was caught at mid-off off Bradley Bury without scoring, his 2nd duck of the season and 22nd of his career, good for 43rd all time. Stewart carries the Debutante Duck badge, sits agonisingly as a Full House nearly-man on 10 of 11 positions, and features in the Lone Duck top 10 — a CV of near-misses and one very reliable miss. On this day in 1973, Tim Thirkell also departed for nought, against Whitstable CC, on the way to a club-record 67 ducks and top spot all time. Two days later at Trengilly Wartha, Stuart Cheesman was back among the wickets, bowled by B- Esses for his 2nd duck of the week and the season, and 26th of his career — a grim little pair stitched together across Kent and Cornwall, though the King Pair club remains safely out of reach for now. On this day in 1972, Benny Bell also made a duck, against Staplehurst CC, en route to 30 career noughts. Week in Summary Three ducks from four matches, two of them courtesy of the touring party, and one club stalwart doing the hard work of a pair single-handed. Quiet by Linton standards, but the ducks always know where to find the tour bus. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 25 inns, 5 ducks, 20.0% 2. David Castle — 20 inns, 3 ducks, 15.0% None of this week's names have troubled the top five yet — plenty of season left for that to change. 🏏 Next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, further additions to the club's most exclusive and least wanted honours board. See you at the crease — or, more likely, on the way back from it. 🍩0️⃣

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 22 August 1998 🦆

0 ducks
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1998
A rare and faintly unsettling occurrence to report from the Linton Park estate this week: nothing. Not a single quack. Across both fixtures played on the 22nd, all comers went to the crease and, one way or another, troubled the scorer before troubling the pavilion. The exclusive zero club, so reliably oversubscribed, sat this one out entirely. Two whole matches came and went without so much as a nervous single-figure LBW shout to get the Duck Club's pulse racing. Whether this represents a sudden and alarming outbreak of competence across Linton Park's batting order, or simply the bowlers taking the weekend off, the record cannot say — only that the tally for the week stands, immovably, at 0️⃣ ducks from 2 matches. Let it be noted for posterity: this is not a triumph. The art of not scoring requires practice, dedication, and a certain fearless disregard for one's own average — and for one week only, it seems, the membership collectively forgot how. We trust normal service, and normal ignominy, resumes forthwith. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard — 1998 🥇🍩 - Piers McDonald — 5 ducks from 24 innings (20.8%) - Ian Brattle — 3 ducks from 7 innings (42.9%) — still the season's most efficient collector, pound for pound - Adam Pooley — 3 ducks from 14 innings (21.4%) - Mike Elworthy — 3 ducks from 15 innings (20.0%) - David Castle — 3 ducks from 19 innings (15.8%) Ian Brattle continues to lead the field on strike rate alone, a scandalous 42.86% duck ratio that the rest of the top five would do well to fear. Piers McDonald holds the outright lead on volume and will want to keep it that way as the season narrows toward its close. So — quiet week, but the season is young enough yet, and Linton Park's bowlers have shown before they can find a fifth stump when it matters. Next fixture, normal hostilities are expected to resume. The ducks, we are reliably informed, are merely regrouping. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 15–16 August 1998 🦆

2 ducks
Week 16 · 15 Aug 1998 – 16 Aug 1998
Slim pickings this round — just the two noughts to report — but what they lacked in volume they made up for in symmetry: both fell in the same innings, at the same ground, to the same bowler. Tim Bentall clearly fancied his chances against Linton's middle order, and Linton obliged. 🏏 Saturday — 2nd XI v Shipbourne CC (home) Opening the innings, Mike Elworthy departed without scoring, snapped up by a fielder the scorebook coyly records only as "Unsure" — an appropriately named accomplice — off Tim Bentall. It's the 3rd duck of Mike's season and the 29th of an increasingly distinguished LPCC career, now 271 innings deep and sitting 29th in the all-time rankings. A paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10, Mike continues his slow, stately climb up the wrong end of the table. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1964, club legend John Thirkell — the all-time record holder with 90 ducks — was busy making his own nought against Bowaters CC. Some traditions never die. Two down at first drop came Trevor Back, comprehensively bowled by the same Tim Bentall for his 1st duck of the season and 47th of his career — good enough for outright 10th on the all-time list from 405 innings. Trevor's honours board reads like a small trophy cabinet: a fixture in the Lone Duck top 10, the Homebody Duck top 10, and the Ducks on the Road top 10 simultaneously — proof he can conjure a nought whether Linton Park is home or away. He also carries standing Quacking Together billing alongside Jon Harris, LPCC's 2nd all-time duck-getter. And, as with Mike, 15 August 1964 is a date etched in the record books: John Thirkell's own duck against Bowaters CC, 34 years to the day before Trevor's. Elsewhere across the club's three fixtures this weekend, bat met ball rather more successfully — no further casualties reported. 📊 The Weekend in Brief Two ducks from three matches, both confined to a single unfortunate 2nd XI innings against Shipbourne. Tim Bentall finishes the day with a tidy brace to his name — the Duck Club extends its reluctant thanks. Richard Piper, also batting at 7, fell b Alistair Mills for his 1st duck of the season and 13th of his career, moving to 60th in the all-time table. A chip off the old block, too: his relative Brian Piper carries 20 career ducks of his own. And by happy coincidence, Richard shares his parallel with Piers above — Paul Brattle's 1979 duck against Maidstone Police keeps good company this week. 🏏 Midweek XI — The Mote CC (away) Tuesday's trip brought Mike Elworthy back for nought at number 2, bowled by Julian Sterry. His 2nd of the season and 28th of his career lifts him to 29th all-time, and draws him level on that exact tally with Richard Ashby, a Tourist Duck top-ten fixture of the 1962-1995 era who reached his 28 in 224 innings. Mike remains a Tourist Duck top-ten regular in his own right — clearly a man who travels well, in every sense but the scoring one. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from five matches — a modest harvest, but a tidy one, headlined by Piers McDonald's golden effort and the curious symmetry of both Linton sides falling to Bearsted CC on the same afternoon, both victims batting at number 7. Two fixtures on the road this bank holiday weekend, but the ducks showed a distinct preference for just the one venue. Wye CC hosted the full quacking ceremony while the other fixture passed off entirely fowl-free. Efficient targeting, or just Wye CC's bowlers having a day. Three names go on the sheet. 🏏 1st XI at Wye CC (away) Piers McDonald opened the innings and the ceremony together, caught Matthew Edge bowled Neil Redding without adding to the total. It's his 4th duck of the summer and 39th of a long and storied career — 14th in the all-time list from 331 innings, a rarefied neighbourhood. With "Duck of the Year" honours already claimed in 1997, and this season's tally keeping pace nicely with the top of the current charts, Piers remains the man to catch in 1998. He also joins the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, a frequent flyer in every sense. On this very day back in 1944, E- Humphreys was doing much the same against the National Fire Service Overseas Contingent — some traditions simply endure. Next in was David Castle at first drop, bowled by a- Spight for his 3rd duck of the season and 31st of his career, good enough for 23rd on the all-time list from a substantial 490 innings. A paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10, David clearly travels well, if not always fruitfully. He shares his own echo of 1944 with Piers — the same National Fire Service fixture claiming another victim on this date, 54 years to the day. And propping up the order at number 11, Ian Brattle departed bowled Colin Blaskett for his 3rd of the season. His career figure edges to 43, remarkably from just 125 innings — a strike rate that has him sat 11th in the all-time standings, and firmly established in the "50 Before a 50" club (51 ducks, high score a lofty 33), as well as a veteran of the Debutante Duck ranks and a stretch of 7 Ducks in a Row that will not be forgotten in a hurry. He shares an Only Two Quacks partnership with Michael Beeney on the honours board, and keeps it in the family too — sibling Paul Brattle carries 26 career ducks of his own, so the gene for the golden nought runs deep. Like his teammates, Ian's fall lines up with the 1944 vintage of E- Humphreys, making it a full team echo of that wartime fixture. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, all concentrated in a single innings at Wye CC — the away trip proving considerably more hazardous to the scorebook than the fixture it faced off against, which returned home without a single addition to the club's tally.
