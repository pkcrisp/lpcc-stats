🗓️ 25 Sep – 01 Oct 1965 · 1965 — 2 matches

Sun 26 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWLDD

Sunday the twenty-sixth of September, and Linton Park CC came calling. I won the toss and had no hesitation in taking first knock — the sort of decision that looks either shrewd or reckless depending entirely on what follows, and I'm pleased to report it fell, on balance, into the former category.

We laboured to 136 for 8 off our thirty-nine overs, which on paper looks a modest total and in practice felt like one too, though not without its merits. John Thirkell batted with real class for an unbeaten 63, the innings' one point of genuine certainty, and found a willing ally in Stan Wratten, whose 22 gave the total some much-needed backbone. Then, as so often happens in this maddening game, the wheels came off with startling efficiency — 126 for 5 became 136 for 8 almost before anyone had settled back into their deckchair. Poor Roy Clarke will not thank me for mentioning his duck, but the scorebook is unforgiving and so, occasionally, must I be.

Elsewhere in the ledger, John Thirkell continues to lead the First XI's run charts for the season with 863 to his name, while Robert Chance heads the Second XI's with 31 — every column matters when the tea urn's on the line.

In the end neither side could force the issue, and honours were shared. A draw, then — not the stuff of legend, but a fair reflection of an afternoon where good batting met better bowling, and Sunday tea was, as ever, the true victor.

Sat 25 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv RX Rambling Rose XI · HomeformDDWLD

Rambling Rose XI came to Linton Park on a fine Saturday and left, I think, rather relieved to escape with a draw.

Winning the toss, we sent them in, and they crawled to 168 for 9 off 34.5 overs — a total that flattered to deceive given the wobble at 5 for 1 early on. Dave Sayer, to his credit, played the innings of the day with a well-made 89, anchoring a stubborn 63-run stand for the fifth wicket that did most of the damage to our afternoon. Mike Foster and Eddie Page were both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and it was Dick Langridge who did the real hurt with a beautiful 4 for 28, backed up nicely by Alan Tucker's 4 for 51 and a tidy 1 for 35 from Terry Whittaker. I chipped in with the ball myself, though the wickets column that mattered belonged to others.

In reply, we never quite found our rhythm, subsiding to 78 for 7 from 19.3 overs before both sides accepted the inevitable handshake. Basil Sears top-scored with 21 — no mean feat given he was also marking his 300th appearance in Linton colours, a landmark that rather puts my own contribution in the shade. Dick Langridge's four wickets, meanwhile, nudged him past Jack Fryer and up to 17th on the club's all-time list.

A draw, then — honours shared, pride mostly intact, and tea, as ever, the true winner of the day.

🗓️ 18 Sep – 24 Sep 1965 · 1965 — 2 matches

Sun 19 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLDDWL

Sunday the 19th of September found Anchorians CC as our guests, and having won the toss I had no hesitation in taking first knock — the right call, I felt, and I shall go to my grave believing it. We stumbled early, three down for just the one run, but the innings found its feet soon enough: William Buck top-scored with a fine 33, I was glad to chip in with 26, and Frank Brattle matched me run for run with his own 26, as we hauled ourselves to 139 for 9 off 44.5 overs. Spare a thought for poor Stan Wratten, who suffered the walk of shame for a duck.

Defending that total, we set about Anchorians with real purpose, and history rather repeated itself — they too slipped to three down for one, then slid further to four down for four, with Veitch, Cazalet and Ashby all sent packing without troubling the scorers. Only J- Harris (27) and Jim Fish (23) offered any real resistance as they laboured to 90 for 9 from their 45 overs. Alan Tucker was magnificent, four wickets for 18 runs with seven miserly maidens to his name, well supported by Terry Whittaker's 3 for 17 and a tidy 1 for 19 from my own bowling. Poor Roy Clarke had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, leaking 12 byes, though we forgave him readily enough over tea.

With Don Clayton sitting second in our season's run charts and Whittaker topping the wicket pile, the form within the club is plain to see — yet on this occasion neither side could quite force the issue. A draw it was, honours shared, and, if I am honest, a fair reflection of a good, hard-fought contest.

Sat 18 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · AwayformWLDDW

Saturday the 18th of September took us out to Bowaters, and for reasons I still can't quite defend, I won the toss and stuck us in. Not my finest hour, as it turned out.

Ibbotson both departing for ducks, adding their names to a growing gallery of batsmen undone that afternoon.

Six wickets the margin in the end — not disgraceful, but no cause for celebration either. Frank Brattle continues to churn out the runs, fifth now in the season's charts, and I'll allow myself a quiet pride in leading the wicket-takers with 55 for the year — small comfort on a long drive home. We'll be sharper next week; we usually are, eventually.

🗓️ 11 Sep – 17 Sep 1965 · 1965 — 1 match

Sun 12 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformWWLDD

Sunday last took us down to Staplehurst, and having called correctly for once, I had no hesitation in batting first. It wasn't pretty. Robert Chance, Basil Sears and Dick Langridge all chipped in with 21 apiece, but between them and 106 all out there was a spell where the innings simply fell over — 48 for 3 became 55 for 6 before anyone had really settled back into their deckchair, and William Buck and Colin Eldred made the long walk back without troubling the scorers.

106 looked distinctly ordinary set against a Staplehurst side with wickets in hand, but cricket has a way of humbling the confident and rewarding the persistent. Alan Tucker bowled with real discipline, eight maidens and 2 for 18 that simply refused to give an inch, and Dick Langridge followed his runs with the ball to take 3 for 12. I had one of those afternoons where everything spun exactly where it was meant to, finishing with 5 for 43 as we ran through them from 26 for 1 to 35 for 4 in a blur, with R. Newick and L. Mitchell both back in the pavilion without scoring. Staplehurst were all out for 79.

A 27-run win, then, snatched from what looked a middling total — and a fine one for Dick Langridge, whose three wickets carried him past Robert Wood into 18th on the club's all-time list. Character over class today, and I'll happily take it.

🗓️ 04 Sep – 10 Sep 1965 · 1965 — 2 matches

Sun 05 Sep 1965 🧢 1st XIv HC Harrietsham CC · HomeformWWWLD

Sunday the 5th of September, and Harrietsham arrived to find the Linton Park coin obliging me for once — heads it was, and, feeling unusually decisive for a man who normally can't choose a tea order, I sent us in.

What followed was fifteen overs of watchful, faintly nervous cricket, the sort where every run feels earned through negotiation rather than conquest. We crept to 31 for the loss of one, the scoreboard ticking over with the enthusiasm of a kettle that can't quite decide whether to boil. Wickets stayed largely intact, ambition rather less so, and by the close both sides seemed to have silently agreed that discretion was the better part of valour.

In the end neither team could summon the wit, the weather, or perhaps the will to force a result, and honours were shared — a draw that satisfied no one and offended no one, which in village cricket terms is practically a triumph of diplomacy. There was no real cut and thrust, more a gentle handshake of an afternoon, the kind where the tea interval threatens to be the highlight and very nearly is.

Still, we came, we batted, we didn't lose — and against Harrietsham, on a day like this, I'll happily take a draw and a decent slice of cake as a job honourably done.
