🦆 Duck Report — 25 to 26 September 1965

2 ducks
Week 23 · 25 Sep 1965 – 26 Sep 1965
A quiet weekend for the Duck Club by our lofty standards, but two names still queued dutifully at the crease for the club's least sought-after honour. Two matches, two ducks, and both fell against the very same opposition on the Sunday — Linton Park CC clearly fancied a spot of exclusivity. 1st XI - Roy Clarke fell caught and bowled by Alan Tucker, batting at number 7, for his 4th duck of the season and 40th of a long and storied career — now 230 innings deep and sitting 3rd in the all-time chart of 802. Roy is a paid-up Duck of the Year champion from 1959, and his listing alongside John Ames on the Quacking Together honours board remains one of the club's more distinguished double acts, even if it wasn't this weekend's business. A chip off the old block, too — sibling H Clarke carries 3 career ducks of his own. And in a pleasing bit of calendar symmetry, on this very day in 1964 Len Conway (1954–1965, 12 career ducks, 27th of 802, and a Debutante Duck himself) also strolled back for nought, against Anchorians CC. 2nd XI - Fred Greenfield edged behind, caught John Abnett off the bowling of Terry Whittaker, also at number 7 — his 5th duck of the season and 8th of his career, moving him to 41st of 802 all time from 52 innings. Fred is already inducted onto the Retirement Duck honours board from a farewell duck of years gone by, a standing distinction rather than news for today. His 8th career duck draws him level with none other than Colin Eldred, this club's reigning 1965 Duck of the Year — kindred spirits in the art of not scoring. Week in Summary A modest haul: 2 ducks from 2 matches, both on the Sunday, both against Linton Park CC, and both departing from the unglamorous surrounds of the number 7 slot. No pairs, no families of ducks, no king pairs to report this time round — just two solid, professional additions to the ledger. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Colin Eldred — 7 ducks, 40 innings, 17.5% 2. Dick Langridge — 5 ducks, 19 innings, 26.32% 3. Richard Ashby — 5 ducks, 21 innings, 23.81% 4. Fred Greenfield — 5 ducks, 30 innings, 16.67% 5. Roy Clarke — 4 ducks, 14 innings, 28.57% Both of this week's ducklings feature in the season's top five, proof if any were needed of true dedication to the craft. The race for the 1965 crown may already have a name on it, but the exclusive zero club never truly closes its books. Next fixture, same invitation stands — bat well, or don't, and either way we'll be watching. 🦆🍩

3 ducks
Week 22 · 18 Sep 1965 – 19 Sep 1965
🏏 1st XI v Bowaters CC (away, 18 September) Colin Eldred, at number 7, edged Donald Simler through to Peter Sivyer for his 7th duck of the season and 8th of an increasingly distinguished career — a career, it should be noted, that has just been formally retired to the Retirement Duck wing of the honours board, and which already carries the reigning Duck of the Year 1965 sash. Colin sits pretty at 39th of 802 all time, level on 8 with a small parliament of fellow duckers including Arthur Cradduck (1950–1959) and the venerable George Champion, who was doing much the same thing for this club back in 1886. Neatly, on this very date in 1960 Cliff Anscombe was also walking back for nought, against Tilling Steven no less — some things simply repeat themselves down the decades. Down at number 10, D. Ibbotson made an entrance and an exit in the very same stroke, caught John Evans bowled Alf Jewiss without troubling the scorers. A debut duck, and with it instant, simultaneous membership of three separate honours categories in one ball faced — Debutante Duck, One and Done, and Retirement Duck all claimed before he'd had time to take guard properly. Efficiency of a very particular kind. He joins a long and select line of one-match wonders on exactly 1 career duck, rank 222 of 802, in the company of names like Musgrave and Sessions who came, ducked, and were never seen again. 🏏 1st XI v Anchorians CC (home, 19 September) Opening the batting, Stan Wratten fell caught P. Lawrence bowled Ingham for his 4th duck of the campaign, moving him to 25 for his career — a lofty 9th of 802 all time, and already a paid-up member of Quacking Together (with John Ames). He now stands shoulder to shoulder on 25 with Frank Brattle (1937–1965) and Gordon Fenner (1941–1965), two names who between them have seen rather a lot of Linton Park cricket. And on this same date back in 1959, G. Martin was likewise dismissed without scoring, against Bearsted CC — the calendar has a long memory for these things. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches — a tidy, unspectacular haul, no pairs and no family affairs this time round, but a debut retirement and a top-ten career tally both arriving in the same weekend is no small thing. The 1st XI carried the entire load; whatever Bowaters and Anchorians were doing right, it evidently worked. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Colin Eldred — 39 innings, 7 ducks, 17.95% 2. Dick Langridge — 18 innings, 5 ducks, 27.78% 3. Richard Ashby — 20 innings, 5 ducks, 25.00% 4. Stan Wratten — 16 innings, 4 ducks, 25.00% 5. William Buck — 17 innings, 4 ducks, 23.53% 🍩 The season draws to a close but the Duck Club never truly rests. Whoever takes guard next will want to mind their first ball especially closely — the honours board has plenty of room left and, as ever, it's watching.

🦆 Duck News — 12 September 1965

2 ducks
Week 21 · 12 Sep 1965
A lean week for the harvest, but the exclusive zero club still opened its gates twice, both down at Staplehurst CC. 1st XI — Away at Staplehurst CC William Buck strode out at first wicket down and departed without troubling the scorers, bowled by R Newick. His fourth duck of the 1965 season and 26th of a mighty career spanning 292 innings, a tally that holds him a lofty 6th in the all-time list of 801. Buck remains a paid-up member of the Quacking Together honours board, his name forever twinned with John Thirkell's in shared ignominy from a past occasion. And on this very date back in 1959, S Lusher was doing much the same against Bowaters CC — some traditions simply refuse to die. At number six, Colin Eldred fared no better, caught D Heathfield bowled R Newick, again without scoring. His sixth duck of the season and seventh career nought from just 48 innings lifts him — or rather lowers him — to 45th of 801 all time. That sixth duck keeps Eldred handsomely clear at the top of the 1965 Duck of the Year race, a crown nobody looks likely to prise off him just yet. He's also long carried membership of the Retirement Duck board, having once bowed out on a nought in a past farewell — no suggestion he's going anywhere this week. And fittingly, on this same date in 1959, S Lusher was also making a duck, against Bowaters CC — the calendar, it seems, has a sense of humour. Week in Summary Just the one fixture and two ducks to show for it, both falling to the very same bowler at Staplehurst — R Newick can consider his afternoon's work thoroughly done. Quality over quantity this week, as the connoisseurs of nought would put it. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 - Colin Eldred — 6 ducks / 37 innings (16.22%) - Dick Langridge — 5 ducks / 17 innings (29.41%) - Richard Ashby — 5 ducks / 20 innings (25.00%) - William Buck — 4 ducks / 16 innings (25.00%) - Gordon Fenner — 4 ducks / 17 innings (23.53%) With Eldred setting the pace and Buck snapping at his heels, the 1965 Duck of the Year race is far from settled. Roll on the next fixture — the club's finest are surely only one more edge away from adding to the tally. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 4-5 September 1965

0 ducks
Week 20 · 4 Sep 1965 – 5 Sep 1965
Two fixtures. Two full afternoons of licence to hunt. And not a single quack to show for it. For once, both Linton sides took guard, saw the new ball off, and simply refused to cooperate with the club's grand tradition of scoring nothing. Every batsman across both matches found his way off the mark — a genuinely rare weekend where the art of not scoring went entirely unpractised. The exclusive zero club issued no new membership cards this week. Whether this counts as a triumph of technique or simply a conspiracy of good fortune, the committee cannot say. What we can say is that somewhere out there, the ducks are laughing at us — evaded, uncaptured, and no doubt regrouping for next weekend. Let it be noted for the record: a blank sheet is still a sheet. The Duck Club's own honours boards gained nothing this week, but nor did they lose anything — the season's runners and chasers hold firm exactly where they stood. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 Dick Langridge — 5 ducks from 16 innings (31.25%) — the runaway pace-setter, giving away nearly a third of his innings for nought 🥈 Richard Ashby — 5 from 19 (26.32%) — hot on his heels and closing fast 🥉 Colin Eldred — 5 from 36 (13.89%) — getting there the long way round, patience rewarded with quantity if not quality 4️⃣ Gordon Fenner — 4 from 17 (23.53%) 5️⃣ Fred Greenfield — 4 from 27 (14.81%) Dick Langridge and Richard Ashby remain locked together at the summit on 5 apiece, with Colin Eldred lurking a fair way back on the same tally but taking more than twice the innings to get there — consistency of a very particular kind. 🦆 Duck News — 28th to 30th August 1965

9 ducks
Week 19 · 28 Aug 1965 – 30 Aug 1965
Three fixtures, nine ducks, and one opposition bowler having the round of his life. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. 🏏 Saturday 28th — vs A. Trembeths (home) A torrid morning for the Linton order, with K- Lingard doing almost all of the damage — four wickets, four ducks, not a run given away. Some men chase Ashes glory; Lingard settled for a clean sweep of the Duck Club. Stan Wratten went first, bowled by Lingard for his 3rd duck of the season and 24th of a long career — 9th on the all-time list, and one half of the "Quacking Together" pairing with John Ames. He didn't wait long for company. John Ames followed, run out for his own 3rd of the season and 34th overall — 5th all-time, a fixture of the Ducks in a Row and Homebody Duck honours boards, and reunited with Wratten on the Quacking Together roll right on cue. Colin Eldred was next, caught for his 5th duck of the season — level at the very top of this year's charts and firmly in the hunt for Duck of the Year 1965. John Abnett (Lingard again) added his 1st of the season and 18th career, 14th all-time and another Ducks in a Row man. Then Lingard completed the set, removing Terry Whittaker (3rd of the season, 16th career, 17th all-time) and Brian Relf (2nd of the season, 17th career, 15th all-time) — the very two names inscribed together on the Quacking Together board, doing exactly what it says on the tin. Fittingly, this whole top-order collapse echoes this very date in 1893, when W W Tarrance suffered much the same fate against The Mote CC. Some anniversaries just won't let go. 🏏 Monday 30th — vs Bearsted CC (away) Frank Brattle edged his way to his 3rd duck of the season, 25th career and 6th all-time — and, for the family album, a chip off the old block given kinsman Percy Brattle's own 5 career ducks. Terry Whittaker was back at it too, bowled by Bryan Elswood for his 4th of the season and 17th career, nudging up to 17th all-time. Two matches, two ducks — a proper pair for Terry this week. No king pair on offer (he troubled the scorers for at least a ball each time), but a grim double all the same. Sean Carter completed the trio, caught and bowled by Elswood for his 1st of the season and 4th career. All three, by neat coincidence, share their fate with John Abnett, who made a duck on this very date back in 1958 against W. Buck's XI. 📊 The Week in Numbers Nine ducks from three fixtures — Saturday's Trembeths collapse doing the heavy lifting, with K- Lingard the toast (or terror) of the Duck Club for his four-wicket haul. Terry Whittaker's weekend pair keeps him in the headlines, and Colin Eldred's charge at Duck of the Year 1965 remains very much alive. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Dick Langridge — 5 ducks, 16 innings (31.25%) 2. Richard Ashby — 5 ducks, 19 innings (26.32%) 3. Colin Eldred — 5 ducks, 35 innings (14.29%) 4. Gordon Fenner — 4 ducks, 17 innings (23.53%) 5. Fred Greenfield — 4 ducks, 27 innings (14.81%) A genuine three-way tie at the summit — Duck of the Year 1965 is anyone's to lose. Next fixture beckons, and the Club never sleeps. Who fancies joining the queue? 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 14–15 August 1965

4 ducks
Week 17 · 14 Aug 1965 – 15 Aug 1965
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. The 1st XI did all the damage this week — everyone else kept remarkably clean hands. Saturday 14 August — Bowaters CC (away) David Gale, sent in at 10, was bowled by Donald Simler without troubling the scorers. His season's 2nd duck and his career's 8th from 45 innings, planting him at 39th of 800 in the all-time table. Splendidly, on this very day in 1893 A Kent also departed for nought — against Royal Engineers CC no less — en route to lifting the Duck of the Year crown that season. History, as ever, repeats. Sunday 15 August — The Mote CC (home) Phil Briant clearly fancied his chances at Linton Park, claiming a brace of noughts in the same innings. Terry Whittaker edged one back to the bowler for his 2nd duck of the season and 15th of his career from 140 innings — a lofty 18th of 800 all-time. A long-serving member of the Quacking Together honours board alongside Brian Relf, and a paid-up veteran of the Retirement Duck club from a farewell long past. On this day in 1964, none other than John Thirkell — the club's all-time leader on 55 — also fell against Bowaters CC. Good company to keep. Colin Eldred followed him back, caught behind off the same Briant over. His 4th duck of the season and 5th of a 42-innings career puts him 59th of 800, and very much in the box seat for the Duck of the Year 1965 title as things stand. Also a Retirement Duck alumnus of old. Like Terry, he shares that same 1964 echo of John Thirkell falling to Bowaters on this exact date. Gordon Fenner rounded off the misery, caught by Graham Waters off J- Webb. His 4th of the season and a chunky 25th career duck from 186 innings lands him a lofty 6th of 800 — breathing distance from the very top of the table. A proper family affair too: Gordon joins siblings Joe Fenner (5 career ducks) and Maurice Fenner (1) on the household ledger. And yes, he too echoes John Thirkell's 1964 fall against Bowaters, on this same date. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, the lot of them 1st XI, and Mote CC's Phil Briant doing the heavy lifting with a two-wicket haul of noughts. A quietly efficient weekend's work for the Duck Club scouts. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Dick Langridge — 5 ducks from 14 innings (35.71%) 2. Richard Ashby — 4 ducks from 16 innings (25.00%) 3. Gordon Fenner — 4 ducks from 17 innings (23.53%) 4. Fred Greenfield — 4 ducks from 24 innings (16.67%) 5. Colin Eldred — 4 ducks from 31 innings (12.90%) Dick Langridge holds a narrow lead but with Fenner and Eldred both freshly restocked this weekend, the Duck of the Year race is very much alive. Next fixture, more willow, presumably more nought. We'll be watching. 🦆🍩

Week in Summary Three matches, three ducks, no repeat offenders, and — mercifully for the umpires — no king pairs or family collections this week. Reeds CC did the damage twice over on Saturday, Horsleydown CC picked off one more on Tuesday, and the rest of the fixture list stayed frustratingly clean. The season isn't done yet — get out there and give the Duck Club something fresh to celebrate next week. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 DUCK NEWS — Weekend of 17–18 July 1965 🦆

0 ducks
Week 13 · 17 Jul 1965 – 18 Jul 1965
A rare and troubling dispatch from the Duck Club this week: two matches played, two matches in which the entire batting order stubbornly declined to contribute to the cause. Not a single nought to report across the whole weekend. Every batsman who walked to the crease this week committed the cardinal sin of getting off the mark. Bats were seen. Runs were scored. His 55th career duck from a staggering 481 innings, and he remains utterly unrivalled at #1 of 794 all time — nobody in Linton's long history has practised the art of not scoring quite so devotedly. A three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955), permanent resident of the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and a veteran of Quacking Together honours with Bob Arnold and William Buck. A career, not a coincidence. - Terry Donovan, at seven, c Vinit Moore b J Hume. His 9th career duck from 44 innings, 35th on the all-time table, drawing level with B Westover (1940–1945, 28 innings). Donovan already owns Duck of the Year 1964, and sits agonisingly one position shy of Full House membership, having now ducked in 10 of the 11 batting slots. The completionist in him must be twitching. 🏏 Sunday 25th — home to Maidstone AKCC - John Ames, opening the batting no less, b R Kemp. His 32nd career duck from 225 innings vaults him to 5th of 794 all time, and extends his Ducks in a Row honour to four on the bounce — a streak worth watching. A Homebody Duck top-ten regular, and holder of Quacking Together and Only Two Quacks honours alongside Roy Clarke, Stan Wratten and Alan Wilkes. - Colin Breed then went to work with the ball, claiming a brace. Charles Crellin (b Breed) recorded his 2nd career duck from 31 innings, joining A Mitchell, a fellow 1964–65 Debutante Duck alumnus, on the same tally. Norman Mattingley (also b Breed) took his to 5 ducks from a mere 7 senior innings — a strike rate for failure that borders on the vocational — level with Edwardian great Allan Peach (1903–1907). - Last man Ron Hickman, b P Barnes, moved to 7 career ducks from 14 innings, joining Victorian-era A Startup (1868–1882) on the same mark. Hickman already carries a Quacking Together honour with Alan Cradduck, and his card is flagged for the 1965 Runaway Duck should the runs continue to elude him this season — one to watch as the summer unfolds. 📊 Week in summary: 6 ducks from 2 matches, with Colin Breed personally responsible for a third of the tally.
