🦆 Duck News — Sunday 27th September 1981

2 ducks
Week 22 · 27 Sep 1981
As the 1981 season draws toward its close, Linton's 2nds popped out to face Linton Park CC and, as ever, the zero club was ready to welcome new business. Just the one match on the books this week — but it produced two fresh entries for the ledger. 🏏 2nd XI v Linton Park CC Opener Andy Thresher set the tone early, bowled by Scott Smith without troubling the scorers — his 2nd duck of the campaign and 2nd of an already eventful career, arriving in just his 3rd innings for the club (171st of 1011 all-time). Andy's very first knock for Linton was itself a duck, so he remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, this latest effort simply adding to the file. On this very calendar day back in 1964, Robert Murray (1958–1965, 17 ducks, 35th all-time) was also dismissed for nought against Linton Park CC — the fixture clearly has previous. Lower down, Fred Cheesman fell at number 5, caught Andy Woollett off the same relentless Scott Smith, for his 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from 13 innings (139th all-time). Fred already holds the rare Full House honour — a duck recorded at every position from 1 to 11 — and carries an Only Two Quacks pairing with Adam Pooley, so this nought slots neatly into an already distinguished cabinet, one befitting a top-10 Tourist Duck man besides. Fittingly, Fred too was dismissed on the very day in 1964 that claimed Robert Murray — quacking through history together, seventeen years apart. 🎯 Unofficial bowler of the week: Scott Smith, who did for both of them. Two ducks, one bowler, one afternoon — Linton's top order simply couldn't get him away. 📊 Week in Summary A single fixture, but a perfect two-for-two strike rate for the Duck Club. Fred Cheesman's effort also earns him a spot in the current season's top five — proof that even a solid top-order man isn't safe from the zero. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Jon Harris — 5 ducks, 27 inn (18.52%) 🥈 Paul Bowles — 5 ducks, 48 inn (10.42%) 🥉 Andy Woollett — 4 ducks, 22 inn (18.18%) 4. Fred Cheesman — 3 ducks, 9 inn (33.33%) 5. Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks, 16 inn (18.75%) With the season winding down, Jon Harris and Paul Bowles remain locked together on 5 apiece — plenty still to play for before the final ball of 1981. See you at the next fixture, where the ducks are, as always, entirely optional but never in short supply. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — Wednesday 23 September 1981

2 ducks
Week 21 · 23 Sep 1981
Just the one fixture to report from this midweek round, but where there's a Hawkenbury there's a way — the exclusive zero club took delivery of two fresh members before the tea urn had even cooled. Midweek XI v Hawkenbury CC (home) First to the crease and first to the departure lounge was C- Wilkins, coming in at three and holing out c & b S Gower without so much as troubling the scoreboard operator. This was Wilkins' maiden senior innings, so by the club's own solemn tradition it counts as a full and proper induction — straight onto the Debutante Duck board on debut itself, a rare honour reserved for those who arrive and depart in the same breath. The career record currently reads a tidy 1 duck from 1 innings, good enough for 289th of 1008 in the all-time list, and it's an early entry too on the season's One and Done reckoning — we live in hope he adds a second innings to go with the second duck before anyone gets carried away. Down at number 8, Steve Petty kept the theme going, caught B Grainger off the bowling of R Kemp for his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from 5 innings — sitting 233rd of 1008 all-time. With 1981 so far a stubbornly run-free campaign for Steve, he's quietly qualifying for the season's Runaway Duck honour, the special commendation for going an entire year without once bothering the scorebook. Curiously, both dismissals share the very same footnote in the record books: on this day in 1972, LPCC great Tim Thirkell — he of 49 career ducks, 4th of all time, and a name that adorns half the honours boards in the pavilion — also strolled back for nought, against West Farleigh CC. Two Linton batsmen, nine years apart, both finding history repeating itself on the calendar if not on the scoreboard. Week in Summary One match, two ducks, and a spooky shared anniversary — the kind of coincidence the Duck Club exists to notice and everyone else politely ignores. Consistency of a very particular kind, on display twice in the same innings. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Jon Harris — 5 ducks in 26 innings (19.23%) - Paul Bowles — 5 ducks in 47 innings (10.64%) - Andy Woollett — 4 ducks in 21 innings (19.05%) - Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks in 15 innings (20.0%) - Martin Thompson — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.0%) Jon Harris keeps his nose in front, but with Paul Bowles matching him duck for duck and Andy Woollett breathing down both their necks on percentage, this season's crown is very much up for grabs. Next fixture awaits, and with it the inevitable next round of nominations for the art of not scoring. See you at the crease — or, more likely, on the way back from it. 🍩🏏

🦆 Duck News — 12–15 September 1981

1 duck
Week 20 · 12 Sep 1981 – 15 Sep 1981
Three fixtures across the closing weeks of the season, and the ducks mostly stayed in hiding — but one significant visitor turned up to remind everyone why September belongs to the connoisseurs of nought. 🏏 1st XI v Southborough CC (home) Jon Harris strode out at number 2 and departed for a duck, bowled by Brian Farrier after seeing off seven balls — no golden duck here, just old-fashioned, unhurried failure. This is Jon's 5th duck of the 1981 season and his 53rd for the club overall, a tally that plants him at a barely believable 2nd of 1004 in the all-time standings. Only one man in Linton Park's long history has made a more determined habit of it. The honours boards read like a testimonial dinner menu — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club since his very first innings, a three-time Duck of the Year (1976, 1980 and, on today's evidence, very much still in the 1981 hunt), and the proud holder of a Full House, a duck banked in every position from 1 to 11 over the years. Throw in top-ten billing on both the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck rolls, plus frequent-flyer status on the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck lists, and you have a man who long ago made nought a second career. His son Richard is no slouch either, quietly building his own tally of 36 — clearly a family gift for the art of not scoring. A neat historical footnote: on this very day back in 1959, S Lusher also trudged back for a duck, against Bowaters CC. Twelve September has form. 📋 Elsewhere The other two fixtures this weekend were disappointingly duck-free — a rare weekend where the bowlers had to earn their wickets the hard way. 📊 Week in Summary One duck from three matches is a quiet return by Linton Park standards, but it was a heavyweight one. Jon Harris's fifth of the season keeps him locked at the top of the Duck of the Year table, level on numbers with Paul Bowles but well clear on strike rate. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 25 innings, 5 ducks (20.0%) 2. Paul Bowles — 46 innings, 5 ducks (10.87%) 3. Andy Woollett — 21 innings, 4 ducks (19.05%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 15 innings, 3 ducks (20.0%) 5. Trevor Back — 19 innings, 3 ducks (15.79%) 🍩 As the season winds towards its close, the race for Duck of the Year is still very much alive. Get yourselves to the middle next weekend, gentlemen — there are noughts still to be had.

🦆 Duck News — 29 August to 4 September 1981 🦆

5 ducks
Week 18 · 29 Aug 1981 – 4 Sep 1981
Four fixtures, five noughts, and one properly historic sibling ceremony down in Cornwall. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. Saturday 29th August — 1st XI away at British Rail Ashford Andy Woollett got the week's collapse under way at the top of the order, bowled N Gurr without scoring. His 2nd duck of the season, 3rd of a career now ticking along nicely at 42 innings. On this very date back in 1954, Stan Wratten (1943–1980, 35 career ducks, rank 12 of 1000) was doing exactly the same thing — proof the calendar has form. Saturday 31st August — 1st XI away at Bearsted CC A day for Andrew Horne to feast on Linton openers. Brian Piper fell first, caught B Lewis bowled Horne for nought — his 2nd of the season and 10th of a 177-innings career, rank 54 of 1000. He's not even top duck in his own house: brother David Piper carries 6 of his own. Brian's fall matches Allan Cooper, who made his own duck on this exact date in 1980 (25 career, rank 21). Moments later Andy Woollett was back out, caught Anthony Hunt bowled Horne — a second failure inside three days. That completes a grim little pair for the week: season tally 2 then 3, career 3 then 4. Andy joins Allan Cooper on the on-this-day roll too, keeping poor company twice over. Sunday 4th September — Tour XI at Cornwall Police CC The touring party delivered the week's genuine rarity: a Two of a Quack, brothers Nigel Thirkell and Tim Thirkell both bowled by D Jenkin in the same innings. A shared-bloodline duck is the rarest ceremony this club offers, and the Thirkells delivered it in style. Nigel's is his 1st of the season but 29th of a serious career — 390 innings, rank 19 of 1000, a fixture of both the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, plus Only Two Quacks honours alongside Jim Hall. Tim goes one further: season duck number 3, and a mighty 49th career duck from 393 innings, rank 4 of 1000. Tim's honours board reads like a testimonial — Duck of the Year 1972, Ducks on the Road top 10, Homebody Duck top 10, Lone Duck top 10, and the rare Full House, a duck at every position 1 to 11. Both brothers, fittingly, echo P- Martin, who ducked for the club on this same calendar day in 1949 against East Peckham & Nettlestead. Worth noting the family isn't finished there — father John Thirkell sits on a barely believable 90 career ducks, the club's all-time number one, with siblings Simon (2) and Stuart (1) also paid-up members. A dynasty built entirely on the art of not scoring. Week in summary Five ducks from four matches. Bearsted's attack claimed a Piper and a Woollett inside the same innings, while Cornwall hosted the season's first sibling double. Consistency of a very particular kind, all round. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Paul Bowles — 5 ducks, 39 innings (12.82%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25.00%) 3. Andy Woollett — 3 ducks, 17 innings (17.65%) 4. Martin Thompson — 3 ducks, 17 innings (17.65%) 5. Jon Harris — 3 ducks, 21 innings (14.29%) Tim Thirkell's 25% strike rate has him breathing down Paul Bowles's neck at the top — one more nought and the crown could change hands. Next fixture, same purpose: bat first, worry later. See you all out there 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — Week of 22–26 August 1981

3 ducks
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1981 – 26 Aug 1981
Three matches, three ducks — a quiet week by Linton standards, but each one earns its place in the ledger. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (h) Brian Piper opened the week's account, caught R Neale bowled B Heather at number five. His 1st duck of the season and 9th of a steadily building career, good for 59th on the all-time list from 175 innings. On this very day back in 1952, D Simmons was doing much the same against Charles Arkcoll — four ducks across a 1949–64 career — and Brian continues to close the gap from the modern end. Further down the order, Jon Harris departed for nought at number eight, caught R Bushell bowled J Lycett — his 3rd of the season and a weighty 51st of his career, trailing only one player in the entire 999-strong all-time list. Jon is a three-time Duck of the Year — 1976, 1980, and this very 1981 season — and a fully paid-up member of Full House (a duck at every position, 1 through 11), sitting proudly in the Lone Duck, Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens, with shared quacks logged over the years alongside Allan Cooper, Jim Hall and Trevor Back. A CV of failure that most of the club can only aspire to. Fittingly, he shares his day in history with the same D Simmons of 1952. 🌱 Wednesday 26th — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC (a) Opening the batting, David Piper was bowled by Kevin Masters without scoring — his 1st duck of the season and 6th of his career, 85th on the all-time list from a still-fresh 18 innings. A tricky few days for the Piper household, with brother Brian falling three days earlier in an entirely separate fixture — not quite the stuff of a formal joint honour, but noted with interest nonetheless. David's day also carries history: on this date in 1979, Tim Thirkell — 48 career ducks, 4th all-time — made a duck of his own, and remains this season's in-form quacker below. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from three matches — restrained, almost polite, by Duck Club standards. 3 ducks

Three fixtures on the go this week, but Whitstable CC did all the damage — the 1st XI's trip at home to the Kent coastal side produced the club's entire weekly haul, three noughts in a single innings, while the other two matches passed disappointingly duck-free. The art of not scoring, alive and well. 🏏 1st XI v Whitstable CC (home, 16 Aug) - David Iliffe (No.2) — c Andy Stroud b P Keen. His 2nd duck of the season and a tidy 12th for the career from 51 innings, installing him at 44th in the all-time ledger of ignominy. Webbed feet run in the family too — kinsman Mike Iliffe already carries 42 career ducks of his own. - Paul Bowles (No.6) — c Andy Stroud b George Moon. Season duck number 5 and career number 38 from 474 innings, 10th on the all-time roll. A double Duck of the Year honouree (1966 and 1968), a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, and a paid-up member of Only Two Quacks (with Adam Pooley). Right now he's also this season's most prolific collector — see below. - Simon Thirkell (No.7) — b Sid Janes. His first duck of the campaign and only his 2nd in a five-innings career, ranking 228th overall — but the surname does plenty of talking. Son of club legend John Thirkell, the all-time No.1 with a staggering 90 career ducks, and brother to Tim (48) and Nigel (28), with kinsman Stuart on 1. A dynasty of dubious distinction if ever there was one, and Simon already sits on both the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens. Spookily, all three fell on the very same calendar date — 16 August — that club great John Ames also made a duck, against Mote A, back in 1953. Some anniversaries you'd rather not share. 📊 Week in Summary 3 matches, 3 ducks, all crammed into one unlucky Whitstable innings — Andy Stroud alone had a hand in two of the three catches, quietly building a reputation as the Duck Club's most reliable accomplice. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. 🦆 Duck Report — 8-11 August 1981

1 duck
Week 15 · 8 Aug 1981 – 11 Aug 1981
Slim pickings this week, but the exclusive zero club never turns away a single new arrival, so let's give it the ceremony it deserves. Midweek XI — home to West Moors, Tuesday 11th Trevor Hales departed for nought at number 7, run out — the one dismissal in cricket where the batsman can protest his innocence with a straight face. It's his 1st duck of the season and his 1st career duck too, arriving in only his 3rd innings for the club, which places him a tidy 444th of 998 in the all-time reckoning. Trevor already holds a place on the Retirement Duck honours board from a past farewell innings, so the club has long known how his story ends — just not, evidently, when. Fittingly, on this very day in 1973, Nigel Thirkell also strolled back for a duck, against Headcorn CC. Nigel — who turned out for Linton between 1969 and 1981 — finished his career on 28 ducks, good for 19th of 998 all-time, with seats on both the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, plus an "Only Two Quacks" pairing with Jim Hall. Trevor keeps history ticking along nicely. Week in summary Three matches, one duck — the other two fixtures passed off entirely fowl-free, which either speaks well of the batting or poorly of the bowling, we shan't speculate. A quiet week by Duck Club standards, but the season race certainly isn't slowing down. 🦆 Duck Report — 1st to 5th August 1981

3 ducks
Week 14 · 1 Aug 1981 – 5 Aug 1981
A quiet week by Linton standards, but quality over quantity: three matches, three ducks, and one thumping entry into the record books that'll be the talk of the bar for weeks. 🏏 Saturday 1st August — 1st XI vs Old Anchorians CC (h) The top order took a hammering. In at three, Jim Hall was run out for nought — the fielder responsible lost to history, recorded only as "Unsure" — his second duck of the season and 47th of an extraordinary career, good enough for 6th on the all-time list of 997. A paid-up Debutante Duck, two-time Duck of the Year (1970 and 1977), and a fixture of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, Jim remains one of the club's most decorated non-scorers, and still chipping away at his Full House. On this very day in 1964, Basil Sears (1947–1975, 33 career ducks) was adding to the archives against Headcorn CC — some things never change. Moments later, Gareth Thomas holed out caught and bowled by J. Lycett without scoring — his first duck of the season and second of a young career, from just 8 innings, sliding him to 193rd all time. He shares the same on-this-day billing as Jim: Basil Sears, 1964. A member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, Gareth has a long way to go before that honour is relevant again. 🔥 Wednesday 5th August — Midweek XI vs Maidstone Police (h) Now, the headline. Opening the batting, Jon Harris was caught by Harry Dunk off Evans without scoring — a routine line in the scorebook, but a landmark one in the ledger. It's his 50th career duck, moving him to 2nd on the all-time chart of 997, second only to the legendary John Thirkell (1943–1977, 90 ducks) and clear of everyone else to have pulled on a Linton shirt. It's also his second duck of the season, and with Duck of the Year 1981 already on his honours board he's handsomely placed to defend the crown. A Full House campaigner and Homebody and Lone Duck top-ten regular, Jon now stands one nought closer to cricketing immortality — or at least a testimonial cake. On this day in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden (1881–1912) made his own duck against Rochester CC — 86 years on, the club is still finding new ways to fail with style. 📊 Week in Summary Three matches, three ducks, no pairs and no family affairs to report — a restrained week that let one man's milestone take centre stage. The 1st XI's top order fancied an early bath, while the Midweek XI's contribution came dressed as history. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season 1981 leaderboard - Paul Bowles — 4 ducks in 36 innings (11.1%) - Martin Thompson — 3 in 15 (20.0%) - David Carden — 2 in 6 (33.3%) - Tim Thirkell — 2 in 8 (25.0%) - Trevor Back — 2 in 10 (20.0%) 🍩 Next up The season presses on and the ducks show no sign of retiring, even if some of the club's finest occasionally do. Get down to the ground, watch the runs — or better still, watch for the nought. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 25th to 28th July 1981

3 ducks
Week 13 · 25 Jul 1981 – 28 Jul 1981
Four fixtures took the field this week, but the Duck Club's harvest was a concentrated affair — all three additions arriving in the very same match, away at Margate CC on the 28th. Quality over quantity, evidently. 🏏 Midweek XI at Margate Opening the batting and opening the day's account, Paul Bowles was held at slip by Graham Venn off the bowling of a- Carter — no golden duck here, at least he made the bowler work for it. It's his 4th duck of the season and 37th of an extraordinary career spanning 470 innings, good enough for 10th on the all-time list. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966 and 1968), a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, and one half of the "Only Two Quacks" double act with Adam Pooley — Bowles' honours board is getting crowded. On this very day back in 1957, D- Jordan suffered the identical fate against Folkestone CC. Some things never change. Batting at two, Andy Woollett was pouched by Desmond Gregory off M- Ellis for his first duck of the 1981 season and only his 2nd in a 39-innings career, nudging him to 168th in the all-time standings. Curiously, 1957 keeps cropping up — on this same date that year, D- Jordan was also trudging off against Folkestone CC. Down at number 8, debutant D- Leppard suffered the cruellest possible welcome to senior cricket, bowled by the same Desmond Gregory for nought on his very first appearance. Desmond clearly enjoyed his afternoon, involved in two of the three dismissals.
