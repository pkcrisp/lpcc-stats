Sun 24 Sep 2017 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · AwayformWWDWD

Up at Tenterden on a grey but pleasant Sunday, I lost the toss and we were put in — never my favourite start to proceedings, though the tea urn assured me it would be fine.

Batting first, we posted a healthy 191/8 from our 41 overs, built almost entirely around a wonderful unbeaten 86 from Tobey Castle, who these days seems constitutionally incapable of getting out. Darrell Houghton-Judge chipped in with a well-made 33, and the pair put on 58 for the fourth wicket to give the innings its spine. Elsewhere Luke Welsman made a golden duck, which is one way of getting your name in the paper. I managed a couple of overs with the ball later that proved tidier than expected, finishing with 2-18 and four maidens — the sort of figures that make you feel briefly like you know what you're doing.

Tenterden replied with 184/8 from their 44, Guy Day the chief architect with a fine 61, well supported by Joe Betteridge's 40 and Mark Kokkinos's 20, their sixth-wicket stand of 55 threatening to take the game away from us before Tobey Castle, not content with his runs, turned the screw with 4-30. James Piper weighed in with one, and Matt Goldsmith departed for a duck at our hands. Nine byes will have given John Karassellos some uncomfortable reading behind the stumps.

A word too for Darrell, whose 33 came in his 100th appearance in Linton colours — a proper milestone, marked, fittingly, in a losing draw of a battle rather than a rout either way.

In the end honours were shared, neither side quite able to force it home. A fair result, and on this evidence, with Tobey Castle in this sort of form, we shan't be complaining too loudly.

Sat 23 Sep 2017 🧢 1st XIv SC Selling CC · AwayformWLWWW

Beaten by 88 runs, but I'll say this for us — we didn't go quietly. Selling won the toss, took first knock, and made us regret it, racking up 262/7 from their forty overs. Sun 17 Sep 2017 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv SC Sidcup CC · HomeformWWWDW

Sunday 17 September, and Sidcup CC rolled into Linton Park on a damp, faintly menacing afternoon — the kind where the covers grumble but the game goes on regardless. I lost the toss, as is customary these days, and Sidcup elected to bat, leaving us to make first use of a ball that, for once, actually did some talking.

And talk it did. Sidcup were bowled out for 101 in 30.2 overs, never quite finding their rhythm after losing an early wicket with just the solitary run on the board. Daniel Seaman (26) and Lalit Mangalarapu (25) did their best to steady things, but the damage was already being inflicted at the other end — Darrell Houghton-Judge simply ran through them with a superb 4-21, backed up handsomely by Luke Welsman (3-21) and the ever-parsimonious Tobey Castle, who took 2-7 including three maidens that must have felt like penance to face.

Chasing 102, we made a start of sorts — 18/1 from five overs — before the heavens, or the umpires, or perhaps just good sense intervened, and honours were shared. David Rolls and Andrew Copp will want their names back off the pavilion honours board of ducks, but no matter.

A draw, then, and on another day we surely finish the job. Still, with bowling like that on show, I'll take it as a very useful afternoon's work.

Sat 16 Sep 2017 🧢 1st XIv Stowting CC · HomeformWWLWW

A pleasant, damp Saturday saw Stowting CC roll into Linton Park on 16 September, and for once the toss actually went my way — I had no hesitation in taking first strike on a soggy but true-enough surface.

What followed was as handsome an opening stand as I can recall. Kevin Presneill was in imperious touch, cashing in for 86, while I was happy to play the supporting role for a fifty of my own, 66, before Tobey Castle finished things off in style with an unbeaten 39. The pair of us put on 161 before anyone so much as troubled the scorers with a wicket — a statistic that rather flattered the rest of the order, not least poor Trevor Back, whose golden duck will be logged for posterity in the club's ledger of shame. Still, 220 for 3 from our forty overs was a total to be proud of, and a platform from which to defend.

Stowting never quite got away from us. An early wobble at 6 for 1 promised much, and though Mick Jackson (61) and Nick Graham (42) briefly threatened to make a match of it with 82 for the fourth wicket, the innings subsided from 180 for 6 to 183 for 9 under sustained pressure. Anton Bourdillon was superb with 3 for 34, ably backed by the miserly Abishek Pushpavinothan (2 for 16, three maidens) and Tim Thirkell, whose two wickets nudged him past Alan Boyns into 19th on the club's all-time list.

A 37-run win, and thoroughly deserved. Tea was, as ever, the real highlight.

A proper club cricket afternoon, then – early wobbles from both sides, a milestone innings, and a result that owed as much to nerve as to talent. Two points, thoroughly earned, and Sam's century of appearances made it a day to remember regardless of the scorebook.

Sun 27 Aug 2017 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv CC Chiddingstone CC · HomeformWDWWW

Skipper's Log — Sunday 27 August 2017, Chiddingstone CC at home

Warm, overcast, and the sort of afternoon that promises rain but delivers only mild suspense — we lost the toss, were duly asked to field first, and had to make our peace with batting second on a ground that felt, by four o'clock, entirely too pleasant for hard feelings.

With the bat we set about repairing that early setback in some style. Benjamin Austin and Tobey Castle put on 73 for the first wicket before anyone troubled the scorers with a wicket falling, and from there Austin simply refused to get out, ending unbeaten on a majestic 102 — three figures, no less, and precious few of them lucky. Trevor Back's unbeaten 47 and Castle's 33 gave the innings real backbone, and 234 for 5 off 36.4 overs felt like a total with some bite to it.

Defending it, though, is where cricket reminds you it owes you nothing. Tony Cooper played the innings of the day, 73 not out and utterly immovable, with Brian Leppard (33 not out) and Alex Hanwell (21) doing just enough alongside him — their fourth-wicket stand of 66 the one that really hurt us. James Piper bowled beautifully for 2-19, well supported by Harrison Fermor and Darrell Houghton-Judge, and I did what I could with bat and ball, though it wasn't quite enough to prise out the last of them. Chiddingstone closed on 189 for 5 off their 39, and honours were shared.

A draw, then — which in village cricket is really just a truce dressed up as a result. Nobody lost, nobody quite won, and everybody went home for tea satisfied enough. I'll take it.
