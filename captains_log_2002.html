🗓️ 28 Sep – 04 Oct 2002 · 2002 — 1 match

Sat 28 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv BC Bidborough CC · HomeformWLWWL

Under leaden Kentish skies I won the toss and, with my usual reckless optimism, chose to bat first. It proved, for once, the correct decision. We posted a healthy 188/6 from our forty overs, built around a well-made fifty from yours truly (66) and handsomely supported by Clive Newman's fine 65. Not every innings sparkled — poor Rob Jackson trudged back for a duck, and I'll be buying him a consolatory pint for that one — but the total gave us something to bowl at.

And bowl at it we did, though Bidborough had other ideas. R. Warner played the innings of the day, a fine half-century of 58, ably backed by G. Gask (29) and Gary Thorne (28), and between them they chased our total down with something to spare. It wasn't for want of effort in the field: Rob Jackson atoned handsomely with 2-15 to lead the attack, Jon Harris and Darren Styles chipped in with a wicket apiece, and Mark Christmas bowled with real discipline, sending down two maidens. In the end, though, Bidborough's batsmen simply had the better of us, closing out victory by four wickets with overs to spare.

A losing afternoon, then, but not a dispiriting one — there was plenty to admire in both our batting and our bowling, even if the sums didn't quite add up. We'll dust ourselves down, and we'll be better for it next week.

🗓️ 21 Sep – 27 Sep 2002 · 2002 — 3 matches

Wed 25 Sep 2002 🧢 Midweek XIv P Penshurst · AwayformDLWLW

Jon Harris again, and this time I get to write the good sort of report. Wednesday saw us travel to Penshurst, and having called correctly for once in my life, I stuck us in — a decision that looked considerably more inspired by close of play than it did at the toss. Michael Beeney set about proving why he's worth his corn with a classy 71, and with Craig Sands unbeaten on 45 and Trevor Back chipping in 32, we posted a healthy 217/7 from our forty overs. Less said the better about Mike Elworthy and Mark Christmas, who both trudged back for ducks — the Duck Club ledger groans under the weight of them, but such is village cricket.

With the ball, Penshurst's Connelly made a fight of it with 49, well supported by Mitchell (40) and Exton (30), but they could only manage 203/8 in reply. Mark Christmas atoned handsomely for his batting sins with a superb 3-38, backed up nicely by Back (1-20) and Beeney (1-24), and I was pleased enough with my own small part in proceedings to mention it just the once and move swiftly on.

A special mention, too, for Michael Beeney, who brought up 1,000 career runs for the club — a landmark worth raising a glass to. Back now sits second in the season's run charts, with Christmas second among the wicket-takers, and both look set to climb further yet.

A fine win by 14 runs. Onwards.

Sun 22 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWWLWW

From the captain's chair — Sunday 22 September 2002, and it was Anchorians CC who made the trip to grace us with their presence. I lost the toss, as seems to have become something of a habit this season, and duly consigned the lads to the field for the afternoon.

Elsewhere, the season rolls on regardless of our Sunday wobbles — Clive Newman continues to plunder runs at the top of the 1st XI charts with 821, and Michael Beeney's wicket haul climbs to 36. As for this one: a spirited effort, a whisker short, and we'll be better for it next week.

Sat 21 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv EC Elliotts CC · AwayformWWWLW

Saturday, 21 September 2002 saw us make the trip to Elliotts CC, and losing the toss meant an afternoon in the field to open proceedings — hardly the worst sentence on a fine day, though the lads might disagree by the fortieth over. A generous 26 extras did no harm at all, while poor Craig Sands had a torrid time behind the stumps, coughing up eight byes for his troubles.

A scrappy, occasionally comic, but thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work. Four wickets to the good, and the run chase safely negotiated — I'll take that every week of the season.

Michael Beeney's solitary wicket was itself historic, easing him past Terry Donovan into 17th on the club's all-time list, and he continues to lead our 2002 wicket charts with 30, with Daniel Thirkell second in the run charts on 684.

We ran out winners by 91 runs. A very good day for the club indeed.
