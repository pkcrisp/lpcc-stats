🗓️ 28 Sep – 04 Oct 2002 · 2002 — 1 match

Sat 28 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv BC Bidborough CC · HomeformLLLWL

Under leaden Kentish skies I won the toss and, with my usual reckless optimism, chose to bat first. It proved, for once, the correct decision. We posted a healthy 188/6 from our forty overs, built around a well-made fifty from yours truly (66) and handsomely supported by Clive Newman's fine 65. Not every innings sparkled — poor Rob Jackson trudged back for a duck, and I'll be buying him a consolatory pint for that one — but the total gave us something to bowl at.

And bowl at it we did, though Bidborough had other ideas. R. Warner played the innings of the day, a fine half-century of 58, ably backed by G. Gask (29) and Gary Thorne (28), and between them they chased our total down with something to spare. It wasn't for want of effort in the field: Rob Jackson atoned handsomely with 2-15 to lead the attack, Jon Harris and Darren Styles chipped in with a wicket apiece, and Mark Christmas bowled with real discipline, sending down two maidens. In the end, though, Bidborough's batsmen simply had the better of us, closing out victory by four wickets with overs to spare.

A losing afternoon, then, but not a dispiriting one — there was plenty to admire in both our batting and our bowling, even if the sums didn't quite add up. We'll dust ourselves down, and we'll be better for it next week.

Wed 25 Sep 2002 🧢 Midweek XIv P Penshurst · AwayformDLWLW

Wednesday last, we took ourselves off to Dummy Site, and for once I won the toss — a rare enough event that I nearly forgot what to do with it. Batting first seemed the sensible course, and 217 for 7 from our forty overs was a tidy return. Michael Beeney played the anchor beautifully for a well-made 71, with Craig Sands (45 not out) and Trevor Back (32) lending sturdy support. Less gloriously, Mike Elworthy and Mark Christmas both took the long walk back for nought — the Duck Club welcomes two new members, and no doubt they'll hear about it over tea for weeks to come.

In the field we gave everything we had. Their innings closed on 203 for 8, with Connelly's 49 the backbone of the chase, backed by Mitchell (40) and Exton (30). Mark Christmas was excellent with the ball, 3 for 38, and found willing accomplices in Trevor Back (1-20) and Michael Beeney (1-24).

There was proper cause for cheer amid the disappointment: Michael Beeney brought up 1,000 career runs for the club, a landmark worth raising a glass to. Trevor Back and Mark Christmas continue to sit second in the Midweek XI's season charts for runs and wickets respectively — form that bodes well for the rest of the campaign.

In the end, though, we fell short by 14 runs. Honest effort, honestly beaten. We'll dust ourselves down and come back stronger next week.

Sun 22 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLLLLW

From the captain's chair — Sunday 22 September 2002, and it was Anchorians CC who made the trip to grace us with their presence. I lost the toss, as seems to have become something of a habit this season, and duly consigned the lads to the field for the afternoon.

Elsewhere, the season rolls on regardless of our Sunday wobbles — Clive Newman continues to plunder runs at the top of the 1st XI charts with 821, and Michael Beeney's wicket haul climbs to 36. As for this one: a spirited effort, a whisker short, and we'll be better for it next week.

Sat 21 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv EC Elliotts CC · AwayformLLLLL

Saturday, 21 September 2002 saw us make the trip to Elliotts CC, and losing the toss meant an afternoon in the field to open proceedings — hardly the worst sentence on a fine day, though the lads might disagree by the fortieth over. Elliotts battled to 129 all out in 48.2 overs, Dave Gracey top-scoring with 26 and Mark Jasper chipping in with 21, but there was little to trouble the scoreboard beyond that. I managed 1-12 myself, happy enough to play a supporting role, and both Dan Buckle and Cliff Pegg departed without troubling the scorers.

I was fortunate enough to bat through and finish 42 not out, with Mike Redman a fine ally at the other end on 35 not out — between us we saw the side home, though Mike Elworthy and Russell Baldwin will want no reminder of their ducks with the bat. A scrappy, occasionally comic, but thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work. Four wickets to the good, and the run chase safely negotiated — I'll take that every week of the season.

Sun 15 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv OO Old Owens · HomeformDLLLL

Skipper's Log — Sunday 15th September 2002. Old Owens the visitors, and the coin let us down before a ball was bowled; theirs was the call, and they chose to bat, which on a day like this felt like the first sign of trouble.

To be fair, we bowled with a bit of heart. Zuber gave us a proper chasing down the order, 102 of pure class that we simply couldn't buy a way past, with Nagarajo (38 not out) and Foley (34) chipping in to help Old Owens post 222/4 from their 38 overs. Stuart Cheesman was the pick of the attack with 2-32, and I managed a bit of a say myself with 2-37 — can't grumble at that on a shirt-front of a pitch. Ian Brattle bowled beautifully in the middle overs, two maidens and barely a run to be had off him, proof that not everything went for a walk that afternoon.

Then came the small matter of chasing it down, and here the lads simply refused to be troubled. Russell Baldwin batted like a man who'd read the script and decided he preferred his own ending, 127 not out of the most serene stroke-play, while Clive Newman purred to 83 not out at the other end. Between them they knocked off 225 without loss in just 29 overs — barely time to finish the tea urn.

So, not our day with the ball, but an afternoon to file happily under "the good kind of thrashing" — down by ten wickets, heads held high, and back to the nets we go.

Sat 07 Sep 2002 🧢 1st XIv M Mersham · HomeformLLDLL

Skipper's Log, Saturday 7th September — Mersham at home, and for once the coin behaved itself. I won the toss and had no hesitation whatsoever in taking first knock, and I'll admit a small note of self-congratulation crept in as the day wore on.

What followed was as serene an afternoon's batting as Linton has enjoyed in some while. Daniel Thirkell simply refused to get out, ambling to a magnificent 101 not out with the unhurried air of a man who had nowhere else to be, while I found some rare form of my own to finish unbeaten on 95 — agonisingly shy of the century, though I shan't pretend I wasn't rather pleased with it. Together we carried us to 206 for 1 off our 36 overs, a total that had the tea urn practically boiling over with excitement.

With the ball, we set about defending it with real discipline. Russell Baldwin led the attack beautifully for 1-2, Kevin Presneill and Mark Christmas chipped in with a wicket apiece, and Christmas in particular bowled with a miserliness bordering on the parsimonious — three maidens' worth. K. Brown top-scored gamely for the visitors with 42, but Mersham could only muster 115 for 6 off their 35.

It was also a day for the record books: Russell Baldwin brought up his hundredth appearance in Linton colours, a milestone earned the hard way, and Michael Beeney's solitary wicket nudged him past Terry Donovan into 17th on the club's all-time list — quietly historic stuff.

A fine team performance, then, and a thoroughly good day to be captain.
