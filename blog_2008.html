🦆 Duck News — 27 September 2008 🦆

1 duck
Week 23 · 27 Sep 2008
Season's tail end at Linton Park, and Chelsfield CC popped by to collect the club's one and only contribution to the zero column this week. 1st XI Batting at four, Kevin Presneill fell to the classic combination of Caught Unsure, Bowled Wakeman — a fielder whose identity remains, fittingly, as blank as the scorecard next to Kevin's name. It's his 4th duck of the season and the 15th of a career now standing at 124 innings, good enough to rank him 69th of 1364 names on Linton's all-time roll of dishonour. A man building a reputation, one blob at a time. Spookily, on this very day back in 1964, Robert Murray was going through precisely the same ritual against Linton Park CC — one of 17 ducks across a career that ran from 1958 to 1965, and worth 65th place in the all-time standings. Cricket's ducks may be dumb animals, but apparently they know their calendar. A slim week for the exclusive zero club — one match, one duck, one man left to explain himself to the tea urn. Even the Duck Club needs a rest day occasionally. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Mark Christmas — 31 innings, 7 ducks, 22.58% - Rob Doick — 14 innings, 5 ducks, 35.71% - Joe Martin — 17 innings, 5 ducks, 29.41% - Thomas Beeney — 27 innings, 5 ducks, 18.52% - Chris Newman — 29 innings, 5 ducks, 17.24% Rob Doick's numbers remain the standout worry — more than a third of his innings this season have ended before they got going. A man giving the top of the table a real fright. That's the lot for this round, but the season isn't done yet. Next fixture brings fresh opportunity for someone to join the honours board — pads on, bats loose, and may the odds be ever in your favour. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 20-21 September 2008

1 duck
Week 22 · 20 Sep 2008 – 21 Sep 2008
Two fixtures on the go this weekend and, for once, the ducks proved elusive — only a single addition to the exclusive zero club to report. Quality over quantity, evidently. 🏏 Saturday, Sidcup CC The lone representative came at number 7, where Matthew Carter fell b Daniel Dark without troubling the scorers. It's his first duck of the 2008 campaign, and his 17th in senior colours from 110 innings — good enough for 63rd on the all-time leaderboard, a table of 1,363 names deep. Consistency of a very particular kind. Carter is, of course, a fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his LPCC account with nought all those years ago, and he still carries the 2004 Duck of the Year sash in the trophy cabinet from a rather more prolific vintage. This one merely adds to the pile. He now sits shoulder to shoulder with Clive Newman, who plied his trade from 1992 to 2008 and parked himself on exactly the same 17 career ducks across 369 innings, one place above at 62nd. Two careers, seventeen noughts apiece — the art of not scoring, perfected independently. 📅 Week in Summary Two matches, one duck — a quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, and frankly a triumph of adversity avoided rather than embraced. The rest of the batting lineups, on both days, declined to cooperate with our correspondent. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 - Mark Christmas — 7 ducks, 31 innings, 22.58% - Rob Doick — 5 ducks, 14 innings, 35.71% - Joe Martin — 5 ducks, 17 innings, 29.41% - Thomas Beeney — 5 ducks, 27 innings, 18.52% - Chris Newman — 5 ducks, 28 innings, 17.86% Rob Doick still leads the pack on strike rate with better than one duck in every three visits to the crease — a percentage the rest of the club can only admire from a respectful distance. Quiet week, but the season's far from over. Next fixture, more willow, more wickets, and surely a fresh crop of nought-scorers to welcome into the fold. Bats up, standards low — see you all next weekend 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 13th to 17th September 2008 🦆

4 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 2008 – 17 Sep 2008
A modest but perfectly formed haul this week: four ducks laid across four fixtures as the 2008 season enters its final furlong. The exclusive zero club welcomes no new faces but shuffles its existing membership nicely. Saturday 13th September — 1st XI v National Winners Andrew Moss fell for nought at first drop, done in by a bowler even the scorer gave up trying to identify ("b Unsure" — cricket's oldest and most honest confession). It's his 1st duck of the season and 11th of a fine career, lodging him at 86th in the all-time table from 135 innings. Andrew is also a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell innings. Spookily, on this very date in 2003 Christopher Thirkell (1998–2008) also strolled back for a duck, against Upchurch CC — the calendar repeating itself five years on to the day. Wednesday 17th September — Midweek XI v Penshurst Park CC Michael Beeney was bowled by Bass Simpson without scoring, his 3rd duck of the season and 29th of an increasingly decorated career, good enough for 34th all-time from 223 innings. That tally draws him level with the legendary Paul Gibson (1984–2008, 486 innings), a fellow member of the Platinum Duck set. Michael himself carries the rare Diamond Duck (2002, out without facing a ball) and Only Two Quacks honours — and keeps it in the family, with kin Thomas Beeney sitting on 12 career ducks of his own. Sunday 14th September — Sunday 1st XI v Anchorians CC A productive afternoon for the club, if not for the batting card. Joe Martin was trapped lbw by James Hockley for his 5th duck of the season — a tally that now sees him featuring in the season's own top five — and 8th career duck from 40 innings, 117th all-time. Joe is a paid-up Debutante Duck club member from his actual first senior innings some time ago. He now joins Adam Jackson (2001–2007, 87 innings) on exactly 8. Later in the same innings, Iain Rawson edged behind for his 2nd duck of the season, caught off James Hockley for his 4th career nought from 31 innings, 178th all-time. Nicely, that leaves him level with his own brother Glen Rawson, who also sits on precisely 4 career ducks — sibling solidarity in the art of not scoring. Week in summary Four ducks from four matches, with the Anchorians fixture alone responsible for half the week's harvest. One fixture in the period stayed refreshingly duck-free — a rare vegan affair for the club's poultry connoisseurs. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Mark Christmas — 7 ducks, 30 innings (23.3%) - Rob Doick — 5 ducks, 14 innings (35.7%) - Joe Martin — 5 ducks, 16 innings (31.3%) - Chris Newman — 5 ducks, 27 innings (18.5%) - Thomas Beeney — 5 ducks, 27 innings (18.5%) Rob Doick's strike rate remains the most alarming statistic in the club, while Joe Martin's Sunday effort keeps him firmly in the hunt for Duck of the Year. 🏏 Next fixture looms and, as ever, the zero club has vacancies. Bring your bat, bring your luck, and we'll see who's added to the honours board next time. 🍩0️⃣🦆

Seven ducks in a season is precisely the kind of form that gets a man's name etched on the Duck of the Year 2008 board, and with the campaign's top spot currently his (see below), Mark is very much the man to catch. Adam Tyler thus doubled up in the same innings, a fine advert for line and length over the art of scoring. Kevin, too, finds himself keeping Brian Piper company on this same 1981 anniversary — a duck seems to be quite the traditional way to mark 23 August at Linton Park. 📊 Week in Summary A modest but efficient haul: 2 ducks from 2 matches, both credited to the same Headcorn bowler in the same innings — Adam Tyler will sleep well tonight. Early season, and the exclusive zero club has already opened its books for 2008. Two matches were played across the weekend and, refreshingly restrained, only one man was summoned before the committee. 🏏 1st XI — away at Mersham Sports CC, Saturday 26th Batting at five, Rob Doick departed caught off the bowling of Alan Stevens, the fielder recorded in the book only as "Unsure" — which, frankly, feels like the entire club's verdict on how it happened. It's Rob's 1st duck of the 2008 season and his 26th for the club in 214 innings, good enough for 40th in the all-time standings of 1337. That tally of 26 lands him level with Michael Beeney, who played 1995–2008 and reached the same mark in 205 innings — one place above Rob at 38th all-time, and a fully paid-up member of the Diamond Duck club for a 2002 vintage nought without facing a ball, plus the Only Two Quacks honour shared with Ian Brattle. Good company to keep, if "company" is the right word. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, one duck — the herd was thin on the ground this early in the season, with only Rob Doick answering the roll call. Whether that's opening-weekend rust settling elsewhere or everyone else simply keeping their heads down, the committee cannot say. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Rob Doick — 1 innings, 1 duck, 100% A leaderboard of one for now — plenty of season left for challengers to make their case, and history suggests they will. 🍩 Next weekend brings fresh fixtures and, in all likelihood, fresh company for Rob at the top of the table. Bats up, heads down — the Duck Club is always recruiting.
