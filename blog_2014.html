🦆 Duck News — 27 September 2014

0 ducks
Week 23 · 27 Sep 2014
Duck Hunting Season correspondent here with a rare and frankly disappointing bulletin: the ducks evaded capture entirely this week. One solitary fixture on the books, and not a single naught to show for it. Eleven players (or however many turned out) all found their way off the mark, leaving the Duck Club's nets empty and its correspondent reaching for words. A clean sheet is a fine thing in goalkeeping. In this club, it's a wasted weekend. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or simply a temporary lapse in the club's usual generosity with its wickets remains to be seen. History suggests normal service resumes shortly — the exclusive zero club rarely stays quiet for long. 🍩 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Thomas Beeney — 21 innings, 6 ducks, 28.57% 2. Richard Piper — 31 innings, 5 ducks, 16.13% 3. Will Thirkell — 25 innings, 4 ducks, 16.00% 4. Tim Wright — 32 innings, 4 ducks, 12.50% 5. Darrell Houghton-Judge — 9 innings, 3 ducks, 33.33% Thomas Beeney continues to lead the 2014 Duck of the Year race with real conviction — six ducks from just 21 innings is the kind of consistency the club holds dear, even if the scorebook disagrees. Darrell Houghton-Judge, meanwhile, keeps his percentage eye-wateringly high at over a third of innings ending in nought — a small sample, but an ominous one for anyone chasing him down. With the season drawing towards its close, there's still time for movement at the top. A quiet week is no guarantee of a quiet finish. 0️⃣ Until the ducks return — and they always do — keep your bats straight, your eyes on the ball, and your expectations appropriately low. See you at the next fixture, when normal ignominious service should, in all likelihood, resume. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 20-21 September 2014 🦆

1 duck
Week 22 · 20 Sep 2014 – 21 Sep 2014
A quiet weekend in the exclusive zero club — only two fixtures on the go and the ducks mostly evaded the trap. But one man never lets the club down. Sunday 1st XI — away at Tenterden CC Coming in at four, Richard Piper departed bowled by Tom Richards, adding a fifth duck to his season tally and his 68th career nought — good enough to hold station at 6th of 1423 in the all-time standings. A stalwart of the honours boards, Richard's name already sits proudly among the Duck of the Year alumni (2003), the Full House club (a duck at every position, 1 to 11 — a truly comprehensive body of work), and the top tens for both Homebody and Ducks on the Road. Consistency, as they say, of a very particular kind. 0️⃣ And it appears the art of not scoring runs in the family — brother James (13 career ducks) and, going back a generation, Brian Piper (23) are both paid-up members of the same dubious tradition. A chip off the old duck egg, you might say. Fittingly, on this very date back in 1958, Stan Wratten — who turned out for the club from 1943 to 1984 and sits on 35 career ducks (26th all-time) — also trudged back for nought, against Tilling Steven of all sides. Some anniversaries are best celebrated quietly, and this one arrived right on schedule. 📊 Week in summary One duck from two matches — the flock stayed largely airborne this week. But when only one man fronts up for the club, you could do worse than Richard Piper doing the honours in style. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Thomas Beeney — 6 ducks in 21 innings (28.57%) 2. Richard Piper — 5 ducks in 30 innings (16.67%) 3. Will Thirkell — 4 ducks in 24 innings (16.67%) 4. Tim Wright — 4 ducks in 32 innings (12.5%) 5. Darrell Houghton-Judge — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) Thomas Beeney holds firm at the top, but Darrell's blistering 33% strike rate in a smaller sample suggests he's one bad shot away from a serious late charge. 🏏 The season isn't done with us yet — next fixture, next opportunity for the zero club to welcome new members. Pads on, bat straight, eyes on the honours board. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 13–17 September 2014

2 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 2014 – 17 Sep 2014
As the 2014 season creaks toward its close, the Duck Club found time for one last flourish of failure. Three matches were played across the closing days of the campaign, and Meopham CC's Harry Tompkins did his level best to single-handedly keep the honours board ticking over, claiming two Linton wickets for two identical noughts in the very same innings. 🏏 2nd XI v Meopham CC (13 September, away) Batting at 8, Darrell Houghton-Judge fell to Tompkins without troubling the scorers — his third duck of the season, and third of an increasingly consistent career. That draws him level with Daniel McCarthy, who played 2006–1907… make that 2006–2007, and needed 19 innings to reach the same total where Darrell has managed it in just 10. Darrell remains a nearly-man of the exclusive Full House club, having ducked at 10 of his 11 career batting positions — a place of dubious distinction indeed. One ball later and one place down the order, Tobey Castle followed his teammate straight back to the pavilion, also bowled Tompkins, for his second duck of the season and his second in senior cricket. That's level with Alan Probert, who reached the same tally in just two innings across 2008–2009 and is enshrined among the club's Retirement Duck alumni, his own farewell innings a duck to match. Tobey, meanwhile, bats in the long shadow of parent David Castle's rather more prolific 45 career noughts — a chip off the old block if ever there was one. 📋 Week in Summary Only two ducks fell across three matches this week, both to the same bowler, in the same innings, one place apart in the order — proof that Tompkins earned his tea rather more comfortably than most visitors manage at Linton Park. A quiet week for the club numerically, but a memorable one for its sheer economy. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Thomas Beeney — 6 ducks in 20 innings (30%) - Will Thirkell — 4 ducks in 22 innings (18.18%) - Richard Piper — 4 ducks in 28 innings (14.29%) - Tim Wright — 4 ducks in 31 innings (12.9%) - Darrell Houghton-Judge — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) Darrell's fresh addition this week nudges him straight into the season's top five on a startling 33% strike rate — the sort of consistency of a very particular kind that ought to worry the four names above him. As the season winds down, the Duck Club watches on, ever hopeful a few more batsmen find their way into the zero club before the covers go on for winter. Triumph through adversity, as ever. 🦆🍩

Two Thirkells, two grounds, one weekend — the family name really does travel. 📋 Week in Summary Two ducks from four matches is a quiet return by Duck Club standards, but quality over quantity — both fell at the same batting position, both to catches, and both wearing the same surname. 🦆 Duck News — 19 July 2014

1 duck
Week 13 · 19 Jul 2014
A quiet weekend for the executioners of Linton Park, but the Duck Club never truly closes its doors — and Saturday's trip to Lenham was enough to keep the lights on. 🏏 1st XI — away at Lenham CC Daniel Thirkell strode out at first wicket down and lasted all of two deliveries before Tom Curtis had him lbw — not quite golden, but no one's handing out medals for surviving one ball longer. It's Daniel's 1st duck of the campaign and the 26th of an already formidable career spanning 418 innings, lodging him at a very respectable 43rd of 1418 in the all-time roll of shame. He also carries membership of the Tourist Duck top 10 — a standing honour for a man who, it seems, is just as capable of getting out on the road as he is at home. He joins Paul Brattle — who played from 1969 to 2008 and finished on the same 26 ducks from 308 innings, sitting one place above at 45th — on the exclusive tally of 26. Different eras, identical arithmetic. Cricket's version of poetry. And spare a thought for the surname itself — Daniel is merely the latest entrant from an entire duck dynasty.
