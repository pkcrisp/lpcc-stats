🗓️ 28 Sep – 04 Oct 1985 · 1985 — 1 match

Sat 28 Sep 1985 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformWDWDL

Called it right at the toss — you'd think it'd be second nature by now, but no, there's still that little flutter — and had no hesitation in taking first knock against Hastings & St Leonards Priory. The lads built us a competitive if hardly imperious 152 for 8 from our 48 overs. Paul Bowles led the way with a fine 32, Nigel Thirkell chipped in handsomely with 25, Sean Charlton added 23, and I added a modest 22 of my own to the cause — nothing to write home about, but useful runs when the scoreboard needed feeding.

Defending that total looked a tall order on paper, but the bowlers had other ideas. Paul Bowles was magnificent with the ball, following his runs with 4 for 37 and five overs where not so much as a single leaked through — the sort of spell that makes a captain's job embarrassingly easy. Jon Harris backed him up superbly with 3 for 30, and Matt Large chipped in with 1 for 21. Hastings limped to 123 for 9 from their 44, Alan Booth-Jones top-scoring with 27, well supported by S Leach's 23 and Ian Gillespie's unbeaten 22. Three of them — Paul Grace, Geoff Collins and D Rafferty — never troubled the scorers at all.

Poor Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, eight byes escaping his gloves, but no one held it against him. In the end honours were shared, neither side quite able to force the issue — a fair result, if I'm honest, and a thoroughly pleasant way to spend a September Saturday.

🗓️ 21 Sep – 27 Sep 1985 · 1985 — 3 matches

Wed 25 Sep 1985 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWDLLL

Here's the report:

We won the toss at Penshurst and I had no hesitation in electing to bat — one of those decisions that either gets you a pat on the back or a stern word in the bar afterwards, and I'm delighted to report it was the former. It wasn't all plain sailing: we shipped an early wicket with the total still reading a rather embarrassing nought, and both David Castle and Nigel Thirkell contributed ducks to the scorebook, which is never a comfortable sight from the pavilion. But Andrew Hales played beautifully for 49, Paul Bowles chipped in an unbeaten 43, and Jim Hall's 22 gave the innings some much-needed backbone, with a stand of 64 for the seventh wicket the real turning point. A defiant last-wicket stand added a further 58 to drag us up to a respectable 166 for 9 off our 40 overs.

Then came the small matter of defending it. Penshurst threatened for a while — Mark Jenner's 40, with useful support from P Grayland (34) and an unbeaten 25 from C Fry, kept us honest — but they never quite got away from us, sending back N Fowler, P Johnson and Michael Meade without troubling the scorers. Nigel Thirkell, atoning handsomely for that earlier duck, ran through them with a magnificent 5 for 25, Paul Bowles picked up three, and I managed one for 43 to do my bit. Penshurst were bowled out for 155 from 38 overs, leaving us winners by 11 runs.

A tight, twitchy finish, but a thoroughly good day for the club — the sort of win that makes the tea taste that bit sweeter.

Sun 22 Sep 1985 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWWDWD

Sunday last saw Anchorians CC visit for what turned into one of those afternoons that reminds you why you keep turning up. I called correctly and, with the square looking true, never seriously considered doing anything but batting first.

Credit where due, though: we did have our moments, none finer than an early wicket with the board still reading nought, and three ducks fell to us along the way. In the end Anchorians got home by two wickets — desperately close, desperately frustrating, but no disgrace. We'll dust ourselves down and come back stronger next week.

Trevor Back was the pick with 1 for 9, Paul Bowles toiled away for 1 for 40 with three tidy maidens, and I added 1 for 47 for my trouble.

A word of cheer amid the gloom: Malcolm's 26 nudged him past me into 13th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, while Ian Hammond continues to lead our 1985 Midweek run charts with 108, and I still lead the wickets with 11.

Down by 6 wickets, then, and no arguing with it. Heads up, back to the nets — Hawkenbury deserved their day, and we shall have to find ours again soon.

Sat 14 Sep 1985 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · HomeformDLLWW

Saturday the 14th of September brought Southborough CC to Linton Park, and true to form, I lost the toss — some things in this job never change. They elected to bat, which suited me well enough; a long afternoon in the field beats a long afternoon in the pavilion any day.

The day belonged to Matt Large, whose 5 for 53 — with four maidens thrown in for good measure — was a spell thoroughly deserving of the headlines. Sean Charlton and Jon Harris each chipped in with a wicket apiece; Harris, incidentally, now leads our 1985 wicket charts with 75 to his name, a tally that rather speaks for itself.

In reply we made steady if unspectacular progress, closing on 57 for 2 from 17 overs. I was glad to finish unbeaten on 24, runs that at least kept the scoreboard ticking over respectably — and which nudge my own season's tally along nicely, past 1489 runs now.

So honours shared, a draw with neither side quite able to land the decisive blow. Not a classic, perhaps, but a thoroughly pleasant way to spend a Saturday, and on this form I'll happily take a share of the spoils.

Six runs the margin, two points the reward, and rather more grey hairs than I'd have liked. A famous, nerve-shredding win, and one the lads will dine out on for weeks.
