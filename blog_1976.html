🦆 Duck News — 18–19 September 1976

0 ducks
Week 22 · 18 Sep 1976 – 19 Sep 1976
Two fixtures went into the book this weekend and, remarkably, the Duck Club has nothing whatsoever to show for it. Not a single nought, not a solitary quack, across both matches. Every batsman who walked to the crease found a way to trouble the scorers, however modestly, and departed with the exclusive zero club firmly barred to new entrants. Frankly it's a scandal. The art of not scoring is a proud and difficult discipline, honed over decades by LPCC's finest, and this weekend the club's batting order simply couldn't be bothered to uphold the tradition. Somewhere, the standings committee is quietly relieved — the leaderboard stays exactly as it was, unmoved and undisturbed. Consider it a fallow weekend for the connoisseur. The ducks are out there, biding their time, and normal service will surely resume before the season closes. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏆 1. Jon Harris — 26 innings, 6 ducks, 23.08% 2. Tim Thirkell — 41 innings, 5 ducks, 12.2% 3. Tony Gauna — 9 innings, 3 ducks, 33.33% 4. Allan Cooper — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.0% 5. Ian Brattle — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.0% 🥇 Ian Brattle sits menacingly atop the percentage charts on a coin-flip 50%, though Jon Harris's volume lead keeps him honest at the head of the table proper. Tony Gauna and Allan Cooper lurk not far behind, each capable of a leap up the standings the moment application deserts them for an over or two. Don't get comfortable out there, batsmen of Linton Park. The next fixture is never more than a rash shot away from restocking the pond, and this correspondent will be watching every dismissal with the keenest of interest. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 12 September 1976 🦆

1 duck
Week 21 · 12 Sep 1976
A quiet week in the exclusive zero club — just the one match, just the one duck — but it's a big name doing the honours, so quality over quantity this time round. 🏏 1st XI v Mersham Hatch (home) Jon Harris strode out at number 8 and departed without troubling the scorers, bowled by D- Bromley. Nothing new for Jon, mind — this is his 6th duck of the 1976 season and his 32nd for the club, keeping him a commanding 12th of 933 in the all-time charts. With 191 innings under his belt he's the very definition of consistency of a very particular kind. Today's effort also ticks another box on the honours board — Jon is a Full House member, having now collected a duck at every position from 1 to 11, and number 8 has duly been stamped. He's also a long-standing Debutante Duck, having gone for nought on his very first senior innings all those years back — proof the habit was there from the start. With 6 already banked this season, Jon sits firmly atop the Duck of the Year 1976 race, and on today's evidence nobody's catching him before the season's out. He's not the only duck-prone Harris in the family either — a relative in the club carries 36 career noughts of his own, so the gene runs deep. And in a lovely bit of calendar symmetry — on this very day back in 1959, S Lusher also trudged off for a duck, against Bowaters CC. History does love to repeat itself in this particular discipline. 📊 Week in Summary One match, one duck, one very familiar face. Not the busiest week the Duck Club has seen, but Jon Harris single-handedly kept the lights on — a true triumph through adversity. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 26 inn, 6 ducks, 23.08% 2. Tim Thirkell — 41 inn, 5 ducks, 12.2% 3. Tony Gauna — 9 inn, 3 ducks, 33.33% 4. Allan Cooper — 12 inn, 3 ducks, 25.0% 5. Ian Brattle — 4 inn, 2 ducks, 50.0% Jon leads the way but the chasing pack isn't far behind — Tony Gauna's 33% strike rate in particular suggests he could make a late charge. Roll on next weekend, when the ducks will no doubt be back in greater numbers. 🍩0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 4th to 8th September 1976

0 ducks
Week 20 · 4 Sep 1976 – 8 Sep 1976
A rare and troubling silence descends on Linton Park this week. Three fixtures played, not a single member of the top order, middle order, or indeed any order at all, managed to depart for nought. The exclusive zero club, so reliably oversubscribed, sat entirely empty this week. Somewhere in 1976 a Linton batsman is presumably still out there, mid-innings, quietly not getting out for nothing, and frankly it will not do. 🏏 Saturday and beyond Across all three matches the story was the same: bat met ball, runs were scored, however grudgingly, and the Duck Club's honours board gained not a single new entry. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs of the Eloff or Gray variety this week — just eleven-a-side games of conventional, uneventful competence. A dark day for connoisseurs of the art of not scoring. We can only assume the ducks have migrated for the season, or are simply biding their time. Consistency of a very particular kind has, this week, eluded us entirely. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 5 ducks from 25 innings (20.0%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks from 41 innings (12.2%) 3. Tony Gauna — 3 ducks from 9 innings (33.3%) 4. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks from 11 innings (27.3%) 5. Ian Brattle — 2 ducks from 4 innings, a startling 50.0% strike rate for nothing whatsoever Jon Harris holds top spot on outright numbers, but Ian Brattle's 50% return is the kind of ratio that turns heads at committee meetings. Tony Gauna and Allan Cooper lurk with menace, both one soft dismissal away from real movement in this table. 📅 Looking ahead Rest assured, the drought will not last. History suggests the ducks are never far away at Linton Park — merely resting, gathering strength, waiting for an unplayable inswinger or a moment's rash impatience against a part-time trundler. Members are reminded that triumph through adversity remains very much on the table, and the honours board has plenty of room left. Until then — bats up, heads down, and may nobody's luck hold much longer. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck Club Report — 29 to 31 August 1976

2 ducks
Week 19 · 29 Aug 1976 – 31 Aug 1976
A quiet weekend on the duck-hunting front, but what little sport there was came concentrated in one glorious top-order collapse at Staplehurst. Two ducks, one innings, plenty of history disturbed along the way. 1st XI v Staplehurst CC (away, 29 August) Brian Piper departed at first wicket down, caught I Pullen off the bowling of a- Thirkell, for his 2nd duck of the season and 5th of an LPCC career now 49 innings long — good enough for 83rd on the all-time list of 933. Keeping it in the family, his brother David sits on an identical 5 career ducks, so the Pipers are level pegging in the exclusive zero club. And in a pleasing quirk of the calendar, on this very day in 1954 Stan Wratten also strolled back for nought, against D Hambrook XI, en route to a career 33 ducks and 10th place all-time. Tim Thirkell followed him back not long after, run out at number six for his 5th duck of the season and 33rd of his career — 11th on the all-time roll of dishonour. This one has extra weight for the honours board: Tim already holds the Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), and number six was merely filling in a well-worn groove. He's also a paid-up member of Duck of the Year 1972, plus the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and shares Quacking Together status with Jim Hall. Fittingly, he too shared today's date with Stan Wratten's 1954 effort at D Hambrook. It runs in the blood — father John Thirkell owns the club's all-time record outright on 88, brother Nigel sits on 25, and cousin Stuart has 1 of his own. A proper poultry dynasty. The only other fixture of the weekend passed off entirely duck-free — a rare outbreak of competence LPCC will not be dwelling on. Week in Summary Two ducks from two matches, both from the same top order, both at Staplehurst. Tim Thirkell's effort draws him level at the top of this season's charts — the race for Duck of the Year is very much alive. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Jon Harris — 5 ducks in 25 innings (20.0%) - Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks in 40 innings (12.5%) - Tony Gauna — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.3%) - Allan Cooper — 3 ducks in 11 innings (27.3%) - Ian Brattle — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.0%) A slender field this week, but there's a full programme still to come before the season closes the books. Get out there, middle it — or don't, and give the Duck Club something to write about. See you all next weekend 🦆🏏0️⃣

The remaining two fixtures were, disappointingly, entirely fowl-free — eleven-a-side vegan affairs on both counts, with not so much as a nibble for the Duck Club to report. On this very date in 1909, C Stevens made his own duck against the Royal West Kent Regiment — the calendar has a long memory. Further down, Jon Harris departed for nought too — his 5th duck of the season, making him the outright pace-setter for Duck of the Year 1976, and his 31st career duck (12th all-time). A paid-up Debutante Duck, a Full House completist (a duck at every position, 1 to 11) and a fixture of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, Jon's history runs deep with two of Sunday's ducklings — long-standing "Quacking Together" and "Only Two Quacks" honours shared with Jim Hall and Allan Cooper respectively. Jim Hall (2nd of the season, 23rd career, 24th all-time) and Allan Cooper (3rd of the season, 14th career, 37th all-time) both departed without scoring in the very same innings — the pair already share an "Only Two Quacks" board from seasons gone, and duly renewed it. Jim remains the Full House nearly-man, agonisingly one position short. Both men's parallel points to Brian Piper, whose duck against Sennocke CC fell on this same date back in 1973. 🥇 Wednesday 11th — home to West Kent Wanderers CC, Midweek XI No name commands more respect in these pages than John Thirkell, and he obliged again — his 2nd duck of the season but a scarcely believable 88th of his career, still comfortably first of 930 on the all-time list. Five separate Duck of the Year titles (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967 and 1971) already grace his mantelpiece. Fittingly, his on-this-day parallel is his own flesh and blood — son Nigel Thirkell, who ducked on this exact date in 1973. Nigel (25 career ducks) and fellow son Tim (32) keep the family name well represented on the honours boards. Alongside him, David Buck opened his season's account with his 2nd career duck from just 6 innings — early days yet, but he comes from good stock, with father William Buck sitting on 28 of his own. By pure coincidence David's parallel is also Nigel Thirkell's 1973 duck. Week in Summary Six ducks fell across three matches. Bearman for his 2nd duck of the summer and 10th of an already storied career, 18 innings in and good enough for 50th on the all-time list of 927. Long since inducted into the 50 Before a 50 club and still trading on a celebrated run of 7 consecutive ducks in the record books, Ian's name is forever twinned with Michael Beeney's under the Only Two Quacks honour — and with brother Paul Brattle sitting on 17 career noughts of his own, the art of not scoring clearly runs in the Brattle blood. Neatly, on this very day in 1960, Robert Murray (1958–1965) also trudged back for nought against Tovil, en route to 17 career ducks and 32nd on the all-time table. 🏏 Sunday 4th July — Aylesford Paper Mills CC (Away) A rich afternoon for the collection at Aylesford. Martin Mears, batting at eight, was caught A. Lombard bowled Fred Baker without scoring — his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of his career from 29 innings, 136th on the all-time chart. He already carries the Quacking Together honour alongside Jim Hall from an earlier vintage. That same date back in 1973 also claimed Cliff Anscombe (1957–1975), fittingly against Northend Sunday, on his way to 25 career ducks, 20th all-time and Duck of the Year in 1957. Last man Tony Gauna followed not long after, stumped by X. Noel off the bowling of Colin Watson — his 3rd duck of the season and 17th of his career from 72 innings, a lofty 34th on the all-time chart.
