🗓️ 25 Sep – 01 Oct 1993 · 1993 — 2 matches

Sat 25 Sep 1993 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · AwayformDDWLL

Saturday 25 September 1993 found us on the road to Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC, and I lost the toss — never a great start to a Saturday, but there we are, we bowled first regardless.

They batted with some purpose, reaching 184/6 from their 48 overs. Barry Dixon led the way with a well-crafted 55, well backed up by B- Phillips' 47 and an unbeaten 30 from Peter Finch, the pair adding a crucial 76 for the second wicket that threatened to run away from us. But just as the floodgates looked set to open, 160/3 becoming 172/6 in a hurry, we found our discipline: Carl Read, N Davies and Tim Morgan all departed without troubling the scorers. Jon Harris was the chief architect of the fightback, a superb 4-24, with Rob Doick chipping in at 1-34. Poor Clive Newman had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 12 byes going begging, but the rest of us more than made up for it.

Chasing 185, David Castle played the innings of the day, 73 well past his fifty, ably supported by Dave Charlton's 26 and Piers McDonald's 24, the openers setting the tone with 52 for the first wicket. I chipped in with the bat myself, glad to see us home 186/6 with overs to spare, at 42.4.

A fine win by four wickets, and one to enjoy on the coach home. Onwards.

Sat 25 Sep 1993 🧢 2nd XIv YC Yalding CC · HomeformDDWWD

Called it right at the toss on that Saturday, 25 September, and never seriously considered anything but batting first against Yalding. The openers gave us the ideal start, motoring us to 105 before the first wicket fell — a platform too good to waste, and Mike Elworthy made sure it wasn't, cruising past his fifty to 61. I chipped in with 39, Ron McCormick added a tidy 21, and we closed on 147/5 from our 42 overs. Poor old Trevor Back will want this one struck from the record, undone for a duck, though with such a solid platform already laid, few of us dwelt on it.

Yalding's reply never threatened. From 45/2 the innings unravelled alarmingly to 54/5, and three of their number — Liam Hawkins, M Ashton and D Shrimpton — trooped back without troubling the scorers. Don Dansie was the chief tormentor, miserly and menacing with 4/20 including three maidens, and I found some rhythm with the ball too, picking up 2/6 in support, while Chris Tymms chipped in with 1/13. All told they mustered 78/8 from their 29 overs, a good forty-odd runs light.

By rights a home win, but cricket being cricket, the clock beat us and honours were shared. As it stands this season I lead the 2nd XI wicket charts with 30, and Tim Thirkell sits second in the run charts on 388 — encouraging signs, even if today ended all square. A draw, then, but a thoroughly satisfying one to have engineered.

Wed 22 Sep 1993 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWWDDL

Wednesday 22 September 1993, and off we trundled to Penshurst Park, blessed with a coin toss that actually went our way for once — I had no hesitation in batting first.

What followed was rather splendid. Nigel Thirkell played the innings of a man born to bat on Penshurst's outfield, striking a magnificent 115, while Paul Bowles chipped in with a classy 55 alongside him — the pair adding 135 for the second wicket and doing most of my captaincy for me. We closed on 221 for 5 from our 38 overs, a total I was rather proud to defend, and a fitting stage for Nigel to bring up 27,000 career runs for the club — not a milestone one reaches by accident.

Defending it, however, proved less straightforward than the scorebook suggests. We had them 6 for 1 and dreaming of an early bath, but C Williamson (67) and Simon Leegood (50) stitched together a stubborn 122 for the third wicket that had me checking my watch rather too often, with John Darby's unbeaten 24 threatening to see them home. Thankfully Piers McDonald had other ideas, spinning his way to a magical 5 for 45, John Hicks fell for a golden duck, and Fred Cheesman (2-48) and the miserly Darren Styles (1-5, four maidens and not a run given away lightly) squeezed the last drops out of them. Penshurst finally folded for 210.

Eleven runs the margin, nerves thoroughly tested, but a very good day for the club indeed.

Sun 19 Sep 1993 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWDDWL

Sunday 19 September 1993, and Anchorians CC rolled up looking rather pleased with themselves — I won the toss and, with barely a flicker of hesitation, stuck us in to bat. That confidence took an early battering when we slipped to 1 for 1, the sort of start that has a captain suddenly very interested in his shoelaces. But cricket, mercifully, has a habit of forgiving early sins. I found some form and got us moving with 56, well supported by David Castle's 31 and Rob Doick's unbeaten 26, our stand for the fifth wicket worth a handsome 65. Even when wickets went again, the last pair weren't reading from the script — a defiant 41 added to drag us to a respectable 154 for 6 off our 42 overs.

Respectable, it turned out, was not quite enough. Anchorians simply strolled to it, Keith Wooding making an imperious 72 not out and Gary Myers keeping him company with a polished 52 not out, the pair chasing down 155 in just 30.1 overs without so much as a hiccup. Poor Clive Newman had an afternoon of it behind the stumps, 27 byes going begging — not his finest hour, though I doubt anyone was catching that lot cleanly.

Beaten by four wickets, then, and no dressing it up. Small consolation that I remain top of the 1993 run charts with 1106 for the season — runs I'd happily trade for a result today. We'll regroup, we'll dust ourselves down, and we'll be better for it next week.

Sat 18 Sep 1993 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · AwayformWWDDW

Skipper's Log — 18 September 1993, away to West Farleigh CC.

Lost the toss, had no say in the matter, and found ourselves in the field on a day when a bit of first use of the bat might have suited us rather better. West Farleigh crawled and occasionally cantered to 135 for 8 off their 42 overs, Ed DeLucy the chief architect with 40, Glen Aukett chipping in a useful 27, and a stand of 63 for the second wicket doing the real damage after an early wobble at 8 for 1. Credit where it's due: Fred Cheesman was excellent with 3 for 20, Ian Brattle miserly and probing for 3 for 33 with four maidens to his name, Mick Beeney back to the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and I picked up a wicket myself for 15 runs, which I shall claim as a highlight of an otherwise chastening afternoon.

With 136 needed, Clive Newman's 28 and Dave Charlton's 20 gave us something to build on, but building has never been our strong suit — 102 for 6 became 109 for 9 with the kind of speed usually reserved for the tea urn, Matt Large's duck a particularly grim footnote, and we folded for 117 in 38.3 overs.

A word of pride amid the wreckage: Ian Brattle's three wickets nudge him up to 14th on the club's all-time list, and my own season's run tally ticks along nicely at 1050. Beaten by 18 runs. We'll be better for it next week — we rather have to be.

Sat 18 Sep 1993 🧢 2nd XIv RC Rainham (Kent) CC · HomeformLDDWW

Dear Members,

Rainham (Kent) arrived on the 18th, won the toss, and – showing not a flicker of sentiment – put us into the field first. Alfie Fisher then proceeded to remind us why hospitality can be a mug's game, compiling a superb unbeaten 124, with George Foote (46) offering sturdy support in a second-wicket stand of 126 that rather took the game away from us for a spell. Credit where due: Don Dansie kept plugging away for 2-61, Daniel Thirkell chipped in with 1-22, and Jon Harris bowled with the miserly discipline of a man counting his own teabags, nine maidens and 1-38 to show for it. They declared us down to 205/4 from their 54 overs.

Chasing 206, we were steady rather than swashbuckling, closing on 135/5 from 44 overs. Ron McCormick anchored proceedings nicely with an unbeaten 45, Don Dansie added a brisk 23 on top of his bowling shift, and I contributed a watchful 21 not out, doing my best impression of a man in no hurry whatsoever – though poor Paul Clayton will have less fond memories, back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers.

So, honours shared, a draw when neither side could quite land the killer blow. Fisher's century will be talked about long after our modest chase is forgotten, but character was shown, wickets were shared about, and nobody's tea went cold. A fair result, all told, and one we'll happily settle for against a side batting like that.

Sat 11 Sep 1993 🧢 1st XIv High Halstow CC · HomeformWWWDD

Saturday 11 September 1993, and a rare thing occurred: I called correctly at the toss. Even rarer, I knew exactly what to do with it — no umming, no consulting the senior pros, straight up and bat. High Halstow CC were the visitors, and by close of play they'll have wished I'd fluffed the call.

David Castle set about proceedings as if he had all afternoon, which, as it turned out, he very nearly did — 96 not out, the innings built squarely on his shoulders, with Clive Newman's fifty and Dave Charlton's 40 lending sturdy support. The openers put on 117 before anyone troubled the scorers, which rather set the tone, and even our extras column turned in a handy 27, as if embarrassed to be left out. We closed on 220/2 from 49.2 overs — a total that had High Halstow's bowlers reaching for the liniment.

In the field, the lads were merciless. Alan Boyns led the charge with 3-9, Jon Harris chipped in 3-14 with four maidens of the miserly sort that sap a batting side's will to live, and Dave Todd took 2-36. Poor Chris Dockwray departed for a duck without so much as troubling the scorers, and High Halstow slid from 91-6 to 97 all out, Tony Wallace's battling 38 not out the only real resistance.

We won by 123 runs. And a word for the mantelpiece: David Castle brought up 10,000 career runs for the club during that innings — the sort of milestone that puts a lump in an old skipper's throat. A very good day indeed.

Wed 08 Sep 1993 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformLWWDD

Wednesday last, we set off for The Mote in reasonably good spirits, which in hindsight was our first tactical error of the day. Lost the toss, got put in to field first, and I confess a small part of me was relieved to delay the reckoning with the bat.

That relief didn't last. We were bowled out for 97 in 45.1 overs, an innings that never quite found its rhythm, and featured golden ducks for both Adrian Caddick and Richard Piper — two for the price of nought, as it were. It's the sort of scoreline that has you studying your bootlaces rather awkwardly during the tea interval.

With the ball we at least showed some fight, but The Mote's openers simply had other ideas. Steve Tunnicliffe cruised to 49 not out, with A Baxter keeping him company on 45 not out, and between them they knocked off 98 without loss in a brisk 18 overs — over before some of us had finished our second cup of tea. Elsewhere, credit where due: Mike Caddick continues to lead the 1993 Midweek XI run charts with 461 for the season, a rare bright spot to cling to on the drive home.

Ten wickets is a proper hiding by anyone's measure, and there's no dressing it up. Heads up, though, lads — back to the nets, and better luck at the next fixture.
