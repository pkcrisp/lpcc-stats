🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 27 September 1969 🦆

3 ducks
Week 23 · 27 Sep 1969
One match, one queue for the crease, and Mote A did the honours as the LPCC 1st XI supplied a full house of the season's dying embers. 🏏 1st XI v Mote A Terry Whittaker came in at five and departed bowled by M. Taylor without scoring — his 3rd duck of the season and a hefty 29th for the career, good enough for 10th on the all-time roll of shame from 257 innings. He remains a paid-up member of the exclusive Retirement Duck bench, and this one extends his long-running Quacking Together bond with the very next man in — Brian Relf below, proof the two were made to fall together. Spookily, this very calendar date has previous: on 27 September 1964, Robert Murray also trudged off for nought against Linton Park CC, one of the 17 he left behind across 1958–1965. Brian Relf followed at ten, caught by A. Levick off A. Baker, also his 3rd of the season and his 24th career duck — a startling return from just 57 innings, and 12th all time. A founder member of the Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack societies, and now further cementing his Quacking Together partnership with Whittaker above. Like his opening-day comrade, he too has 1964 for company — Robert Murray's duck fell on this same date, against this same fixture's ancestral rival. Jon Harris closed the innings at eleven, caught Levick bowled Tom Osborn, his 1st duck of the season but a well-worn 6th of the career from just 20 innings — 65th on the all-time list. Harris carries one of the heavier honours boards going: Full House for a duck at every position 1 to 11, plus top-ten billing in Lone Duck, Tourist Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road, with Quacking Together ties to both Jim Hall and Trevor Back. A chip off the old block too — his forebear Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own. And yes, 1964 strikes a third time: Robert Murray, same day, same fate, against Linton Park CC. 📅 Week in Summary Three ducks from one match, the entire haul supplied by the 1st XI at the hands of Mote A. Two of the three shared a bat-together, quack-together bond, and all three fell on a date that has apparently had it in for Linton batsmen since at least 1964. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Percy Brattle — 5 ducks in 8 innings (62.5%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks in 13 innings (38.46%) 3. Basil Sears — 4 ducks in 16 innings (25.0%) 4. Paul Brattle — 3 ducks in 6 innings (50.0%) 5. Brian Relf — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.86%) Brian Relf's afternoon at Mote A wasn't just a career milestone — it keeps him lodged firmly in this season's top five. The chase for Duck of the Year continues. Next fixture, next queue at the crease — the Duck Club never closes its books. See you all out there. 🍩0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — 13-14 September 1969

1 duck
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1969 – 14 Sep 1969
A quiet weekend for the hunting fraternity, but never let it be said the Duck Club goes hungry for long — St Lukes Maidstone CC made sure of that. 🏏 Saturday 13th September — 1st XI vs St Lukes Maidstone CC Coming in at number 8, Derry Evans had barely settled before the fielders conspired against him — run out amid enough confusion that nobody present could quite agree whose call it was. The purest kind of duck: not bowled, not caught, just quietly undone by geometry and a misplaced "yes." It's Derry's 3rd duck of the season and the 8th of a career now spanning 35 innings — a duck rate nudging 23%, and enough to slot him in at 47th of 846 in the all-time reckoning. Distinguished company for a run-out. And rather splendidly, the calendar obliges: on this very day back in 1959, John Ames also trooped back for nought, against Tunbridge Wells CC. Ames went on to finish with 42 career ducks — 3rd of 846 all-time — across a Linton career running from 1952 to 1969, part of a four-in-a-row Ducks in a Row spell and a fixture of the Homebody Duck top ten. Derry keeps fine historical company on this date, even if the company keeps rather better figures. 🌱 Sunday 14th September The second fixture of the weekend passed off entirely fowl-free — eleven players in, eleven players off the mark. The ducks, it seems, took the day off. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, one duck, and a single name added to September's tally. Not a vintage weekend for the collectors among us, but quality over quantity — a run-out duck married to a genuine on-this-day echo from a decade prior is the sort of coincidence the honours board was built for. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Percy Brattle — 5 ducks / 8 innings (62.5%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks / 12 innings (41.67%) 3. Basil Sears — 4 ducks / 15 innings (26.67%) 4. Paul Brattle — 3 ducks / 6 innings (50.0%) 5. Derry Evans — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) Percy Brattle still sets the pace, but with Derry Evans now firmly inside the top five and the season's business far from concluded, there's every chance the standings get another shuffle before autumn draws in. 🍩 Next fixture, same exclusive zero club awaits. Bat well, or don't — either way, we'll be watching. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 6th & 7th September 1969 🦆

8 ducks
Week 20 · 6 Sep 1969 – 7 Sep 1969
A vintage weekend for the exclusive zero club, as the 1st XI alone served up eight ducks across two days — proper triumph through adversity. 🏏 Saturday, vs A.Trembeths (h) Paul Bowles opened the misery, bowled by I. Foster, for his 2nd duck of the season and 19th of a storied career — 125 innings, 16th on the all-time list. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966, 1968) and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, he remains one half of Only Two Quacks with Adam Pooley. On this very day in 1967, William Buck was suffering identically against Birchington CC, en route to 28. Derry Evans went the same way, bowled by B. Lewis — his 2nd of the season, 7th career (53rd all-time). Back in 1953, Gordon Fenner made the same walk against Gillingham CC, Kent, on his way to 33. Tim Thirkell, of Full House and Lone Duck top 10 pedigree, was caught by R. Friend off A. Trembeth for his 5th duck of the season — 6th career, from just 20 innings, 66th all-time. His father John tops the entire club charts on 65, so the talent is clearly hereditary. In 1953, Richard Harris trod the same path against Gillingham CC, Kent, en route to 36. Straight after, brother Nigel Thirkell was run out for nought — his first career duck from only 4 innings, and a proper Two of a Quack alongside Tim. In 1958, John Ames was doing likewise against Tovil, on his way to 42. Closing the innings, Paul Brattle was bowled by B. Lewis — 2nd of the season, 2nd of a young career, and a paid-up Debutante Duck club member from his very first knock. Roger Craddock made the identical journey on this day in 1967, against Birchington CC. 🏏 Sunday, vs Anchorians CC (h) Benny Bell was trapped lbw by Jim Fish — 3rd of the season, 15th career duck, 23rd all-time from 56 innings. Cliff Anscombe fell to these very Anchorians in 1958, en route to 17. Then the day's showpiece: Percy Brattle and Paul Brattle, both bowled by K. Brown in the same innings — a proper Family of Ducks for the Brattles. Percy's was his 5th of the season, 13th career (29th all-time), and keeps him squarely in the hunt for Duck of the Year 1969. Alan Wilkes fell to these same Anchorians back in 1958, on his way to 20. For Paul it was duck number two of the weekend — his 3rd career, and a grim pair to file alongside Saturday's effort 🍩🍩. Basil Sears trod similar ground on this date in 1968, against A.Trembeths. 📋 Week in Summary Eight ducks, two matches, one very industrious 1st XI. The Brattles supplied three between them, capped by a shared Family of Ducks moment, while the Thirkell siblings doubled up in the very same over. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Percy Brattle — 5 ducks / 8 inns (62.5%) - Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks / 12 inns (41.7%) - Basil Sears — 4 ducks / 14 inns (28.6%) - Paul Brattle — 3 ducks / 6 inns (50%) - Benny Bell — 3 ducks / 14 inns (21.4%) Percy and Tim locked together on five apiece — the race for the 1969 crown goes to the wire. Next fixture awaits, and the zero club never rests. See you all in the middle 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 30 August to 1 September 1969

5 ducks
Week 19 · 30 Aug 1969 – 1 Sep 1969
Three fixtures, five ducks, and Linton Park's top order determined to make the last weekend of the season one to remember, if not to boast about. 🏏 Saturday 30th — Whitstable CC (away) Basil Sears got the weekend's proceedings underway, caught behind off D. Barham without scoring — his third duck of the summer and 28th for the club, sitting 8th in the all-time standings. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1965, Frank Brattle (1937–1969, 34 ducks, all-time rank 6) also strolled back against Bearsted CC. Some things simply happen on the 30th of August. 🏏 Sunday 31st — Staplehurst CC (away) Basil wasn't finished. Bowled by P. Holland for another blank, he brings up a grim pair for the weekend — season duck number four, and 29th for the career — two matches, two failures, not a run between them. On this same date in 1952, none other than John Thirkell, the club's all-time record holder with 65 career ducks and four-time Duck of the Year, made his own nought — good company for misery. He had company of his own in the confessional. Tim Thirkell — a name that runs in the family, kin to that very same John Thirkell — was run out for nought at number 7, his fourth duck of the season from just 11 innings and 5th for the career. Tim already holds a Full House (a duck at every position, 1 through 11) and features on the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens — a proper connoisseur of the art of not scoring. 🏏 Monday 1st — Bearsted CC (away) Keith Walker joined the club for the first time in 15 innings, bowled by Dave Bromley — his maiden duck of the season and of his career alike. On this same date in 1968, twice Duck of the Year Paul Bowles (18 career ducks) made an identical trip back to the pavilion against Staplehurst CC. And a debut to remember for other reasons: R- Pocock, caught behind off Ian Lambert first time out, goes straight onto the Debutante Duck board 🍩. He's now only one more failure away from the rare and inauspicious double of One and Done and Retirement Duck — though the club sincerely hopes to see rather more of him yet. Like Keith, he shares his nought with Paul Bowles' 1968 vintage. 📊 Week in Summary Five ducks 0️⃣ from three matches, with Basil Sears doing the work of two men — quite literally, given the pair — and Sunday proving the cruellest day of the lot, claiming both a Sears and a Thirkell. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Percy Brattle — 4 ducks, 7 innings (57%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 4 ducks, 11 innings (36%) 3. Basil Sears — 4 ducks, 14 innings (29%) 4. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks, 22 innings (14%) 5. Brian Fuller — 2 ducks, 5 innings (40%) Three men level on four apiece at the top of the table — a season race finely poised. Next weekend's fixtures await, and the Duck Club, as ever, will be watching closely. 🦆

Two fixtures out on the paddock this weekend, and for once the exclusive zero club has nothing to show for it. 🦆 Duck News — 16-17 August 1969

3 ducks
Week 17 · 16 Aug 1969 – 17 Aug 1969
A short but perfectly formed weekend for the exclusive zero club, with Linton's 1st XI doing all the honours: three ducks across two fixtures at home to Bowaters CC and Mote A. Quality over quantity, as the man said. Saturday 16 August — v Bowaters CC Andrew Moss departed at first drop, caught Alf Jewiss off Peter Sivyer, for his maiden duck of the campaign — and, from 30 career innings, his very first for the club, landing him 211th of 845 in the all-time table. A paid-up if premature member of the Retirement Duck honours board, though nobody's suggesting he's going anywhere just yet. On this very day in 1953, John Ames was doing much the same, walking back for nought — Ames went on to 42 career ducks and 3rd all-time, so Andrew has some catching up to do. Down at eight, Tim Thirkell fell lbw to Trevor Mannering, his 3rd duck of the season and 4th in just 18 career innings — 96th of 845 and climbing. Tim already carries handsome silverware: Full House (a duck at every position 1-11) and top-ten placings in both the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck charts. The family name is no stranger to this honours board either — kinsman John Thirkell sits at Number One all-time with 65 career ducks, a mark Tim can only dream of troubling. And, as with Andrew, 16 August 1953 also produced a John Ames special — the coincidence department is working overtime this week. Sunday 17 August — v Mote A Percy Brattle was bowled by C- Robbins for his 4th duck of the season and 12th of his career, 33rd of 845 all-time — and with it he seizes the 1969 Duck of the Year crown outright, a title very much live and very much his to lose. Six innings, four ducks, a magnificent 66.67% strike rate of failure — the season's runaway leader in the art of not scoring. The Brattle name runs deep in Duck Club lore too: father Frank sits on 34 career ducks, with Paul Brattle also on the books with one. On this day in 1957, none other than John Thirkell — that same all-time record holder — made a duck against Bowaters CC. The ghosts of ducks past are clearly paying close attention this August. Week in summary: 3 ducks from 2 matches, the 1st XI cornering the entire market single-handedly. No pairs, no golden ducks, no family doubles this time — just honest, workmanlike collapses. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Percy Brattle — 4 ducks / 6 inns (66.67%) - Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks / 10 inns (30.0%) - Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks / 22 inns (13.64%) - Brian Fuller — 2 ducks / 5 inns (40.0%) - Brian Relf — 2 ducks / 6 inns (33.33%) Percy leads the chase for now, but the season's far from done — plenty of time yet for the chasing pack to strike. Same time next fixture, same dubious honour up for grabs. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 9 to 13 August 1969

5 ducks
Week 16 · 9 Aug 1969 – 13 Aug 1969
Five ducks fell to the exclusive zero club this week, across a Saturday fixture and a midweek gathering that turned into a proper Gravesend cluster. Saturday 9 August — 1st XI away to Headcorn CC Tim Thirkell was the lone senior casualty, trapped lbw by C. Smith for his second duck of the season and third of his career (17 innings, 129th of 845 all-time). Tim already holds down the Full House board (a duck at every position, 1 through 11), plus the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, and a Quacking Together entry with Jim Hall. Neatly, on this very day in 1964 his own father John Thirkell - the club's most decorated duck-maker ever with 65 - was also out for nought, against Harrietsham CC. The art of not scoring runs in the blood. Wednesday 13 August — Midweek XI away to Gravesend CC Four ducks in one innings, and Gravesend's bowlers won't have needed asking twice. Fittingly, all four fell on the anniversary of a 1960 duck against Bowaters CC by one Pat Hardwick (8 career ducks, Duck of the Year 1960) - this date has form. David Tolhurst opened his senior account the Duck Club way, caught and bowled by Mukul Lall without scoring on debut - instant, automatic membership of the Debutante Duck board. Welcome to the family, David. Terry Whittaker went next, caught behind off Graham Hinks for his second duck of the season and 28th of a long career (247 innings, 9th of 845 all-time) - level with club great William Buck on that tally. Terry carries a Quacking Together entry with Brian Relf, and the historic, entirely presumptive Retirement Duck board from a past farewell. Ron Hickman was bowled by Michael Sampson for his second duck of the season and 12th of his career from just 25 innings. Ron holds a Quacking Together honour with Alan Cradduck, and the infamous Runaway Duck board from a barren 1965. Percy Brattle rounded things off, caught by Peter Beynon off Graham Hinks for his third duck of the campaign, moving to 11 for his career (65 innings, 36th of 845). He isn't fighting alone - father Frank Brattle carries 34 career ducks, with young Paul chipping in one more. With three already this season, Percy currently holds the Duck of the Year 1969 crown. Week in summary Three matches, five ducks, and one crowded Gravesend innings that did the season's tally no favours. The 1st XI kept it tidy with a single casualty; the Midweek XI let the side down in the finest traditions of the club. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Percy Brattle — 3 ducks in 5 innings (60%) - Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks in 22 innings (13.6%) - Brian Fuller — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40%) - Brian Relf — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40%) - Ron Hickman — 2 ducks in 6 innings (33.3%) Percy leads the chase, but there's a season yet to run for a challenger to emerge. Next fixture, same invitation as ever: bring your bat, and your sense of humour. The Duck Club never sleeps. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 26-27 July 1969

3 ducks

Sibton Park CC came calling this weekend and left with rather more of the 1st XI's tail than anyone would like to admit. 🏏 Saturday — Sibton Park CC (away) The innings finished in spectacular style as numbers 9, 10 and 11 trooped out and straight back in again — a full tail-end clean sweep, three ducks in short order, mercifully by more than three actual balls given there was a run out in the mix. First to go was Brian Relf, bowled by B- Fletcher for his 2nd duck of the season and 23rd of a long and storied career — good enough for 12th on the all-time list of 841. Brian remains a fixture of several honours boards: a Debutante Duck from his earliest days, one half of Quack-to-Quack and Quacking Together alongside Terry Whittaker, and — best of all — a presumptive Retirement Duck holder from a farewell that, 54 innings later, evidently wasn't final in the slightest. On this very day back in 1940, J Short was doing much the same thing against Boughton Monchelsea CC — some traditions simply endure. Next in, next out, was Ian Cridland, bowled by G Keutenius — a genuine, bona fide debut duck, his very first senior innings ending in the most traditional way possible. Ian is inducted on the spot to the Debutante Duck club and 1969's own One and Done register, a career currently standing at a perfectly symmetrical 1 duck from 1 innings. J Short, on that same day in 1940 against Boughton Monchelsea CC, would know exactly how he feels. Completing the collapse, Brian Fuller was run out — "Unsure" being a fittingly named accomplice — for his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of his career, sitting 78th all-time. J Short's 1940 nought against Boughton Monchelsea CC rounds off a hat-trick of identical parallels this week — clearly a big day for ducks down the decades. 🌱 Sunday The second fixture passed off entirely duck-free — the 1st XI presumably resting on their considerable laurels after Saturday's tail-enders' trilogy. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, and all three fell in the very same innings — the last three men in going in a row is not quite a pair, and certainly no king pair, but it is a tidy reminder that the tail remains the club's most dependable source of noughts. 🦆 Duck Report — 20 July 1969

1 duck
Week 13 · 20 Jul 1969
