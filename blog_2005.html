🦆 Duck News — 24-25 September 2005

0 ducks
Week 23 · 24 Sep 2005 – 25 Sep 2005
Two matches came and went this weekend and, remarkably, not a single Linton batsman could manage to get out for nought. The zero club sat empty, the honours board gathered no new names, and the Duck Hunting Season's most prolific marksmen were, for once, made to look rather ordinary at their trade. A lament, frankly. Somewhere out there a duck escaped the net, and it will not happen again in a hurry. 🍩 The Week That Wasn't Both fixtures passed off without incident — bat met ball, runs were accumulated, and the art of not scoring took an unscheduled weekend off. No pairs, no kings, no family affairs to report. Even the leaderboard's usual suspects were denied their weekly top-up. A quiet weekend for the club, but a loud one for anyone hoping to see history made. Still, as any seasoned member of the Duck Club will tell you, form is temporary — the zero is eternal. There is always next Saturday. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Christopher Thirkell — 9 ducks in 27 innings (33.33%) 2. Gareth Davies — 5 ducks in 11 innings (45.45%) — the division's most efficient marksman by some distance 3. Richard Piper — 5 ducks in 37 innings (13.51%) 4. Chris Newman — 4 ducks in 13 innings (30.77%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.00%) Gareth Davies remains the man to watch on sheer strike rate — a near coin-flip chance of a duck every time he walks out, which is either alarming or admirable depending on your allegiance. Christopher Thirkell holds the volume crown for now, but with Simon also flying the Thirkell colours, this could yet become a family monopoly on the season's top honours. 📅 Looking Ahead The ducks evaded capture this week, but they always resurface. Get your fixtures in, keep an eye on those top-order nerves, and let's see who's first to add to the tally next time out. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 0️⃣🏏🦆

🦆 Duck News — 17-21 September 2005 🦆

0 ducks
Week 22 · 17 Sep 2005 – 21 Sep 2005
A lament, if you will permit one. Three matches played across the closing days of the season, and not a single duck to show for it. Eleven-plus batsmen per side, all of them, apparently, minded to trouble the scorers. The Duck Club committee has convened in emergency session and can confirm: the exclusive zero club held no inductions this week. The art of not scoring, it seems, took a rare Saturday off. Whether this represents a shocking outbreak of competence across the club or simply a run of very forgiving bowling attacks, the record does not say. What it does say is that for three whole fixtures, every single dismissal carried the indignity of at least one run against the batsman's name. Frankly, un-Linton-like behaviour, and the committee trusts normal service resumes shortly. Still - a quiet week for the hunters is not a wasted one. The season standings remain fiercely contested, and with the campaign drawing to a close there's still time for the also-rans to make a late run at glory. 🍩 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🍩 1. Christopher Thirkell — 9 ducks from 27 innings (33.3%) 2. Gareth Davies — 5 ducks from 11 innings (45.5%) — the division's most efficient collector, pound for pound 3. Richard Piper — 5 ducks from 35 innings (14.3%) 4. Chris Newman — 4 ducks from 13 innings (30.8%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks from 12 innings (25.0%) Gareth Davies, it should be noted, is putting in a serious shift at the top of the percentages - a 45% strike rate for nought is the kind of consistency the club exists to honour. Meanwhile the Thirkell name holds down two of the top five spots, which either speaks to a family gene for the golden zero or simply a lot of overs faced. The committee makes no further comment. 📅 Onward The season isn't done yet, and neither is the hunt. Somewhere out there a batsman is shouldering arms to one that clips the top of off, or shuffling across their stumps for a plumb lbw first ball. We simply await the paperwork. Next fixture, normal service expected to resume. The ducks, we are reliably informed, have merely gone to ground - not extinct. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 10–11 September 2005

0 ducks
Week 21 · 10 Sep 2005 – 11 Sep 2005
Two fixtures. Zero ducks. The Duck Club regrets to announce that, for one weekend only, Linton Park batted like a side that had actually turned up to score runs. It happens. Even the most reliable poultry farm has a fallow week, and this was it — 22 (or so) trips to the crease across Saturday and Sunday and not a single nought among them. No golden ducks, no king pairs, no family gatherings on the honours board, nothing for the scorers to smirk about. The exclusive zero club held no inductions this week, and its existing membership can rest easy in their positions on the all-time boards a little longer. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or simply a temporary evasion of fate, only next weekend will tell. The ducks are out there. They are, this week, merely uncaught. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏆 Standings remain as they were, and what standings they are: 1. Christopher Thirkell — 9 ducks from 27 innings (33.3%) 🥇 2. Gareth Davies — 5 ducks from 10 innings (50.0%) — a genuinely alarming coin-flip of a season 3. Richard Piper — 5 ducks from 33 innings (15.15%) 4. Chris Newman — 4 ducks from 13 innings (30.77%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks from 12 innings (25.0%) Gareth Davies remains the season's great cautionary tale, converting one in every two innings into a duck with the grim efficiency of a man who has simply stopped looking at the bowler. Christopher Thirkell leads on volume, but the percentage race is Gareth's to lose. So there we are — a quiet week for the Duck Club, but the season is far from settled and the honours board never sleeps for long. Next fixture, normal service will surely resume. Bats at the ready, and may the odds be ever un-favourable. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 3-4 September 2005 🦆

1 duck
Week 20 · 3 Sep 2005 – 4 Sep 2005
A quiet weekend for the Zero Club — only one soul answered the call of the nought across three fixtures. Sparse pickings, but quality over quantity, as ever. Saturday 3 September 2nd XI v Lord Raglan (home) Number seven Chris Quirk strode out and returned scoreless — run out before he could get off the mark, the identity of the fielder lost to history ("run out Unsure" says it all, the scorebook shrugging along with the rest of us). The Zero Club welcomes him regardless of the manner of his despatch. This is Chris's first duck for the club — his 1st of the season and 1st of what may prove a long and distinguished career of noughts — arriving in only his second senior innings. That's rank 391 of 1314 in the all-time standings, plenty of room to climb from here. He already carries a place on the Retirement Duck board among his colours, a badge for the ages rather than any comment on his current form. On this very day back in 1972, a young Benny Bell was doing exactly the same thing against Staplehurst CC — the first of what became 30 ducks across a 1966-1975 career, good enough for 29th of 1314 all time. Some traditions never die. Elsewhere across the club's other two fixtures this weekend, the bowlers came up empty-handed and the batting order stayed frustratingly intact — a rare weekend where the ducks well and truly evaded capture. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Christopher Thirkell — 9 ducks / 26 innings (34.6%) - Gareth Davies — 5 ducks / 10 innings (50.0%) - Richard Piper — 5 ducks / 31 innings (16.1%) - Chris Newman — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.8%) - Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks / 11 innings (27.3%) Gareth Davies quietly posts the most alarming strike rate of the lot, but Christopher Thirkell's raw tally keeps him out in front for Duck of the Year. Next weekend brings more fixtures and, no doubt, more names for the honours board — get down to the ground and see who joins Chris in the exclusive zero club. 🏏🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 13–17 August 2005

2 ducks
Week 17 · 13 Aug 2005 – 17 Aug 2005
A modest haul this week, ladies and gentlemen. Three fixtures on the fixture list, and the exclusive zero club admitted just two new entries — both from the same afternoon, both to the same bowler. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏏 Sunday 1st XI — away to Harvel CC, 14th August Batting at first drop, Tom Motum departed for nought, bowled by Tom Emmitt. It's duck number 2 of the season for Tom, and his 2nd in a career now standing at 16 innings — placing him 321st of 1314 in the all-time roll of dishonour. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1988, club great Simon Thirkell (1980–2005, 51 career ducks, all-time rank 9) was also strolling back for nought, that time against Whitstable CC. Some anniversaries you don't choose. Two down at number four came Jack Jenner, also undone by Tom Emmitt — who was clearly in no mood to let anyone troouble the scorers. It's Jack's 2nd duck of the season and 4th of his career from 27 innings, good for 170th of 1314 all-time. Jack is also a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell innings, a standing honour rather than anything to do with today's dismissal — he's very much still opening the innings, not closing the book. And he too joins the on-this-day almanac alongside Simon Thirkell's 1988 vintage — a second reminder this week of what August the 14th can do to a batting line-up. 🔥 Elsewhere The rest of the club's fixtures passed off in disappointingly vegan fashion — no further fowl to report from either the Saturday sides or the other Sunday outing. Harvel CC and, specifically, the right arm of Tom Emmitt, can claim sole credit for this week's tally. 📊 Week in Summary Three matches, two ducks, one bowler doing all the damage. Not a vintage week for volume, but quality control was evidently high — both victims fell in the same over-ish spell, to the same man, in the same game. Make of that what you will. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Christopher Thirkell — 9 ducks / 26 innings (34.6%) 2. Gareth Davies — 5 ducks / 10 innings (50.0%) 3. Christopher Thirkell leads on volume and shows no sign of slowing. 🍩 Next fixture awaits, and with it, fresh opportunity for triumph through adversity. 🦆 Duck News — Week of 6–10 August 2005 🦆

6 ducks
Week 16 · 6 Aug 2005 – 10 Aug 2005
A tidy little haul this week: six ducks laid across three matches, with one fixture escaping entirely unscathed. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees below. Saturday 6th — 2nd XI v Town Malling CC Batting at 10, Dave Todd was snaffled behind, caught Brady bowled Simon Uttridge, for his 2nd duck of the season and 18th of a long and distinguished career — a tally that plants him at 56th of 1313 in the all-time roll of dishonour. On this very day in 1989, Dave Charlton was doing much the same against Chadwell Thors Oak CC, en route to 25 career ducks and 41st on the list. Some traditions simply refuse to die. Sunday 7th — Sunday 2nd XI v Selling CC Tom Motum, bowled by the splendidly initialled H-a-R- Alexander at first drop, recorded a rarer beast: his maiden career duck, arriving only after 15 quietly successful innings. Season duck number 1, career duck number 1 — the purest kind of debut, even if the scorebook insists it wasn't. On this day in 1976, Colin Dicker fell the same way against Headcorn CC, a man who'd go on to claim Duck of the Year honours in both 1973 and 1975. Tom, the bar has been set. Wednesday 10th — Midweek XI v The Mote CC A proper clatter here — four ducks in one innings, the kind of collapse the club secretly treasures. Opener Russell Baldwin fell first, caught Alex Boorman bowled Dane Smith, his 3rd of the season and 17th career duck (62nd all-time). This fixture, on this day in 1991, also claimed Paul Gibson, a paid-up member of the Platinum Duck club for once departing first ball of the game entirely — Russell's effort was comparatively leisurely. Adam Jackson followed, bowled Tom Parsons for his 2nd of the season and 6th career duck, joining the great and good at 125th all-time — a chip off the old block, with sibling Rob Jackson sitting on 13 career noughts of his own. 1991 also brought Simon Thirkell to grief in this fixture, a man who'd finish with 50 ducks before his 50th run and two Duck of the Year titles to his name. Mark Christmas was next, bowled Nicholas Daking, his 3rd of the season and 8th career duck (107th all-time) — a Full House nearly-man, having ducked at 10 of the 11 possible batting positions and surely eyeing off the last. Nick Stephens shares this fixture's history, a rare owner of the Quack-to-Quack double, debut and farewell both marked with a nought. And to complete the set, Matthew Carter fell lbw to the same Nicholas Daking, his 1st duck of the season but 12th of a career that already includes a Duck of the Year title in 2004 and paid-up Debutante Duck membership from years back. Back in 1958, Roy Clarke made his own contribution to this fixture's history, later crowned Duck of the Year in 1959. Week in summary Six ducks from four matches, with one fixture keeping its scorecard spotlessly fowl-free. Nicholas Daking can rightly claim a brace of his own this week, accounting for two of Midweek's four departures. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30.0%) Gareth Davies still boasts the division's most alarming strike rate — one duck every two innings. Can anyone catch him before season's end? See you all at the next fixture — the ducks are never far away. 🦆🏏0️⃣

Later in the same innings, Mark Christmas joined the queue, bowled by John Sawyer for nought — his 2nd duck of the season and 7th of his career. Mark can console himself with membership of the "Full House nearly-man" club, having managed ducks at 10 of the possible 11 batting positions — one shy of completing the set. Room for one more this season, perhaps. He shares his on-this-day company with the same Gareth Thomas — a doubly historic afternoon at Downham & Bellingham. 🕰️ Midweek XI v Bexley CC (2 August) Michael Beeney, in at 10, was caught by George Wells off Chris Woolley for his 1st duck of the season and 21st of his career — 52nd on the all-time rankings from a hefty 169 innings. His son Thomas already has a career duck to his name — the apple, evidently, not falling far. On this day in 1944, E- Humphreys was doing much the same against the National Fire Service Overseas Contingent, part of a brief 1941–1945 career and a single career duck to show for it. 📊 The Week in Brief Two matches, three ducks, and Downham & Bellingham providing the rare pleasure of a same-innings double. Nothing to trouble the Duck of the Year race just yet — but the season is young, and Linton's finest are only warming up. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. A charter member of the Debutante Duck society (his very first Linton knock was also a blob) and a Duck of the Year winner back in 2000, Stewart remains one 11th-position innings shy of completing the Full House. Sibling Phillip Morrison, meanwhile, watches the family average from a respectful 2 career ducks. 2nd XI — Christopher Thirkell was bowled by Thomas Judges for his 8th duck of the campaign, 20 innings in at a startling 40% strike rate for nought, all but crowning himself this year's Duck of the Year before August has even arrived. His 21st career duck moves him to 51st all-time, and heaven help the rest of the Thirkell dynasty trying to keep pace — John (90), Tim (78), Nigel (57), Simon (51) and Daniel (20) all still outrank him. Gordon Fenner, 1960 vintage, obliged with a duck of his own on this same day. Dennis Salisbury followed him back, caught behind off Jason Preston-Reeves, for his 2nd of the season and 8th career duck in just 39 innings — 103rd on the club's all-time roll of dishonour. Gordon Fenner, three ducks deep into this report already, was once more at it on this day in 1960. 🌱 Sunday, Blue Star CC Opener David Castle was caught Harper bowled Chenery for his 40th career duck from a marathon 691 innings — 17th all-time and a paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10. On this day back in 1993, Nick Froom (1989–1994) was likewise dismissed for nought against Kennington CC, Kent.
