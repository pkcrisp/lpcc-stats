🦆 Duck News — 22-23 September 1962

3 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1962 – 23 Sep 1962
The season's final furlongs and Linton Park's bowlers found the edge just often enough to keep the honours board ticking over. Three matches, three ducks, and not a golden one among them — a weekend of quietly professional failure. Saturday 22nd — 1st XI at Maidstone YMCA CC Jack Knowler fell for nought at number seven, bowled by E- Page. Only his second senior innings for the club, and he's already opened his OTHER account — a maiden career duck that lands him 265th of 769 all time. On this very day in 1951, John --'s Taylor was performing the identical trick against Hunton Wanderers CC, one of 15 ducks he'd amass across a decade-plus at the club (1949-1963, 16th of 769). Fine company for a first entry, Jack. Sunday 23rd — Married v Singles The club's oldest grudge match delivered its own casualties on both sides of the ledger. For the Married eleven, Terry Whittaker departed bowled David Gale — his second duck of the season and 11th for the club from 75 innings, 25th on the all-time list. Terry carries the Retirement Duck badge from a past farewell, and more topically, holds Quacking Together jointly with a certain Singles batsman. Sure enough, Brian Relf obliged at the other end, stumped Cliff Anscombe off the bowling of John Crellin, also his second of the season and his 15th career duck from just 30 innings — 15th of 769 all time. Brian is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack clubs from innings long past, but today was simply the pair renewing their shared honour in real time, Married and Singles united in nought. Both share the same on-this-day echo: 23 September 1961, Robert Wood fell against Bearsted CC in an identical fixture, one stop on the way to the 20 career ducks (6th of 769) he racked up between 1958 and 1962. Week in brief Three matches, three ducks, no golden ducks, no repeat offenders — just tidy, one-per-match departures from Maidstone to the Married v Singles decider. Robert Wood gets two mentions this week, once in the history books above and once below in the live chase. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - R Davison — 8 inns, 5 ducks (62.5%) - Basil Sears — 20 inns, 5 ducks (25%) - Bob Arnold — 14 inns, 3 ducks (21.43%) - Roy Clarke — 21 inns, 3 ducks (14.29%) - Robert Wood — 24 inns, 3 ducks (12.5%) R Davison sets the pace at a startling 62.5% — the art of not scoring, perfected. Basil Sears matches him on volume, and Robert Wood lurks in fifth having just featured twice above. 🍩 More cricket next weekend means more chances for the exclusive zero club to welcome new members, or for old hands to extend their leases. See you at the crease. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 15th & Sunday 16th September 1962 🦆

4 ducks
Week 21 · 15 Sep 1962 – 16 Sep 1962
A quiet weekend for the run-scorers but a busy one for the Duck Club ledger — four fresh entries recorded across two fixtures, home and away. 🏏 Saturday — v Bowaters CC (Home) Frank Brattle opened the innings and, alas, opened the Duck Club's account for the day too, bowled by Harry Jeffery without troubling the scorers. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 17th for the club in 157 innings, lifting him to a lofty 10th on the all-time chart. He now stands shoulder to shoulder with John Abnett, who reached the same 17 in a rather more efficient 44 innings across 1954–1961, and who owns a place on the "Ducks in a Row" board for four on the bounce. Duck-collecting evidently runs in the family too — relative Percy Brattle already carries 5 career noughts of his own. At number 8, Roy Clarke joined the fun, caught Robin Scott off the bowling of Bernard Collins. It's his 3rd duck of the season, enough to lodge him at 4th in the current Duck Hunting Season table, and his 25th career duck — good enough for an imperious 5th on the all-time list. Roy already holds the 1959 Duck of the Year title and a place on the "Quacking Together" board alongside the legendary John Ames, so this one merely burnishes an already glittering record of not scoring. His brother-in-arms H Clarke isn't far behind on 3 career ducks himself. 🌱 Sunday — v Staplehurst CC (Away) At Staplehurst, Richard Ashby fell for nought at number 6, run out in circumstances the scorebook diplomatically records as "unsure." It's his 2nd of the season and, remarkably, only his 2nd for the club in 12 innings — though he's already built a five-strong "Ducks in a Row" streak and a spot on the Tourist Duck top 10, evidently a man who saves his finest noughts for away grounds. Spookily, on this very day in 1961, Terry Whittaker also strolled back for a duck against Bowaters CC — history quietly repeating itself. Batting at 9, Brian Relf was stumped by Jack Eastland off A Thirkell, his 1st duck of the season and 14th of his career from just 29 innings, good for 16th all-time. Brian is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club — his very first Linton Park innings was a blank — and sits on the "Quacking Together" board with none other than Terry Whittaker, who, as it happens, is also Richard's parallel above. Whittaker, it seems, haunts this whole weekend without ever taking guard. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks, two matches, Bowaters CC and Staplehurst CC both leaving their mark on the ledger. Openers and middle order alike found their way back to the pavilion for nought — proof, if any were needed, that the art of not scoring respects no batting position. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - R Davison — 5 ducks in 7 innings (71.43%) - Basil Sears — 5 ducks in 18 innings (27.78%) - Bob Arnold — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) - Roy Clarke — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.0%) - Robert Wood — 3 ducks in 24 innings (12.5%) 🏏 Roll on the next fixture — the exclusive zero club never sleeps, and there's always room in the honours board for one more. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 DUCK NEWS — Weekend of 8-9 September 1962 🦆

4 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1962 – 9 Sep 1962
Saturday brought Linton Park's 1st XI face to face with St Lukes Maidstone CC, and the home top order and tail combined to stage what can only be described as a mass sacrifice to the Zero Club. Four ducks in one innings — a proper harvest. 🏏 1st XI v St Lukes Maidstone (home) Basil Sears opened and didn't trouble the scorers long, bowled by W. Summerfield. It's his 5th duck of the season and 15th for the club in 240 innings, holding a formidable #14 in the all-time standings — rarefied company for a man still very much active. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1945 Stan Wratten also strolled back for nought against 8th Sherwood Foresters, en route to 17 career ducks and a place at #11 all-time. At first drop, Robert Wood was trapped lbw by B. Pearce. His 3rd duck of the season nudges him to 20 for his career from 140 innings, an eye-watering #6 in the club's all-time charts — and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first knock, long before this week's effort. Like Basil, Robert shares his September 8th with Stan Wratten's 1945 escapade against the Sherwood Foresters. Down at nine, Bob Arnold became W. Summerfield's second victim of the innings, caught by M. Eastman. It's his 3rd of the season, 39th of a storied career spanning 179 innings, and keeps him lodged at a barely believable #2 in the all-time rankings — a proud member of the Ducks in a Row club (four on the spin), and half of two separate quacking partnerships, with Alan Wilkes and with John Thirkell. Bob too joins Stan Wratten's 1945 anniversary club. And at ten, Terry Whittaker was run out in a mix-up nobody could quite explain afterwards. A first duck of the season for Terry, his 10th career, from 73 innings, sitting at #27 all-time. He carries the Quacking Together honour with Brian Relf, and belongs to the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell innings — no danger of that today, he'll be back. Terry rounds out the day's quartet of Wratten anniversary companions. 🌱 Sunday A blank sheet — Sunday's fixture passed entirely duck-free. The club's bowlers elsewhere clearly didn't get the memo Summerfield and Pearce were working to. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, all crammed into one Saturday innings at St Lukes — top order and tail alike paying tribute. Summerfield claimed two of the four himself, a fine shift for the opposition, less fine for Linton's card. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 R Davison — 5 ducks, 7 innings (71.43%) 🥈 Basil Sears — 5 ducks, 17 innings (29.41%) 🥉 Bob Arnold — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25.0%) 4. Robert Wood — 3 ducks, 23 innings (13.04%) 5. Len Conway — 2 ducks, 6 innings (33.33%) Basil and Bob both add to season tallies that already had them in the top three — the race for Duck of the Year is very much alive. Next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, fresh recruits to the club nobody wants to join but everybody secretly admires. See you all out there. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 1 & 2 September 1962

1 duck
Week 19 · 1 Sep 1962 – 2 Sep 1962
A quiet weekend on the poultry front — just the two fixtures and a single duck to show for it. Sometimes the hunting season goes like that. Even the finest game keepers have their off days. Saturday 1 September 1st XI v A. Trembeths (home) Coming in at ten, Richard Harris departed caught Bowey, bowled Tootle — a tidy dismissal for the scorebook, if not for Richard. It's his first duck of the season, but the 36th of a long and storied career spanning 174 innings, and it plants him at a genuinely eye-watering 3rd in the entire 767-strong all-time standings. This is not a man dabbling in the art of not scoring — this is a master craftsman. And in a rather splendid quirk of the calendar: on this very day back in 1934, Bert Buckoke also walked back for nought, against Charles Arkcoll of all people. Buckoke, who turned out for Linton Park between 1928 and 1953, finished his career on 12 ducks and a highly respectable 19th all-time, with back-to-back Duck of the Year honours in 1928 and 1950 to his name. Twenty-eight years apart, same fixture window, same fate — the club's ducks have a long memory. Sunday's fixture, mercifully or otherwise, passed off entirely duck-free — eleven batsmen apiece got off the mark, denying the honours board any further custom. Week in Summary Two matches, one duck. Slim pickings by Linton Park standards, but Richard Harris made it count, climbing to within a whisker of the all-time podium. The rest of the club will need to pull their socks up — or rather, pull them down and get back in the hutch — if the season table is to move. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - R Davison — 5 ducks / 7 innings (71.43%) - Basil Sears — 4 ducks / 16 innings (25.0%) - Len Conway — 2 ducks / 6 innings (33.33%) - Alan Tucker — 2 ducks / 10 innings (20.0%) - Bob Arnold — 2 ducks / 11 innings (18.18%) R Davison sits well clear at the top with a frankly alarming 71% strike rate for nought — a genuine contender for Duck of the Year if this form holds. 🥇🍩 Next fixture looms, and with it, fresh opportunity for the zero club to welcome new blood or crown old hands. Keep your bats loose and your expectations low — see you all pitchside. 🦆0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 25th & 26th August 1962

3 ducks
Week 18 · 25 Aug 1962 – 26 Aug 1962
Two fixtures on the card this weekend, and only one troubled the scorers — the other passed by entirely fowl-free. But the 1st XI's home date with Sevenoaks Vine CC on the Sunday made up for it in style, with three names added to the ledger in the very same innings, courtesy of just two Vine bowlers. The art of not scoring, alive and well at Linton Park. 1st XI v Sevenoaks Vine CC (home, 26th August) John Ames opened the afternoon's misery, bowled by Cliff Judd for his 2nd duck of the season and 27th of an increasingly distinguished career — 193 innings in and still holding 4th place in the all-time standings, rarefied company indeed. Ames already carries a handsome collection of honours: four ducks in a row at one stage, a fixture on the Homebody Duck top ten, and paired-quack history on the books with both Alan Wilkes and Stan Wratten. On this very day back in 1945, Herbert Rayner (1939–1948, 6 ducks, 48th all-time) was making his own contribution to the calendar against 1st Innings Fifth Leicester — Ames keeps that anniversary ticking over, eighty-one years on. Eddie Lawrence followed, caught Rodney Crang bowled R Nash, for a 1st duck of the season and 7th of his career from 39 innings — good enough for 39th on the all-time list. He shares this very Rayner anniversary with Ames, proof that some dates are simply cursed. And then Roy Clarke, undone by the same Cliff Judd delivery that had already claimed Ames, for his 2nd of the season and 24th of his career — 150 innings deep and 5th on the all-time roll of dishonour, one place behind Ames himself. Clarke still wears the 1959 Duck of the Year crown, and his "Quacking Together" pairing with Ames, usually a quiet historical footnote, was very much reactivated this week — the two of them walking off to the same bowler in the same over. A word too for sibling H Clarke, a fellow paid-up member of the family firm on three career ducks. Clarke's anniversary company, once again, is Herbert Rayner. The Week in Numbers Three ducks from two matches, the lot of them delivered by Sevenoaks Vine's opening attack — Cliff Judd claiming two outright. A fine afternoon's work for the bowler, considerably less fine for Linton's middle order. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. R Davison — 5 ducks from 6 innings (83%) 2. Basil Sears — 4 ducks from 16 innings (25%) 3. Len Conway — 2 ducks from 6 innings (33%) 4. Alan Tucker — 2 ducks from 10 innings (20%) 5. Bob Arnold — 2 ducks from 10 innings (20%) Davison leads by a distance — at 83% he is scarcely troubling the scorers at all this season. Roll on the next fixture. The ducks never take a week off, and neither do we. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 18–19 August 1962

3 ducks
Week 17 · 18 Aug 1962 – 19 Aug 1962
Another weekend, another trio of noughts added to the Linton ledger — the 1st XI doing all the heavy lifting this time out, home and away, and the exclusive zero club welcoming its newest inductees with the usual solemn ceremony. 🏏 Saturday 18th — away to Bowaters CC Basil Sears opened the innings and opened the scoring column with nothing at all, bowled by Edward Chick. It's the 4th duck of Basil's season and the 14th of a long and storied career — 238 innings deep and now sitting at a lofty 15th in the all-time charts. Rather splendidly, on this very day back in 1956, none other than club great John Thirkell also fell for nought against these same Bowaters opponents. Thirkell, of course, sits imperiously atop the all-time standings with 46 career ducks and three Duck of the Year titles to his name (1952, 1954 and 1955) — proof that even the very best occasionally forget how the bat works. Not to be left out, R Davison joined the party at number 7, lbw to Roy Ecclestone. That's duck number 5 of the season for Davison, and — since career and season tallies are one and the same at this early stage — 5 for the career too, in just 17 innings. A fearsome 83% strike rate for nought that puts him top of this season's leaderboard, and rightly so: the Duck of the Year 1962 board already has his name inked in as the man to catch. Davison, too, shares his day with the ghost of Thirkell's 1956 vintage — Bowaters CC clearly a happy hunting ground for the art of not scoring. 🌱 Sunday 19th — home to The Mote CC Robert Murray kept the family tradition alive at number 4, bowled by Phil Briant for his 2nd duck of the season and 13th of his career, moving him to 18th on the all-time list from 130 innings. And in a pleasing bit of symmetry, on this exact date in 1956 one John --'s Taylor also departed for nought against Mote A — Taylor's own career yielded 15 ducks between 1949 and 1960, now just two ahead of where Murray currently stands. 📊 Week in summary Three ducks, two matches, one very generous weekend for the opposition bowlers. Bowaters CC alone accounted for two of the three — a proper harvest for a single fixture — while the Mote added a solitary but no less cherished contribution on Sunday. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - R Davison — 6 innings, 5 ducks, 83.3% - Basil Sears — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.7% - Len Conway — 6 innings, 2 ducks, 33.3% - Bob Arnold — 9 innings, 2 ducks, 22.2% - Alan Tucker — 10 innings, 2 ducks, 20.0% R Davison sets the pace by some distance, but there's plenty of season left for the chasing pack to make their move. Bats up next weekend, gentlemen — the zero club is always recruiting. 🦆🏏0️⃣

Some things really do happen on schedule. 🦆 Duck Report — 14–15 July 1962

3 ducks
Week 12 · 14 Jul 1962 – 15 Jul 1962
