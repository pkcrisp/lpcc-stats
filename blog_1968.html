🦆 Duck Report — 21 September 1968 🦆

2 ducks
Week 22 · 21 Sep 1968
A quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club, but two names still queued up patiently for their certificate of dubious distinction. 1st XI v West Farleigh CC Opening the innings and opening the account for nought, Frank Brattle departed c Trevor Fermor b Ron Kemp — his 1st duck of the season and 32nd of a long and storied career, good enough for 7th on the all-time list of 837. On this very day back in 1958, Stan Wratten (1943–1968, 28 career ducks, a paid-up member of the Quacking Together honours board) also strolled back for nought against Tilling Steven — Frank keeps fine historical company. A nod too to the bloodline: kinsman Percy Brattle carries 8 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring evidently runs in the family. Batting at 10 and clearly saving the best for last, Derry Evans fell c Micky Back b Reg Oldfield for his 1st duck of the season, his 5th career nought from 25 innings, sitting 67th of 837 all-time. And remarkably, on this very same date in 1958, Stan Wratten was already writing exactly this chapter of duck history — some calendar dates simply attract the golden thread of failure. Week in Summary Just the one fixture, just the two ducks — a modest haul by Linton's usual standards, but every nought still swells the greater ledger. West Farleigh CC may consider themselves honorary co-conspirators in this week's proceedings. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Paul Bowles — 7 ducks / 33 inns (21%) - Benny Bell — 5 ducks / 12 inns (42%) - Gordon Fenner — 4 ducks / 9 inns (44%) - Terry Whittaker — 4 ducks / 29 inns (14%) - Louis Buels — 3 ducks / 4 inns (75%) Special mention to Louis Buels, whose 75% duck rate remains a masterclass in triumph through adversity — three innings out of every four ending in the long walk back. 🏏 Fixtures continue apace — who'll be adding their name to the honours board next time out? We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 14 September 1968

0 ducks
Week 21 · 14 Sep 1968
A single fixture out on the park this week, and for once the exclusive zero club had absolutely nothing to show for its trouble. Not one nought. Not so much as a golden glint of a first-baller. Eleven men went to the crease, presumably, and eleven men are reported to have troubled the scorer in some fashion. 🍩 A Quiet Week for the Quackers It happens. Even the most dedicated hunter has an off day, and this week the ducks evaded capture entirely — scattering into the reeds the moment bat met ball. Somewhere, the honours board breathes a small sigh of relief. The rest of us are left twiddling our thumbs, scanning the scorecards for a nought that simply isn't there. Let it be known this is not cause for celebration in the traditional sense. The art of not scoring is a proud Linton tradition and one match without a single contribution to it is, frankly, a wasted opportunity. We expect better application next time out. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Paul Bowles — 32 innings, 7 ducks, 21.88% - Benny Bell — 12 innings, 5 ducks, 41.67% - Gordon Fenner — 9 innings, 4 ducks, 44.44% - Terry Whittaker — 28 innings, 4 ducks, 14.29% - Louis Buels — 4 innings, 3 ducks, 75.00% 0️⃣ Louis Buels continues to set the pace on sheer percentage alone, a rate that suggests either extraordinary misfortune or a genuine gift for the craft. Meanwhile Paul Bowles holds the outright lead on raw tally and will not thank anyone for a quiet week interrupting his rhythm. 🥇 Onwards The season presses on, and with it the certainty that somewhere out there a duck is simply biding its time. Get out to the next fixture, take guard, and do your bit for the club's oldest and most dubious tradition. The zero club never sleeps for long. 🦆

5 ducks

A double dose of Trembeth and Anchorian trouble left the exclusive zero club five members richer this weekend, every single one of them from the 1st XI. Consistency of a very particular kind. Saturday 7th — v A. Trembeths (h) - Basil Sears opened and departed just as promptly, c L. Fell b J. Mortlock. His 3rd duck of the season and 25th of a mighty 350-innings career, good enough for 11th on the all-time roll of dishonour. Splendidly, on this very day in 1963 club stalwart Frank Brattle (1937–1968, 31 ducks, 7th all-time) also strolled back for nought. - Mike Iliffe followed straight in, c R. Tottman b A. Trembeth — his 3rd of the season and 9th career, keeping him at 43rd all-time from just 51 innings. He too shares this date in duck history with the great Frank Brattle. - Terry Whittaker went the same way at four down, c J. Norris b J. Mortlock, his 4th duck of the season and 26th career — 10th on the all-time list. A long-serving name on the Quacking Together board alongside Brian Relf, and yet another Linton man keeping company with Frank Brattle on this September date in 1963. - Jon Harris, batting at nine, c J. Mortlock b G. Davis for his 1st duck of the season and 5th career, joins the honours board pantheon as a proud Full House member — a duck recorded at every position 1 through 11 — and a paid-up Debutante Duck alumnus from a past first-innings nought. His relative Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own, a proper family tradition of the art of not scoring. Frank Brattle, unsurprisingly, was there too on this day in 1963. Sunday 8th — v Anchorians CC (h) - Terry Donovan fell lbw b M. Brown for his 1st duck of the season and 14th career, 26th all-time from 108 innings. A former Duck of the Year (1964) and long-time Full House nearly-man, agonisingly stuck on 10 of 11 positions. On this same day in 1945, club legend Stan Wratten (1943–1968, 28 ducks, 8th all-time) also made the walk for nought. Week in Summary Five ducks, two matches, all wearing 1st XI colours — a rare monopoly on misfortune. 🦆 Duck News — 31 August to 2 September 1968 🦆

4 ducks
Week 19 · 31 Aug 1968 – 2 Sep 1968
A tidy little haul this week: four ducks from three matches, every single one laid by the 1st XI. The exclusive zero club never sleeps. Saturday 31 August — away at Beverley CC, Kent Opening the batting and, alas, opening the account for the wrong reasons, Basil Sears was bowled by John Stickells without scoring. His 2nd duck of the season and 24th of a long and storied career — 349 innings deep and 11th on the club's all-time roll of dubious honour. Neatly, on this very date back in 1952, the legendary John Thirkell (63 career ducks, four-time Duck of the Year, top of the pile at number one of 837) was doing precisely the same thing to A Levick XI. Some traditions really do run in the blood. Sunday 1 September — away at Staplehurst CC A double helping at Staplehurst. Paul Bowles, promoted to number five, was bowled by R- Westacott for his 6th duck of the season and 16th of a career now level with 1960s regular Fred Greenfield. Paul arrives already garlanded — Duck of the Year in both 1966 and 1968, and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top ten — so this was simply business as usual. Back on this day in 1934, Bert Buckoke was enjoying an identical afternoon against Charles Arkcoll. Lower down, Ron Hickman was given lbw to the same tireless R- Westacott, his first duck of the season and 10th of his career from a brisk 19 innings — a rate that has already earned him a Quacking Together pairing (with Alan Cradduck) and, less flatteringly, a place on the Runaway Duck board for an entire scoreless 1965 season. Monday 2 September — away at Bearsted CC Paul Bowles could not resist an encore, lbw to Dave Bromley for a 7th duck of the season and 17th career, level now with Robert Murray. Staplehurst to Bearsted without troubling a single scorer — a proper pair for the weekend, and Paul's consistency of a very particular kind continues unabated. On this day in 1945, D Clark was marking the same occasion against 1st Innings MCPC. In summary: four ducks, three matches, one repeat offender. Triumph through adversity, thy name is Bowles. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - Paul Bowles — 29 innings, 7 ducks (24.14%) - Benny Bell — 12 innings, 5 ducks (41.67%) - Gordon Fenner — 9 innings, 4 ducks (44.44%) - Louis Buels — 4 innings, 3 ducks (75.00%) - Terry Groves — 7 innings, 3 ducks (42.86%) Louis Buels' 75% is the sort of average the batting coach would rather not frame. Next weekend brings fresh opportunity for the art of not scoring — the ducks await, as ever, with open wings. 🍩0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck Report — 24-25 August 1968 🦆

3 ducks
Week 18 · 24 Aug 1968 – 25 Aug 1968
A modest but distinguished haul this week: three fresh entries to the exclusive zero club, all courtesy of the 1st XI, who made ducks something of a house speciality this weekend. 🏏 Saturday — v Aylesford Paper Mills CC (home) Openers don't always open well, and Benny Bell felt it first, bowled by J- Perrin without scoring. His 5th duck of the season and 12th of a career now standing at 42 innings puts him 28th of 836 in the all-time table - consistency of a very particular kind. On this very day back in 1901, J H Coles was going through precisely the same ritual against Yalding CC - some traditions never go out of fashion. Gordon Fenner followed at six, caught behind off David Keynes via Wayne Sprigmore, for his 4th duck of the season and 33rd of a career that now sits 6th on the all-time leaderboard. A chip off the old block, and then some - siblings Joe (5) and Maurice (1) Fenner are also paid-up club members. Fittingly, 1901 was clearly a vintage year for nought-hunting, with J H Coles at it again on this same date. 🏏 Sunday — v Harrietsham CC (away) The away trip brought the week's headline act. John Thirkell, bowled by Keith Chaney for nought, moves to 2 for the season and a staggering 63 for his career - officially the club's greatest ducker of all time, 1st of 836. Four Duck of the Year titles (1952, 1954, 1955 and 1967) sit alongside membership of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, plus twin "Quacking Together" honours shared with Bob Arnold and William Buck - triumph through adversity, indeed. Relative Tim Thirkell, on just 1 career duck, has rather a lot of catching up to do. And on this same date in 1904, T Woollett was similarly dismissed for nought against the Leeds Castle CC house team - the art of not scoring evidently transcends the decades. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, three ducks, and the 1st XI cornering the market entirely - the 2nds, 3rds and 4ths escaped scot-free. 🦆 Duck Report — 17–18 August 1968 🥇

2 ducks
Week 17 · 17 Aug 1968 – 18 Aug 1968
A quiet weekend for the numbers, but not for the Duck Club — one man did the work of a whole XI, twice. 🏏 Saturday – Bowaters CC (away) Paul Bowles, first XI number three, was trapped lbw by Mervyn Stanton without ever really settling in. His 4th duck of the 1968 season and 14th in 86 career innings, moving him to 23rd on the all-time list of 836 — rarefied company for a man who also picked up the Duck of the Year honour back in 1966. On this very day in 1957, the immortal John Thirkell (1943–1968, still perched at number one all-time with 62) also fell to Bowaters CC. The fixture, it seems, has previous. 🌱 Sunday – The Mote CC (home) Bowles wasn't done. Twenty-four hours later he was bowled by P- Coatsworth for his 5th duck of the season and 15th of his career, holding at 23rd all-time. And in a splendid quirk of the calendar, John Thirkell ducked on this same date too, back in 1963 — also against The Mote CC. Two Bowles ducks, two Thirkell echoes, six years apart. Two in two days is a proper pair — a grim old double in the Club's own parlance, and one Bowles wears with the practised air of a man defending his title, since he's very much the reigning 1968 Duck of the Year in progress. He also remains a fixture on the Ducks on the Road top 10 and holds lifetime membership of the exclusive "Only Two Quacks" club alongside Adam Pooley, though that particular ignominy long predates this weekend. Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one name on the card both times. Not the volume Linton weekends are famous for, but concentrated brilliance where it counts — Bowles single-handedly kept the exclusive zero club open for business. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Gordon Fenner — 8 inns, 3 ducks (37.5%) Bowles leads by a distance, but Louis Buels' 75% strike rate is the kind of consistency that gets a man talked about in committee. A tidy little haul this weekend, ducks-wise. Just the one team troubled the scorers with noughts, but Linton's 1st XI made up for the rest of the club's quiet weekend, delivering three quacks across two days — including a proper weekend double. 1st XI Saturday at Sibton Park CC: Fred Greenfield, batting at five, was caught behind off E- Impett for his 2nd duck of the season and 15th of an illustrious career, good enough for 19th on the all-time leaderboard of 835. The same 1957 echo applies here too, a Jordan duck having fallen on this date as well. Week in Summary Three ducks, two matches, one team doing all the heavy lifting while the 2nds, 3rds and Sunday sides kept their heads down entirely. Fred Greenfield's weekend pair is the standout — quality control was evidently optional at both Sibton Park and Paddock Wood. 🦆 Duck Report — 6–7 July 1968

2 ducks

A brisk weekend at Linton Park saw the 1st XI alone answer the call of the zero club, with two dismissals worth a proper fuss and one quiet Sunday that got away scot-free. Saturday 6 July — 1st XI away to Charles Arkcoll In at three and gone without troubling the scorers, Paul Bowles was caught Brian Aiston bowled Don Cleggett for his second duck of the season and 12th of an illustrious career spanning 80 innings, good enough for 30th on the club's all-time list of 834.
