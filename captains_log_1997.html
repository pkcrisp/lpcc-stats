Sun 28 Sep 1997 🧢 1st XIv M M.Large · HomeformDWLDD

It was M.Large at home on Sunday 28 September 1997, and having lost the toss I found us stuck out in the field on what was, by rights, a chasing man's afternoon. Still, no complaints from the ranks — we simply got on with it. M.Large closed on a stiff 152/7 from their 25 overs, Mike Elworthy playing the anchor role with a fine 44, well supported by Rob Doick's 29. But the ball did its share of talking too: Carl Davis was superb with 2-25, Daniel Thirkell chipped in with 2-35, and I managed to have a small say in proceedings myself with 1-16.

Chasing 153 to win, we made a fair fist of it, closing on 160/6 from our allotted 25 — although with the equation what it was, honours were always going to be shared rather than seized. Daniel Thirkell was in irresistible touch, carrying his bat for an unbeaten 54, a proper well-made fifty that held the innings together. Roly Edwards weighed in handsomely with 37, and young Nigel Thirkell added a useful 20 — no mean feat given he sits second in the 1997 first XI run charts with 827 to his name already.

So, honours shared it was — a draw when neither side could quite force the door open. Not the story of daring conquest the tea ladies might have hoped for, but a thoroughly good tussle, and on a warm September Sunday with bat and ball both earning their keep, I'll happily settle for that.

Sun 28 Sep 1997 🧢 1st XIv T T.Thirkell · HomeformLDWLD

Sunday 28 September brought T. Thirkell to Linton Park, and with the season winding down there was still plenty worth playing for. Winning the toss, I sent us into the field, and the visitors made steady progress to 160/6 from their 25 overs — nothing that had us panicking, thanks in no small part to Stuart Cheesman, who tore through with a fine 2-8, well backed up by David Castle's 1-19 and a wicket apiece for yours truly.

Chasing 161, we made a fair fist of it without ever quite getting there, closing on 152/7. Michael Beeney was the class act with a rich 44, Clive Newman chipped in handsomely with 34, and I was pleased to add 29 of my own down the order — though seven overs short and nine runs shy is the sort of arithmetic that makes for a long walk to the tea urn.

There was, mind you, a rather lovely footnote to the afternoon: David Castle brought up 15,000 career runs for the club, a landmark that rather puts one's own modest contributions in perspective and deserved every one of the cheers it got. Season-long, the old boy leads our run charts with 1,364, while I've somehow stumbled into pole position with the ball on 49 wickets — figures I shall enjoy for exactly as long as nobody looks too closely at my economy rate.

In the end, honours shared and nobody quite able to force the door open — a draw that felt, on balance, entirely fair, and as good an excuse as any for a second pint.

A draw, then, honours even. Not the win we wanted, but a thoroughly good afternoon's cricket all the same.

Wednesday last took us to Penshurst Park, and for once the coin behaved itself: I called correctly and had no hesitation electing to bat, the pitch looking far too inviting to leave alone. Not every name from the card wants dwelling on, mind — poor Mike Elworthy's duck can be filed quietly under "one to forget."

Defending that total, we found Penshurst in no mood to surrender tamely. Andy Zaltzman batted with real authority for his 56, well supported by Good, unbeaten on 48, and Hunter with 42, the pair dragging the hosts to 207 for 7 from their fifty overs. Michael Beeney led the attack beautifully, miserly with two maidens on his way to 3 for 57, and I was glad to chip in myself with 3 for 67, having earlier made 19 with the bat — a fair day's toil, and enough at least to see off Clark for a duck.

In the end honours were shared, a draw when neither side could quite force the door. Between Paul Gibson's magnificent hundred, Michael Beeney's tidy bowling, and my own season's haul now up to 24 wickets, there was plenty to be cheerful about — a fine advertisement for patient, watchful cricket, even on a day the scoreboard refused to name a winner.

Carl Davis was the pick of the attack with 3 for 28, Dave Todd weighed in with 3 for 38 and Ian Brattle took 2 for 19 — no mean feat given he already sits third in this year's 1st XI wicket charts with 35 to his name. I pottered along with three maidens myself, more container than contributor, but happily so.

Eighty-nine runs the margin in the end, and two points thoroughly, unarguably earned. Elliotts never really got a foothold, and nor should they have. A grand day for the club, and one I'll enjoy retelling long after the tea urn's gone cold.

In the end Northend fell short at 187/5 from their 42, and honours were shared.

A draw, then — honest, hard-fought, and with Castle and Newman's batting the abiding memory of the day. Neither side could quite force the win, but on this evidence we're not far off finding one.
