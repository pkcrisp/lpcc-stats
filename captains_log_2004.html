Sun 26 Sep 2004 🧢 1st XIv ML Matt Larges · HomeformWWLWD

A dead coin toss and Matt Larges won the call, so we were straight into the field on a Sunday when I'd have quite fancied batting first myself. Twenty overs later they'd racked up 130/6, though it could have been considerably worse had Gareth Davies not bowled beautifully for 2-16, with Dennis Salisbury (2-17) and Daniel Thirkell (1-20) chipping away either side of him to keep things respectable.

Chasing 131, we got home in some style, 133/5 with an over to spare, and the top order did the heavy lifting. Mike Elworthy anchored things nicely with an unbeaten 27, Adam Jackson kept pace with 26 not out, and Simon Thirkell weighed in with 25 not out to see us over the line. My own contribution, for the record, was a golden duck, and the ledger will note it whether I like it or not — I fell for nought and we shall say no more about it.

Away from my personal shame, there were two proper landmarks worth toasting: Tim Thirkell brought up 100 career wickets, and Dennis Salisbury reached 50 of his own, both thoroughly deserved milestones for men who've put in the hard yards over the years. Daniel Thirkell, meanwhile, continues to lead from the front across the season, top of the run charts on 459 and second in the wickets on 20.

Five wickets in the bank, two points on the board, and a result thoroughly earned by the side even if my own scorebook entry does rather let the side down.

Sun 26 Sep 2004 🧢 1st XIv RB Russell Baldwins · HomeformWWWLW

Sunday 26 September 2004, and Russell Baldwins rolled up expecting a stroll. Winning the toss and electing to bat felt like the obvious call, and for once I wasn't left second-guessing myself at square leg. Matthew Carter played the anchor role beautifully, 35 not out, while Richard Knowler and I both chipped in unbeaten with 26 apiece to nudge us to 130/6 from our twenty overs — not a total to frighten anyone, but enough, I hoped, to make them work for it.

And work they did. Craig Sands, presumably still trudging back to the pavilion, departed without so much as a nudge for his first golden duck of the day — small mercies. Richard Knowler was the star of the defence, ripping through with 3-4, while Jack Jenner and Matthew Carter tidied up a wicket apiece for 21 runs each. Between the four of us we squeezed the visitors hard, though our man behind the stumps had an afternoon to forget, leaking eight byes that will doubtless be mentioned again at the next nets session.

In the end Russell Baldwins matched us almost run for run, closing on 133/5, and honours were shared. A draw is cricket's great diplomatic compromise — nobody quite beaten, nobody quite triumphant — and on this occasion it felt about right: a scrappy, spirited afternoon that neither side deserved to lose.

Wed 22 Sep 2004 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformLLDLD

Skipper's Log — Wednesday 22 September 2004

No luck with the coin at Penshurst Park, and once again I watched it land the wrong way up, so out we trooped to field first. Still, we've a habit of making the best of a bad toss, and so it proved: 224 for 6 from our forty overs, with Craig Sands the man of the moment, cracking a fine 79 not out to anchor the innings from start to nearly finish. Matthew Carter chipped in with a composed 34 not out and Trevor Back weighed in with 27 — though the less said about Jim Hall's contribution the better; a golden duck to add to the club ledger, and no doubt a pint or two owed at the bar for that particular walk of shame.

Two hundred and twenty-four looked a defendable total on paper, and for a good while it was. Mark Christmas bowled beautifully for his 3 for 55, and I was pleased enough with my own 1 for 25 in support, with Cliff Mitchell back in the pavilion for a duck at our hands. But there's no legislating for a batsman in the form of his life, and K. Exton simply had other ideas, marching to an imperious 106 not out to see Penshurst home at 227 for 4 with two balls to spare, ably assisted by Nick Rees and Peter Whyte.

There was, at least, one moment to raise a genuine cheer amid the gloom: Trevor Back brought up 10,000 career runs for the club, a landmark worth savouring even on a losing afternoon. But six wickets is six wickets — not our day, lads. Heads up, and back to the nets.

Sat 11 Sep 2004 🧢 2nd XIv Teston CC · AwayformWDWWL

Clive Newman's Match Report — Saturday 11 September 2004, away to Teston CC

The toss went against us, as tosses so often do when I'm the one calling it, and we were put into the field on a pitch that, frankly, looked far too kind for our own good. Not that it mattered in the end, because Jack Jenner decided he fancied bowling us to victory more or less single-handed, tearing through Teston's order for a magnificent 7-29. Dan Tyler (24), Geoff King (22) and Peter Webley (21) put up the only real resistance, but with Joe Martin (1-13, three tidy maidens) and Jon Harris (1-21) squeezing from the other end, and Martin Hubbard, Rory Stroud and R King all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, Teston were all out for a modest 95. A thoroughly satisfying morning's work.

Chasing such a small total should have been a formality, and yet we contrived to make it interesting, losing a wicket with the score still on nought — the sort of start that has a captain reaching for the antacids. Christopher Thirkell and I both fell for ducks, which I mention once and shall not dwell on further, thank you very much. It was left to Richard Piper, unbeaten on a splendid 43, to marshal us home at 96 for 4. Poor Ron McCormick had a torrid time behind the stumps, conceding nine byes, but nobody's perfect.

Two points, five wickets to spare, and a Jenner five-for to remember. A fine day out, even if my own contribution was best forgotten.
