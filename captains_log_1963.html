🗓️ 21 Sep – 27 Sep 1963 · 1963 — 1 match

Sun 22 Sep 1963 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWWDDD

It was Linton Park CC at home, and win the toss I did not have to think twice about — flat pitch, quick outfield, I put us in.

The lads didn't let me down. Dick Langridge played the innings of the day, 48 at the top, and had good company from Stan Wratten's 37 and Gordon Fenner's 26 to drag us up to 166/7 from our 42 overs. Not every visit to the crease was so glorious, mind — Bob Arnold and Cliff Anscombe both trudged back for ducks, and I daresay the tea urn had a good laugh at their expense before they'd even reached the boundary rope. Fair play to the extras column too, which weighed in with 28 of its own and, on this showing, deserves a place in the middle order.

I chipped in myself with bat in hand, glad to say, though it was very much a team total built on Langridge's effort more than mine. Worth noting, as the season's ledger stands, that Dick now leads our 1963 Married run charts with that very 48, while Ray Howard continues to lord it over the Singles with 88 — the rivalry, gentle as it is, rumbles on.

A tidy 28-run win in the end, and a thoroughly satisfying Sunday for club and captain alike. Onwards we go.

🗓️ 14 Sep – 20 Sep 1963 · 1963 — 2 matches

Sun 15 Sep 1963 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformWDWDW

As we travelled down to Staplehurst on Sunday, I lost the toss – no change there, then – and we were put into the field.

Staplehurst huffed and puffed their way to 91 all out in 29.2 overs, and frankly it could have been a good deal worse for them. A wicket at 2-1 set nerves jangling early, and once 37-3 became 37-6 the innings was in freefall, three batsmen departing without troubling the scorers. At the heart of it all was Michael Chambers, spinning a web few of them could read, finishing with a magnificent 6-28 including four maidens – not a run given away lightly. Alan Wilkes (3-25) and David Gale (1-22) tidied up admirably in support, with J- Blunt's unbeaten 28 the only real resistance.

Chasing 92, I'm rather pleased to say I led from the front with an unbeaten 54, though Norman Mattingley and Dick Langridge both fell for ducks along the way to keep things interesting for the crowd. Roy Clarke had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 15 byes testament to a wicket with ideas of its own, but we got home by four wickets.

A day of milestones too – David Gale passed 100 career wickets, and Michael Chambers's six-for lifted him past J Donovan to 22nd on the club's all-time list. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a fine advert for village cricket at its most chaotic best.

Sat 14 Sep 1963 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformLWDWD

Off John Thirkell we lost the toss on Saturday and Bowaters CC sent us in the field, which on a September afternoon felt like being asked to fetch the coal before you'd even taken your coat off. Still, no complaints from this quarter, especially once Alan Tucker got his fingers round the ball. Bowaters slid from a wobbly 8-1 to a rather forlorn 82 all out in 35.1 overs, William Preston's 36 the only real resistance, and the rot from 81-7 to 82-10 suggested nerves were fraying nicely down their order.

Chasing 83 should have been the easy part, and mostly it was, though we contrived to lose a wicket with just 3 on the board to keep everyone honest. I chipped in with 40 to see us home, while poor Roy Clarke had a torrid time behind the stumps, coughing up 10 byes in the process — the sort of thing that gets mentioned at tea whether you like it or not.

A five-wicket win, then, and a landmark day for Tucker, who leapfrogs Ernest Cruttenden into 18th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list. Onwards we go, in good heart and better form.

🗓️ 07 Sep – 13 Sep 1963 · 1963 — 2 matches

Sun 08 Sep 1963 🧢 1st XIv HC Harrietsham CC · HomeformLLWDW

Sunday 8th September dawned fine over Linton Park, and with Harrietsham the visitors, winning the toss felt like the first stroke of luck we'd need all season. I had no hesitation in taking first knock — a decision I'll happily take credit for, since it's not often my hunches survive contact with reality.

John Ames set about proving me right with a fine 41, the kind of innings that makes a captain look wiser than he is, before Frank Brattle chipped in with an unbeaten 39 to help us to a tidy 103 for 1 off our 26 overs. One wicket down all afternoon rather rather undersold the control with which we batted — Harrietsham's bowlers must have wondered what they'd done to deserve such calm treatment. Brattle, for what it's worth, continues his quiet march up the season's run charts, now sitting second in the club's 1st XI standings with 396 to his name — a man clearly saving his best for when it matters least urgently, if such a thing exists in cricket.

Alas, for all our restraint with the bat, neither side could summon the wit or the weather to force a result, and honours were shared. A draw, then — the great English compromise, satisfying no one and offending no one. Still, with Ames purring and Brattle ominously in form, I left the field more encouraged than the scorebook alone might suggest. Onwards.

Sun 01 Sep 1963 🧢 1st XIv HB Hambros Bank · HomeformDDLLW

Sunday the first of September, and Hambros Bank rolled up the drive with the sun doing its level best to cooperate. The coin bounced my way and, ever the optimist, I put us in to bat. William Buck was in fine touch, 52 to his name, and found a willing ally in Robert Murray, whose 46 helped stitch together a third-wicket stand of 53 that gave the innings its backbone. I chipped in with 21 of my own, more graft than grace, and we closed on a respectable 168 for 6 from our 46 overs.

Defending it proved a different sort of afternoon. Pavitt and Raiser put on a stand of 102 for the second wicket that had us checking the sightscreen for sympathy, Pavitt reaching 55 and Raiser a tidy fifty of his own. But we chipped away — Alan Wilkes the chief tormentor with 4 for 48, David Gale and Michael Chambers a wicket apiece, Chambers miserly enough to bowl three maidens, and poor D McEuan trudging off for a duck. Richard Ashby had his gloves full behind the stumps, eight byes the only real blemish. Hambros closed on 145 for 7.

Not all workaday, mind: Robert Murray brought up 3,000 career runs for the club, and William Buck's 52 nudged him past Stan Wratten into second on our all-time run charts — proper history made between tea and close.

A draw, then, honours shared, and both sides sent home no worse for wear — which, on a day this pleasant, felt about right.

Saturday 24 August 1963, Aylesford Paper Mills at home, and I won the toss and fancied our chances with the bat. I fancied wrong.

Defending 58 is a job for miracle workers, and while we aren't quite that, we gave it a proper go. Doug Milner had the opposition one down with just a single run on the board, and for a few overs we allowed ourselves a flicker of hope — Colin Cheeseman and Rodda both departed without scoring, courtesy of some fine bowling. David Gale was the pick of us by some distance, mopping up 3 for 27 and, in the process, taking his season's tally to 43 wickets — a effort that deserves rather better support than we managed to give him. But Cliff Anscombe had other ideas, batting through unbeaten on 44 to see Aylesford home at 62 for 4 in just 12.2 overs.

Five wickets the margin, and a fair reflection of the day. Not our finest hour with bat or ball — heads up, chins slightly less so, and back to the nets we go.

Sat 17 Aug 1963 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · AwayformLLLLD

Saturday 17th August 1963, and off we trundled to Bowaters CC in what I can only describe as cautious optimism. I won the toss and elected to bat — the right call, I still maintain, whatever the scorebook might suggest to the contrary.

Up the season's run charts I find myself leading the 1st XI with 470 runs, which is some comfort on the long drive home. Character-building stuff, this cricket lark.

William Buck stood firmest for his unbeaten 33, well supported by Roy Clarke's 20, but three of us — myself included — trooped back for ducks, and I shall draw a discreet veil over my own contribution beyond admitting it. Thirty-five byes rather summed up the afternoon behind the stumps.

There was, at least, one ray of sunshine amid the wreckage: Roy Clarke's knock nudged him up to 18th on the club's all-time run list, and he now sits second in this summer's charts with 331 runs, while David Gale continues to lead the wicket-takers with 36.

A hammering by 177 runs, then, and no way of dressing it up. Heads up, chins out, and back to the nets we go.

Seventy-seven never looks like enough on paper, but cricket has a habit of making fools of paper. Senacre Staff XI lost an early wicket with just 2 on the board and never truly recovered their composure, as we ran through the top order from 2-1 to 11-4 in a blur of movement and nerve. Owen and B Cannell both fell for ducks, Gordon Fenner picked up 2-1 to atone handsomely for his earlier failure with the bat, and Terry Donovan (2-4) and Michael Chambers (2-8) chipped in to leave the visitors bowled out for 60.
