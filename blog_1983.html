🦆 DUCK REPORT — 25th to 28th September 1983 🦆

4 ducks
Week 21 · 25 Sep 1983 – 28 Sep 1983
Four fresh names on the slate this week, all falling in the same fixture against Linton Park CC — a proper vintage haul for the ledger. Unknown XI Robert Moss opened the batting and opened his account in the wrong column, bowled Malcolm Pay for a debut duck. That's innings number one, duck number one — a fresh entry on the 1983 One and Done board, a career built so far entirely on a single, solitary zero, and he also picks up a Retirement Duck credential, though at this early stage that's surely a long way off. Eerily, Len Conway (1954–1965, 12 ducks) fell the very same way on this exact calendar date back in 1960, against these same opponents. 1st XI No debut nerves for Jim Hall, whose 3rd duck of the season, bowled David Iliffe, is business as usual for a man sitting 3rd on the entire 1050-strong all-time list with 54 career ducks from 343 innings. Two Duck of the Year titles (1970 and 1977), one batting position shy of the Full House, and shared "Quacking Together" honours with Jon Harris, Martin Mears and fellow duck royalty Tim Thirkell all sit on his groaning shelf. Conway's 1960 ghost strikes again — same date, same opponents. 2nd XI Sean Riley fell for his 3rd duck of the season and of his career alike, bowled Michael Chambers, and already holds a Retirement Duck credential from a past farewell of his own. A third man this fixture sharing Conway's exact 1960 date. Simon Thirkell rounded things off, bowled Mike Iliffe for his 5th duck of the season and 10th of his career — and with it keeps a firm grip on the Duck of the Year 1983 crown. It runs in the family: father John (90 career ducks), brothers Tim (52) and Nigel (32), and relative Stuart (1) all fly the Thirkell colours on the honours board. A fourth and final nod to 1960 — Conway really was everywhere that weekend. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, all falling to Linton Park CC on the same afternoon. The Iliffe surname did brisk business too — David and Mike shared the wickets between them, while David can't resist adding to his own tally, as the leaderboard below shows. Consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 5 ducks / 7 innings (71.43%) 2. Ian Webb — 4 ducks / 33 innings (12.12%) 3. Martin Larkin — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) 4. David Iliffe — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) 5. Mike Elworthy — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) Simon leads by a full duck with a frankly heroic 71% failure rate this season — the field has some catching up to do. The next fixture looms and the exclusive zero club never rests. Get your ducks in a row, Linton — or, in the spirit of the thing, don't. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — Hexham Tour, 5-6 September 1983

3 ducks
Week 18 · 5 Sep 1983 – 6 Sep 1983
Two fixtures away at Hexham this week, and only one of them proved fertile ground for the zero club. The first passed off disappointingly duck-free, but the second delivered three added to the books, the lot of them from the touring party, in the same innings, at the same ground - a proper away-day cluster for the exclusive zero club. 🏏 Tour XI v Hexham (away) At number four, Mike Elworthy fell to a bowler the scorebook could only describe as "Unsure" - some things are best left a mystery. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 5th of a career now standing at 38 innings, good enough for 106th on the all-time list, and another entry for his growing Tourist Duck top 10 collection. Neatly, on this very date in 1967 Roger Craddock, who turned out for the club 1966-1968, was suffering an identical fate against Birchington CC - the calendar has form for these things. Down at eight, Simon Thirkell continued an increasingly torrid summer, caught behind for his 4th duck of the season and 9th of his career from a mere 35 innings, landing him 70th on the all-time roll - a strike rate that has already earned him the season's Duck of the Year 1983 honours and a place on the club's storied 50 Before a 50 board. The surname carries weight in these parts: John sits at the very summit of the all-time charts with 90, while brothers Tim (52) and Nigel (32) are never far off the pace themselves - a proper family trade. Like Mike, Simon shares his misfortune with Roger Craddock, out for nought on this same day back in 1967. Two down became three when Allan Cooper, at nine, was also caught behind without scoring - his 2nd of the season and 28th for the club, from 189 innings, 20th on the all-time list. Allan's honours board is a roll call of old Only Two Quacks double acts with Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back, though none of his usual accomplices were present to share this one. He too keeps company with Roger Craddock's 1967 vintage. A genuine team performance in the art of not scoring, three wickets down for precisely nothing between them, all in the colours of the touring side. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, all confined to a single Tour XI innings at Hexham. The other fixture escaped entirely unscathed - the bowlers there will have to wait their turn. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell - 4 ducks from 6 innings (66.67%) 2. Ian Webb - 4 ducks from 33 innings (12.12%) 3. Martin Larkin - 3 ducks from 9 innings (33.33%) 4. David Iliffe - 3 ducks from 12 innings (25.0%) 5. Mike Elworthy - 3 ducks from 12 innings (25.0%) Simon's two-thirds strike rate leads the field by some distance - a truly heroic commitment to the cause with the season not yet done. 🦆 Until the next fixture, when the touring party will doubtless be back out there giving the zero club fresh recruits. Bring your camera - and, ideally, your bat.

Four fixtures on the go this week and the ducks, in their infinite mischief, decided to concentrate their efforts on a single ground. British Rail Ashford away carried the entire burden of the club's nought-scoring this week, with two names added to the 1983 ledger. 🏏 1st XI — away at British Rail Ashford Mike Elworthy strode out at number 7 and departed as he arrived, bowled by O Wood without so much as a run to his name. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 4th in a career now spanning 37 innings, tidy enough for 124th of 1,047 on the all-time list and another entry for his growing collection on the Tourist Duck top 10 — a man who saves his best nothing for away days. On this very day back in 1949, Richard Harris was doing exactly the same thing, one of an eventual 36 career ducks (1940–1982) that leave him 11th of all time. One place later Simon Thirkell followed him through the gate, caught behind off the very same O Wood for his 3rd duck of the season and 8th of a career now standing at 34 innings — 82nd of 1,047 all time. Simon is paid-up membership material here: a place on the 50 Before a 50 board (59 ducks eventually, high score a mere 48), a spot on the Tourist and Ducks on the Road top 10s, and — with the season still 1983 — the reigning Duck of the Year, a title he appears determined to defend rather than relinquish. He comes by it honestly too: father John tops the entire all-time chart on 90, while brothers Tim (52) and Nigel (32) keep the Thirkell name comfortably stocked in these pages. And, by pleasing coincidence, he shares his on-this-day twin with his own teammate — Richard Harris was at it again in 1949, same date, same fate. 📊 Week in summary Two ducks from four matches, both at British Rail Ashford, both off the bowling of O Wood — the 2nd, 3rd and 4th XIs kept their noses clean and left the honours board untouched this week. 🦆 Duck Report — 20 to 24 August 1983

4 ducks
Week 16 · 20 Aug 1983 – 24 Aug 1983
The exclusive zero club took delivery of four new entries this week, spread across a Saturday double and a Wednesday away day. Not a vintage haul, but quality over quantity has never been the club's motto anyway — quantity is. 🏏 Saturday 20th — 1st XI v Hartley Country Club CC (H) Andy Woollett departed at four, caught Davies bowled Deicher, for his 2nd duck of the season and 9th of an LPCC career now 93 innings deep — rank 60 of 1045 in the all-time roll of dishonour. On this very day back in 1944, W Bunting was performing the identical trick against KCC Maidstone, one of five ducks in a 1941–1948 career. History, as ever, repeats. Down at ten, Sean Cook followed him back, caught Vickery bowled Whitelaw, his own 2nd of the season and 6th for the club from a still-tender 15 innings — rank 99 of 1045. Sean is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having famously nought'd on his actual first-ever knock, though today's effort was merely business as usual rather than a repeat performance. Fittingly, he shares Andy's on-this-day companion in Bunting, 1944. ⚡ Wednesday 24th — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC (A) Trevor Back, caught Hinks bowled Barden at four, chalked up his 2nd duck of the season and 21st for the club in 146 innings — rank 34 of 1045. Trevor is thoroughly furnished on the honours board, holding down places in the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, with shared quacking credits alongside Allan Cooper and Jon Harris. On this day in 1968, Benny Bell was doing exactly the same against Aylesford Paper Mills, one of thirty en route to a fine career tally. At seven, David Lines ran himself out for nought — Unsure being the umpire, apparently, as much as anyone else. His 1st duck of the season and, remarkably, his 1st in a career of just 3 innings — rank 313 of 1045. David already carries the presumptive Retirement Duck honour on the club's books, though nobody has called time on him yet, so read nothing into it. He shares Trevor's on-this-day parallel in Benny Bell. 📊 Week in Brief Three matches, four ducks, and a top order that largely held its nerve — both of this week's casualties came from the middle and lower order. Week 15 · 13 Aug 1983 – 17 Aug 1983
A curiously quiet week for the exclusive zero club. Three matches played, and only one man answered the call of the naught. Make no mistake though — one duck can still make history, and this one did. 🏏 Midweek XI — Gravesend CC, Wednesday 17th August Batting at five, A Nichols became this week's sole representative of the art of not scoring, bowled by K Barden. Not a golden duck, mind — he weathered a ball or two before the inevitable arrived — but arguably rarer still: this was his very first senior innings for the club, and he has marked it with an immediate and unsolicited duck. Straight onto the Debutante Duck board he goes, a dubious honour bestowed on debut and worn for a lifetime. One career duck from one career innings puts Nichols at 267th of 1045 in the all-time standings — a long road ahead, but every journey starts somewhere, and his started at nought. Fittingly, the parallel books throw up good company: on this very day back in 1969, Percy Brattle also trudged off for a duck, against Mote A. Brattle went on to rack up 16 across a career running 1934 to 1973, good enough for 39th all-time, and was crowned Duck of the Year that same season. If young Nichols fancies following that path, he's picked an auspicious date to start. 📅 Week in Summary Just the one duck from three fixtures this period — the other two sides evidently kept their noughts locked away. 🦆 Duck Report — Midweek Marvels, 6th–10th August 1983

2 ducks
Week 14 · 6 Aug 1983 – 10 Aug 1983
Slim pickings this week from the LPCC diary — just three fixtures and a modest brace of noughts — but what the Duck Club lacks in volume it makes up for in a rather tidy piece of symmetry: both of this week's victims fell to the very same bowler, in the very same match, in the space of a few overs. T Drury, take a bow. 🏏 Midweek XI v Bobbing Court CC (home, 10 August) First to the crease and first to depart was Nigel Thirkell, undone at number five, caught and bowled by T Drury. It's duck number two of the season for Nigel and a chunky 32nd for the career — 470 innings deep and still finding new ways to nick off. That tally plants him at 15th of 1,044 in the all-time table, comfortably inside the Homebody Duck top ten and a paid-up member of the Only Two Quacks club alongside Jim Hall. Ducking, it seems, runs in the blood — the Thirkell name already carries entries for father, brothers and cousin alike, a proper dynasty of the dismissed. On this very day in 1958, a young Roy Clarke was suffering an identical fate against Harrietsham CC — 40 ducks and a Duck of the Year gong to his name by the time he was done. Nigel, the company you keep. Coming in at number ten, Ian Brattle couldn't improve on the theme, stumped — the umpire apparently unsure of the finer points, but the scorebook in no doubt — again off T Drury. It's his first duck of the season and 13th for the career from just 32 innings, a rate that has him sitting pretty (or badly, depending on your view) at 45th of 1,044 all time. Ian is also a card-carrying member of the 50 Before a 50 club — 51 career ducks against a highest score of just 33, a ratio that tells its own story — and once put together seven ducks in a row, a run of futility the honours board still remembers fondly. And wouldn't you know it, 1958 strikes twice: Ian too finds Roy Clarke's ghost waiting for him from Harrietsham, the same duck echoing down the decades to claim two Linton men on the same August afternoon a quarter-century apart. 📋 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches, both against Bobbing Court, both at the hands of one relentless bowler. Not quite a king pair between them, but a fine demonstration that when Drury's on song, the Duck Club takes notice. The other two fixtures passed by disappointingly fowl-free. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ian Webb — 4 ducks / 28 innings (14.29%) - David Iliffe — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.33%) - Martin Larkin — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.33%) - Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks / 3 innings (66.67%) - Sean Riley — 2 ducks / 8 innings (25.0%) Note the family connection at the top end — young Simon Thirkell keeping the surname firmly in the conversation while brother Nigel does his bit lower down the career table. Next fixture awaits, and with it the ever-present chance of fresh nominations to the exclusive zero club. 🍩0️⃣ Bring your bats, gentlemen — or don't bother, some of you clearly aren't using them anyway. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 9–10 July 1983

1 duck
Week 10 · 9 Jul 1983 – 10 Jul 1983
Two fixtures on the books this weekend and, frankly, the ducks made themselves scarce — only one specimen bagged across both. A lean haul, but what it lacked in quantity it made up for in pedigree. 🏏 Saturday, 1st XI v Horton Kirby CC Jon Harris strode out at number six and departed caught A Lee, bowled J Massiah — a tidy dismissal for a man who has made an entire second career out of getting out for nought. This is Jon's 2nd duck of the season and his 58th for the club overall, a figure that plants him at a positively imperious #2 of 1044 in the all-time standings from 361 innings. Numbers like that don't happen by accident. And the honours board reads like a testimonial dinner menu. Jon is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having ducked on his very first Linton innings back in the mists of time, and has since claimed Duck of the Year no fewer than three times — 1976, 1980 and 1981.
