🦆 Duck Report — 30 September–1 October 1978

2 ducks
Week 22 · 30 Sep 1978 – 1 Oct 1978
Autumn's last hurrah at Linton Park, and the 2nd XI made sure the season didn't slip away without one final offering to the zero club. 🏏 2nd XI v Linton Park CC Openers beware — Brian Piper, aided and abetted by the safe hands of Tony Gauna, ran up a small production line at the crease this weekend, claiming two LPCC batsmen in the same manner within the same innings. A ruthless double act. First to go, at number two, was Ian Webb, caught Gauna bowled Piper for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of an increasingly eventful career — now 138th of 960 in the all-time roll of dubious distinction, from 19 innings. Ian is a long-standing paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his account with a nought back on debut, though this one's very much a repeat offence rather than a first. Keep it in the family — his relative Ken Webb also carries 3 career ducks, so the trait, such as it is, runs true. And in a fine bit of calendar symmetry, on this very day in 1977 Tim Thirkell — 39 ducks across 1967–1978, 6th on the all-time list, Duck of the Year 1972 no less — was doing exactly the same thing against Lordswood CC. One place down the order, John Bloomfield followed him back, caught Gauna bowled Piper for nought — his 5th duck of both the season and his career, from just 18 innings, and a leap to 94th of 960 overall. A rapid accumulation by any standard. Fittingly, this very fixture also produced an on-this-day echo: back in 1977, none other than his own teammate Ian Webb was recording a duck against Lordswood CC on the same date, then sitting on 3 for his career. History does like to rhyme in this club. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one bowler-keeper combination doing all the damage. Modest numbers, but concentrated carnage — Piper and Gauna will want their names in lights, or at least in this newsletter, which is close enough. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Keith Miller — 5 ducks, 14 innings (35.71%) 2. John Bloomfield — 5 ducks, 15 innings (33.33%) 3. Jim Hall — 5 ducks, 36 innings (13.89%) 4. Malcolm Pay — 5 ducks, 39 innings (12.82%) 5. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30.00%) John Bloomfield's weekend effort keeps him snapping at Keith Miller's heels for top spot — the race for Duck of the Year is well and truly alive. Next fixture, fresh victims. The zero club never closes its books. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 23–27 September 1978 🦆

4 ducks
Week 21 · 23 Sep 1978 – 27 Sep 1978
A quieter but no less ceremonious week for the exclusive zero club, with three fixtures in the period and four new entries to the ledger. One match passed by entirely unmolested by the Duck Club — the art of not scoring, apparently, optional that day. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — 1st XI v West Farleigh CC (home) John Bloomfield, sent in at nine, fell lbw to Fred Baker for his 4th duck of the season and 4th of a growing career in just 17 innings — an efficient rate of failure that plants him 111th of 960 in the all-time standings. Curiously, on this very day back in 1972, club legend Tim Thirkell also made a duck against West Farleigh — some anniversaries are best not celebrated twice. Batting at eleven, A Donovan opened his senior account in the most understated way possible, bowled by Norman Underdown for nought on debut. A duck first time out secures immediate and unarguable induction into the Debutante Duck club, a rite of passage as old as the fixture list itself, and sets him off at 236th of 960 all-time. He follows in family footsteps too — relative Terry Donovan already sits on 18 career ducks, so the art of the nought clearly runs in the blood. Remarkably, he shares his historical echo with teammate Bloomfield: two Linton batsmen, one 1972 ghost, one afternoon. 🌙 Wednesday 27th — Midweek XI v The Mote CC (away) Opening the innings — and, evidently, opening the account for the day — Paul Bowles was caught by David Morris off Roger Selleck without scoring. His 29th career duck moves him to a rarefied 17th of 960 all-time, propping up an honours cabinet that already includes back-to-back Duck of the Year titles in 1966 and 1968, a fixture on the Ducks on the Road top ten, and the celebrated Only Two Quacks pairing with Adam Pooley. Fittingly for an away-day regular, this one came on the road. On this same date in 1964, Robert Murray also departed for nought. At five, Malcolm Pay was bowled by Malcolm Bristow for his 5th duck of the season, drawing level with Jim Hall and Keith Miller at the top of the current Duck Hunting Season table, and his 9th career duck from 72 innings — good enough for 59th of 960. His sibling Colin Pay carries a single career duck of his own, so between them the Pay family keeps a cautious foot in the club. Like Bowles, Pay finds company in Robert Murray's 1964 duck — a ghost doing double duty this week. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from three matches, with West Farleigh and The Mote both taking their pound of Linton flesh. Two separate deliveries in the period found a decades-old echo waiting for them — the ghosts of ducks past clearly keeping their own fixture list. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Keith Miller — 5 ducks / 14 innings (35.71%) - Jim Hall — 5 ducks / 36 innings (13.89%) - Malcolm Pay — 5 ducks / 38 innings (13.16%) - John Bloomfield — 4 ducks / 14 innings (28.57%) - Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.33%) Next fixture, next opportunity. The exclusive zero club never sleeps — bring your bats, gentlemen, and try not to bring us business. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 16 to 20 September 1978

0 ducks
Week 20 · 16 Sep 1978 – 20 Sep 1978
Three fixtures played this week across the club and, remarkably, not a single batsman could be persuaded to walk back for nought. The Duck Club convenes this week to report... nothing. An entire weekend's cricket and the exclusive zero club gained not one new signature in its book. Whether this reflects a sudden and troubling outbreak of competence, or merely three sides quietly agreeing not to feed the ledger, we cannot say. What we can say is that somewhere out there, bats were middled, ones were nudged into the covers, and the art of not scoring went entirely unpractised. A quiet week for the connoisseurs of ignominy. Rest assured — normal service, and normal humiliation, will resume shortly. The season's front-runners are watching nervously from the honours board, and it would be a shame to let the campaign go stale. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 🥇 Keith Miller — 5 ducks in 14 innings (35.71%) — a startling strike rate that puts him top of the pile and comfortably top of the wrong kind of averages 🥈 Jim Hall — 5 ducks in 35 innings (14.29%) — level on numbers with Miller but getting there the scenic way, one long campaign of attrition 🥉 Malcolm Pay — 4 ducks in 37 innings (10.81%) — steady, dependable, the reliable also-ran of the nought column 4️⃣ Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks in 8 innings (37.5%) — the season's most efficient collector by percentage, threatening to overhaul the leaders on sheer economy of effort 5️⃣ John Bloomfield — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) — quietly accumulating, one soft dismissal from climbing the table Keith Miller retains top spot for now, but with Mike Iliffe posting the best strike rate in the division by some distance, the race for Duck of the Year 1978 is far from settled. One careless waft on a green top could change everything. 0️⃣ The ducks may have evaded capture this week, but the Duck Club's nets remain out for next weekend's fixtures. Bring your bats, gentlemen — and for a select few, try not to bring them back too soon. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — The Cornwall Tour, 9-10 September 1978

5 ducks
Week 19 · 9 Sep 1978 – 10 Sep 1978
The touring party pushed on into Cornwall this weekend and returned with exactly the sort of yield the Duck Club was founded to honour: five ducks in two days, a rare sibling double, and the club's own Duck of the Year extending their lead over the field. 🏏 Saturday 9th — Tour XI at Perranarworthal CC Keith Miller went first, bowled by B Bennett, a 4th duck of the season and 8th of a career now 20 innings deep, 58th in the all-time list of 955. Already installed as this season's Duck of the Year 1978, on this evidence the title is being defended with real commitment. Jim Hall followed, caught Brotherton off Copelin, a 5th duck of the season and a mighty 39th career duck from 216 innings — 5th on the all-time chart, a two-time former Duck of the Year (1970 and 1977), and a name so entwined with the honours board they have separate "Quacking Together" entries with three different teammates, Jon Harris among them, as proved again today. Then S Miller, bowled Sloggett, a 2nd duck from a Duck Club career only 2 innings old — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck fraternity from their very first knock. Fittingly, the day was shared with sibling Keith: the Millers' joint failure earns a "Two of a Quack" entry on the family honours board, the rarest ceremony this club has to offer. Closing the innings, Jon Harris — caught Docking bowled Sloggett — brought up a 3rd duck of the season and 38th of a career (230 innings, rank 8 of 955) that already boasts the exceedingly rare Full House, a duck recorded at every position from 1 to 11. The habit runs in the family too: kin Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of their own. On this very day in 1977, Andrew Moss also made a duck against Cornwall Crusaders — fitting anniversary company for all four names above. 🏏 Sunday 10th — Tour XI at Paul CC Keith Miller went again, run out for nought, completing a grim pair across the weekend's two fixtures — a 5th season duck, drawing level with Jim Hall, and a 9th career duck. On this same day back in 1955, John --'s Taylor also made a duck, against Old Roffensians. 📊 Week in Summary Five ducks from two matches, the touring party thoroughly restocking the zero column. The Miller siblings' shared failure is the headline of the weekend, Keith's pair the footnote nobody asked for. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Keith Miller — 13 inns, 5 ducks (38.46%) 2. Jim Hall — 34 inns, 5 ducks (14.71%) 3. Malcolm Pay — 35 inns, 4 ducks (11.43%) 4. Mike Iliffe — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.86%) 5. John Bloomfield — 13 inns, 3 ducks (23.08%) Keith and Jim share the season lead on ducks, but Keith's fearsome 38% strike rate keeps them out in front where it counts. The tour rolls on — who joins the club next? 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 3rd to 7th September 1978

3 ducks
Week 18 · 3 Sep 1978 – 7 Sep 1978
A quieter week for the exclusive zero club — just three ducks laid across three fixtures — but what they lacked in volume they made up for in pedigree. 1st XI — away to Staplehurst CC, Sunday 3rd Keith Miller fell for nought at first drop, bowled Anthony West. His third of the season and 7th career duck (74th of 955 all-time) confirms him as 1978's reigning Duck of the Year — a crown he now wears with the solemn dignity the honour deserves. On this very day in 1972, Benny Bell also made a duck against Staplehurst CC, en route to 30 career noughts and 14th spot on the all-time roll — proof that Staplehurst have form for it. Lower down, David Iliffe joined him in the pavilion, bowled Eric Hotson for his 2nd of the season and 2nd career duck from just 6 innings (171st of 955). Keep an eye on the family tree — his father Mike Iliffe is having quite the campaign himself, currently sat 3rd on this season's duck charts. Tour XI — away to Grampound Road CC, Thursday 7th A first-ever LPCC innings for S Miller ended exactly as first innings sometimes do — bowled Howard Nicholls without scoring, and straight onto the Debutante Duck honours board. A tidy way to announce yourself. Neatly enough, S Miller's sibling is none other than Keith Miller of Saturday's Staplehurst match above — two Millers, two ducks, one very ordinary week for the family. On this day in 1977, club legend Tim Thirkell also strolled back for nought, against St Just CC, on his way to 39 career ducks and 5th on the all-time list. The week in brief Three ducks from three matches — the third fixture, wherever it was, stayed frustratingly clean. Two of the week's three came on the very same afternoon at Staplehurst, and pleasingly both found a same-day echo from seasons gone by. The Duck Club remains in rude health. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 4 ducks, 33 innings (12.12%) 2. Malcolm Pay — 4 ducks, 35 innings (11.43%) 3. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks, 7 innings (42.86%) 4. Keith Miller — 3 ducks, 11 innings (27.27%) 5. John Bloomfield — 3 ducks, 13 innings (23.08%) Mike Iliffe's 42.86% is a number to be admired rather than emulated. The season race remains wide open with plenty of cricket left to play — bring your bats, gentlemen, the ducks are watching. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 26th–30th August 1978

5 ducks
Week 17 · 26 Aug 1978 – 30 Aug 1978
Four fixtures this week and the Duck Club needed only two of them. The 1st XI carried the entire load — five ducks, one honours board rewritten, and Old Anchorians CC's bowlers doing most of the damage. Sunday 27th August — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (home) Malcolm Pay went first, caught off T- Sutton at four. His 4th duck of the season and 8th of a career now 67 innings deep, holding at 65th all-time. On this very day back in 1949, Richard Harris was doing exactly the same thing to Hunton Wanderers CC — he'd finish with 36 career ducks, 10th on the all-time list. Malcolm's sibling Colin Pay is also a paid-up club member with 1 career nought to his name — clearly runs in the family. Trevor Back followed at five, also off T- Sutton, his 3rd of the season and 9th career duck from 56 innings, 59th all-time. Trevor keeps distinguished company on the honours boards — Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck all list him in their top ten, and he shares Quacking Together and Only Two Quacks pairings with Jon Harris and Allan Cooper respectively. He too shares this date with Richard Harris's 1949 heroics. Giles Daubeny was next, caught off K- Hewitt at seven — his first duck of the season, and only his 2nd in a slender 6-innings career, 183rd all-time. Early days yet. John Bloomfield completed the collapse at nine, T- Sutton's third victim of the innings, his 3rd duck of the season and, remarkably, his 3rd in only 15 career innings — 139th all-time. T- Sutton finishes with three ducks to his bowling figures, a proper poultry farmer's afternoon. Monday 28th August — 1st XI v Bearsted CC (away) Jim Hall, stumped by Anthony Hunt off Bryan Elswood, for his 4th of the season and — take a breath — 38th career duck from 213 innings. That's 7th on the all-time list, and Jim's honours cabinet is groaning: Duck of the Year in both 1970 and 1977, a Full House nearly-man on 10 of 11 batting positions, top-ten entries in Tourist Duck, Homebody Duck, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road, and five separate pairing honours besides. On this day in 1974, Roland Parr was doing his own small bit for the cause against Gravesend CC — a single career duck, 405th all-time, but he'll always have this date. Week in Summary Five ducks from two matches, all 1st XI. T- Sutton's hat-trick of scalps at Old Anchorians deserves its own line in the bowling figures, even if nobody's writing it down. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 4 ducks, 31 inns, 12.9% 2. Malcolm Pay — 4 ducks, 33 inns, 12.12% 3. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks, 7 inns, 42.86% 4. John Bloomfield — 3 ducks, 12 inns, 25.0% 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks, 16 inns, 18.75% Mike Iliffe's 42.86% still leads the percentage race by some distance — efficiency, of a sort. Next fixtures await. The Club stands ready to welcome new — and returning — members. 🦆🏏0️⃣

0 ducks
Week 16 · 19 Aug 1978 – 23 Aug 1978
Three matches played this week across the club, and — brace yourselves — not a single duck to report. The exclusive zero club, so often bursting at the seams, sat entirely empty this week. Every batsman who walked to the crease found a way to trouble the scorers, however modestly. A vegan week, in the parlance of the honours board: completely free from fowl. Whether this represents a sudden and alarming outbreak of competence, or simply a quiet week for the men with the bat, only time will tell. The Duck Club does not celebrate absence, but nor does it panic — the season is long, the bowlers are keen, and 0️⃣ has a habit of finding its moment when least expected. Jim Hall sits level on 3 ducks, though across a far sterner 29 innings (10.34%) — a grafter's route to the same total. Malcolm Pay also holds 3 ducks from 31 innings (9.68%), keeping the top of the table a genuine three-way squabble. Tim Thirkell rounds out the podium proper on 3 ducks from 34 innings (8.82%) — proof that quantity of innings is no defence against quality of failure. Keith Miller brings up the rear of the top five with 2 ducks from just 7 innings (28.57%) — an efficient understudy waiting in the wings should any of the leaders falter. With four names locked on 3 apiece, the race for Duck of the Year 1978 remains wide open going into the run-in. A quiet week is, if anything, the calm before the quack. Bats up next fixture, gentlemen — the ducks will surely return. 🏏🍩

🦆 Duck News — 12–16 August 1978

3 ducks
Week 15 · 12 Aug 1978 – 16 Aug 1978
Three matches on the fixture list this week, but only one of them troubled the scorers in the way we like best. Headcorn CC away turned into a proper duck shoot for the 1st XI, with three names added to the ledger in a single innings. The other two fixtures passed off disappointingly duck-free. 🏏 1st XI — Headcorn CC (away, 12 August) Paul Brattle fell early at first drop, caught B- Penney off Dave Harden — his 1st duck of the season and his 22nd for the club from 244 innings, good enough for 27th on the all-time list. Paul remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his LPCC account with a nought on debut some years back, and he does it in fine company — his father Frank Brattle sits on 38 career ducks, with Percy (16) and brother Ian (10) also on the family honours board. A proper dynasty of the dreaded nought. On this very day in 1970, Mike Iliffe also strolled back for a duck, against Gravesend CC, on his way to 37 career ducks and 9th spot all-time. Tim Thirkell followed him back not long after, bowled by the rampant Dave Harden, his 3rd duck of the season and 39th of a distinguished career from 302 innings — 5th in the all-time standings, no less. Tim's honours board reads like a testimonial: Duck of the Year 1972, Full House, and top-10 places in both Lone Duck and Homebody Duck. He is also son of the legendary John Thirkell, the club's all-time number one on 90, and brother to Nigel on 26 — the ducking clearly runs in the blood. Tim, too, echoes Mike Iliffe's duck of this day in 1970 against Gravesend CC. Down at nine, John Bloomfield rounded things off, bowled by John Phipps for his 2nd duck of the season, his 2nd of a young LPCC career from just 14 innings, sitting 195th all-time. No honours yet for John, but the board awaits — and he too joins the on-this-day list alongside Mike Iliffe's 1970 vintage against Gravesend CC. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks, three matches, one crime scene — Headcorn CC did all the damage, with Dave Harden claiming two of the three victims and John Phipps mopping up the tail. The 2nd and 3rd XI fixtures this week were, disappointingly, entirely fowl-free. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Mike Iliffe — 7 innings, 3 ducks, 42.86% 2. Jim Hall — 27 innings, 3 ducks, 11.11% 3. Malcolm Pay — 29 innings, 3 ducks, 10.34% 4. Tim Thirkell — 33 innings, 3 ducks, 9.09% 5. Keith Miller — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% Mike Iliffe sets the pace with an eye-watering strike rate, but Tim Thirkell is quietly closing in with numbers that suggest this season's crown is very much live. 🦆 The club returns to action next week — plenty of overs left for the exclusive zero club to welcome new members. Bring your bat, and your sense of humour. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck Report — 29 July to 1 August 1978

3 ducks
Week 13 · 29 Jul 1978 – 1 Aug 1978
A quiet week in the exclusive zero club — just three ducks from two outings — but one of them is a genuine collector's item, so read on. Saturday 29 July — 1st XI away to Sibton Park CC - Robert Chance opened up and departed early, bowled David Rawlings without troubling the scorer. Season duck number 1, and his 8th for the career from 149 innings — a solid rank 68 of 951 all-time. He's also long since inscribed on the club's presumptive Retirement Duck roll from an earlier campaign, proof that even a "final" duck doesn't always stick. - At number 7, M Davison fell to Keeler for nought — and this was his very first senior innings. A proper Debutante Duck, the purest possible welcome to the crease, and with the ink barely dry he's already provisionally down for 1978's "One and Done" honours. Whether he collects a second is rather the point of the exercise. - A day for company: both Sibton Park ducks fell on the very anniversary of a 1963 nought from Gordon Fenner (1941–1973, 34 career ducks, 11th all-time), who made his against Birkenhead Grasshoppers CC. History repeating, thirty-something years on. Tuesday 1 August — Midweek XI away to Margate CC - David Carden, in at first drop, fell caught off T. Bond — the fielder's identity apparently lost to history, which feels fitting. His season and career tallies both tick to 1, from just 7 innings, good for rank 281 of 951. On this same date in 1964, Basil Sears — one of the true titans of the craft with 33 career ducks (rank 12 of 951), across a mighty 1947–1975 span — was also walking back for nought. Illustrious company for a first-timer.
