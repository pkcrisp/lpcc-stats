🦆 Duck News — 27 September 1986

1 duck
Week 22 · 27 Sep 1986
A quiet week for the hunters, but Linton Park's 1st XI still found room for one moment of ceremonial nothingness away… well, at home, against Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC. 🏏 1st XI (home) Dave Todd departed at number five, caught Geoff Lawson, bowled Geoff Collins, contributing his customary nought to the cause. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from 12 innings, tucking him in at 151st of 1080 in the all-time standings — the exclusive zero club welcomes him back like an old regular. On this very day back in 1964, Robert Murray, who turned out for Linton between 1958 and 1965 and racked up 17 ducks along the way, good for 39th all-time, also strolled back for nought against Linton Park CC. Some anniversaries are best not marked with bat in hand, Dave. 📊 Week in Summary One match, one duck — the fowl were evidently keeping their heads well down this week. Still, consistency of a very particular kind never takes a day off, and every nought moves the tally on. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Nigel Thirkell — 5 ducks, 40 innings, 12.5% - Trevor Back — 4 ducks, 11 innings, 36.36% - Matt Large — 4 ducks, 16 innings, 25.0% - Allan Cooper — 3 ducks, 11 innings, 27.27% - Dave Todd — 3 ducks, 11 innings, 27.27% Dave Todd's fresh addition draws him level with Allan Cooper at the foot of the podium — the battle for Duck of the Year is very much alive. Roll on the next fixture, when the ducks will no doubt come home to roost once more. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 20 to 24 September 1986

1 duck
Week 21 · 20 Sep 1986 – 24 Sep 1986
A lean week for the exclusive zero club — three matches played, and the ducks mostly gave Linton Park the slip. Only one soul was inducted into the art of not scoring this time round, but it's a quality entry. 1st XI v Anchorians CC Matt Large, walking in at number 8, was undone lbw to A Siveyer — a tidy, unfussy dismissal for a man who does not do things by halves in this department. It's his 4th duck of the season and the 7th of an increasingly storied career, moving him to 28 innings played and a lofty 91st of 1080 in the all-time table. Matt is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from way back, and quietly haunts three more top-10 boards — Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck — while sitting agonisingly one position short of a Full House, having now failed to score from 10 of the 11 batting slots. Only number 11 stands between him and true immortality. Splendidly, the calendar has form here too — on this very day back in 1958, club legend Stan Wratten also strolled back for nought, against Tilling Steven no less. Wratten, who graced Linton from 1943 to 1984, finished with 35 career ducks and sits 14th of 1080 all-time, a proud member of the Quacking Together honours alongside John Ames. Matt keeps good company across the decades. The other two fixtures passed off in disappointingly fowl-free fashion — no additions to report, which either means good batting or a shortage of quality opening bowling. We'll assume the former. Week in summary One duck from three matches is a quiet return by Duck Club standards — consistency of a very particular kind, just not the kind we usually celebrate here. Still, Matt Large has done his bit to keep the season ticking over nicely. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Nigel Thirkell — 5 ducks, 39 innings (12.82%) 2. Trevor Back — 4 ducks, 11 innings (36.36%) 3. Matt Large — 4 ducks, 15 innings (26.67%) 4. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks, 11 innings (27.27%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 11 innings (27.27%) Trevor Back's strike rate of failure remains genuinely heroic — over a third of his innings end in nought, triumph through adversity indeed. Next fixture looms, and with it, no doubt, fresh candidates for the honours board. Pads on, guard taken, fingers crossed 🏏🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 6th to 10th September 1986

1 duck
Week 19 · 6 Sep 1986 – 10 Sep 1986
A quiet week in the paddock. Three fixtures on the go and the Club could only tempt a single soul into the exclusive zero club. Consistency, it seems, took the weekend off. 1st XI Home to Mad Hatters on Sunday, a-Hayzelton provided the round's lone offering, undone at first drop and sent back bowled a-E- Robertson. It's the fledgling first duck of his LPCC career, arriving from just 5 innings and lodging him at 499th of 1080 in the all-time standings — plenty of company still to overtake. Neatly, the calendar obliged with a spot of history: on this very day in 1977, none other than club great Tim Thirkell also trudged off for nought, against St Just CC. Thirkell went on to finish with 56 ducks for the Club, good enough for 4th on the all-time roll of dishonour, a Duck of the Year winner, and a fixture on the Full House, Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens besides. Fine company to keep, even in defeat. Elsewhere The other two matches of the period passed off entirely duck-free — every batsman troubling the scorers, however modestly. Whether that's good form or simply bad luck for the Duck Club is a matter of perspective. Week in Summary - Matches played: 3 - Ducks recorded: 1 - A rare week where the hunters returned home with barely a feather to show for it. The pond will refill soon enough. Jim Hall — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) Trevor Back's 40% strike rate remains the standout act of self-sabotage this season — four ducks from just ten trips to the crease is triumph through adversity of the very highest order. 🍩 Onwards to the next fixture, where the art of not scoring will surely find fresh volunteers. Bats at the ready, gentlemen — the ducks are watching. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 DUCK REPORT — Linton Park Tour XI, 31 August–1 September 1986

2 ducks
Week 18 · 31 Aug 1986 – 1 Sep 1986
The touring party rolled into Cornwall for a two-match sojourn against Mullion CC, and true to tourist form the exclusive zero club found two willing new subscribers on day one. The second fixture, remarkably, passed off with the bat behaving itself throughout — more on that anomaly below. 🏏 Tour XI v Mullion CC (away) Nigel Thirkell strode out at first drop and departed shortly after, bowled by White, for his 5th duck of the summer. That takes him to a formidable 38 for his LPCC career — 11th on the all-time leaderboard of 1,079 duck-collectors — and keeps his grip firmly on the 1986 Duck of the Year crown, a title he's not about to relinquish lightly. Add in his standing among the Tourist Duck top 10 and this was very much business as usual on foreign soil. Ducking, it should be noted, rather runs in the family: father John sits on 90 career noughts, brother Tim on 56, brother Simon on 17 — a Thirkell dynasty built on the art of not scoring. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1980, Allan Cooper also trudged off for nought against Staplehurst CC, en route to 36 career ducks and a spot at number 12 in the all-time list. Piers McDonald, at seven, edged behind — caught by White off the bowling of Baker — for his 2nd duck of the season and, with career and season tallies matching, his 2nd in senior cricket from just 9 innings. He joins the Lone Duck top 10 and, fresh off the same away trip, the Tourist Duck top 10 too. And as with his teammate, history rhymed rather than repeated: the same 1980 date that undid Allan Cooper against Staplehurst also proved the last word for many a Linton man over the years — Piers now keeps that particular anniversary good company. 📅 Week in Summary Two matches on tour, two ducks, both falling in the same innings at Mullion — a tidy hunting ground for the Duck Club, if nowhere else. The follow-up fixture, by contrast, was a strictly vegan affair: all XI got off the mark, a rare and faintly suspicious outbreak of competence from a touring squad usually so reliable for noughts. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Nigel Thirkell — 35 inns, 5 ducks (14.3%) - Trevor Back — 9 inns, 4 ducks (44.4%) - Simon Thirkell — 9 inns, 3 ducks (33.3%) - Allan Cooper — 10 inns, 3 ducks (30.0%) - Jim Hall — 12 inns, 3 ducks (25.0%) Nigel leads the chase in some style, with brother Simon lurking ominously in third — a family photo finish for 1986 honours is not out of the question. 🥇0️⃣ Next fixture beckons, and with it, no doubt, fresh recruits to the club. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆🍩

🦆 DUCK NEWS — Cornwall Tour, 23–29 August 1986 🦆

4 ducks
Week 17 · 23 Aug 1986 – 29 Aug 1986
Down on tour and far from home comforts, the Duck Club still found time to set up stall in the West Country, with four ducks harvested across two matches against Falmouth CC and Helston CC. Thursday 28th — v Falmouth CC Opening the innings and opening the tour account, David Castle departed for nought, caught Paul Stephens bowled Keith Meneer. His third duck of the season lifts him to 7 for his LPCC career — 88th of 1079 all time from 51 innings — and keeps him snug in the Tourist Duck top 10, a club within a club for those who can't resist laying an egg on the road. On this very day in 1974, Roland Parr was doing much the same against Gravesend CC. Friday 29th — v Helston CC A tail-end collapse of genuinely ceremonial proportions saw three wickets fall for three ducks in a single innings. Piers McDonald (No.7) went first, caught P Holloway bowled J Bain, for his maiden LPCC duck — 1st of the season and 1st of his career from just 7 innings, straight into 439th of 1079 on the all-time list. A brand new member of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top 10s. Richard Ashby (No.8) followed, bowled C Stubbs, his 27th career duck (23rd of 1079 all time, from 214 innings) and, with a nod to the Ducks in a Row board, his fifth consecutive innings without troubling the scorers. Consistency of a very particular kind. Simon Thirkell (No.9) completed the set, also bowled C Stubbs, his third duck of the season and 17th of a career now standing at 66 innings (40th of 1079). Simon sits proudly in the 50 Before a 50 club (59 ducks against a top score of just 48) and was Duck of the Year back in 1983 — hardly a surprise given the surname, with father John (90 career ducks), brothers Tim (56) and Nigel (37), and cousin Stuart (1) all paid-up Duck Club royalty in their own right. Fittingly, all three Helston victims share the same echo down the years: Stan Wratten, who made his own duck on this date in 1954 against D Hambrook XI, on his way to 35 career ducks and 14th spot on the all-time board. 📊 Week in Summary Four matches on tour, four ducks, the lot falling across just two innings — including a triple strike in the Helston tail. No pairs and no family affairs this week, just honest toil in the Cornish sun. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Trevor Back — 4 ducks, 9 innings (44.4%) 2. Nigel Thirkell — 4 ducks, 33 innings (12.1%) 3. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 8 innings (37.5%) 4. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30.0%) 5. Jim Hall — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25.0%) Simon Thirkell's tour exploits keep him within striking distance of the summit — can he run down Trevor Back before the season's out? 🦆 The tour rolls on and home fixtures loom soon after — plenty more scope for the ducks to follow. See you all at the crease. 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 16 to 20 August 1986 🦆

3 ducks
Week 16 · 16 Aug 1986 – 20 Aug 1986
A modest but perfectly formed harvest this week: three ducks from three matches, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its members with the usual mix of ceremony and sympathy. 🏏 Saturday — 1st XI at Whitstable CC A grim afternoon on the road saw Linton lose two men without troubling the scorer. Nigel Thirkell, in at three, was bowled by R Mills for his 4th duck of the season and 37th of a long and storied career — 11th on the all-time list from 562 innings. With the season stamped 1986, Nigel currently sits proud atop the Duck of the Year 1986 standings, a crown he will no doubt defend jealously. On this very day in 1969, Percy Brattle (1934–1973) also fell for nought against Mote A, his 16th of an eventual tally of the same number — Nigel walks in fine company. Moments later Jim Hall, at six, edged behind for his 3rd of the season and a startling 65th career duck — good enough for 2nd on the all-time roll from 414 innings. Jim too echoes Percy Brattle's 1969 nought. And with Nigel dismissed alongside him, the two also share the club's Only Two Quacks honour between them — a fitting frame for a joint collapse this weekend. Nigel's duck-hunting is rather a family trade — father John Thirkell tops the entire club's all-time list on 90, brothers Tim (56) and Simon (16) hardly innocent either. The apple, and indeed the duck, rarely falls far from the tree. 🌙 Midweek XI at Gravesend CC Trevor Back was bowled by Spencer Nye for his 4th duck of the season, taking him to 26 for his career — 25th on the all-time list from 190 innings. On this day back in 1944, W Bunting made a duck of his own against KCC Maidstone, the 5th of his eventual haul. Trevor's season is shaping up to be a vintage one for all the wrong reasons. 📊 Week in Summary Three matches, three ducks, no repeat offenders and no family affairs this week — just honest, solitary failure, evenly spread across the club. Whitstable claimed a brace in one innings, Gravesend settled for a single scalp. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Trevor Back — 4 ducks / 9 innings (44.4%) - Nigel Thirkell — 4 ducks / 30 innings (13.3%) - Allan Cooper — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.3%) - Matt Large — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) - Jim Hall — 3 ducks / 12 innings (25.0%) Trevor leads on ruthless efficiency, Nigel on sheer volume — the race for 1986 Duck of the Year is far from settled. Next fixture awaits. Pads on, guard taken, and the zero club stands ready to welcome its newest members. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 9 to 13 August 1986

4 ducks
Week 15 · 9 Aug 1986 – 13 Aug 1986
Four ducks laid across three fixtures this week, with the exclusive zero club welcoming two fresh entries and reshuffling the season's leaderboard nicely into a proper scrap. Saturday 9th — Headcorn CC (away) Nigel Thirkell fell in fine company, caught M- Watson bowled Ali Lee at first wicket down — his 3rd duck of the 1986 season and 36th of an extraordinary career, good enough for 12th on the club's all-time roll of dishonour (of 1079). Nigel currently holds the Duck of the Year 1986 crown and is a fixture of the Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens, half of the "Only Two Quacks" double act with Jim Hall — on this very day in 1964, his own father John Thirkell (1943–1977, still the club's all-time record holder with 90) was doing exactly the same thing, albeit against Harrietsham CC rather than Headcorn. Dave Todd joined him in the pavilion soon after, caught and bowled by Dave Harden at number 7. It's his 2nd duck of the season and, remarkably, his 2nd in senior cricket full stop — from just 9 innings, a start to a career the honours board will be watching closely. Like Nigel, Dave shares his moment with John Thirkell's 1964 vintage. Sunday/Monday — Benenden CC (away) The Thirkell name struck twice in as many days. Simon Thirkell joined the queue at Benenden, caught and bowled by M- Oldaker for his 2nd duck of the season and 16th of his career — a fully paid-up member of the 50 Before a 50 club (59 ducks projected against a top score of 48) and a former Duck of the Year himself back in 1983. Simon's departure echoed one from 1958, when Roy Clarke made a duck on this same date against Harrietsham CC. Allan Cooper kept him company, run out for nought at number 8 — his 3rd of the season, and 36th for his career, drawing him level with brother-in-arms Nigel Thirkell near the very top of the all-time table, 11th to Nigel's 12th. Allan is a serial pair-maker on the honours board, sharing "Only Two Quacks" billing with no fewer than three club stalwarts — Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back. Like Simon, he shares his date with Roy Clarke's 1958 effort. Week in summary: four ducks from three matches, split evenly between Headcorn and Benenden, with the Thirkell dynasty responsible for half the week's tally between brothers Nigel and Simon — sons of the legendary John Thirkell, evidently keeping it in the family. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Trevor Back — 3 ducks, 7 innings (42.86%) - Allan Cooper — 3 ducks, 9 innings (33.33%) - Matt Large — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30.00%) - Paul Gibson — 3 ducks, 24 innings (12.50%) - Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks, 27 innings (11.11%) Five players locked on three apiece — the tightest Duck of the Year race in years, and Nigel's crown is anything but safe. Roll on the next fixture, when the honours board will doubtless need updating all over again. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 2nd August 1986

0 ducks
Week 14 · 2 Aug 1986
A single fixture on the books this week, and — brace yourselves — not a single duck fell to it. Eleven Linton batsmen, one solitary match, and every last one of them got off the mark. The exclusive zero club held no inductions, no re-elections, and not so much as a golden nick to report. It happens. Even the most reliable poultry farm has the odd barren week, and this was one of them. The art of not scoring is, apparently, a fickle mistress — she simply didn't fancy Linton Park on the 2nd of August. Somewhere, a scorer sat with pencil poised and nothing whatsoever to underline. Do not mistake this for good news. A quiet week for the Duck Club merely means the drought is loading — statistically, someone is due, and due soon. Sharpen your pencils. Every single visit to the crease has ended in nought. Truly a purist - Allan Cooper — 2 ducks in 8 innings (25%) — quietly building a season to remember, for all the wrong reasons Trevor Back leads the chase for Duck of the Year, but with J- Milloy running a perfect 100% strike rate, nobody in this table should be feeling comfortable. That's the lot for this period — a rare and quiet week in the annals of Linton ignominy. Normal service, we're reliably informed, resumes next match. Keep your bats loose and your expectations lower still. 🦆🍩0️⃣

Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches, both bowled, both from the same Midweek XI innings against Leigh On Sea — proof, if any were needed, that misfortune enjoys company. The rest of the club's batsmen kept their heads down and their bats employed. A tidy little haul rather than a bumper one, but the Duck Club doesn't do quiet weeks for long. 🦆 Duck News — Sunday 6th July 1986

3 ducks
Week 10 · 6 Jul 1986
A tidy little away day at Reeds Aylesford for the 1st XI, and the exclusive zero club welcomed three new entries in the very same innings. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏏 1st XI at Reeds Aylesford Trevor Back went first, caught G Wood off the bowling of Michael Beeney at number three. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 24th of a Linton career now 186 innings deep, keeping him at a lofty 29th in the all-time table — a man who turns up regularly on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top 10s, and has standing dishonours-board entries alongside both Allan Cooper and Jon Harris.
