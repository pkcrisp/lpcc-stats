🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1998 · 1998 — 2 matches

Sun 27 Sep 1998 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDLWW

Sunday 27 September 1998 saw us out on the road as visitors, and when the coin came down my way I didn't dither for a second — bat first, no question, on a day that looked made for runs.

And runs there were. Paul Gibson set the tone with a thoroughly well-made 78, batting with the sort of authority that makes a captain's life easy, while Michael Beeney chipped in a handsome fifty of his own, 56 to his name, and Clive Newman added a brisk 45 for good measure. Not everyone's afternoon sparkled quite so brightly — poor Peter Whyte trudged back for a duck, the sort of dismissal that draws sympathetic groans and rather too much laughter from the boundary. Even so, 235 for 7 from our thirty overs was a total to be proud of, and gave the bowlers something substantial to defend.

Defend it they did, seeing us home by 26 runs in the end, comfortable enough to enjoy the tea rather than chew our nails through it. It rounds off a fine season for Clive Newman too, who now sits second in the 1998 1st XI run charts with 610 runs to his name, while over on Sundays Roly Edwards leads the 2nd XI's charts with 70.

All told, a thoroughly good day for the club, the sort of Sunday that reminds you why you turn up in whites in the first place.

Sat 26 Sep 1998 🧢 1st XIv BC Bidborough CC · HomeformWWDLW

Saturday 26 September brought Bidborough to Linton Park, and having won the toss I had no hesitation in taking first knock — the right call, I felt, and one I was rather pleased with myself about by teatime.

We batted our way to 167 all out in 36.5 overs, a total built on Clive Newman's fine 42 and handsomely backed up by Brian Piper's 37 and David Castle's 21. Not every name will trouble the record books, mind — Nigel Thirkell and Jon Harris both departed for golden ducks, the sort of dismissal that gets you a consoling pint but not much sympathy.

With the ball, Bidborough fought hard, reaching 160 all out in 39.4 overs on the back of P Wood's 39 and useful contributions from Mark Mitchell (33) and J Waller (22). Stephen Towner and A Dobson went for ducks of their own — misery loves company. But the day belonged to yours truly: I bowled the spell of my life, 8 for 44, a five-for and then some, richly deserved if I say so myself. Daniel Thirkell (1-19) and John Taylor (1-26) chipped in nicely, while Nigel Thirkell bowled three miserly maidens to keep the squeeze on. With the bat I'd added a workmanlike 16 earlier — sandwich filling to the top order's fine work.

We got over the line by seven runs. A tight, twitchy finish, but a thoroughly good day for the club — and I'll allow myself just this once to say the captain had rather a say in it.

Sun 20 Sep 1998 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLWWDL

Skipper's Log — Sunday 20 September 1998, and a rare day when the toss actually mattered and I got it right, electing to bat against Anchorians on our own patch. What followed was, by our modest standards, something of a batting clinic: 220/5 from our 35 overs, built on a proper platform from Piers McDonald, who cruised past fifty to 72, while I found some unexpected form of my own, carrying my bat all the way to 105 not out. Three figures — I still can't quite believe it looking back at the scorebook. Not every contribution sparkled, mind — Richard Piper and Trevor Back both made the lonely walk back for ducks, and the club ledger duly noted it.

Two hundred and twenty is a total that asks questions of any side, and to their credit Anchorians never really found the answers. Stuart Cheesman set the tone with 2-15, Jon Harris chipped in with 2-19, and young Daniel Thirkell picked up a wicket for the princely cost of a single run. Trevor Back, having atoned nicely for his duck, bowled two tidy maidens and, in doing so, edged past John Thirkell into 11th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — a fine bit of history-making on a Sunday afternoon. Adam Pooley, it must be said, had a rather testing time behind the stumps, with 11 byes leaking through — we shall put that down to a lively pitch and say no more.

Anchorians were bowled out for 115 in 31 overs, and we walked off 105 runs to the good. A personal contribution of 105 was satisfying indeed, but this was very much a team performance from top to bottom. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a result to make the long drive home feel considerably shorter.

Sat 19 Sep 1998 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformDLWWD

Saturday the nineteenth of September, and Hastings & St Leonards Priory rolled up as our final visitors before autumn set in properly. Won the toss, and with a decent look at the square I fancied runs first — in we went to bat.

Paul Gibson chipped in a handsome 48, Clive Newman 32, and between them they gave the innings a proper backbone even as wickets went down at the other end.

Collins (36) offering sturdy support, they cantered home in 24.3 overs — 176 for 2. Seven wickets is a comprehensive beating by any measure, and there's no dressing it up as anything else. Heads up all the same — we scored well enough to win most weeks, we just met a man in irrepressible form. Back to the nets, and better luck next Saturday.

Wed 16 Sep 1998 🧢 Midweek XIv Tunbridge Wells Wednesday CC · HomeformWDDLD

Won the toss on a fine Wednesday evening and, with visitors Tunbridge Wells Wednesday in town, I fancied runs on the board and sent us in. It proved a mixed blessing. Twenty-seven extras arrived like a benevolent tail-ender, and we closed on 163 all out from 41 overs — respectable, if not one to frighten the neighbours.

I did my bit too, taking 2-32 with five maidens thrown in for good measure — not a run given away lightly. Iain Barker went the way of McDonald and Cheesman, back in the pavilion without scoring, though Withers (22) and Tedder (20) held firm enough to see their side to 109/9 from 47 overs and safety.

A landmark evening for Brattle, whose four wickets nudged him past Gordon Fenner into eighth on the club's all-time list — a round of applause well earned. Honours, in the end, were shared: a draw that satisfied no one and disappointed no one either. Fair result, on reflection.
