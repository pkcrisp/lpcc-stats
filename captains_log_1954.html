🗓️ 25 Sep – 01 Oct 1954 · 1954 — 2 matches

Sun 26 Sep 1954 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDD

Sunday last, with the gazette gods smiling on us at last, I won the toss and had no hesitation in putting Linton Park CC in — sorry, in putting the opposition in, and settling us in to bat first, as any captain with a scrap of sense would when the wicket looks kind and the tea urn is already on.

We did rather more than settle: 12/1 from four overs told only half the story, for what followed was the sort of innings a captain dreams of and rarely gets to write about himself without blushing. I found some form and carried my bat to 95 not out, and I'll allow myself the indulgence of saying it felt rather good to lead from the front for once rather than merely from the boundary edge with a scorebook and a worried expression. J. Coomber weighed in handsomely with 38, and Tony Tipples chipped in a brisk 23, the pair of them doing the sensible thing of keeping the scoreboard ticking while I did the showing off.

There was rather more to celebrate than the runs themselves — I brought up 1,000 career runs for the club in the process, and my 95 nudged me past Richard Harris into 14th on the all-time run list, which is the sort of statistic one tries not to mention twice and fails. I lead the Married run charts for the season too, while Stan Wratten flies the flag admirably for the Singles.

In the end honours were shared, a draw with neither side able to force the issue — but on a day like this, with milestones tumbling and the tea just right, I shan't complain one bit.

Sat 25 Sep 1954 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · AwayformWDDDW

The toss went against us, as it so often does at West Farleigh, and I trudged out to lead the side into the field wondering if the wicket would offer anything at all for our bowlers. As it turned out, it offered rather a lot. West Farleigh were bundled out for 114 in 36.4 overs, S. Kitchen's 27 the only innings of real substance, while Reg Fermor, A. Heard and R Gibbons all trooped back for ducks without troubling the scorers. Taylor was the chief tormentor, spinning through them for 6-64 with six maidens to his name — bowling of real quality, the sort that has batsmen playing for turn that isn't there. I found some rhythm of my own, finishing with 4-24, and was quietly chuffed to learn afterwards that it had carried me past a hundred career wickets and up to eighth on the club's all-time list — the kind of milestone one tries to look nonchalant about in the pavilion and fails entirely.

With 115 to chase, Ron Aiston played the innings of the day, unbeaten on 78 and the model of calm authority, well supported by John Basten's brisk 21. Tom Osborn's tidy 13 nudged him past John Ames into fifteenth on the run-scorers' roll of honour, and we cantered home at 168 for 5 in 42 overs. Tony Levick, sitting second in this season's run charts on 347, continues to bat like a man who knows exactly where he's going. A fine win by five wickets — onwards we go.

🗓️ 18 Sep – 24 Sep 1954 · 1954 — 2 matches

Sun 19 Sep 1954 🧢 1st XIv TC Tunbridge Wells CC · HomeformDWDDD

Under the shade of St Mark's oaks on the 19th, with Tunbridge Wells CC our visitors, I called it right at the toss and stuck us in the field — and for once the hunch paid off handsomely.

Their innings had a proper old-fashioned shape to it: Charles Wicker anchored things with a fine 51, Alan Burton chipped in a tidy 39 and James Scrace 22, the pair adding a crucial 57 for the third wicket that threatened to set us a stiff target. But once that partnership broke, the wheels came off in glorious fashion — 134 for 5 became 146 for 8 before they'd barely had time to reset the sightscreen, Reg Hobbins departing without troubling the scorers. Gordon Fenner was the chief tormentor, spinning his way to a magnificent 6 for 45 with three maidens thrown in for good measure, while Ray Tippen mopped up with 2 for 24. All out 149 for 9 felt, by the end, like a relief for them rather than a total to defend.

Chasing it down, we made ourselves entirely comfortable. Tony Levick played the anchor role beautifully for an unbeaten 45, William Buck supported nicely with 31, and I was happy enough to contribute 26 not out while the extras column — a generous 32 — did its bit too. We coasted home by 8 wickets with overs to spare.

A day thick with milestones besides — Stan Wratten's 100th appearance, Tippen's 50th career wicket, Levick and Tippen both climbing the club's all-time lists. All told, a thoroughly good day for Linton Park.

Sat 18 Sep 1954 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformLDWDD

Saturday saw us welcome Bearsted CC, and having called correctly at the toss, I didn't spend more than half a second thinking about it before opting to bat — some decisions make themselves. What followed was a proper treat: 188 for the loss of just one wicket in 44 overs, built on a marvellous unbroken innings of 92 from Tom Osborn and fine support from Basil Sears, who cruised nicely past his own fifty to finish on 89. Two lads well set and, frankly, making the rest of us look rather superfluous with the bat.

Bearsted's reply never quite got away from us, though J Bastin batted with real composure to see them to 78 for 4 from his 35 overs, unbeaten on 45 and doing most of the carrying himself. Our bowling was the story of the innings — Michael Morton probing away for figures of 2 for 19, with Stan Wratten and Ray Tippen chipping in a wicket apiece. Special mention for Ray, who bowled six maidens and simply refused to be hit off his length; not a run given away lightly all afternoon.

With Morton now sitting second in the club's 1954 wicket-takers list with 38 to his name, the season's shaping up nicely for him. In the end neither side could force it, and honours were shared. A draw, then — but on this evidence, a thoroughly satisfying one to be part of.

🗓️ 11 Sep – 17 Sep 1954 · 1954 — 2 matches

Sun 12 Sep 1954 🧢 1st XIv MT Medway Towns · HomeformWLDWD

From the captain's chair — Sunday 12 September 1954. We hosted Dummy Site, and the coin gave us nothing to work with; they won the toss, elected to bat, and off we trotted into the field to make the best of it.

Best of it we duly made. Forty-four overs of honest endeavour saw the visitors bowled out for a modest 77, R. Griffiths their one real resister with 48, while H. Doust, A. Lawson and G. Woodward all trudged back for ducks, done in by our attack. John Ames was quite magnificent, 4 for 11, with Stan Wratten (3-14) and Ray Tippen (2-26) offering sterling support, and Tony Snell tying up an end with five miserly maidens — the sort of spell that makes tea taste sweeter.

Chasing 78 should never trouble a side with William Buck in this sort of form, and so it proved — 34 from him, 20 from Tom Osborn, and we cruised to 62 for 2 off 16.5 overs before the heavens, or at least the umpires, decided honours would be shared.

A word too for Ray Tippen, whose brace of wickets eased him past H. Freed to 21st on the club's all-time list — a small but pleasing footnote in a season where Buck (785 runs) and Ames (34 wickets) continue to lead our charts. A draw, then, but one that felt rather more like a victory denied than a defeat avoided.

🗓️ 04 Sep – 10 Sep 1954 · 1954 — 1 match

Sun 05 Sep 1954 🧢 1st XIv GK Gillingham CC, Kent · HomeformWLWLD

Sunday last, Gillingham CC came to Linton Park, and I called correctly at the toss. With the sun out and the square looking a good one, I had no hesitation in choosing to bat.

We were made to work for our runs at times, 172 for 8 from our 47 overs the eventual return. Tony Levick batted beautifully for his 83, well supported by William Buck's 43. Sandwiched between those two fine innings were three ducks — John Larking, Richard Harris, and yours truly, whose own contribution to the scorebook amounted to precisely nothing. The less said, the better.

Defending 172, we set about Gillingham with real purpose. Gordon Fenner was magnificent — five wickets for 39, four of them maidens, bowling of real discipline — well backed by John --'s Taylor's three and Alan Wilkes's one. A Fairbrass battled to 57 for the visitors, but wickets fell steadily elsewhere, J Gelder and X Jenner both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and Gillingham were all out for 134 in 35.1 overs. Eleven byes crept into the tally, a rare off day behind the stumps for Tony Levick.

There was cause for celebration beyond the result, too: Tony Levick reached 1,000 career runs for the club, while William Buck's 43 lifted him above me to the very top of our all-time run-scorers list — a bittersweet line in the ledger for your captain. John Ames's solitary wicket nudged him up to 11th on the all-time wicket-takers list. All told, a thoroughly deserved 38-run win and two points banked — not a bad way to spend a Sunday, ducks and all.

🗓️ 28 Aug – 03 Sep 1954 · 1954 — 2 matches

Sun 29 Aug 1954 🧢 1st XIv DX D Hambrook XI · HomeformDWLWL

Sunday 29 August, and D Hambrook XI rolled up hoping for sunshine and a soft touch — they got the sunshine. Put in first, our attack did the honours before lunch and after it in equal measure: Frank Taylor found his groove for 3-34, I chipped in with 3-48 of my own, and Tony Snell bowled with the sort of miserly discipline that has opening batsmen muttering into their gloves — five maidens and 2-39. R Aiston's 42 and J Basten's 28 kept the visitors respectable, and they closed on 162/9 from their 61 overs, which felt like a total we could chase down with a bit of application.

Application, alas, is not always our strong suit. We laboured to 123/6 from our 32, saved from real embarrassment by Maurice Fenner, who middled it beautifully for 66 and, in doing so, quietly overtook Arthur Cornwallis into 9th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — no fuss, no fireworks, just a proper county-standard fifty from a man who makes the rest of us look like we're batting with a stick. Tom Osborn and John Ames both weighed in with 20 apiece, while poor Stan Wratten will be buying the teas for a while yet after his golden duck.

Elsewhere, Tony Snell's two wickets nudged him past Dennis Smith into 14th on the all-time wicket-takers' list, and sits proudly 5th in this year's bowling charts with 22 to his name, with Maurice not far behind him in the batting table at 2nd on 284. All told: honours shared, pride intact, and a draw that flattered nobody and disappointed nobody either.

Sat 28 Aug 1954 🧢 1st XIv HC Hunton Wanderers CC · AwayformLDWLW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 28 August 1954, away to Hunton Wanderers CC.

Won the toss and, in a fit of optimism I'd rather forget, stuck us in. What followed was 15.4 overs of grim theatre: 19 all out. Basil Sears, William Blight and I all contributed golden ducks to the cause — three of us marching back without troubling the scorer, which even by Linton Park standards takes some doing. I shan't dwell on my own effort beyond saying the less said, the better.

Nineteen to defend is not a total, it's a dare, and to their enormous credit the lads dared. Michael Morton did the damage with 2-18, ably supported by Alan Wilkes (1-4, and miserly enough with three maidens to make the openers weep) and Gerald Wright (1-4). For a spell it almost felt like cricket rather than damage limitation, but Hunton crossed the line at 36/4 from 16 overs and took it by six wickets.

A shaft of light amid the wreckage: Morton's brace nudges him past Bert Buckoke into 16th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list, while Wilkes continues to lead the 1954 First XI charts with 53 wickets — the bowling, at least, remains a going concern.

Verdict: not our day, not our week, arguably not our season, batting-wise. Heads up, chins out, back to the nets.

Sat 21 Aug 1954 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformLDLDW

John Thirkell's Captain's Log — Charles Arkcoll, 21 August 1954

Lost the toss again, which these days feels less like bad luck and more like a personal vendetta from the coin itself. Charles Arkcoll elected to bat, and for a good long while it looked as though we'd made a hash of it, until Michael Morton decided otherwise. Bowling with real venom, he ran through their order to finish with a magnificent 6-45 — a five-for and then some, richly deserved and, as it turns out, a landmark spell that carries him past Bill Hall into 17th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list. Gerald Wright chipped in with 3-19 and eight miserly maidens, Frank Taylor took one, and between us we reduced the visitors to 97 all out in 46.1 overs, with Stan Wratten's 32 the only real resistance and three of their number departing without troubling the scorer.

Ninety-seven should have been eminently gettable. It was not gettable, at least not by us. Our reply folded for 51 in a chastening 19.4 overs, Frank Taylor's 21 the solitary point of pride on an otherwise threadbare card. I fell for nought myself, and we shall draw a veil over it — as we shall, less successfully, over the further ducks of Michael Morton and Gerald Wright, both of whom had rather better luck with the ball than the bat.

Beaten by 46 runs, then, and no arguing with it. Morton's landmark spell was the one shaft of sunlight in an otherwise damp afternoon. We'll be better for it next week.

Mon 02 Aug 1954 🧢 1st XIv LX Lord Cornwallis XI · HomeformWLWLD

John Thirkell's Match Report — Monday 2nd August 1954

Well, the coin let us down again — you'd think after all these years I'd learn to call the other side, but no, Lord Cornwallis XI had first use of the willow, and settled in to make a proper afternoon of it. Webb top-scored with a watchful 39, Marsham chipped in with 35 and Harland 25, the three of them stitching together a 63-run stand that looked, for a while, like it might bat us out of the game entirely. But cricket has a way of turning, and turn it did: from 121 for 3 to 140 for 6 in the blink of an eye, as Alan Wilkes span his way to a quite unplayable 6 for 65, with John Ames busy at the other end, miserly through three maidens on his way to 4 for 68. All out for 190 off 54.1 overs — a total, but not an insurmountable one.

Our reply began in fine style, William Buck and Stan Wratten putting on 72 for the first wicket before the first breakthrough arrived, and Maurice Fenner then playing the innings of the day, a fine half-century well supported by Tony Levick's 39 and Buck's 28. I will confess, with as much dignity as I can muster, that my own contribution was a duck — the less said the better, though I note Tony's 39 nudged him past F S W Cornwallis into 14th on the club's all-time run charts, and Alan's six-for lifted him past me into 7th among the wicket-takers, which is either a proud day for the club or a pointed one for me, depending on how you look at it.

All out for 146, forty-four runs shy. Not our day. Heads up, and back to the nets.

🗓️ 24 Jul – 30 Jul 1954 · 1954 — 1 match

Sat 24 Jul 1954 🧢 1st XIv Town Malling CC · HomeformDLLWL

Lost the toss again — Town Malling took one look at our square and decided batting first was the wiser course. Not that I could complain once we got out among them: put in to field, we bowled them out for a paltry 69 in 27.4 overs, with both A. Checksfield and J. Hepsted trudging back for ducks.
