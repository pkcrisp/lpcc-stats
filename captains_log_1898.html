Mon 01 Aug 1898 🧢 1st XIv RC Rochester CC · HomeformLLLDW

Winning the toss on a bright first of August, I had no hesitation in sending us in — Linton Park pitches in high summer are usually good for runs, and I fancied ours would be a home for batting rather than survival. So it proved, up to a point. H G Smith led the way with a fine 38, I contributed 36 in support, and B W Haynes chipped in a useful 25, while our extras column swelled by a generous 34 — not the tidiest way to build a total, but we'll take runs however the umpires care to gift them. Eventually the whole side filed back to the pavilion, leaving Rochester a target to chase.

And chase it they did. F M Atkins played the innings of the day with a commanding 71, well backed by E J Moon's 65 and O Tritton's unbeaten 58 — three half-centuries is a punishing amount of quality batting for any afternoon. Only H Newsome departed cheaply, sent back for a duck without troubling the scorers. With the ball I found some rhythm of my own, taking 4 wickets without conceding a run, and J N Tonge chipped in with one — but it wasn't nearly enough to halt the flow, and Rochester came home by 5 wickets.

There was a silver lining amid the gloom: H G Smith's 38 nudged him past Captain H Hamilton into 14th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — one for the mantelpiece indeed. Ernest Cruttenden still leads our 1898 run charts with 147, and I'm pleased to say I lead the wickets with 17. Not our day, all told — heads up, and back to the nets.

🗓️ 23 Jul – 29 Jul 1898 · 1898 — 1 match

Sat 23 Jul 1898 🧢 1st XIv Town Malling CC · HomeformLLLLD

Skipper's Log — Saturday 23 July 1898. We hosted Town Malling CC, and having called correctly at the toss, I had no hesitation in putting them into the field. Not a decision that always covers a captain in glory, but on this occasion the lads made it look positively inspired.

Ernest Cruttenden set about the bowling with real purpose, compiling a fine 73, well supported by F Smith (44) and H W Smith (41) as we built a total to be proud of. Only C N Watney let the side down, undone for a duck without so much as troubling the scorers — these things happen to the best of us, and no doubt he'll make amends next time out.

With the ball, we set about Town Malling in similarly ruthless fashion, dismissing W Cole, F G Busbridge and F Longhurst without a run between them, though T J Dewe played a lone hand of real quality, unbeaten on 96, ably assisted by W Cheale (31) and X Humphreys (23). It was not quite enough, and we came home 80 runs to the good.

A particular note for the club history books: Cruttenden's innings sees him leapfrog J N Tonge into fifth on our all-time run-scorers list, and he now sits proudly atop this season's 1st XI charts with 126 runs. All told, a thoroughly good day for Linton Park — the tea was well earned, and so, for once, was the walk home.

Sat 16 Jul 1898 🧢 1st XIv YC Yalding CC · HomeformLLLLL

It called correctly, chose to bat, and never looked back — 47 of my own alongside J N Tonge's classy 93 and a solid 37 from Arthur Cornwallis, with extras chipping in a generous 53 to round out our total. Yalding gave as good as they got in reply, S Mendel making a fluent 51 with support from A W Reid (37) and H T Manwaring (33), and though Tonge did the early damage with the ball (3-0), backed up nicely by W H Forshaw (2-0) and George Champion (1-0), the Yalding lot simply refused to be shifted the last mile.

It ended, as these things sometimes must on a warm Saturday with more will than time remaining, in a draw — honours shared, teapots refilled, nobody quite satisfied and nobody quite disappointed either.

Worth a line in the club history book, mind: Tonge's 93 sees him leapfrog Ernest Cruttenden into 5th on our all-time run list, and his three wickets nudge him past Tom Peach Snr into 8th among the wicket-takers — not a bad afternoon's work for one man. He also sits proudly atop this season's run charts, while George Champion continues to lead the wicket column with 13 for the summer.

As for the cricket itself: a good toss won, a good total posted, and a stubborn lot from Yalding who wouldn't be beaten. I'll take a draw against that kind of resistance and call it a fair result — though I dare say a few of the lads went home muttering about the two we didn't quite grab.
