🦆 Duck News — 25 September 1971

0 ducks
Week 22 · 25 Sep 1971
A quiet one this week, ducklings. One solitary fixture went under the microscope on 25 September, and for once the ducks made themselves scarce entirely. Every bat that walked to the crease walked back having troubled the scorer at least once — not a single nought to log, not a golden duck in sight, not so much as a nervous single-figure near miss on file. The zero club's doors stayed firmly shut. Whether this counts as a triumph of application or simply a lucky afternoon for Linton's top order is not for this correspondent to say. What we can say is that somewhere out there, the ducks are circling, sharpening their beaks, waiting for their moment. They always come back. They always do. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 Still very much live at the top of the table this season: 1️⃣ John Thirkell — 6 ducks from 17 innings (35.29%) — the early pace-setter, making a proper habit of it 2️⃣ Alan Cradduck — 5 ducks from 14 innings (35.71%) — technically the higher strike rate, if that's a thing worth boasting about 3️⃣ Dick Langridge — 3 ducks from 10 innings (30.00%) 3️⃣ Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks from 10 innings (30.00%) — the Thirkell name doing rather too much work on this table 5️⃣ Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks from 17 innings (17.65%) — getting there on volume alone A reminder that this table exists purely in the spirit of celebration, never shame. Consistency of this particular kind is its own sort of achievement. 0️⃣ A week for the "nearly men" of the honours board to breathe easy, and for the rest of us to wonder who'll be first to break the drought. Next fixture, normal service will surely resume — the ducks are patient, and so are we. See you all out there. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 18–19 September 1971

1 duck
Week 21 · 18 Sep 1971 – 19 Sep 1971
A quiet one for the exclusive zero club this week, but the honours board never truly rests. 🏏 1st XI — home to Harrietsham CC Coming in at number eight, Benny Bell found Michael Gear's bowling one delivery too many, snapped up behind the stumps by Philip Lurcock without troubling the scorers. It's his first duck of the season and a tidy 20th of his Linton career — good enough to hold him at 18th in the all-time standings out of 867 hopefuls. Triumph through adversity, as ever. On this very day back in 1959, G Martin was going through much the same ritual, falling for nought against Bearsted CC — a fellow charter member of the Debutante Duck club, whose own Linton career began with a duck on debut. The calendar, it seems, has a sense of humour. 📅 Week in Summary Two fixtures played across the weekend, and the ducks mostly kept their heads down — just the one confirmed sighting, courtesy of Benny at home to Harrietsham. The art of not scoring remains a minority pursuit for now, but rest assured the club is always recruiting. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 6 ducks in 17 innings (35.29%) - Alan Cradduck — 5 ducks in 14 innings (35.71%) - Dick Langridge — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.00%) - Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.00%) - Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks in 17 innings (17.65%) John Thirkell clings to top spot, but Alan Cradduck's superior strike rate for failure means this race is anything but settled. 🦆 Until next week — the nets await, the bowlers are sharpening up, and somewhere out there another name is one thin edge away from joining the club. See you at the crease.

1 duck

Consistency of a very particular kind. 0 ducks
Week 19 · 4 Sep 1971 – 7 Sep 1971
Three matches played. Zero ducks recorded. Let that sink in for a moment. The exclusive zero club held its doors firmly shut this week — not a single soul walked the walk of shame across all three fixtures. Every batsman who took guard apparently remembered which end of the bat goes first, denying the Club its usual weekly harvest. 🦆 Duck News — August Bank Holiday Weekend 1971

4 ducks
Week 18 · 28 Aug 1971 – 30 Aug 1971
The traditional Bank Holiday triple-header did what it always does and delivered a tidy little harvest for the exclusive zero club — four ducks across three matches, Linton Park once again proving that runs are optional and ignominy is forever. 🏏 Sunday 29th — Staplehurst CC (away) Mike Iliffe went early at four, run out for the second time this season — a chaotic dismissal that at least spares him any blame for shot selection. It's his 18th career duck, tucking him into 23rd spot on the all-time list of 867, and on this very day back in 1954 Stan Wratten was doing exactly the same thing against D Hambrook XI. Some traditions simply refuse to die. 🎗️ Monday 30th — Bank Holiday at Bearsted CC (away) A proper Bank Holiday massacre, this one, with three names added to the list in a single innings. Leading the charge, inevitably, was John Thirkell — caught Mick Gibson, bowled Bryan Elswood, for his 6th duck of the season and 73rd of a career that already sits him unrivalled at the very top of the all-time standings, number 1 of 867. The reigning Duck of the Year for 1971 extends his own lead over his own field, a man competing largely with his own legend at this point, and a proud member of the Quacking Together set alongside both Bob Arnold and William Buck down the years. On this day in 1965 Frank Brattle also fell to nought against these very same Bearsted opponents — the ground clearly has form. Not far behind him, Richard Ashby was bowled by Derek Morgan for his first duck of the season, the 24th of his career and good enough for 13th on the all-time list. Already a fixture of the Ducks in a Row club for five in succession, and a regular in the Tourist Duck top 10 — fitting, given this one came on the road too. He shares Brattle's 1965 anniversary with Thirkell above. And propping up the innings at number 11, Alan Wilkes fell to the very same Derek Morgan — the bowler's second scalp of the afternoon — for his own first duck of the season, his 22nd career, 14th on the all-time roll. A veteran of the fabled 1964 Runaway Duck campaign, when he contrived to go an entire season without a run, and a paid-up member of Only Two Quacks with both Bob Arnold and John Ames. He too keeps Frank Brattle company from 1965. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks, three matches, one very productive Bank Holiday Monday for Derek Morgan, who accounted for two of Bearsted's victims personally. Staplehurst managed one scalp by more chaotic means entirely. A quiet weekend for everyone except the scorebook's zero column. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. John Thirkell — 6 ducks / 16 innings (37.5%) 2. Alan Cradduck — 5 ducks / 11 innings (45.45%) 3. Dick Langridge — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.33%) 3. Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.33%) 5. Cliff Anscombe — 3 ducks / 19 innings (15.79%) Thirkell leads by tally, but Cradduck's strike rate remains the more terrifying statistic of the two. Roll on next weekend — the ducks never take a Bank Holiday off, and neither should you. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 22 to 25 August 1971

5 ducks
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1971 – 25 Aug 1971
Two fixtures, five ducks, and Linton's exclusive zero club took in three new arrivals in a single Wednesday innings that Gravesend will remember fondly. As ever, the art of not scoring found its practitioners. Saturday 22 August — 1st XI away at Mersham Hatch David T Lowen departed at first wicket down, bowled by W Jeanes, for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of an otherwise perfectly respectable career — 34 innings deep, ranked 115th of 867 in the all-time reckoning. On this very day in 1953, J Barclay was doing much the same against Charles Arkcoll, en route to 7 career ducks and the 1953 Duck of the Year crown before bowing out, as tradition dictates, on a duck of his own. Down at number 11, Percy Brattle offered token resistance before falling to B Campbell — his 1st of the season, 16th of a career that has already earned him the 1969 Duck of the Year sash and 26th spot in the all-time table. The Brattle name runs deep in these parts: father Frank carries 38 career ducks of his own, with kinsman Paul adding 4 more to the family ledger. Percy, too, finds a companion in history — J Barclay, 1953, same day, same fate. Wednesday 25 August — Midweek XI at home to Gravesend CC A proper collapse into the zero club here, with three ducks inside one innings. David Tolhurst, bowled by Kevin Jarvis, recorded his 1st duck of the season — and, remarkably, his 2nd in just 2 career innings, a flawless 100% record so far. A long-standing paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his first senior knock, he now finds company on this same calendar date back in 1904, when T Woollett suffered identically. Dick Langridge, trapped lbw by the same Kevin Jarvis, brought up his 3rd duck of the season and 20th of a long career — 134 innings and counting, 18th on the all-time list. Son Barry has 6 career ducks of his own, so the trait is evidently hereditary. Dick, too, tips his cap across the decades to T Woollett's 1904 effort. Nigel Thirkell completed the rout, bowled by John Casely, for his 3rd duck of the season and 5th career nought from just 18 innings, ranking 78th all-time. Nigel is a fixture of the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and shares his Only Two Quacks distinction with clubmate Jim Hall. The surname carries some weight in these records — father John Thirkell sits top of the all-time pile with a scarcely believable 72 career ducks, while sibling Tim adds 11 more, and kinsman Stuart a further 1. Nigel, like his teammates, joins T Woollett on this day in 1904. Week in summary Five ducks from two matches, with Kevin Jarvis alone accounting for two of Gravesend's victims, and Dick Langridge and Nigel Thirkell both advancing to their 3rd of the season in the very same innings — misery, it seems, loves company. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Alan Cradduck — 5 ducks / 10 innings (50.0%) 2. John Thirkell — 5 ducks / 15 innings (33.3%) 3. Dick Langridge — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.3%) 4. Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.3%) 5. Cliff Anscombe — 3 ducks / 19 innings (15.8%) Alan Cradduck still leads the chase, but with the Thirkell dynasty stacking ducks at both ends of the order, the race for 1971's crown is far from settled. Same time next fixture — the zero club never sleeps. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Club Dispatch — 8th to 11th August 1971 🏏

2 ducks
Week 15 · 8 Aug 1971 – 11 Aug 1971
Only two fixtures on the card this week, and only one of them misbehaved — but it more than made up for the quiet one. Saturday 8th August — 1st XI v Sibton Park CC (home) The afternoon's first casualty was John Thirkell, undone at number 5 by P McKen. It's their 5th duck of the summer and the 72nd of an extraordinary career spanning 682 innings — a tally that sits them at number 1 of 865 in the entire LPCC record book. The honours board reads like a roll of dishonour in itself: Duck of the Year in 1952, '54, '55 and '67, a fixture in the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, and joint custodian of Quacking Together with both Bob Arnold and William Buck. With the club deep in the 1971 season, their board already carries a Duck of the Year 1971 entry too — evidently this campaign belongs to them, running at a positively economical 11% duck rate for the all-time leader. Neatly, on this very day back in 1892, Jack Fryer — who played 1881-1906 and finished with 12 career ducks, good for 33rd all time — also strolled back for nought, against Royal Engineers CC. History repeating itself, as it so often does in the Duck Club. But the real story came one place later. At number 6, Tim Thirkell followed straight after, bowled by G Keutenius — and given the family connection to John, this is instantly a Duck and Ducklings moment, a parent and child both back in the hutch in the very same innings, a ceremony the club offers all too rarely. Tim's duck is their 1st of the season and 11th of the career from 48 innings, a 23% strike rate placing them 38th of 865 all time. They're also a fully paid-up member of the Full House club, having now made a duck from every position 1 through 11 in a Linton career, and keep regular company in Quacking Together with Jim Hall. Fittingly, they share their on-this-day company with John — Jack Fryer's 1892 nought at Royal Engineers CC covers the whole family this time round. For the record, sibling Nigel already carries 4 career ducks and family member Stuart 1 — clearly a household that takes to the crease with quiet ambition and leaves it rather more quietly still. Week in Summary Just the two ducks recorded this week, and both fell in the very same innings, to the very same family. The other fixture of the period passed off entirely fowl-free — a rare and presumably relieved outcome for whoever was watching. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🏆 - Alan Cradduck — 5 ducks / 9 innings (55.56%) - John Thirkell — 5 ducks / 14 innings (35.71%) - Cliff Anscombe — 3 ducks / 18 innings (16.67%) - Graham Abel — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.0%) - Ron Hickman — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.0%) Cradduck leads for now, with Thirkell snapping at their heels — that battle looks set to run the length of the season. 🦆 Same time next fixture, same dubious honour up for grabs. Get your ducks in a row, Linton — or rather, don't. 0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 10–11 July 1971 🦆

2 ducks
