Week 23 · 28 Sep 1957 – 29 Sep 1957
28–29 September 1957 Two fixtures to see out September, and the zero club still found time to induct four more members before the season's books close. Fitting, really — nothing says "end of term" like a brace of noughts. Saturday — 1st XI v A. Trembeth's XI (home) - Ray Howard — bowled A. Trembeth at number six. Season duck #2, 4th career (37 innings), sitting 57th of 694 in the all-time ledger. - John --'s Taylor — caught Steadman, bowled C. Rye down at eight. Season duck #1, but a startling 13th career duck from just 133 innings — good enough for 6th of 694 all-time, a place most players spend a lifetime chasing (for entirely the wrong reasons). Neatly, both gentlemen share their anniversary with history: on this very day back in 1952, Ray Tippen — a Retirement Duck club man of the 1949–1955 vintage — also trudged off for nought against Linton Park CC. The ghosts of Duck Club past clearly like a reunion. Sunday — Married XI v Linton Park CC (home) - Stan Wratten — caught Frank Wright, bowled John Ames, at first drop. Season duck #1, 11th career (146 innings), 12th of 694 all-time. A long-standing member of the Quacking Together board alongside John Ames — though today Ames did the bowling rather than the batting, so that particular duet stays firmly in the archives. - Colin Haylor — bowled Clive Smith at number ten. Season duck #4 already — the collector's habit continuing nicely — and 9th career from a lean 24 innings, 17th of 694 all-time. A paid-up Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell, though nobody's suggesting he's going anywhere just yet. Both Married men share their own on-this-day company: in 1956, Frank Brattle (1937–1957) was likewise dismissed for nought, against A. Snell XI. Week in summary Four ducks from two fixtures, both played out on Linton Park's own turf — the home advantage evidently not extending to the middle order. Colin Haylor's fourth of the season in just eight innings is the standout rate of the weekend, and he's not the only one under pressure — the season's top table is getting crowded. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Season duck #1, but his 27th career nought is enough to sit him 2nd on the all-time list of 693 — a rank built on decades of consistency of a very particular kind. Taylor's 1951 shade keeps him company again. - Cliff Anscombe — bowled Bailey, his third victim of the innings. 🦆 Duck News — 14–15 September 1957

1 duck
Week 21 · 14 Sep 1957 – 15 Sep 1957
Two fixtures took the field this weekend and the ducks, it must be said, made themselves scarce — only one nought worth its place on the record. Quality over quantity, apparently. Saturday 14 September 1st XI v Bowaters CC (home) Batting at four, Bob Arnold was undone by a sharp take from Frank Hallard off the bowling of Harry Jeffery — the 3rd duck of his season and the 22nd of a long and thoroughly decorated Linton career, now spanning 108 innings. That tally holds him at 3rd on the all-time list of 698, a position few will ever trouble. Bob remains a fixture of several of the club's more exclusive sub-societies: Ducks in a Row (four in a row, a monument to sustained futility), Only Two Quacks (with Alan Wilkes), and Quacking Together (with John Thirkell) — proof, if any were needed, that misery loves company in the Duck Club as much as anywhere else. 🍩 Sunday 15 September The second fixture of the period passed off entirely fowl-free — all eleven batters got off the mark, and the honours board gathers no new names today. Week in summary One duck from two matches this period — a quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, but Bob Arnold made sure the noughts column wasn't left empty. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 🥇 Cliff Anscombe — 3 ducks in 4 innings (75%) 🥈 William Buck — 3 ducks in 11 innings (27.27%) 🥉 Bob Arnold — 3 ducks in 14 innings (21.43%) - John Abnett — 2 ducks in 3 innings (66.67%) - C- Haylor — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40%) With the season running into its closing weeks, Cliff Anscombe sits proudly atop the perch on a startling 75% strike rate — but there's plenty of cricket left for the chasing pack to have their say. 🦆 Duck News — 7th & 8th September 1957

4 ducks
Week 20 · 7 Sep 1957 – 8 Sep 1957
Two fixtures, four ducks, and Linton's 1957 vintage doing its level best to keep the tradition alive as the season heads for the finish line. Saturday 7th — 1st XI away at Tovil Frank Brattle became the weekend's opening casualty, bowled by a-S- Epsley at first wicket down. It's his 1st duck of the summer and 5th for the Club from 35 innings, tying him at 36th all-time with Allan Peach, who reached the same tally in a far briefer stay at the crease between 1903 and 1907. The family name runs deep in these parts too — his parent-child kin Percy Brattle carries 4 career ducks of his own. Sunday 8th — 1st XI host Anchorians CC A far more generous helping at home, with three names added to the tally. Tony Levick opened the procession, bowled by B Mitchell for his 1st duck of the season and 5th of his career from a mighty 104 innings — a fine defensive record breached just the once more. Neatly, on this very date back in 1945, Stan Wratten also walked back without scoring, against the 8th Sherwood Foresters — Stan went on to 10 career ducks and a place on the Quacking Together board alongside John Ames. Cliff Anscombe followed, bowled by Don Wykes, and the numbers are becoming a habit — his 3rd duck of the season from just 4 innings, a startling 75% strike rate for nought. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club since his very first knock, Cliff also currently holds Duck of the Year 1957, and on this evidence he's not relinquishing it lightly. He too shares today's date in history with Stan Wratten's 1945 blank. John Abnett rounded things off, bowled by H Turner, for his 2nd duck of the season and 7th career quack from 21 innings — good enough to keep his name on the Ducks in a Row board for four in succession. Like his teammates, John shares the date with Stan Wratten's own 1945 nought. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every one of them bowled, and three of the four falling in a single Anchorians innings — the kind of top-order collapse that makes the Duck Club's job easy and the scorebook's job miserable. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Tony Snell — 2 ducks / 7 innings (28.57%) Cliff sits handsomely atop the pile, though with plenty of season left there's every chance a fresh name muscles in before it's done. 🦆 Until next weekend — bat well, or at least fall with style. 🍩0️⃣

A rare and faintly disappointing entry in the club's long history — two fixtures took the field this weekend, twenty-two-odd LPCC batsmen strode out with bat in hand, and not a single one of them managed to get themselves out for nought. The exclusive zero club, so reliably busy most weekends, sat entirely idle. No new members, no fresh additions to anyone's tally, nothing but runs on the board from start to finish. Frankly, it's the sort of weekend that gives triumph through adversity a bad name — adversity requires someone to actually fail, and this lot simply declined to co-operate. Somewhere the ghosts of Ducks past are shaking their heads. Still, the art of not scoring is a fickle mistress, and she rarely stays away for long. The season's front-runners remain very much in the hunt, and with the summer drawing on there's plenty of cricket left for someone to put their name back in lights — or rather, in noughts. 17–18 August 1957 Two fixtures, two ducks, both from the 1st XI — a tidy little brace to open this week's ledger, and one of them from the most decorated collector the club has ever produced. Season duck #2, and his 37th career duck from 236 innings — which keeps him exactly where he has sat for some time: 1st of 691 in the club's entire all-time duck standings, a throne nobody is threatening to unseat. A three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955), fixture of the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and a Lone Duck stalwart to boot — he also carries "Quacking Together" honours alongside both Bob Arnold and William Buck from past campaigns, though neither joined him at the crease this week. On this very day back in 1881, Jack Fryer (1881–1906, 12 ducks, #7 all-time, twice Duck of the Year) was likewise trudging back for nought against 1st Innings Leeds Nil Desperandum — the art of not scoring, it seems, is timeless. Sunday — 1st XI v Mote A (home) - Colin Haylor — bowled C. Horton, batting at 8. Season duck #2 from 5 innings this year, and his 7th career duck from just 21 innings, good enough for 24th of 691 all-time — sharp work for a relatively short career. Colin is also a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck board, a standing honour from a past, presumed farewell innings rather than any comment on today's effort. Fittingly, his parallel takes us back exactly one year to this very date in 1956, when — of all people — John Thirkell himself made a duck against Bowaters CC, the very side Thirkell fell to only a day before this report. History does love to rhyme. Week in summary Just the one duck per day, but when one of them is the club's all-time #1 duck-collector extending his lead, quality trumps quantity. A rare and troubling weekend at Linton Park: two matches played, twenty-two innings on offer, and not a single one surrendered for nought. The ducks, it seems, took the weekend off. For an institution built on the noble art of not scoring, this is nothing short of scandalous. Every batsman who walked out across both fixtures found runs — however few, however unconvincing — and trudged back with at least one to his name. The exclusive zero club held no induction ceremony this week. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to report. A place of dubious distinction briefly found itself with nothing to distinguish. We can only assume the bowlers were off form, the fielders generous, or the ducks themselves simply lying low, gathering strength for the weekend ahead.
