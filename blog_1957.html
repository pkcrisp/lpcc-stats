🦆 Duck Report — 28-29 September 1957

4 ducks
Week 23 · 28 Sep 1957 – 29 Sep 1957
The season is drawing to a close but the exclusive zero club shows no sign of adjourning for winter. Four fresh inductions across the weekend's two fixtures, and the art of not scoring is alive and well at Linton Park. Saturday 28 September — 1st XI vs A. Trembeth's XI (h) - Ray Howard, in at six, was bowled by A- Trembeth for his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of a growing career collection in 37 innings — 58th in the all-time roost. Splendidly, on this very date back in 1952, Ray Tippen (1949–1955) also strolled back for nought in this same fixture, one of four ducks in a career that itself ended, fittingly, on a duck — a Retirement Duck club man through and through. - John --'s Taylor followed at eight, caught Steadman off C- Rye, for his 1st of the season but a weighty 13th of a career now standing at 133 innings — a lofty 6th in the all-time table, the sort of company reserved for the true stalwarts of failure. He keeps Ray Howard's memory of Ray Tippen fresh, sharing the very same on-this-day echo from 1952. Sunday 29 September — Married vs Linton Park CC (h) - Stan Wratten, at three, was caught Frank Wright bowled John Ames — his 1st duck of the season, 11th of his career in 146 innings, 12th all-time. Stan carries the honour of "Quacking Together" with John Ames on the board, though today Ames did the bowling rather than the joining — a rematch for another day. On this day in 1956, Frank Brattle (1937–1957) also fell, against A. Snell XI, one of five in a long and varied career. - C- Haylor, last man in, was bowled Clive Smith — his 4th duck of the season already, and 9th of his career from a mere 23 innings, a startling strike rate for the wrong kind of record. A paid-up Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell, Haylor shares Stan's on-this-day nod to Frank Brattle's 1956 nought. Week in summary Two matches, four ducks, no pairs and no family affairs this time round — though with C- Haylor's rate of return, a family gathering on the honours board can surely only be a matter of time. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏🍩 1. Cliff Anscombe — 4 ducks, 5 innings, 80.0% 2. C- Haylor — 4 ducks, 7 innings, 57.14% 3. William Buck — 4 ducks, 13 innings, 30.77% 4. John Abnett — 3 ducks, 4 innings, 75.0% 5. Bob Arnold — 3 ducks, 14 innings, 21.43% The chase for Duck of the Year is finely poised, and with fixtures still to come there's ample time for someone to make their mark — or rather, fail to. See you at the next match, where the zero club stands ready to welcome its newest members. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 21–22 September 1957

6 ducks
Week 22 · 21 Sep 1957 – 22 Sep 1957
Two fixtures, six ducks, and one bowler having the time of his life. Saturday 21st — 1st XI v Bearsted CC (h) Richard Harris was run out for nought at 9, no fault of the bat. His 1st duck of the season is his 27th career quack from 143 innings, which plants him 2nd on the all-time leaderboard of 698. A colossus of the crease-less arts. Sunday 22nd — 1st XI v Tilling Steven (h) Tilling Steven's R- Bailey bowled himself into Linton folklore, accounting for FIVE Duck Club members in a single innings — a proper five-for of the naught variety. William Buck (b Bailey, 3) fell for his 4th of the season and 10th career duck, 15th all-time, and a paid-up member of the Quacking Together honours alongside John Thirkell. C- Haylor (b Bailey, 8) added his 3rd of the season and 8th career duck, a Retirement Duck club veteran from a past farewell who is very much still turning out. Cliff Anscombe (b Bailey, 9) took his 4th of the season from just 5 career innings, an 80% strike rate that keeps him atop this year's own Duck of the Year 1957 standings. John Abnett (b Bailey, 10) notched his 3rd of the season and 8th career duck, proud holder of Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive) — consistency of a very particular kind. Alan Wilkes (c Mills b Bailey, 11) closed the innings with his 2nd of the season and 7th career duck, a double member of Only Two Quacks, alongside both Bob Arnold and John Ames. On this very day in 1951, John --'s Taylor also made a duck, against Hunton Wanderers CC — Buck, Haylor, Anscombe, Abnett and Wilkes all keep that anniversary company this weekend. Week in Summary 6 ducks from 2 matches, five of them to one bowler in one innings. The art of not scoring, thoroughly demonstrated. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. 🦆 Duck News — 14–15 September 1957

1 duck
Week 21 · 14 Sep 1957 – 15 Sep 1957
Two fixtures took the field this weekend and the ducks, it must be said, made themselves scarce — only one nought worth its place on the record. Quality over quantity, apparently. Saturday 14 September 1st XI v Bowaters CC (home) Batting at four, Bob Arnold was undone by a sharp take from Frank Hallard off the bowling of Harry Jeffery — the 3rd duck of his season and the 22nd of a long and thoroughly decorated Linton career, now spanning 108 innings. That tally holds him at 3rd on the all-time list of 698, a position few will ever trouble. 🦆 Duck News — 7th & 8th September 1957

4 ducks
Week 20 · 7 Sep 1957 – 8 Sep 1957
Two fixtures, four ducks, and Linton's 1957 vintage doing its level best to keep the tradition alive as the season heads for the finish line. Saturday 7th — 1st XI away at Tovil Frank Brattle became the weekend's opening casualty, bowled by a-S- Epsley at first wicket down. It's his 1st duck of the summer and 5th for the Club from 35 innings, tying him at 36th all-time with Allan Peach, who reached the same tally in a far briefer stay at the crease between 1903 and 1907. The family name runs deep in these parts too — his parent-child kin Percy Brattle carries 4 career ducks of his own. Sunday 8th — 1st XI host Anchorians CC A far more generous helping at home, with three names added to the tally. Tony Levick opened the procession, bowled by B Mitchell for his 1st duck of the season and 5th of his career from a mighty 104 innings — a fine defensive record breached just the once more. Neatly, on this very date back in 1945, Stan Wratten also walked back without scoring, against the 8th Sherwood Foresters — Stan went on to 10 career ducks and a place on the Quacking Together board alongside John Ames. Cliff Anscombe followed, bowled by Don Wykes, and the numbers are becoming a habit — his 3rd duck of the season from just 4 innings, a startling 75% strike rate for nought. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club since his very first knock, Cliff also currently holds Duck of the Year 1957, and on this evidence he's not relinquishing it lightly. He too shares today's date in history with Stan Wratten's 1945 blank. John Abnett rounded things off, bowled by H Turner, for his 2nd duck of the season and 7th career quack from 21 innings — good enough to keep his name on the Ducks in a Row board for four in succession. Like his teammates, John shares the date with Stan Wratten's own 1945 nought. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every one of them bowled, and three of the four falling in a single Anchorians innings — the kind of top-order collapse that makes the Duck Club's job easy and the scorebook's job miserable. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Tony Snell — 2 ducks / 7 innings (28.57%) Cliff sits handsomely atop the pile, though with plenty of season left there's every chance a fresh name muscles in before it's done. 🦆 Until next weekend — bat well, or at least fall with style. 🍩0️⃣

🦆 DUCK NEWS — Weekend of 17-18 August, 1957 🦆

2 ducks
Week 17 · 17 Aug 1957 – 18 Aug 1957
A modest but historically resonant weekend for the exclusive zero club, with just two ducks recorded across the two fixtures — both, fittingly, from the 1st XI. 🏏 Saturday 17th — Bowaters CC (away) John Thirkell continued his season of dubious distinction, caught John Woollett off the bowling of Eric Wilson at number two. It's his 2nd duck of the summer and a remarkable 37th of an extraordinary career, a tally that keeps him perched at the very summit of the all-time honours board — 1st of 696 duckers in club history. A three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954 and 1955), John also sits proudly in the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens, having mastered the art of not scoring whether on the road or at home. He even carries Quacking Together billing alongside both Bob Arnold and William Buck, though neither joined him at the crease this week. On this very day in 1881, Jack Fryer was doing much the same for the club against 1st Innings Leeds Nil Desperandum. Fryer went on to 12 career ducks and a couple of Duck of the Year titles himself (1891, 1900) — proof, if any were needed, that the zero club has always kept a full membership book. 🥇 Sunday 18th — Mote A (home) C- Haylor departed for nought at number eight, bowled by C- Horton. It's his 2nd duck of the season and 7th of his career from just 20 innings, enough to see him sit 24th on the all-time list. Haylor is also a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck club, having bowed out on a duck at some point in a career gone by — though happily there's no suggestion he's going anywhere just yet. His parallel needs no trip back to the 1800s — on this very day in 1956, none other than John Thirkell (see above) was also dismissed for nought against Bowaters CC. The Duck Club, it seems, keeps things in the family even when the family isn't related. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both courtesy of the 1st XI — a quiet weekend by the club's usual standards, but with John Thirkell extending his reign at the top of the all-time charts, quality has more than compensated for quantity. Tony Snell — 2 ducks, 5 innings (40%) 5. Bob Arnold — 2 ducks, 9 innings (22%) Special mention to Cliff Anscombe, whose two innings have yielded two ducks and not a single run — consistency of a very particular kind. Onwards to the next fixture, where the honours board waits, as ever, with open arms. 🦆🏏0️⃣

7 ducks

Two fixtures, one two-innings marathon, and the exclusive zero club gained no fewer than seven new subscriptions this week. The art of not scoring was practised with real dedication at Linton Park. 🏏 Saturday, Aylesford Paper Mills CC (away) William Buck got the weekend's proceedings under way, bowled by D Mason without so much as a squeak off the bat. It's his 1st duck of the season and 7th of his career, keeping him handsomely placed at 18th of 695 in the all-time roll of dishonour — grand company he shares with clubman John Thirkell on the "Quacking Together" board, Thirkell himself very much not present in this week's casualty list. On this very date in 1952, a young Gordon Fenner (1941–1957) was performing the identical ceremony, en route to 14 career ducks and 5th place in the all-time standings. Buck, alas, was only warming up. 🎉 Monday, Hon F Cornwallis (home, two innings) The Bank Holiday two-inningser proved a positively bountiful harvest. First dig: Buck again, this time bowled by K Goodwin — his 2nd of the season, 8th of his career. D- Jordan followed, bowled Gladdish, his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his young career from just 12 innings. Frank Wright went the same way to Gladdish, his 5th career duck — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck, Quack-to-Quack, and Retirement Duck boards from earlier chapters of his story, though happily still very much an active batsman today. Remarkably, all four found themselves keeping historical company with Ernest Cruttenden, who ducked on this exact date in 1895 against Rochester CC. J Lea also fell, bowled J Smith, opening his personal account with his 1st ever duck from just 4 innings — and, fittingly, joining Cruttenden's 1895 party as well. 📋 Week in Summary Seven ducks from two fixtures — a spread nicely dominated by the Cornwallis bowling attack, with Gladdish alone accounting for three victims in a single innings. 🦆 Duck News — 13–14 July 1957

2 ducks
Week 12 · 13 Jul 1957 – 14 Jul 1957
A quiet weekend for the parish's most exclusive club by numbers, but no less eventful for it — two names added to the ledger, one of them a fully paid-up member on his very first appearance. 🏏 Saturday 13th — 1st XI v A.
