🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 25 September 1993

1 duck
Week 22 · 25 Sep 1993
A quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club — two fixtures on the go and the ducks mostly stayed hidden. Only one man answered the call, but he answered it with proper pedigree. 2nd XI v Yalding CC At number four, club stalwart Trevor Back departed c Liam Hawkins b Chris Quirk without troubling the scorers — his 1st duck of the season and the 37th of an extraordinarily durable career spanning 313 innings. That plants him at 14th in the all-time standings, a lofty perch he shares good company around, being a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, the Homebody Duck top 10 and the Lone Duck top 10 alike — evidently there is no ground, home or away, on which Trevor cannot contrive to nought. He also carries standing memberships of Only Two Quacks (with Allan Cooper) and Quacking Together (with Jon Harris) — a man who likes company in his misery. And rather splendidly, the almanac obliges: on this very day back in 1960, Len Conway also made a duck against Linton Park CC. Conway, who turned out for the club from 1954 to 1965, retired with 12 career ducks to his name — good for 59th all-time — and remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his account the same way it ended: for nought. Thirty-three years apart, same date, same fate. The art of not scoring, it seems, has no expiry date. Elsewhere, the other fixture proved a strictly vegan affair — every batsman got off the mark and the Duck Club's second table sat entirely undisturbed. Can't win them all, and on this occasion, quite literally, nobody lost for nought. Week in summary One duck from two matches — a lean harvest by Linton's usual standards, but a quality one, with Trevor Back's contribution alone stretching back three decades in company. Consistency of a very particular kind continues. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 33 inn, 6 ducks (18.18%) - Simon Thirkell — 13 inn, 5 ducks (38.46%) - Ian Brattle — 10 inn, 4 ducks (40.0%) - Mike Elworthy — 18 inn, 4 ducks (22.22%) - Piers McDonald — 45 inn, 4 ducks (8.89%) Simon Thirkell and Ian Brattle both operate at a truly fearsome strike-rate for nought — over a third of their innings ending before they've begun. Matt Large leads on raw volume, but the percentage game belongs to Brattle for now. 🍩 Next fixture looms, and with it the promise of fresh recruits to the honours board. The ducks are out there somewhere — see you at the crease. 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 18-22 September 1993

1 duck
Week 21 · 18 Sep 1993 – 22 Sep 1993
Four fixtures on the books this week and the Duck Club's nets came back with a solitary catch — but oh, what a catch. 🏏 1st XI — West Farleigh CC Matt Large, ever the club's most reliable exporter of noughts, was trapped lbw by Glen Aukett at number 8, chalking up his 6th duck of the campaign and 40th of an increasingly distinguished career — now 10th in the all-time standings out of 1190 names on the books. That tally draws him level with Roy Clarke, who compiled his own 40 across 233 innings between 1957 and 1966, proof that some feats echo down the decades regardless of the pitch. On this very day back in 1960, Cliff Anscombe was doing much the same thing against Tilling Steven — a neat little coincidence of the calendar that the Duck Club insists on noting for posterity, if nothing else. Matt remains a fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first innings, and already carries Duck of the Year honours from 1987, 1988, 1989 and 1992 — with the 1993 crown looking distinctly his to lose given this week's season-topping form. He also lingers agonisingly at Full House nearly-man status, ten of eleven positions ticked off, plus fixtures in the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens. Genuinely, is there a category this man hasn't graced? 📅 Week in Summary Four matches, one duck — a quiet week by the Club's usual gluttonous standards, and Matt Large did the entire honours board's work single-handedly. No pairs, no family affairs, no king ducks to report. The bowlers of West Farleigh CC will nonetheless be very pleased with themselves. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 32 inns, 6 ducks (18.75%) 2. Simon Thirkell — 13 inns, 5 ducks (38.46%) 3. Ian Brattle — 10 inns, 4 ducks (40.00%) 4. Mike Elworthy — 17 inns, 4 ducks (23.53%) 5. Piers McDonald — 44 inns, 4 ducks (9.09%) Simon Thirkell and Ian Brattle both boast fearsome duck-rates north of 35% — the sort of consistency the club can only applaud from a respectful distance. 🍩 Next fixture awaits, and with it, fresh opportunities for the art of not scoring. The zero club never sleeps — see you all at the crease. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — 11 September 1993 🦆

0 ducks
Week 20 · 11 Sep 1993
A quiet one for the Duck Club this week, I'm afraid. Linton took to the field on the 11th of September and, scandalously, every single soul who walked out to bat found a way to trouble the scorer. Not a single nought to report, not so much as a golden glimmer of ignominy anywhere in the XI. The zero club's membership desk stood entirely unvisited. Whether this counts as a triumph of application or simply a dereliction of duty by the club's more reliable duck-hunters is, frankly, a matter of some debate down the clubhouse. Rest assured the committee is looking into it. Still, the season's honours board doesn't rest just because one Saturday went straight. The race for Duck of the Year 1993 remains very much alive, and there's no shortage of history being written elsewhere in the ledger. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season 1993 — Leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 5 ducks from 13 innings (38.46%) — a genuinely fearsome strike rate for all the wrong reasons, and the early pacesetter 2. Matt Large — 5 ducks from 30 innings (16.67%) — matching Simon on quantity, if not quite on ruthless efficiency 3. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks from 9 innings (44.44%) — the purest duck-per-innings return in the top five, a masterclass in the art of not scoring 4. Mike Elworthy — 4 ducks from 16 innings (25.0%) — quietly keeping pace in the shadows 5. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks from 41 innings (9.76%) — proof that consistency of a very particular kind can be achieved even across a long, weary campaign Simon Thirkell holds top spot on numbers, but Ian Brattle's frankly alarming 44% conversion rate suggests he could overhaul the lot of them with one more bad Saturday. Matt Large and Piers McDonald, meanwhile, are playing the long game — proof that patience, or perhaps just longevity, has its own rewards in this particular league table. 📅 Looking ahead The ducks evaded capture this week, but they never stay hidden for long. The season's still young enough for records to fall, milestones to loom, and at least one unlucky soul to discover the joys of the exclusive zero club all over again. Bats at the ready, gentlemen — the next fixture surely won't be so well-behaved. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report – 4th to 8th September 1993

4 ducks
Week 19 · 4 Sep 1993 – 8 Sep 1993
Four matches, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club found new inductees both on the home patch and on tour this week. 🏏 Midweek XI – Home to The Mote CC (8th Sept) Adrian Caddick was bowled by Toby Smith to record his 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from just 6 innings — a small but perfectly formed collection. On this very day in 1981, Richard Ashby was doing the same trick against Paul CC, en route to a career haul of 28 and places on both the Ducks in a Row and Tourist Duck top 10 boards. Adrian keeps rather distinguished company. At the other end of the order, last man Richard Piper fell to the same Toby Smith — a brace of Linton wickets in the innings for him. It's Richard's 2nd duck of the season and 8th of a career now spanning 16 innings, and with Full House, Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top 10 memberships already secured, he remains one of the club's great scholars of not scoring. A chip off the old block, too — his father Brian carries 20 career ducks of his own. Richard shares Adrian's on-this-day honour: Richard Ashby, 1981, still setting the standard twelve years later. 🌴 Tour XI – Cornwall (5th–6th Sept) Some tours are remembered for the cricket. This one will be remembered for Paul Bishop's grim pair. Bowled by Kevin Thomas at Beacon CC on the Sunday for his 2nd duck of the season, Paul returned less than a day later against Redruth CC to be caught off A Hood for his 3rd — a pair to nil, and not the souvenir he'll have wanted to bring home from Cornwall. He now sits on 3 career ducks from just 8 innings, and already carries Retirement Duck club membership from a past farewell duck. His Sunday parallel takes us back to 1970, when David T Lowen made his own duck against A.Trembeths on this exact date, also on 3 for his career, while Monday drew him level with Roger Craddock's solitary 1967 duck against Birchington CC. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from four matches — a modest harvest, but Paul Bishop's back-to-back effort in Cornwall did more than its share of the heavy lifting. No family affairs and no king pairs this week, but a proper old-fashioned pair is nothing to sniff at. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell – 5 ducks in 13 innings (38.46%) 2. Matt Large – 5 ducks in 30 innings (16.67%) 3. Ian Brattle – 4 ducks in 9 innings (44.44%) 4. Mike Elworthy – 4 ducks in 16 innings (25.0%) 5. Piers McDonald – 4 ducks in 40 innings (10.0%) 🍩 The season's not done handing out its dubious honours yet — see you at the crease next time, and good luck avoiding the club.

🦆 Duck Report: 28 August – 3 September 1993 🦆

7 ducks
Week 18 · 28 Aug 1993 – 3 Sep 1993
Seven matches, seven ducks — a week of admirably efficient failure across county and coast alike. 1st XI v Sheldwich CC (away) Piers McDonald departed for his 4th duck of the season, bowled by P Hammond, and his 19th career duck (42nd of 1190 all-time) — a paid-up member of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. On this very day in 1974, Roland Parr also strolled back for nought, against Gravesend CC. 2nd XI v Sheldwich CC (home) Richard Piper opened his 1993 account the hard way, bowled S Lurcock for his 7th career duck in a brisk 15 innings — enough to already hold a Full House (a duck at every position 1-11) plus places in the Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. He's not the first Piper to make the honours board either — father Brian sits on 20. Like Piers above, Richard shares his moment with Roland Parr's 1974 duck at Gravesend. Tour XI – the Cornwall Tour Nigel Thirkell secured the week's rarest feat: a pair. Bowled by Ian Bishop at Truro on the 1st for his 48th career duck, he returned the very next day at Falmouth to fall to Sean Turner for his 49th — a grim weekend double for a man already enshrined for Duck of the Year 1986 and a place in both the Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens. His first echoed brother Tim Thirkell's 1973 duck at Whitstable, his second matched Paul Bowles' 1968 effort against Bearsted. The family name struck again at Mullion CC on the 3rd, where three ducks fell in a single innings. Simon Thirkell — Nigel's brother and son of the legendary John Thirkell — was bowled by D Hallett for his 5th duck of the season and 30th of his career, edging him onto the "50 Before a 50" board. Rob Doick followed, bowled D Bostock, for his 11th career duck, and Adrian Caddick completed the set, caught Bostock bowled Bossusto for his 2nd duck in just 4 career innings — an alarming start. All three keep company with Benny Bell, who made his 30th duck on this exact date back in 1972 against Staplehurst. Week in summary Sheldwich claimed a duck from each Linton XI, and Mullion CC did the same to three Tour batsmen in one afternoon — Cornwall clearly agrees with the club's less celebrated traditions. Simon Thirkell's 5th of the season keeps him locked at the top of the Duck Hunting Season standings. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell – 5 ducks, 12 innings (41.67%) 2. Matt Large – 5 ducks, 28 innings (17.86%) 3. Ian Brattle – 4 ducks, 9 innings (44.44%) 4. Mike Elworthy – 4 ducks, 15 innings (26.67%) 5. Piers McDonald – 4 ducks, 38 innings (10.53%) Next fixture awaits, and with the Thirkell clan in this kind of form, the honours board may need a new page. See you all there. 🦆🏏0️⃣

It's his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of an LPCC career now standing at 29 innings, keeping him at 146th in the all-time roll. On this same calendar day in 1906, R W Mitchell was suffering identically against the Royal West Kent Regiment CC Officers XI, en route to 2 career ducks of his own. Not long after, Ian Brattle joined him, also falling to T Gould. It's Ian's 4th duck of the season and a hefty 28th for his career from 85 innings, good enough for 26th in the all-time standings — rarefied company indeed. Ian is a long-standing member of the Debutante Duck club, boasts a run of 7 consecutive ducks in the Ducks in a Row honours, shares the Only Two Quacks distinction with Michael Beeney, and sits on 50 Before a 50 with 51 ducks against a high score of just 33 — consistency of a very particular kind. He also has form in the family: sibling Paul Brattle carries 26 career ducks of his own. And like Adrian, Ian shares this day in cricketing history with R W Mitchell's 1906 effort.
