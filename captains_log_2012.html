🗓️ 15 Sep – 21 Sep 2012 · 2012 — 2 matches

Sun 16 Sep 2012 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv Cowdrey CC · HomeformLWWLW

Sunday the sixteenth of September found us hosting Cowdrey CC, and for once the coin behaved itself — I called correctly and, with barely a flicker of hesitation, sent us in to bat. Glen Rawson set about proving why fifties don't come along for nothing, compiling a fine 82, while Daniel Thirkell (39) and Tim Wright (37) built handsomely around him, their fourth-wicket stand of 83 the innings' spine. Not everyone enjoyed themselves quite so much — Stuart Cheesman and Trevor Back both departed for ducks, proof that cricket remains a great leveller — but a generous 27 extras helped us to a healthy 202 for 6 from our forty overs.

Defending it, we made early inroads, Cowdrey losing their first wicket with just 2 on the board. Glen Warner (55) threatened to drag them back into it with a fifty of his own and a fifth-wicket stand of 52, but David Challis, Robert Fenwick and Will Needham all trooped back for ducks, and the middle order simply couldn't sustain him. Will Thirkell was superb with the new ball, 3 for 16, Richard Knowler chipped in with 2 for 1 and three miserly maidens, and Martin Harris took 2 for 36 as Cowdrey folded for 153 in 39.1 overs.

Rawson had a busier afternoon behind the stumps than in front of them, 15 byes leaking through, but the day belonged to Richard Knowler, who quietly brought up 200 career wickets — a proper mantelpiece moment. A fine win by 49 runs, and thoroughly deserved. Onwards.

Sat 15 Sep 2012 🧢 1st XIv SC Sidcup CC · HomeformWWDWW

Saturday 15 September, and Sidcup CC came to Linton Park with the sun out and, rather typically, the toss going against us — so it was pads off and boots on for the field first. They ground their way to 139 all out in 39.2 overs, Tim Dawes doing most of the damage with a well-made 57, the Stent brothers chipping in with 25 and 23 apiece. Sam Boyns was the pick of the attack with 3-25, Michael Beeney and Martin Harris both weighed in with two apiece, and Will Thirkell strangled the scoring with a couple of tidy maidens. David Rolls and Tom Mothersole both went for ducks, which I'm sure they'll want no further mention of.

Chasing 140 should never be described as comfortable in this fixture list, and true to form we lost our first wicket without a run on the board — the sort of start that has a captain reaching for the flask before the tea interval has even been thought of. But Daniel Thirkell settled things beautifully, farming the strike and the runs in equal measure to finish unbeaten on 75, with a stout 51-run stand for the third wicket doing the real repair work. I chipped in with the ball earlier and was pleased enough with my own afternoon's work. Harry Bee and Kevin Presneill both fell for ducks, proof this was never going to be a stroll.

Home by five wickets, two points banked, and Daniel Thirkell's bat continues to do the talking at the top of the run charts. A scratchy, occasionally alarming, thoroughly deserved win.

Sun 09 Sep 2012 🧢 Sunday 2nd XIv Meopham CC · HomeformWWWWL

Sunday 9th September, and Meopham CC came calling — a fixture that always seems to bring out either the best or the very worst in us. On Sunday, mercifully, it was the former, though not before a batting effort that could charitably be described as "solid rather than spectacular." We limped, nudged and occasionally clattered our way to 149/9 from our 40 overs, a total that owed everything to Matthew Gladwell's unbeaten 32, well supported by Glen Rawson's 30. I chipped in with 26 myself, more through stubbornness than style, but every run counts when you're not entirely sure it'll be enough.

And by tea, it didn't look like it would be. Andrew Powney set about our total with intent, making 39 of the runs that actually mattered, while Alex Bryant (26 not out) and Stephen Innes (23) threatened to make our afternoon a long one. But this is where Michael Beeney earned his tea and biscuits, tearing through their middle order with a magnificent 4-25, Martin Harris chipping in with 2-14 and Sam Boyns with 1-34. Mark Ford and Andrew Dixon both departed without troubling the scorers — quacking all the way back to the pavilion.

In the end, Meopham fell nine runs short, and we breathed a collective sigh of relief. Glen Rawson continues to lead our Sunday 2nd XI run charts with 409, and Martin Harris tops the wicket pile with 23 — form that's carrying the rest of us along nicely. A tight, nervy, thoroughly enjoyable win. Cricket, eh?
