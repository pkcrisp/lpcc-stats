Sun 26 Sep 2010 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv Chelsfield CC · HomeformWWLWW

We won the toss on Sunday and I had no hesitation in taking first knock — a decision I'm rather pleased to say vindicated itself as the day wore on. Batting first against Chelsfield, we posted a tidy 165 for 8 from our 42 overs, the innings built around a really well-crafted fifty from Richard Piper, who made 66 and continues to bat like a man on a mission this summer. Twenty-five extras crept in too, which I like to think of as Chelsfield's small but generous contribution to the cause.

Out in the field, the lads set about defending the total with real purpose. Chelsfield were restricted to 141 for 9 from their 41, with I. Walker unbeaten on 58 the chief obstacle, well supported by M. Pearcy's 20 — though X Robinson and X Ingram went the way of all flesh without so much as a run to their names. The bowling honours belonged to Dan Barkaway, whose miserly 4 for 36, including five maidens, did the real damage, with Sam Boyns (3-10) and Jon Harris (1-28) chipping in handsomely around him.

In the end neither side could quite force it home, and honours were shared. Richard Piper now leads our 2010 Sunday 1st XI run charts with 521 for the season, and Sam Boyns sits third in the wickets with 17 — both quietly building rather memorable summers. A draw, then, but a thoroughly honourable one, and no shame at all in sharing the spoils with a side that made us work for every run.

Sat 25 Sep 2010 🧢 1st XIv CX Chairman's XI · HomeformLWWWL

Saturday 25th September saw the visitors at Linton Park, and yours truly won the toss and had us out in the field first — always a decision that looks either shrewd or daft by close of play. Early on it looked shrewd: a wicket down for nought had the openers grinning into their tea. But cricket has a way of humbling a captain, and the visitors steadied to post 142 for 9 from their thirty overs, largely thanks to a fine half-century from Tom Davey (69) that did the real damage. Credit to the lads, though — we hauled things back sharply, tumbling them from 107-5 to 109-8 in a blur, with both Ron McCormick and Will Bramley-Harker trudging off for ducks. Kevin Presneill was magnificent, 3 for 15, with Sam Boyns (2-6, three miserly maidens) and Harry Bee (1-5) offering splendid support.

With 143 needed, Richard Piper (36) and Iain Rawson (34) laid the platform, Harry Bee chipping in a handy 22 with the bat too, and we reached 143 for 6 in 21.3 overs — poor Nat Presneill enduring the walk of shame for a golden duck along the way.

Elsewhere, the season charts continue to flatter Richard Knowler, third among the 1st XI's run-scorers with 278 and second in the wickets column with 20 — the man's quietly having a summer.

Not our day in the end, going down by four wickets. Heads up, chins high, and back to the nets.

Sun 19 Sep 2010 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · HomeformDWWLW

We knew coming in that Tenterden would test us, and after winning the toss I fancied a bat first on what looked a decent pitch — one of the better decisions I've made this season, if I say so myself. David Castle played the innings of the day, 51 not out of pure application, with Richard Piper's 34 giving the total real backbone as we posted 160/7 from our fifty. Not every duck ledger entry can be a triumph, mind — poor Iain Rawson made the long walk back for nought, though he'd more than make amends later.

With the ball, we set about defending 160 with real purpose. Richard Pearman (43 not out) and Robert Featherstone (36) gave Tenterden something to build on, and for a while it looked close, but we sent Chris Hobbs and James Allen packing without so much as a run to their names. And then Rawson turned from duck to destroyer — spinning through the order for a magnificent 5-29, the sort of spell that has team-mates quietly reassessing their net bowling. Martin Harris and Harry Bee chipped in with a wicket apiece, and Sam Boyns bowled two tidy maidens to squeeze the life out of the chase. Tenterden were all out for 126 in 29.3 overs.

A 34-run win, two points banked, and Rawson's redemption arc complete within a single afternoon. With Piper still topping the Sunday run charts and Boyns lurking fourth among the wicket-takers, the season ticks along nicely. All told: a fine team performance, and — dare I say it — thoroughly professional stuff from the Duck Club.
