🦆 Duck News — 30 September 1972

0 ducks
Week 23 · 30 Sep 1972
A quiet one for the archives, I'm afraid. One match played on the 30th, and not a single soul obliged the Duck Club with so much as a nought. Every batsman who walked out at Linton Park that day had the shocking manners to trouble the scorer. The ducks, it seems, took the day off — hardly in keeping with the season's fine form, and frankly a disgrace to the art of not scoring. Still, the exclusive zero club does not run itself on hope alone, and the season standings remain gloriously intact for those keeping score of the scoreless. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard — 1972 - Tim Thirkell — 26 innings, 6 ducks (23.08%) — the season's undisputed frontrunner, cultivating a duck rate most bowlers would sell their whites for. - John Thirkell — 23 innings, 4 ducks (17.39%) — keeping it in the family, and keeping the pressure on at the top. - Andrew Moss — 20 innings, 3 ducks (15.0%) — a solid mid-table showing in the season's least wanted table. - Mike Iliffe — 25 innings, 3 ducks (12.0%) — matching Moss on tally if not on rate, proof that consistency of a very particular kind comes in many forms. - Ron Hickman — 6 innings, 2 ducks (33.33%) — fewest innings on the board, highest strike rate of the lot. A small sample, but a mighty one. Tim Thirkell will sleep easy tonight knowing his lead holds for another week, but with the season drawing towards its close there is still time yet for the also-rans to come hunting. So — evaded, not extinct. Normal service resumes at the next fixture, when the ducks will no doubt return in their customary style, uninvited and unstoppable. See you at the crease. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 23-24 September 1972

3 ducks
Week 22 · 23 Sep 1972 – 24 Sep 1972
A modest but memorable weekend for the exclusive zero club, with three fresh entries to the honours board across two 1st XI fixtures. Autumn's chill clearly didn't stop the quacking. Saturday 23rd — West Farleigh CC (away) - Tim Thirkell, in at 5, was bowled by D. Gedney for nought — his 6th duck of the season and 17th of a career now 85 innings deep, good enough for 26th on the all-time list. It's a season that already carries the 1972 Duck of the Year crown, alongside membership of the Full House board (a duck at every position, 1 to 11 — a completist's approach to failure) and top ten spots for both Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck. Keeping it in the family, relative John Thirkell (77 career ducks) is currently snapping at Tim's heels in this very season's charts — noughts clearly run in the blood. On this very date a decade earlier, in 1962, Terry Whittaker was going through exactly the same ordeal against Linton Park CC. - Terry Whittaker himself wasn't far behind, caught and bowled by I. Edwards at number 8 for his 1st duck of the season, moving to 30 career ducks in 263 innings — 10th on the all-time roll of dishonour. His honours board already pairs him with Brian Relf under Quacking Together — fittingly, since it's Brian's own 1962 duck against Linton Park CC that shares today's date exactly. Sunday 24th — Anchorians CC (home) - Colin Dicker opened his career account the hard way, bowled by Don Wykes at number 9 in just his 3rd innings — the club's newest paid-up member of the zero club, presently 249th all-time with plenty of climbing left to do. Sharing the date if not the decade, Frank Brattle made a duck against A. Trembeths on this same day back in 1960, en route to a career haul of 38. Week in Summary Three ducks, two matches, all courtesy of the 1st XI — the 2nds, 3rds and 4ths kept their heads down and their bats occupied this week. Triumph through adversity remains a strictly voluntary pursuit, and the 1st XI took it upon themselves alone. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Tim Thirkell — 6 ducks / 26 innings (23.08%) 2. John Thirkell — 4 ducks / 22 innings (18.18%) 3. Andrew Moss — 3 ducks / 20 innings (15.0%) 4. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks / 24 innings (12.5%) 5. Ron Hickman — 2 ducks / 6 innings (33.33%) Ron Hickman's strike rate for nought is one to keep an eye on. Next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, further additions to the honours board. Pad up, and may the odds be ever in your... nought's favour. 🦆0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — Saturday 16 & Sunday 17 September 1972

2 ducks
Week 21 · 16 Sep 1972 – 17 Sep 1972
A quiet weekend for Linton's bowlers to enjoy, courtesy of the 1st XI, who alone flew the flag for the exclusive zero club this week. Two ducks, two matches, and one certified legend among them. 🏏 1st XI Saturday's trip to face Charles Arkcoll saw Mike Iliffe adjudged lbw to Vic Wellard at number six, a decision he no doubt still disputes at the bar. It's Mike's 3rd duck of the season and his 22nd for the club in 147 innings, edging him to a lofty 16th in the all-time standings. On this very day back in 1962, Richard Ashby was suffering an identical fate against Staplehurst CC, en route to 24 career ducks of his own and a place among the Tourist Duck top 10 and the Ducks in a Row club — proof the calendar has a long memory for these things. Sunday brought altogether grander business. Facing Mersham Hatch, John Thirkell departed for nought at number five, caught behind off Breed — his 4th duck of the season and, take a bow, his 77th for Linton Park. That tally sits him at number ONE out of all 880 members of the club's all-time duck standings, a distinction nobody else can touch. A five-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967 and 1971) and a permanent fixture of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, John remains the yardstick against which every future nought is measured. Fittingly, on this same day in 1960, Basil Sears was also trudging off for a duck against Bearsted CC, one of 31 in a career spanning 1947 to 1972 — good company for a man whose kin, Tim and Nigel Thirkell, keep the family business thoroughly alive. 📋 The Week in Brief Two matches, two ducks, both courtesy of the 1st XI — the 2nds, 3rds and the rest of the club escaped entirely unscathed. No pairs, no family affairs this time, just two gentlemen doing what they do best: departing with maximum efficiency and minimum fuss. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks in 24 innings (20.83%) 2. John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 21 innings (19.05%) 3. Andrew Moss — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.00%) 4. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks in 23 innings (13.04%) 5. Ron Hickman — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40.00% — some going) With Tim and John Thirkell locking out the top two spots, the family name is doing some heavy lifting in this season's Duck of the Year race. Roll on next weekend, gentlemen — the bowlers won't rest, and neither should your guard. 🦆

1 duck
Week 20 · 10 Sep 1972
A quiet week for the Duck Club scouts — just the one fixture on the books, but never let it be said the ducks take a day off. One quack was logged, and it's a good one, so let's get into it. 🏏 1st XI — away to Bexhill CC Opening the batting, Robert Chance marched out full of intent and marched straight back again, bowled by Dave Cowley for nought. Not a golden duck, mind — he weathered a ball or two before Cowley found his mark, which in this club counts as a moral victory. It's Robert's 1st duck of the season and his 6th career duck from 107 innings, good enough (or bad enough) for 73rd of 880 in the all-time roll of dishonour. He's also long carried membership of the Retirement Duck club, a standing honour from a past farewell innings — the archive doesn't say this was that day, so no eulogies yet, Robert. Splendidly, the fixtures line up too: on this very day in 1955, John --'s Taylor also trudged back for nought, against Old Roffensians of all sides. Taylor's career (1949–1960) closed on 15 ducks, good for 28th all-time — Robert's got some catching up to do if he fancies the company. 📊 Week in summary One match, one duck — the harvest was thin, but quality over quantity, as they say in the exclusive zero club. Bexhill CC can consider themselves honoured to have witnessed it. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Tim Thirkell — 22 innings, 5 ducks (22.7%) - John Thirkell — 19 innings, 3 ducks (15.8%) - Andrew Moss — 20 innings, 3 ducks (15.0%) - Ron Hickman — 5 innings, 2 ducks (40.0%) - Benny Bell — 9 innings, 2 ducks (22.2%) Tim Thirkell continues to set the pace out front, though with a season still in motion there's plenty of room for a late surge from the chasing pack. 🍩 That's the lot for this round — quiet weeks like this one never last. Duck Report — 2-3 September 1972 🦆

2 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1972 – 3 Sep 1972
Two matches, two fixtures on the road, and the LPCC 1st XI keeping the exclusive zero club fed and watered even without home comforts. A modest weekend for quantity, but never let it be said the art of not scoring goes out of fashion. Saturday 2 September — Whitstable CC (away) Jon Harris, promoted to number six, departed caught J Waters bowled Edric Churchley for his second duck of the season and 12th of a career now standing at 50 innings — good enough for 35th on the club's all-time roll of dishonour. Jon is by now a seasoned collector: a certified Full House man (duck at every position 1 through 11, a grim sort of completeness), a fixture on the Homebody, Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own long-ago first innings. On this very date in 1968, a young Paul Bowles was up to exactly the same mischief against Bearsted CC — he went on to 21 career ducks and back-to-back Duck of the Year crowns in '66 and '68, so Jon has form to chase. Worth noting too that kinsman Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own — evidently a family with an eye for nought. Sunday 3 September — Staplehurst CC (away) No rest for the travel-weary, as the 1s were straight back on the road to Staplehurst, where Benny Bell fell lbw to A West at number eight. It's Benny's second duck of the season and 22nd for his career, lodging him 15th in the all-time standings from 95 innings — rare air. Spare a thought also for Jimmy Jameson, who on this same date back in 1944 suffered identically against the Home Guard's Royal West Kent 11th Battalion. Some things, it seems, are simply written into the calendar. Week in Summary 🍩 A quiet one by Duck Club standards — two matches, two ducks, both away, both 1st XI. No pairs this week, no family affairs, no king ducks to enter into the record. Sometimes the ducks just can't be bothered with the away trip. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks / 21 innings (23.81%) 2. John Thirkell — 3 ducks / 18 innings (16.67%) 3. Andrew Moss — 3 ducks / 20 innings (15.00%) 4. Ron Hickman — 2 ducks / 5 innings (40.00%) 5. Benny Bell — 2 ducks / 9 innings (22.22%) Ron Hickman's 40% strike rate remains the number nobody wants to talk about at tea. The leaderboard is far from settled and the season has plenty left to give — get yourselves to the next fixture, because on current form the zero club is always recruiting. 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 27–30 August 1972

4 ducks
Week 18 · 27 Aug 1972 – 30 Aug 1972
Sunday 27 August — NVKC away to Troon CC Troon's bowlers went through the middle and tail like a knife through butter, and the LPCC nought-count trebled in a single innings. First to go was John Thirkell, lbw for nought — his 3rd duck of the season and a scarcely believable 76th of his career. That's still the unassailable #1 spot in the all-time standings, untouched, a five-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967 and 1971) and a permanent resident of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. On this very day in 1949, Richard Harris also made a duck, against Hunton Wanderers CC — kindred company across the decades. And the Thirkell name doesn't stop there: sons Tim (16 career ducks) and Nigel (7) are carrying the family tradition rather well themselves — a proper quacking dynasty. Next, Graham Mountford fell, his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of his career from just 24 innings, sliding into rank 120 of 880. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from an earlier innings, though this outing was no debut. Same on-this-day company as above. Rounding off the collapse, Ron Hickman was lbw'd for his 2nd duck of the season, taking him to 19 career ducks from 42 innings and rank 22 all-time. He shares "Quacking Together" honours with Alan Cradduck, who's currently propping up the bottom of this season's top five, and carries the grim badge of a 1965 Runaway Duck — a whole season without troubling the scorers. Wednesday 30 August — Midweek XI vs Gravesend CC Ray Grayland was bowled by Michael Sampson for his 1st duck of the season and, remarkably, only his 2nd career innings — rank 196 of 880 and a Debutante Duck club member from way back. On this day in 1965, Frank Brattle also made a duck, against Bearsted CC. Week in summary Four ducks from two matches — three of them arriving in the same unfortunate Troon innings, a fine demonstration that ducks, like buses, prefer to travel in threes. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Tim Thirkell — 19 inns, 5 ducks, 26.32% - John Thirkell — 17 inns, 3 ducks, 17.65% - Andrew Moss — 18 inns, 3 ducks, 16.67% - Ron Hickman — 5 inns, 2 ducks, 40.0% - Alan Cradduck — 10 inns, 2 ducks, 20.0% Two Thirkells occupying the top two spots — father chasing son, or is it the other way round? Either way, the race for Duck of the Year 1972 is very much a family affair. Roll on the next fixture — the ducks are circling, and there's always room in the club for one more member. 🦆🍩0️⃣

Week 17 · 19 Aug 1972 – 23 Aug 1972
Three fixtures went into the book this week, but the harvest was a modest one — a single match yielded the goods, and one bowler did all the damage. 🏏 Midweek XI — away to Gravesend CC The exclusive zero club welcomed two new entries on the same afternoon, both claimed by the same tormentor in Gravesend's S Coe. He's not the only flightless bird in the family either — kinsman John Thirkell carries 75 career ducks, brother Tim 16, and Stuart 1, a proper family firm of noughts, while Nigel himself shares the Only Two Quacks honour with Jim Hall. On this very day back in 1952, D Simmons also strolled back for nought against Charles Arkcoll, one of 4 in a career spanning 1949 to 1964 — history, as ever, repeating itself in the most unremarkable way possible. Last man in, John Lambert went b S Coe for his maiden LPCC duck, the 1st of his season and his career, from just 3 innings — an entry-level membership at 321st of 880 all-time, with plenty of scope to climb. Fittingly, this too fell on the anniversary of D Simmons' 1952 duck against Charles Arkcoll — the calendar clearly has a sense of humour. Consistency of a very particular kind, then, from Gravesend's S Coe — a two-wicket haul that reads rather better in this newsletter than it will in the Midweek XI's own averages. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches, both in the same innings, both to the same bowler — triumph through adversity was in short supply this week, but the art of not scoring found its usual devotees. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks in 19 innings (26.3%) - Andrew Moss — 3 ducks in 16 innings (18.8%) - Alan Cradduck — 2 ducks in 10 innings (20.0%) - Nigel Thirkell — 2 ducks in 14 innings (14.3%) - John Thirkell — 2 ducks in 16 innings (12.5%) Nigel's latest duck keeps the family honours ticking over nicely, though he still trails his own brother Tim by three at the top of the table — a proper sibling rivalry brewing in the least dignified category the club has to offer. Bowaters CC, 1960, claimed another victim on this same date — Pat Hardwick again, keeping Tim company across twelve years. - Paul Brattle (6): b C Wren, 0. A modest 1st of the season but a 5th for the career, 80th of 877 — and a long-standing Debutante Duck, having opened his account with a nought some seasons back. Week 15 · 5 Aug 1972 – 9 Aug 1972
Three fixtures out on the county's grounds this week and, remarkably, the ducks proved almost impossible to trap. Only one bird found its way into the bag — but what it lacked in company, it made up for in pedigree. 🏏 Sunday NVKC v Blunham CC Andrew Moss, promoted to number 2, departed caught P Ranson bowled M Watson — a tidy piece of work from the Blunham attack that leaves Andrew with precious little to show for his afternoon. Sixteen years apart, same fate, same green field somewhere in Kent. 📋 Week in Summary Three matches, one duck — a strangely disciplined week by Linton Park's usual standards. Whether that's a triumph of application or simply a lack of opportunity, the Duck Club isn't in the business of asking questions. Until then, quack on. 0️⃣
