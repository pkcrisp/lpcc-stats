🗓️ 28 Sep – 04 Oct 1957 · 1957 — 2 matches

Sun 29 Sep 1957 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformDDDLD

Sunday last brought Linton Park CC out for a home fixture, and for once the coin obliged me — I called correctly and, in a fit of what I can only describe as misplaced confidence, put us in to bat. It did not, in hindsight, cover me in glory.

Sixty-four all out in 27.2 overs is not the sort of total that has opposition captains reaching for the record books, unless it's the record for shortest tea interval required. Tony Levick was the exception to an otherwise grim afternoon, top-scoring with 27 and looking, briefly, like a man playing a different match to the rest of us. Stan Wratten and C. Haylor, meanwhile, both contributed golden ducks — a matching pair, like bookends, propping up absolutely nothing.

There was, I suppose, a silver lining of sorts up the season table: Tony Levick's 27 keeps him top of our 1957 Married run charts, while Clive Smith continues to lord it over the Singles column with 38. Small comfort when chasing a target that never had a chance of being defended.

We went down by 3 wickets in the end, and no amount of gallows humour in the pavilion could disguise that 64 simply wasn't cricket, in the fullest sense of the phrase. Heads up, chins slightly less so, and back to the nets — there is clearly work to be done before we trouble anyone's scorers again.

Sun 22 Sep 1957 🧢 1st XIv TS Tilling Steven · HomeformWLWLD

It was a bright Sunday, 22 September, and Tilling Steven came to us at Linton Park with the sun on their side, if not, as it transpired, much else. We had first use of the ball, and it proved a shrewd notion — they were shot out for 79 in 34 overs, a total that flattered nobody. Only T. Maskell, unbeaten on 25, showed any real appetite for the crease, while poor R. Bailey trudged off for nought. The pick of our attack was Taylor, whose 5-51 was every inch a five-for and thoroughly earned, well supported by Cliff Anscombe's tidy 4-18 — four maidens included, the sort of miserly spell that makes a captain purr — and a wicket for Alan Wilkes.

Whatever confidence that bowling display bred, we squandered with interest at the crease. Fifty-seven all out in a paltry 23 overs is not a scorecard one frames for the clubhouse wall; William Buck, C. Haylor and Cliff Anscombe all contributed ducks, a hat-trick of golden nothings that rather undid our good work. I managed 20 and did what I could to hold things together, but it wasn't nearly enough.

Beaten by 22 runs, then, and no arguing with the arithmetic. Small consolation from the season charts — I remain top of the 1st XI run tally on 950, and Alan Wilkes sits fifth among the wicket-takers with 29 — but consolation is all it is. We'll be better for it next week.

Sat 21 Sep 1957 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformDWLWL

From the captain's chair — Saturday 21 September 1957. Bearsted CC at home, and I'm afraid I lost the toss again; they had no hesitation putting us in the field, which on a fine September afternoon felt rather like being made to do the washing-up before you'd even had your tea.

Bearsted made a solid 169/7 from their 52 overs, built chiefly around A "Tony" Snook's unbeaten 65, with Gordon Hawkins (45) and George Lambell (30) lending sturdy support. We had them 2 for 1 at one stage and thought we were onto something, but a stubborn second-wicket stand of 63 dragged them back into contention, and an irritating last-wicket flourish of 37 runs we could frankly have done without rounded things off. Bob Brigden was the pick of the attack, 3 for 38 with three tidy maidens, backed up by Tony Snell (2-69) and Len Conway (1-26).

In reply we made a spirited 135 for 5 from 33.3 overs. The openers gave us a proper platform — 95 for the first wicket — before I chipped in with a personal 52, well supported by Basil Sears's 45, and I'll admit that half-century was rather satisfying. Wickets then tumbled in a rush from 121 to 135, and poor Richard Harris departed for a golden duck without troubling the scorers.

Special mention for Bob Brigden, who reached 50 career wickets and climbed past H G Smith into 22nd on the club's all-time list — a fine day's work with the ball. Honours shared, then, in a draw that satisfied nobody and disappointed no one either — a proper stalemate, and no bad way to spend a Saturday.

Here's the match report:

---

Sunday, and Halfway House came to visit us on what I'd call a good toss to win — I had no hesitation electing to bat, and for once the decision looked every inch the masterstroke it was intended to be.

We batted with real substance, posting 216 for 7 from our 46 overs, the innings built around a magnificent hundred from Maurice Fenner — 122 of the finest, and not many better knocks will grace this gazette all season. John's Taylor picked up a wicket too for good support. In the end Halfway House closed on 123 for 7, well short but not without a fight.

A draw, then — honours shared, neither side quite able to land the decisive blow. With Ames now second in our 1957 wicket charts and yours truly leading the run-scoring, I'll allow myself a quiet smile on the walk back to the pavilion. Not a win, but a thoroughly good afternoon's cricket, and the tea urn, as ever, the true match-winner.
