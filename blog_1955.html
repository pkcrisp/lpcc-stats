🦆 Duck Report — 24-25 September 1955 🦆

7 ducks
Week 22 · 24 Sep 1955 – 25 Sep 1955
Two fixtures, seven noughts, and a Sunday match that ate its own tail. Grab a cuppa. Saturday 24 September — 1st XI v Town Malling CC John Carter fell lbw to T- Lower, opening his account for season and career alike — his 1st duck from just 3 innings. He joins the exclusive company of - Dampier, whose entire 1952 Linton career amounted to a single innings and a single duck, a proper "One and Done." 664 names still to catch if John fancies the chase. Sunday 25 September — Married v Singles, Linton Park A rich harvest of six, and a fixture that briefly turned into a circle of poultry justice. C- Haylor and Frank Wright both departed lbw to Bob Brigden, each reaching 2 career ducks and drawing level with A S Day (1944–1949, 2 from 11 innings). Wright does so as a fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck and Retirement Duck honours boards from past campaigns — old glories, not today's. Frank Brattle went lbw for his maiden LPCC duck, bowled by none other than John Ames — a chip off no particular block, though kin Percy Brattle already carries 4 of his own. Frank joins the A S Day club too, on 1. Ray Tippen holed out c&b to Tony Tipples for his 4th career duck from 38 innings, levelling with Alan Wilkes (1953–1955), himself a veteran of the "Only Two Quacks" honours — one of them shared with today's other protagonist. Peter Weedon followed, lbw to Tipples, for his 3rd of the season and a milestone 10th career duck, both he and Tippen fellow travellers on the Retirement Duck board from earlier chapters. Weedon vaults to 11th on the all-time chart, level with Don Hartridge (1940–1952, 86 innings). And then the twist: John Ames, the very bowler who'd accounted for Brattle, walked back himself — bowled by Ray Tippen, the man he'd just watched fall to Tipples. Ames's 5th duck of the season and 11th of his career lifts him to 8th all-time, a name already stitched through the Ducks in a Row, Homebody Duck top 10 and Quacking Together boards. What goes around, comes around, apparently within the same afternoon. Week in Summary Seven ducks from two matches — a quiet Saturday nibble followed by a proper Sunday cull. Special mention to Married v Singles, which for one over resembled an ouroboros: bowler becomes batsman becomes duck. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 1. John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 33 innings (18.18%) 2. John Ames — 5 ducks, 19 innings (26.32%) 3. Peter Weedon — 3 ducks, 9 innings (33.33%) 3. William Blight — 3 ducks, 9 innings (33.33%) 5. John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks, 13 innings (23.08%) Next fixture beckons, and with it more names for the honours board. The zero club never sleeps 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 17–18 September 1955

1 duck
Week 21 · 17 Sep 1955 – 18 Sep 1955
Fowl play was in remarkably short supply this weekend. Across two fixtures on the 17th and 18th, the Duck Club processed just a single new arrival — proof, if any were needed, that even ignominy has its off weeks. 🏏 1st XI v Bearsted CC (home) At number five, C- Haylor became the weekend's sole representative, caught by J- Basten off the bowling of Les Austin without troubling the scorers. It's a landmark of sorts — his first duck of the 1955 season, and, in five innings for the club, his maiden career nought. That's good enough for 159th of 665 in the all-time standings, a place he now shares with - Dampier, whose own single career duck back in 1952 was enough to see him inducted into both the Debutante Duck and One and Done clubs. Haylor, for what it's worth, is already a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell — happily, nobody's suggesting he's bowing out again just yet. 📋 Week in Summary A quiet one by Duck Club standards — two matches, one duck, and precious little else to report. Sometimes the ducks simply decline the invitation. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — 1955 1. John Thirkell — 6 ducks in 31 innings (19.35%) 2. John Ames — 4 ducks in 18 innings (22.22%) 3. William Blight — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.33%) 4. John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) 5. Richard Harris — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.00%) William Blight's strike rate for nought remains the frightening one to watch — a third of his innings ending without score. 🍩 Next fixture awaits, and with it, the ever-present promise of fresh candidates for the honours board. The Duck Club never sleeps — even when, this week, it barely stirred.

🦆 Duck News — 10 & 11 September 1955

2 ducks
Week 20 · 10 Sep 1955 – 11 Sep 1955
A quiet weekend for the Duck Hunting fraternity numerically, but no less notable for it — two fresh entries to the honours ledger, both plucked from the very same innings against Old Roffensians. Consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. 1st XI v Old Roffensians — Saturday 10th, home First to fall was John --'s Taylor, promoted to first-wicket-down and undone caught and bowled by Deer before he could trouble the printer. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 9th for the club in 101 innings — good enough for a lofty 13th on the all-time list of 662. Spookily, on this very date back in 1944, A S Day was performing an identical vanishing act against George Fenner XI, racking up the second of his own two career ducks in a stint running 1944 to 1949. The calendar, it seems, never forgets a nought. He wasn't lonely for long. At number eight, Michael Morton edged behind off Roberts, well held by R Harris, for his 1st duck of the season and 6th of an LPCC career now standing at 40 innings — 27th all-time. Morton too finds himself keeping the anniversary of A S Day's 1944 effort company, same fixture, same date, eleven years and two very different scorecards apart. Top and middle order both accounted for on the same afternoon — a rare show of collaborative underachievement Old Roffensians will no doubt be thanking their bowlers for. Week in summary Two matches, two ducks, both from the same innings — the ducks otherwise kept a low profile elsewhere on the fixture list. Taylor's effort keeps him firmly inside this season's top five, a mere achievement's breadth off the podium. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 29 innings (20.69%) - John Ames — 4 ducks, 18 innings (22.22%) - William Blight — 3 ducks, 9 innings (33.33%) - John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks, 13 innings (23.08%) - Richard Harris — 3 ducks, 18 innings (16.67%) With the season entering its closing weeks, the race for Duck of the Year remains wide open — Thirkell holds a narrow lead but the field is bunched tightly behind him. Plenty of overs left for someone to make their move. Pads on, bails watching — see you at the next fixture for further additions to the club's exclusive zero. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 3-4 September 1955

1 duck
Week 19 · 3 Sep 1955 – 4 Sep 1955
A quiet weekend on the quacking front — two matches played, and old faithful John Ames doing his bit to keep the ledger from a fully fowl-free weekend. 1st XI At Birchington Wanderers, John Ames departed lbw to A Goodenough, batting at five and adding precisely nothing to the total. It's his 4th duck of the season and his 10th for the club overall from 69 innings — good enough, or bad enough, for 10th on the all-time list of 662. Ames arrives with some ceremony too: he's mid-stretch on Ducks in a Row (four consecutive, no less), sits in the Homebody Duck top ten, and keeps distinguished company on the honours board via Only Two Quacks with Alan Wilkes and a pair of Quacking Together entries alongside Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. A man who collects noughts the way others collect stamps. Fittingly, the calendar has form here too — on this very day in 1949, P- Martin also strolled back for a duck, against East Peckham & Nettlestead CC of all sides. Martin, as it happens, is a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from his eventual farewell — a fate Ames will no doubt eye nervously from a safe distance. Week in Summary One duck from two fixtures — the bowlers evidently had the week off, or the rest of the batting simply couldn't be bothered to disappoint us further. Steady as she goes, in the art of not scoring. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🏆 - John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 27 innings (22.2%) - John Ames — 4 ducks, 18 innings (22.2%) - William Blight — 3 ducks, 9 innings (33.3%) - Richard Harris — 3 ducks, 17 innings (17.6%) - Tony Snell — 2 ducks, 6 innings (33.3%) Thirkell holds top spot, but Ames is breathing down his neck after this week's addition — plenty of season left for a late surge on Duck of the Year. Next fixtures await, and with them, no doubt, fresh candidates for the honours board. See you at the crease — or, more likely, in the pavilion shortly after. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — Sunday 28 August 1955

3 ducks
Week 18 · 28 Aug 1955
One match on the board this week, and D Hambrook XI made short work of turning three Linton batsmen into paid-up members of the season's zero club. 1st XI v D Hambrook XI (Home) Tony Tipples fell early at first wicket down, bowled by X Gill without troubling the scorers. It's his 2nd duck of the season and the 3rd of a career now standing at 25 innings — good enough, or bad enough, for 74th on the all-time list of 661. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1893, W W Tarrance was doing exactly the same thing against The Mote CC. John Ames followed at four, run out for nought, the identity of his executioner apparently lost to the scorebook. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 9th of a career that already carries some serious silverware — a place in the Ducks in a Row club for four on the bounce, a spot in the Homebody Duck top 10, and shared quacking honours with Alan Wilkes, Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. He sits 12th all-time out of 661, rarefied if unwanted company. And once more — 28 August, 1893, The Mote CC — Tarrance strikes again. Peter Weedon rounded things off at number eight, bowled by X Deal. His 2nd of the season and 9th for a career spanning 50 innings, good for 13th on the all-time chart. Peter already carries a presumptive place in the Retirement Duck club, awarded for an earlier knock once thought to be his last, though evidently the invitation was declined. Same date, same year, same Tarrance walking back at The Mote CC. Week in Summary Just the one fixture, but D Hambrook made it count — three ducks in a single innings for the 1st XI, top order to lower middle order all sampling the zero club. Quiet everywhere else. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Thirkell — 25 inns, 6 ducks (24.0%) - William Blight — 9 inns, 3 ducks (33.33%) - Richard Harris — 16 inns, 3 ducks (18.75%) - John Ames — 17 inns, 3 ducks (17.65%) - Tony Snell — 6 inns, 2 ducks (33.33%) Thirkell holds firm at the summit, but Ames' latest addition keeps him snapping at the leaderboard's heels. The next fixture awaits — the zero club never sleeps. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 20 & 21 August 1955 🦆

2 ducks
Week 17 · 20 Aug 1955 – 21 Aug 1955
Two fixtures, two ducks, and the 1st XI keeping the exclusive zero club ticking over nicely as August draws towards its close. Saturday 20 August — v Charles Arkcoll Peter Weedon strode out at six and departed without troubling the scorers, b Randolph Thompson, for his 1st duck of the season. It's the 8th of a long and distinguished career spanning 49 innings, enough to keep him lodged at 16th in the all-time standings — and a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, though nobody is suggesting he's off anywhere just yet. Curiously, on this very day back in 1944, W Bunting (who played 1941–1963... sorry, 1941–1948) was doing exactly the same thing against KCC Maidstone. History, like the third slip catch, has a habit of repeating itself. Sunday 21 August — v Halfway House John Ames made it two ducks for the club in as many days, b Roger Mills at number seven, his 2nd of the season. That's career duck number 8 from 67 innings, good enough for 15th all-time. Ames arrives thoroughly decorated — four consecutive ducks in a row, a top-10 spot on the Homebody Duck standings for noughts collected on home soil, and shared Quacking Together honours with both Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten, plus an Only Two Quacks pairing with Alan Wilkes. Speaking of Wratten — on this same calendar date back in 1949, he was himself walking back for a duck against The Mote CC. Some partnerships, it seems, are written in the scorebook as well as the fixture list. Week in Summary A tidy two ducks from two matches — nothing spectacular, but a solid, dependable contribution to the season's tally, and a fine demonstration of the art of not scoring on consecutive days. 🦆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. 🦆 Duck Report — 6–7 August 1955

2 ducks
Week 15 · 6 Aug 1955 – 7 Aug 1955
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, and the exclusive zero club welcomed exactly two new entries — both from the very same afternoon at the very same ground, which is either shocking bad luck or excellent company. 1st XI v Anchorians CC (Saturday 7 August) - William Buck opened the innings and departed for nought, bowled by B Mitchell — his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of his career, good enough for 52nd of 660 in the all-time table. He shares the Quacking Together honour with a certain John Thirkell, and today the pair made it literal, both falling in the same match. On this very day back in 1909, C Stevens was doing much the same against the Royal West Kent Regiment — some traditions refuse to die. - Batting at five, John Thirkell followed him back just as promptly, bowled by A Hart for his 6th duck of the summer and 32nd of a career that sits, remarkably, at #1 in the entire 660-strong all-time standings. Reigning Duck of the Year in 1952 and 1954, and defending the crown again in 1955, Thirkell is also a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens — a man who can manufacture a nought absolutely anywhere on the fixture list. Like Buck, he finds his own historical twin in C Stevens, who made his duck on this same August date in 1909. The Week in Noughts Two matches, two ducks, one shared scorecard of shame — Buck and Thirkell's joint failure at Anchorians is precisely the sort of Quacking Together moment the honours board exists to record. Elsewhere the ducks, mercifully, evaded capture entirely. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 22 innings (27.3%) 2. William Blight — 3 ducks, 8 innings (37.5%) 3. Richard Harris — 3 ducks, 14 innings (21.4%) 4. John Abnett — 2 ducks, 5 innings (40.0%) 5. Tony Snell — 2 ducks, 6 innings (33.3%) Thirkell leads the chase for a fourth Duck of the Year crown, with Blight's blistering 37.5% strike rate lurking as the dark horse. Next fixture looms — the crease awaits, and so, inevitably, does the duck. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 30 July to 1 August 1955

6 ducks
Week 14 · 30 Jul 1955 – 1 Aug 1955
Three matches, three days, six ducks — the 1955 season proving as ungenerous as ever to Linton's top order. Every nought fell to the 1st XI this week, and Monday supplied a full house on its own. Saturday 30 July – Aylesford Paper Mills CC (away) R- Waters went early, bowled by R Baker for his first duck of the season and the second of a career now eleven innings deep — level with A S Day, who reached the same pair between 1944 and 1949. Waters is also a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, though today was merely a rehearsal. Basil Sears followed, lbw to Brian Fridd, his first of the season but a seventh for the career column, now the 17th most decorated duck-collector on Linton's 660-name roll. He joins A Startup, who reached seven between 1868 and 1882, still holder of the club's five-in-a-row record. Sunday 31 July – Maidstone AKCC (home) Number eleven Tony Snell was bowled by David Sayer for his second duck of the season and fourth of a career already carrying the Ducks in a Row badge for four in succession. On this very day in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden also walked back for nought against Headcorn CC, en route to ten career ducks and that season's Duck of the Year crown. Monday 1 August – Lord Cornwallis XI (home) A red-letter day for the wrong reasons: three Linton wickets fell for nought in the same innings. Fittingly, on this date in 1910 the Reverend F A Leveson-Gower also made a duck, against Maidstone Conservative Club CC — a date apparently fated to repeat itself threefold. Opener William Buck went first, caught D- Reader bowled K- Goodwin, his third career duck from 136 innings and 73rd on the all-time list. John Thirkell followed, bowled G- Murray, for a fifth duck of the season — his all-time club record now 31, and Duck of the Year for a third time after 1952 and 1954. Buck and Thirkell share the club's Quacking Together honour, and earned it afresh falling together today. John Abnett completed the set, bowled F- Cornwallis, his second duck of the season and third from just seven innings — already boasting Ducks in a Row for four in succession. Six ducks, three matches, one thoroughly chastened top order. Monday's Lord Cornwallis collapse alone accounted for half the week's tally — a fine advertisement for the art of not scoring. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Thirkell — 5 ducks in 20 innings (25.0%) - William Blight — 3 ducks in 8 innings (37.5%) - Richard Harris — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) - John Abnett — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40.0%) - Tony Snell — 2 ducks in 6 innings (33.33%) Thirkell leads by daylight, though Abnett's 40% strike rate says the chasing pack has teeth. Next weekend's fixtures await — the ducks, no doubt, already limbering up. See you there. 🦆

With Thirkell out in front and Blight breathing down his neck on percentage alone, the race for this season's crown is very much alive. We watch. We wait. The ducks will return. 🦆🍩0️⃣

St Lukes will remember this one fondly. Linton, perhaps, less so. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 17 inns, 4 ducks (23.5%) - William Blight — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.9%) - Richard Harris — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.3%) - Bob Arnold — 7 inns, 2 ducks (28.6%) - John --'s Taylor — 8 inns, 2 ducks (25.0%) Thirkell holds a commanding lead in the race for 1955 Duck of the Year, but with the season far from over there's still time for the chasing pack to close the gap. 🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 9-10 July 1955

1 duck
Week 11 · 9 Jul 1955 – 10 Jul 1955
A quiet weekend by the standards of the exclusive zero club — two fixtures, and only one soul brave (or careless) enough to accept the invitation. Still, a duck's a duck, and this one carries some pedigree. 🏏 Saturday 9th July 1st XI v A.Trembeths (home) Batting at first drop, John --'s Taylor departed for nought, bowled by M- Dengate. It's his 2nd duck of the 1955 season and the 8th of a long and largely respectable career spanning 95 innings — good enough to sit 14th of 659 in the all-time table, a rank most club members would happily retire on.
