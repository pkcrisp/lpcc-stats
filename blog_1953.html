The Duck Report — 27th September 1953

2 ducks
Week 22 · 27 Sep 1953
🦆 A modest afternoon at the crease for the Married eleven against Linton Park CC, but modesty is, as ever, our stock in trade. Two eggs laid, two names added to the ledger, and the season's aggregate creeps quietly onward. Married - Tony Tipples, in at first wicket down, was undone by John Thirkell and returned to the pavilion having troubled the scorers not at all — his 1st duck of the season, and the 1st of a career now standing at 11 innings. He takes his place at 257th of 640 in the club's all-time reckoning, and joins the shade of - Dampier on a career tally of exactly one, though Dampier, it should be noted, took the more decisive step afterwards of never batting again. - John Ames, following him to the wicket at first drop, fell to Michael Morton for the fifth time this season — a figure that also happens to be his career total from 33 innings, and good enough for 28th of 640 on the honours board. Mr Ames arrives, moreover, as a gentleman of substance in these matters: four consecutive ducks already to his name, a fixture of the Homebody Duck top ten, and a familiar face in the club's pairing books alongside Alan Wilkes, Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. He now stands level on career ducks with Allan Peach of the 1900s vintage — kindred spirits separated by half a century but united in the art of not scoring. The Week in Brief One match, one innings apiece for our two unfortunates, and both ducks fell to the bowlers of Linton Park CC — a side evidently in fine order with the ball. No golden ducks this week, no pairs, no family affairs to report — merely the eleven's usual steady contribution to the exclusive zero club. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. J Barclay — 21 innings, 6 ducks, 28.57% 2. John Ames — 18 innings, 5 ducks, 27.78% 3. John Thirkell — 32 innings, 5 ducks, 15.63% 4. Gordon Fenner — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% 5. Gerald Wright — 14 innings, 4 ducks, 28.57% A fine crop of candidates jostling for the season's crown of dubious distinction. The eleven takes the field again next weekend, and the Duck Club stands ready, as always, to record whatever fresh triumphs through adversity await. Until then, gentlemen — mind your straight bat, and good luck keeping off this list.

THE DUCK CLUB GAZETTE — 19th & 20th September, 1953

3 ducks
Week 21 · 19 Sep 1953 – 20 Sep 1953
Two fixtures took the field this weekend, and though Sunday's eleven kept their bats commendably straight, Saturday's visit from Bearsted CC yielded three eggs for the collection. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest subscribers below. 1st XI v Bearsted CC (home, 19th September) - Richard Harris, at six, was run out, the fielders unsure quite whom to credit, which seems only fair given nobody troubled the scorer. His third duck of the season and the fourteenth of a distinguished career spanning 77 innings, good enough for third place on the all-time table. He joins the long-serving Frank Sutton (1925-1949, twice Duck of the Year) on exactly fourteen, fine company to keep in the art of not scoring. - Alan Wilkes, batting at eight, was caught behind by David Lister off the bowling of Vic Pound. A modest first duck of the season, and the first of his career in nine innings, rank 129 of 640, with room yet to climb. He is a paid-up member of the Only Two Quacks club twice over, having shared that particular distinction with both Bob Arnold and John Ames in seasons past. On career ducks he draws level, at one apiece, with a long roll of single-duck careers stretching back to the eighteen-eighties, most recently a fellow named Dampier, whose lone egg in 1952 is remembered as his last innings for any side. - Gerald Wright, last man in at eleven, was bowled by Vic Pound, the same bowler claiming his second victim of the innings. A fourth duck of the season for Wright, and the sixth of his career in a mere nineteen innings, placing him eighteenth on the all-time roll. He draws level on career ducks with D Sells (1943-44, Duck of the Year that year, with four in a row to his name), plucky company for a lower-order man. The Week in Summary All three ducks fell in the one innings on Saturday, Bearsted's bowlers evidently finding the home dressing room short of resolve; Sunday's fixture passed, remarkably, without a single egg laid. Two matches, three ducks: a sticky wicket for the middle and tail alike, though the tail, true to form, wagged not at all. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. J Barclay — 21 innings, 6 ducks, 28.57% 2. John Thirkell — 32 innings, 5 ducks, 15.63% 3. Gordon Fenner — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% 4. Gerald Wright — 14 innings, 4 ducks, 28.57% 5. John Ames — 17 innings, 4 ducks, 23.53% The season's chase for ignominy continues in fine style. Members are warmly invited to the next fixture, where the eleven will doubtless furnish fresh candidates for the honours board, the wicket, as ever, remaining undefeated.

The Duck Club Gazette — 12th and 13th September, 1953

0 ducks
Week 20 · 12 Sep 1953 – 13 Sep 1953
A quiet week, and a disappointing one for connoisseurs of the duck's egg. Across both fixtures played this weekend, the club's watchful eye — ever hopeful, pencil poised over the honours board — found not a single nought worth the recording. Every man who walked to the crease departed, sooner or later, with at least one run to his name, which is all very well for the scorebook but leaves this correspondent with precious little to celebrate. One might almost suspect a conspiracy of straight bats and watchful footwork about the ground this week, though it is more likely simple ill fortune for the Duck Club, whose members hunt in vain whenever an eleven insists on playing sensibly. The tail, we are reliably informed, wagged rather than folded, and not a man among either side troubled the exclusive zero club these two days. Still, one barren weekend does not empty the trophy cabinet, nor dim the lustre of those already enrolled in the club's dubious distinction. The season's leading exponents of the art of not scoring remain handsomely in credit, and the list below stands as testament to sterner, more fruitful days for the pursuit of nought. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - J Barclay — 20 innings, 6 ducks, 30.0% - John Thirkell — 30 innings, 5 ducks, 16.67% - Gordon Fenner — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% - John Ames — 16 innings, 4 ducks, 25.0% - Gerald Wright — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% Take heart, members. The next fixture is never far away, and a sticky wicket or one loose stroke early on is ever but a ball away from restoring the natural order of things. Until then, keep your pencils sharpened and your expectations modest — the ducks, we have no doubt, will return.

The Duck Club Gazette — 5th and 6th September, 1953

4 ducks
Week 19 · 5 Sep 1953 – 6 Sep 1953
A brace of fixtures this week, and the eleven found four ways to depart without troubling the scorer. The exclusive zero club, ever discerning in its membership, drew its new candidates from both ends of the county this week. 1st XI At Yalding, John Thirkell strode out first wicket down and returned bowled by D. Brooker, his fifth duck's egg of the summer and the nineteenth of a long and eventful career — a career distinguished chiefly by its studious avoidance of runs. He sits alone at the head of the club's roll of honour, first of 639, and carries into every innings the laurels of Duck of the Year 1952, a place among both the Ducks on the Road and the Homebody Duck top tens, and shared quacking honours with Bob Arnold and William Buck besides. A gentleman for whom the long walk back has become almost a habit of residence. Further down the order, J Barclay followed him to the pavilion, bowled by G. Davis, his sixth duck of the season and sixth of his career, good enough for twentieth on the all-time list and this year's Duck of the Year honour already resting on his shoulders. He is besides a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell. He now stands level on six career ducks with D Sells, who played his own quiet innings of failure between 1943 and 1944 — kindred spirits separated by a decade but united in arithmetic. At Gillingham the following day, the eleven suffered a curious symmetry: Richard Harris and Gordon Fenner, batting at six and seven, were both bowled by R. Beckenham within the same innings. Harris departed for his second duck of the season, his thirteenth in a career of seventy-six innings and fourth on the club's roll of shame. Fenner followed directly behind him, his fourth duck of the summer and also his thirteenth for his career, third all-time — and now drawing level with Harris himself, the two gentlemen sharing both a bowler's figures and a lifetime tally within the space of a single over. Fenner, it may be added, keeps failure rather in the family, his brother Joe carrying five ducks of his own. The Week in Brief Two matches, four ducks, and something of a pattern: three of the four fell to the bowled dismissal, and Gillingham's R. Beckenham managed the rare distinction of accounting for two Duck Club members in a single innings. A sticky wicket for the top and middle order alike, and no mistake. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - J Barclay — 20 innings, 6 ducks, 30.00% - John Thirkell — 28 innings, 5 ducks, 17.86% - Gordon Fenner — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% - John Ames — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Gerald Wright — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% The season's honours board continues to fill most satisfactorily, and the eleven take the field again next week with every confidence that fresh candidates will present themselves. The Club awaits them, as ever, with open arms and a duly sharpened pencil.

5 ducks
Week 18 · 29 Aug 1953
A single fixture, away to Hunton Wanderers, yet the eleven contrived five entries in the ledger of nought. A sticky wicket indeed, and the zero club welcomes its latest subscribers. 1st XI - John Thirkell, in at three, was taken by Reg Oldfield off J Ludden for his fourth duck of the season and eighteenth of his career — top of the all-time table at 1 of 639, a Duck of the Year laureate of 1952 and a fixture of the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens. He and Bob Arnold, old campaigners of the Quacking Together honours board, duly quacked together again this very afternoon. On this same date in 1949, one E Simmons also strolled back for nought against Town Malling CC — the calendar has its favourites. - N Simmons followed at five, bowled by J Ludden, his maiden season duck and maiden career duck alike in only his fourth innings — rank 222 of 639, and already a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from some past and presumably distant farewell. Like his captain, he shares his date with E Simmons of 1949. - Bob Arnold, at seven, fell leg before to R Venner for his second duck of the season and ninth of his career, tenth on the all-time roll and holder of Ducks in a Row, four consecutive — a spell so barren it merits its own trophy. His Quacking Together honour with Thirkell was renewed in the same innings. - G- Tompkinson, promoted to nine, was caught by J Foley off J Ludden in his very first innings for the club — a debut duck, and entry into the Debutante Duck club at the first attempt. Like every one of today's unfortunates, his fall echoes E Simmons on this same date in 1949 against Town Malling CC. Five ducks from a single match, top and tail of the order both accounted for, and J Ludden the chief tormentor with three scalps. The eleven may reflect that a duck's egg is at least honestly come by. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. J Barclay — 19 innings, 5 ducks, 26.32% 2. John Ames — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% 3. The Duck Club Gazette — Week Ending 22nd August 1953

1 duck
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1953
A modest week for the eleven, home to Charles Arkcoll, yielding but a single fresh entry to the Zero Club's ledger. Modest in number, never in ceremony — the club has always held that one duck properly honoured is worth more than a dozen let pass unremarked. 1st XI J Barclay, promoted to number eight, offered a return catch that Stan Wratten was only too glad to accept — c & b Stan Wratten, and back to the pavilion without troubling the scorers. It is his fifth duck of the season and stands as the twenty-fourth entry on the club's all-time roll of dubious distinction, a list now numbering 638 names in all. With the season itself bearing the same number, 1953, Barclay's effort must be counted toward this year's Duck of the Year — a title awarded not for runs made but, in the club's own particular reckoning, for runs conspicuously not made, and he goes into the reckoning as a serious contender. He remains besides a long-established member of the Retirement Duck fellowship, a past farewell recorded on the honours board from an earlier chapter of his cricketing life, and no reflection upon this week's proceedings. Most curiously of all, on this very date in 1903, a full half-century before, one W Peach fell in precisely the same manner against the 1st Innings of the Kent County Constabulary — the art of not scoring, it seems, keeps a faithful calendar. The Week in Summary One match, one duck, and a single name added to the ledger — the eleven played a generally straight bat this week, leaving the Zero Club's secretary with rather less to record than usual. Whether this reflects improved batting or merely a quiet fixture, the committee declines to speculate. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - J Barclay — 18 innings, 5 ducks, 27.78% - John Ames — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% - Michael Morton — 16 innings, 3 ducks, 18.75% - John Thirkell — 25 innings, 3 ducks, 12.00% Barclay leads the field by a comfortable margin, and the committee awaits with interest whichever member the next fixture sees fit to add to these ranks. Members are reminded that the exclusive zero club never closes its books, and a sticky wicket awaits us all in due course.

The Duck Club Gazette — 15th and 16th August, 1953

3 ducks
Week 16 · 15 Aug 1953 – 16 Aug 1953
Two fixtures in the diary this week, but the duck's egg made its appearance in only one of them — the visit of Mote A, where the home eleven contrived three ways of getting back to the pavilion without troubling the scorer. The other fixture passed off, disappointingly for this correspondent, without a nought to its name. 1st XI — v Mote A, 16th August - John Ames, at first wicket down, was bowled by P. Bryant for his fourth duck of the summer — the fourth of a season that continues to test his patience, and enough, by the club's own ledger, to make it "Ducks in a Row," four in succession, a feat few would boast of but none can deny. It is his fourth career nought in thirty innings, thirty-first — no, forty-first — in the all-time order of six hundred and thirty-eight, and he remains a fixture of the Homebody Duck company besides sharing past quacking honours with Roy Clarke, Stan Wratten and Alan Wilkes. Curiously, on this very day in 1893, one George Champion also strolled back for nought, against the Free Foresters. - John Thirkell, in at four, fell to J. Coomber for his third duck of the season and his seventeenth in a long and distinguished career of one hundred and nineteen innings — a career which, remarkably, sits him at the very head of the club's all-time table, first of six hundred and thirty-eight. A former Duck of the Year, and a name long resident in the Lone Duck and Homebody top tens, he adds merely another line to an already formidable record. He, too, shares his day with George Champion's duck of 1893. - L Skinner, batting at ten, was bowled by S. Capon for his maiden duck of the season, and the first of his short career so far — one nought from two innings, placing him modestly at two hundred and eleventh of all who have ever made the walk. He carries, from some past and unrecorded reckoning, a place among the Retirement Duck company, though we trust his innings with the club is far from concluded. He, like his colleagues, kept company with George Champion's ghost of 1893. The Week in Brief Three ducks from two matches, the whole clutch laid in a single innings against Mote A — a fine morning's work for the visiting bowlers, and a poor one for the home order's middle and lower reaches. The 2nd XI, for once, escape this week's report entirely. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. John Ames — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% 2. J Barclay — 17 innings, 4 ducks, 23.53% 3. Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% 4. Michael Morton — 16 innings, 3 ducks, 18.75% 5. John Thirkell — 24 innings, 3 ducks, 12.5% The season's table remains as competitive as ever, and next week's fixtures will doubtless furnish fresh candidates for its ranks. Members are warmly invited to attend, bat straight if they can manage it, and take their chances of an entry in this column if they cannot.

Two fixtures were put to the eleven this week, and Headcorn CC's bowlers found the home order rather more accommodating than the home order would have liked. Five names go to the book this week — a fine harvest for a side that prefers its runs to its noughts. 1st XI v Headcorn CC (home, 8th August) - Basil Sears, promoted to first wicket down, was bowled by J- Farrant for his second duck of the summer and the fifth of a career now standing at sixty-eight innings — a place of dubious distinction he holds at 22nd of 637 on the club's roll of honour. On this very day in 1892, one Jack Fryer likewise walked back for nought against the Royal Engineers CC — the calendar, it seems, has a long memory for these things. - D Simmons followed him back not long after, also to Farrant, his first duck of the season and the fourth of his career in a mere nineteen innings, good enough for 31st of 637. He sits level on career ducks with J Barclay, four apiece, proof the art of not scoring is thriving this season as much as any other. - P- Tew completed something of a spell for Farrant, becoming the third man bowled by him in the innings — a spree the bowler will recall fondly and the top order rather less so. A maiden duck in only his fourth innings, and 227th of 637 on the all-time list. On this same date in 1943, Frank Sutton was similarly dispatched for nought against the RA Wing. - Robert Muir, at number nine, was run out for his first career duck in eighteen innings, ranking 238th of 637 — sitting level on career ducks with A Eltham, who managed the same solitary nought between 1907 and 1910. Muir is also, from a past farewell, a paid-up member of the club's Retirement Duck fraternity, though this was plainly a mid-innings mishap rather than any parting shot. - Bert Buckoke brought up the innings' fifth duck, stumped by L- Woodcock off R- Excell — his first duck of the season, but a formidable twelfth of a career that places him third of all 637 members the club has ever known. Twice crowned Duck of the Year, in 1928 and in 1950, he shares his dozen with Richard Harris, another old campaigner of the crease. The week in brief Two matches, five ducks, every one furnished by a single afternoon against Headcorn — the second fixture, mercifully, passed without incident for the batting order. Three of the five fell to the same bowler, which rather suggests Farrant found his length early and kept it. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard, 1953 1. J Barclay — 16 innings, 4 ducks (25.0%) 2. The Duck Club Gazette — the 2nd and 3rd of August, 1953

2 ducks
Week 14 · 2 Aug 1953 – 3 Aug 1953
Two fixtures over the weekend, and two subscriptions paid into the exclusive zero club, that place of dubious distinction where consistency of a very particular kind is celebrated rather than mourned. 1st XI - J Barclay, turning out against Birchington Wanderers on the Saturday, came in at number six and was bowled by J Ince without troubling the scorers unduly — hardly the straight bat the eleven had hoped for. It is his fourth duck of the season and the fourth of a career of fifteen innings, placing him thirty-seventh in the club's all-time roll of the dismissed for nought. As it happens 1953 is very much his season for it, and this latest nought keeps him snugly inside the Duck of the Year honours he already carries for the year in question. He remains, besides, a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck club, having bowed out on a duck in an innings some while back — a badge worn with the quiet pride of the veteran. Curiously enough, on this very date in 1944 one E- Humphreys performed the identical trick against the National Fire Service Overseas Contingent, proving that some anniversaries keep themselves. - John Thirkell, a day later against the Lord Cornwallis XI, came in at the same number six and departed caught by a- Milsted off the bowling of K- Goodwin, for nought. It is only his second duck of the present season, but a rather more imposing sixteenth in a career now standing at one hundred and fifteen innings — figures that install him, with a certain grim majesty, at the very summit of the club's all-time table: first of six hundred and thirty-seven, a record the rest of the eleven can only regard from a respectful distance. A fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, and a name twice bracketed under Quacking Together, with Bob Arnold and with William Buck besides, Thirkell wears his Duck of the Year 1952 as history now rather than news. Fittingly, on this same day in 1952 Gordon Fenner — no mean hand at the art of not scoring himself — was also making his own quiet way back to the pavilion. A modest week by the club's usual standards: two matches, two ducks, one apiece between Saturday's and Sunday's elevens, and neither a pair nor a family affair to report. The ducks came singly this time, which by this club's lights counts almost as restraint. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - J Barclay — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Gordon Fenner — 10 innings, 3 ducks, 30.00% - John Ames — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% - Michael Morton — 15 innings, 3 ducks, 20.00% - Gerald Wright — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% The wicket will doubtless prove sticky again before the season is out, and the honours board never wants for fresh names. Members are warmly invited to attend the next fixture, bat in hand and hope undimmed, and to make their own contribution to the ledger in the usual manner.

The Duck Club Gazette — 25th to 26th July, 1953

2 ducks
Week 13 · 25 Jul 1953 – 26 Jul 1953
🦆 Two more names are inscribed upon the ledger of the scoreless this week, both from the 1st XI's excursion to Folkestone, and both, it must be said, achieved by rather more decorous means than the customary golden blob. No first-ball ignominy here — merely two batsmen undone after settling their guard, which the club regards as scarcely better consolation. 1st XI — away to Folkestone CC, 26th July Tony Levick, at number five, played on to a leg-before decision off D Morton and trudged back having troubled the scorers not at all. This is his second career duck in five-and-thirty innings for the 1st XI, placing him 104th of 637 in the club's all-time roll of the dismissed-for-nought — a position he now shares, at exactly two apiece, with a positively distinguished company including Arthur Ash, B Lloyd of the Debutante Duck club, and B- Vince, who once carried off the Duck of the Year honour itself in 1941. By curious coincidence of the calendar, on this very date in 1940 one J Short likewise trudged home for nought against Boughton Monchelsea CC — proof, if any were needed, that the ducks of Linton Park stretch back through the decades in unbroken and faithful procession. Bill Hall, sent in at number eight, was stumped by Len Godden off the same D Morton — a dismissal requiring, one notes, rather more effort from the opposition than the standard variety, though the result upon the scorecard is identical. It is his maiden duck of the season and of his one-and-twenty-innings career, seating him 130th of 637 on the all-time list, level on a single career duck with a full company of one-time offenders, several of them Debutante Duck men and one-season wonders besides. Mr Hall is additionally a paid-up member of the club's Retirement Duck honours board, a distinction earned on some past and presumably dignified departure from the crease — not, it should be stressed, this week's affair, upon which no comment as to his continuing career shall be ventured. He too finds his name matched against J Short's duck of the 26th of July, 1940, against Boughton Monchelsea CC. The week in summary A modest haul: two ducks from two fixtures, both from the 1st XI, both at Folkestone, and both, pleasingly, struck by different means of dismissal — the straight bat undone by swing, and the wandering foot undone by wicketkeeper's swiftness. No pairs, no golden ducks, and no family gatherings at the crease this week — the ducks, for once, kept to their own company. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Gordon Fenner — 8 innings, 3 ducks, 37.5% - John Ames — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% - J Barclay — 14 innings, 3 ducks, 21.43% - Michael Morton — 15 innings, 3 ducks, 20.0% - Gerald Wright — 6 innings, 2 ducks, 33.33% Gordon Fenner sits proudly atop the season's table of dubious distinction, though with matches yet to be played, no man's place upon it may be considered safe. The eleven return to action shortly, and the Gazette shall be watching the close of every innings with its customary keen and slightly malicious interest — for the exclusive zero club never wants for fresh applicants. Until then, may your bats stay straight and your stumps stay standing.

The Duck Club Gazette — 19th July 1953

3 ducks
Week 12 · 19 Jul 1953
