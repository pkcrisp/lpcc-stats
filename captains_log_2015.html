Sun 20 Sep 2015 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · HomeformWLLWW

Sunday 20 September, and Tenterden CC made the trip to Linton Park under grey but agreeable skies. The coin obliged, and I had no hesitation setting us in to bat, a decision I like to think of as tactical rather than merely traditional.

The openers gave us exactly the sort of start captains dream about and rarely receive, ninety-odd runs of calm before the first wicket finally fell. James Piper was the architect of it all, an excellent 90 that anchored the whole innings, with Trevor Back (35 not out) and Kevin Presneill (30) building sensibly around him. Poor Nat Presneill will want this one struck from the record, a duck to forget, but 27 extras and a total of 236 for 5 from our forty overs left us in rude health regardless.

With the ball, Tenterden never quite got away from us. Tom Richards batted beautifully for his 71, well supported by Fred Pearman's unbeaten 20, but 132 for 7 told its own story. Tim Thirkell was the chief tormentor with 2 for 13, while I had the pleasure of chipping in with 1 for 9 and four maidens alongside Trevor Back's 1 for 3. Kevin Presneill's afternoon behind the stumps was rather more testing, judging by nine byes, though I shan't hold it against him.

A splendid win by 104 runs, and notable milestones too, with both Piper and Thirkell climbing the club's all-time lists. Onwards, indeed.

Sat 19 Sep 2015 🧢 1st XIv Stowting CC · HomeformLWLWL

I have three golden-duck runs in this match (Richard Piper and George Sheen for RPCC, Sheen again for the visitors — plus my own 36 to fold in once). Here's the report.

---

A grey old Saturday for it, but there's rarely a bad one when Stowting CC are in town. I called correctly and, with the pitch looking a touch two-paced, had no hesitation in sending us in — a decision I stood by rather more comfortably than Richard Piper stood at the crease, departing for a golden duck that I shall be reminding him of well into the winter nets.

Thomas Beeney was the rock of the innings, ushering us to 159/8 from our forty overs with a composed 40 not out, and I chipped in with 36 of my own alongside Joshua Knowler's brisk 23. It wasn't a total to frighten anyone, truth be told, but on a slow afternoon like this one, runs on the board are runs on the board.

Defending it required nerve rather than fireworks. Darrell Houghton-Judge was superb with the ball, prising out 3-38, with Knowler tight as a drum (two maidens, 1-12) and I managed to squeeze out 1-15 for good measure. George Sheen went first ball for us, poor lad, and Clifford Ball's obstinate 48 was the only real thorn, but Stowting fell six runs short at 153/9, with Benjamin Austin's gloves having rather a torrid time of it behind the stumps — twelve byes is not a number he'll want engraved anywhere.

A nervy finish, a deserved win, and a very good day for the club indeed.

Sun 13 Sep 2015 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv CK Cobham CC, Kent · HomeformWWLLW

As a match, the innings and final result, added colour only where the facts allow.

From the captain's chair, Sunday 13 September. A pleasant, damp sort of afternoon at home to Cobham CC, Kent — the kind of day that makes you wonder whether the covers were worth removing at all, but cricket waits for no drizzle. I won the toss and, with that particular blend of confidence and blind hope that captaincy demands, decided we'd bat.

It didn't start well. Six runs on the board and we'd already lost a wicket, the sort of nervy opening that has you reaching for the flask rather than the scorebook. But James Piper and Glen Rawson had other ideas, putting on a magnificent 163 for the third wicket to drag us back into something resembling control. Piper made a superb 115 — three figures, no less — while Rawson finished unbeaten on a well-crafted 65. We closed on 213/4 from our 40, a total with a bit of steel in it.

Cobham fought back gamely with the bat — Bowles top-scoring with 36, Martin and Marshall chipping in either side of thirty, and poor Callen Fahey departing without troubling the scorers. With the ball I had the satisfaction of picking up 2-25 myself, with Martin Harris and Benjamin Austin both weighing in with two apiece, though Rawson's 16 byes behind the stumps suggested his afternoon with the gloves was rather less serene than his one with the bat. Cobham fell short on 182/9.

A 31-run win, and — quietly pleasing for me — those two wickets nudged me up to 11th on the club's all-time list. A thoroughly good day for the club.

Sun 06 Sep 2015 🧢 Sunday 2nd XIv SC Stockbury CC · AwayformWWLWW

Sunday 6 September at Stockbury, and losing the toss meant an afternoon in the field beckoning us in the customary grey drizzle of an English September, though not before we'd had a bat forced upon us the other way round — we were asked to field first, and duly slogged our way to 117 all out. Glen Rawson played the innings of a captain's dreams, 52 crafted with real class, and Will Thew chipped in handsomely with 42. Beyond that, the middle order rather fell out of a tree — Jack Gwynn, Christopher Thirkell and Ray Dixon all contributing that most democratic of cricketing gifts, the golden duck.

117 looked light on that outfield, and I confess I spent the tea interval doing sums that didn't add up favourably. But cricket, like the Great British summer, delights in confounding forecasts. Jack Gwynn steamed in to record a magnificent 3-11, I managed a tidy 1-6 myself, and Tim Wright weighed in with 2-11, as Stockbury's innings folded like a cheap deckchair for 49-9 from 25.2 overs. Jonathan Brignall's 29 was the only real resistance; James Newell, Aston Monk and Luke Collins all departed for ducks of their own, which at least made me feel less lonely about mine.

Yes, your correspondent contributed a duck to proceedings — the sort of innings best described briefly and forgotten quickly. No matter: a 68-run victory and two well-earned points sent us home cheerful, if still soggy. A fine team performance, and proof that in this game the bowlers can always bail out the batsmen.
