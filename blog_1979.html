🦆 Duck News — 6 October 1979

0 ducks
Week 24 · 6 Oct 1979
One match ventured out onto the field this week, and — remarkably, suspiciously, almost insultingly — not a single duck was laid to rest. The zero club, for once, recorded a zero of its own. Eleven bats went out, eleven bats presumably troubled the scorer in some fashion, and the honours board gathers dust for another week. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or merely a temporary ceasefire in hostilities between bat and ball, the Duck Club cannot say. We can only report what the scorebook tells us, and this week the scorebook is disappointingly free of fowl. Do not despair. The art of not scoring is a skill honed over generations at Linton Park, and it does not simply vanish because of one clean sheet. Normal service — and by "normal" we mean "abnormal, in the very best way" — will surely resume before long. 🍩 A quiet week for the ignominious. Make of that what you will. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 - 🥇 Ross Brice — 6 ducks in 15 innings, a startling 40.0% strike rate for failure - 🥈 Allan Cooper — 5 ducks in 20 innings, 25.0% - 🥉 Jim Hall — 4 ducks in 24 innings, 16.67% - 4️⃣ Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks in 11 innings, 27.27% - 5️⃣ Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks in 14 innings, 21.43% Ross Brice sits proudly atop the pile with two ducks in every five visits to the crease — a consistency of a very particular kind, and one that separates the merely unlucky from the truly committed. The chasing pack will need to pull their fingers out, and their bats in, if they hope to catch him before the season closes. 0️⃣ Until the next time willow meets leather and somebody's stumps meet the same, keep your eyes on the middle and your bat nowhere near the ball. The Duck Club never sleeps for long.

🦆 Duck News — 29 September 1979 🦆

3 ducks
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1979
🏏 1st XI v Lordswood CC (h) A single fixture on the books this week, but Lordswood CC made sure it counted — three additions to the ledger inside one Linton Park innings. Paul Bowles opened the batting and, true to a well-worn habit, opened the account of ducks too — caught Colin Gardener, bowled V. Corbin. It's his 3rd of the season and the 32nd of an extraordinary career spanning 394 innings, keeping him a lofty 14th in the all-time standings of 980. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966 and 1968), a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, and one half of the Only Two Quacks pairing with Adam Pooley, Paul scarcely needs introducing to this column anymore. Fittingly, on this very date back in 1973, club stalwart Nigel Thirkell was doing much the same against Bexhill CC — some anniversaries keep quacking back. At number five, Don Fermor introduced himself to senior cricket in the most Duck Club way imaginable — caught Andy Coltham, bowled Kevin Masters, first innings, first duck. A debut nought is no small thing: it earns immediate, lifelong membership of the Debutante Duck club, that select order whose very first act at the crease was to depart without scoring. Welcome, Don — 1 from 1 already slots you in at 292nd on the all-time list, with a flawless 100% record you'll be quietly hoping not to maintain. As it happens, Nigel Thirkell was on identical date-in-history duty back in 1973 too. Rounding out the innings, Mike Iliffe fell for nought at number eight, caught Colin Gardener again, this time off Henderson Carrington. It's Mike's 3rd duck of the season and 40th of his career from 261 innings, good enough for 7th on the all-time list — rarefied poultry company indeed. He isn't carrying the torch alone either: son David Iliffe already has 4 career ducks of his own, a fine family tradition quietly taking shape. And yes, once more, 29 September 1973 saw Nigel Thirkell doing exactly this. Week in Summary Three ducks, one match, and Colin Gardener rather enjoying himself in the field — two of Saturday's three victims fell into his hands. A tidy, efficient afternoon's work for the Lordswood attack, and a productive one for the Duck Club ledger. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ross Brice — 6 ducks / 15 innings (40.0%) 2. Allan Cooper — 5 ducks / 20 innings (25.0%) 3. Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 24 innings (16.67%) 4. Mike Iliffe — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) 5. Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks / 14 innings (21.43%) Mike's afternoon at Lordswood keeps him firmly inside the top five and breathing down Jim Hall's neck for the podium. That's the lot from this outing — the season presses on, the runs remain optional, and the next fixture promises fresh opportunities for the art of not scoring. See you at the crease 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 22–23 September 1979

0 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1979 – 23 Sep 1979
A rare and faintly troubling turn of events at Linton Park this weekend: two matches played, eleven wickets and more falling in the ordinary course of things, and not a single naught to show for it. 🏏 The Duck Club's scouts were out in force across both fixtures and returned, disappointingly, empty-handed. Every batsman who walked to the crease found his way off the mark before walking back — a state of affairs the committee views with some suspicion. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or simply a temporary drought in the art of not scoring, only next weekend will tell. Rest assured, normal service is expected to resume shortly. The zero club does not take kindly to being kept waiting, and with the season drawing towards its close there remains plenty of time for the standings below to be shuffled once more. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — 1979 0️⃣ 🥇 Ross Brice — 6 ducks from 15 innings (40.0%) — a formidable rate that puts real daylight between him and the chasing pack 🥈 Allan Cooper — 5 from 20 (25.0%) 🥉 Jim Hall — 4 from 23 (17.39%) 4️⃣ Fred Greenfield — 3 from 14 (21.43%) 5️⃣ Trevor Back — 3 from 16 (18.75%) Ross Brice sits handsomely clear at the summit with the season entering its final weeks, though at a 40% strike rate for noughts he'll want to watch his back — nobody wants to be caught napping this close to the finish line, least of all by the very thing that got him there. So there it is — a quiet weekend for the Duck Club, but a quiet weekend only ever means the ducks are gathering strength for the next one. Get out there next fixture and give the honours board something to talk about. 🍩🦆

🦆 Duck News — 15 to 19 September 1979 🦆

2 ducks
Week 21 · 15 Sep 1979 – 19 Sep 1979
A modest haul from the closing weeks of the '79 campaign, but a modest haul in the Duck Club is still a haul worth toasting. Two additions to the ledger this week, both from the 1st XI, both worth a moment's ceremony. 1st XI Saturday 15th at home to Southborough CC, and Malcolm Pay came in at first drop only to depart bowled by Paul Rickwood. It's his 2nd duck of the season and his 11th career nought from 88 innings, keeping him steady at 46th of 979 in the all-time table. Curiously, this fixture has previous — on this very day in 1973, Mike Iliffe also strolled back for nought against Southborough CC, one of 39 ducks across a 1966 to 1979 career that still holds him 8th of all time. Some grounds simply have a taste for it. A nod too to Malcolm's brother Colin Pay, a fellow member of the club's books with a duck to his own name. The following day, away to Mersham Le Hatch CC, Jim Hall was caught off M Epps at number six for his 4th duck of the season and, remarkably, his 43rd career addition from 240 innings — good enough for 4th of 979 all time, a rarefied position occupied by very few in club history. Jim is a paid-up Debutante Duck too, his very first senior innings decades ago having yielded a nought, and he sits agonisingly on the Full House nearly-man board, having ducked in 10 of the 11 batting positions across his career. Twice a Duck of the Year champion, in 1970 and 1977, he's clearly not finished chasing the title. And the calendar struck again — on this same day in 1962, Richard Ashby also made a duck, against Staplehurst CC, one of 24 in a 1962 to 1979 career that ranks 25th all time. A third fixture in the period passed without incident for the ducking classes, everyone getting off the mark, dull as that is for this newsletter. Week in summary Two ducks from three matches, both falling in the top or middle order, both this week's victims already deep into three-figure innings counts — proof that seniority is no protection against the art of not scoring. Consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Ross Brice — 6 ducks from 15 innings (40.0%) 🥈 Allan Cooper — 5 ducks from 19 innings (26.32%) 🥉 Jim Hall — 4 ducks from 22 innings (18.18%) 4. Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks from 14 innings (21.43%) 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks from 16 innings (18.75%) Ross Brice sits well clear at the top with the season drawing toward its close, but Jim Hall's fresh addition keeps him firmly in the frame. The next fixture will no doubt add its own names to the honours board — until then, quack on.

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 8-9 September 1979

0 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1979 – 9 Sep 1979
Two fixtures went into the book this weekend, and for once the exclusive zero club has nothing to report. Not a single nought fell across either match — every batsman who walked to the crease at Linton Park duly troubled the scorers, however modestly. A quiet weekend for the art of not scoring, and a rare one: the ducks, it seems, took the weekend off along with everybody else. It won't last. It never does. 🏏 The Weekend That Wasn't Both sides came home with their dignity, and their nought column, entirely intact. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs at the crease — just runs, glorious ordinary runs, scored by everyone who had a go. The Duck Club's honours board gathers dust for another few days. Spare a thought, though, for the season's front-runners below, who must be almost disappointed not to have extended their leads. Their moment will come again soon enough — early September has a way of being generous to the collection. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ross Brice — 6 ducks from 14 innings (42.86%) — a scarcely believable strike rate, and the man to catch as the season winds toward its close - Allan Cooper — 5 ducks from 19 innings (26.32%) — quietly consistent in the very worst sense - Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks from 13 innings (23.08%) - Trevor Back — 3 ducks from 15 innings (20.0%) - Jim Hall — 3 ducks from 20 innings (15.0%) — proof that patience and poultry can coexist 📅 Looking Ahead Ross Brice sits well clear at the top with 1979 drawing to a close, but a nervy tail can undo a season's work in a single afternoon, and the rest of the pack will fancy their chances of a late flurry. The Duck Club never sleeps for long — normal service, we trust, resumes at the very next fixture. Until then: bat carefully, or don't. We'll be watching either way. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 2 to 7 September 1979

5 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1979 – 7 Sep 1979
The 1st XI stayed home while the rest of the touring party set off for Cornwall this week, and between them they found five fresh entries for the club's exclusive zero club. A quiet week in volume, but not in quality — read on. 🏏 Saturday 2nd — 1st XI away at Staplehurst CC Trevor Back, promoted to four, was bowled by Anthony West without troubling the scorers — his 3rd duck of the season and 12th of a fine 77-innings career, good enough for 44th in the all-time table. A paid-up member of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, Trevor could at least take comfort in company: on this very day in 1968, Paul Bowles was doing much the same thing against Bearsted CC. Bowles, as it happens, hasn't finished with this report yet. Nine wickets later, Allan Cooper joined him in the book, stumped by B- Penny off Q Haywood for his 5th duck of the season and 22nd of his career (125 innings, 28th all time). Trevor and Allan share an Only Two Quacks honour between them, so today's joint effort was really just business as usual. Allan also holds that same distinction with Jim Hall — who, fittingly, would be finding his own way back to the pavilion in Cornwall before the week was out. ⛵ The Cornwall Tour Wednesday saw the tourists at Penryn St Gluvias CC. John Hartridge fell lbw to T- Miles at first drop for his maiden season duck, an early instalment in what is so far a modest 8-innings career (360th all time). On this same day in 1970, David T Lowen was making his own contribution against A. Trembeths. Also departing cheaply at Penryn was the very Paul Bowles trailed earlier — caught off an unnamed fielder, bowled Cyril Hancock, for his 2nd duck of the season and 31st of a mighty 389-innings career, 14th in the all-time standings. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966 and 1968) and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top ten, Bowles remains one of the club's great servants of the zero. Sunday's fixture against Troon CC brought Jim Hall's turn, bowled by P Johns for his 3rd duck of the season and 42nd of his career — 4th on the all-time list, and agonisingly one position short of the fabled Full House (a duck at all eleven positions). Jim's honours board reads like a small pamphlet, but the relevant line today is Quacking Together (with Tim Thirkell) — Tim himself made a duck on this exact day back in 1977, against St Just CC, and returns the compliment on his own board. 📋 Week in Summary Five ducks from five matches, home and away, with the touring party doing the heavier lifting. No golden ducks, no repeat offenders and no family affairs this week — a rare week of restraint from the Duck Club. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ross Brice — 6 ducks in 13 innings (46.15%) - Allan Cooper — 5 ducks in 18 innings (27.78%) - Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) - Trevor Back — 3 ducks in 15 innings (20.0%) - Jim Hall — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.0%) Ross Brice sits pretty at the top, but with the season drawing on there's still time for the also-rans to make their move. Next fixture, same invitation as always: bat first, ask questions later. 🍩0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — Week of 25 August 1979

4 ducks
Week 18 · 25 Aug 1979 – 29 Aug 1979
Four fixtures, four ducks, and Old Anchorians CC contrived to bag a brace of them in the very same innings. A tidy little week for the exclusive zero club. Sunday 26th August — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (home) Tim Thirkell went first, caught I- Barratt bowled C- Daniels, for his 2nd duck of the season and 41st of an already gilded career — 6th on the all-time list of 975. Tim's name already adorns the Full House board (a duck at every position, 1 to 11), sits in the Homebody and Lone Duck top tens, and rounds out the Ducks on the Road roster besides — ignominy at home and away alike. The Thirkell name is already the most decorated in the club's zero-ledger courtesy of kinsman John Thirkell's outright record 90, with siblings Nigel (27) and Stuart (1) chipping in behind. On this very day back in 1962, club great John Ames — 49 ducks across 1952–1978, 2nd all-time — was also strolling back for nought, against Sevenoaks Vine CC. Not to be outdone, Fred Greenfield followed him back moments later, bowled by the very same C- Daniels, for his 3rd duck of the season and 22nd of his career — 28th all-time. Fred is a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell innings, no danger of that repeating itself this week, we trust. Fittingly, the same 1962 date also claimed John Ames, so Fred keeps distinguished company on the calendar. Monday 27th August — 1st XI v Bearsted CC (away) Jim Hall departed caught Richard Campion, bowled Brian Burnett, for his 2nd duck of the season and 41st career — level with Tim Thirkell, and the pair already share more than a number, both inscribed on the Quacking Together board from an earlier shared quack. Jim's cabinet runs deep: Duck of the Year in both 1970 and 1977, a Full House nearly-man on 10 of 11 positions, and entries on the Tourist and Homebody Duck top tens alike. On this day in 1949, Richard Harris (36 ducks, 1940–1970) was also given his marching orders, against Hunton Wanderers CC. Wednesday 29th August — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC (away) David Iliffe was trapped lbw by Richard Walker for his 2nd duck of the season, his career tally now a still-modest 4 in 14 innings — 107th all-time, with plenty of climbing left in him. He shares a surname, if not yet a scoreline, with kinsman Mike Iliffe, already 39 to the good. On this day in 1954, Stan Wratten (34 ducks, 1943–1979) was also adding to the archive, against D Hambrook XI. Week in Summary Four matches, four ducks, Old Anchorians doing the damage twice in one innings. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this week — just honest, solitary failure, exactly as the club likes it. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ross Brice — 6 ducks in 12 innings (50.0%) 2. Allan Cooper — 4 ducks in 14 innings (28.6%) 3. Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) 4. Paul Brattle — 3 ducks in 23 innings (13.0%) 5. Jon Harris — 3 ducks in 26 innings (11.5%) Ross Brice sits imperious atop the standings at a barely believable one duck every other innings — a strike rate for failure the rest of the club can only aspire to. The next fixture looms, and with it fresh opportunity for the zero club to welcome new, or returning, members. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 18-22 August 1979

4 ducks
Week 17 · 18 Aug 1979 – 22 Aug 1979
Three fixtures on the fixture card this week, but the Duck Club only needed one of them to do its grim work. Bobbing Court CC came to Linton Park on Saturday 18th and helped themselves to four scalps in a single innings — a proper collective collapse, and not one of them made it off nought. 🏏 1st XI v Bobbing Court CC (home) Malcolm Pay went first, run out for a dismissal officially logged as "Unsure" — which rather sums up the whole session. It's his 10th career duck from 84 innings, good enough for 54th of 975 all-time, a list on which his own sibling Colin Pay also has a quiet one-duck entry. Curiously, this very calendar date in 1963 saw club legend John Thirkell — the all-time duck king on 90 — also trudge off for nought, against The Mote CC. History repeats itself in the cruellest ways. C- Phillips followed, bowled by Roger Mills, and opened his own account in the zero column — his 1st career duck from just 3 innings, debuting at 275th of 975. Welcome to the fraternity, such as it is. He too falls under the shadow of Thirkell's 1963 duck on this same date. Stan Wratten was next, caught by W Webb off Roy Weller for his 34th career duck, a tally that plants him a lofty 12th of 975 after 350 innings of service. Stan remains a proud member of the "Quacking Together" honours board, forever paired with the great John Ames — and, like his teammates, shares today's date with Thirkell's 1963 effort. Ross Brice completed the rout, bowled by Roy Weller for his 6th duck of the season and 6th of his career, all from a mere 13 innings — an eye-watering strike rate. This keeps him firmly out in front for Duck of the Year 1979, a crown he currently wears with some conviction. Bobbing Court's attack may as well ask for a cut of the royalties. 📅 The Rest of the Week Sunday and the midweek fixture were, sadly, entirely fowl-free — vegan affairs from start to finish. Bobbing Court evidently took their harvest early and called it a week's work. 📊 Week in Summary 4 ducks from 3 matches, every single one landing in the same innings against the same opposition. A middle order that simply vanished — positions 4, 5, 6 and 8 all back in the shed without troubling the scorers. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ross Brice — 12 innings, 6 ducks, 50.0% - Allan Cooper — 13 innings, 4 ducks, 30.77% - Paul Brattle — 21 innings, 3 ducks, 14.29% - Jon Harris — 26 innings, 3 ducks, 11.54% - A Donovan — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100.0% Ross Brice sits well clear at the summit, but there's plenty of season left for a challenger to emerge. Get yourselves down to the ground next weekend — the zero club is always recruiting 🦆🍩0️⃣

Week 16 · 11 Aug 1979 – 12 Aug 1979
Two matches on the fixture list this weekend, and Headcorn CC did the honours for both entries in the book. A lean harvest by Linton standards, but quality over quantity, as ever in this particular field. 🏏 Saturday — Headcorn CC, Home The top order held firm, but the middle and tail found the Headcorn attack rather more persuasive. David Buck departed at number six, bowled by Robert Westrop for a blank, his second duck of the campaign and 8th for his LPCC career from 44 innings — good enough for 64th on the all-time list of shame. He now sits shoulder to shoulder with a small platoon on exactly eight: Alan Tucker, Arthur Cradduck, Derry Evans, Eddie Lawrence, George Champion and Pat Hardwick all made their own eight-duck stands across various decades, so David joins a well-attended club meeting. A nice aside for the family scrapbook too — father William Buck already carries 30 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring is evidently hereditary at the Buck household. Ross Brice followed at nine, bowled Dave Harden, his 5th duck of the season and, remarkably, his 5th in only 11 career innings — a strike rate for failure that few will ever touch. That keeps him planted at 97th all-time and, with the season only fitfully underway, already wearing the Duck of the Year 1979 crown that the honours board has stamped in his name. Spookily, both dismissals fall on the very anniversary of 11 August 1973, when Nigel Thirkell (1969–1979, 27 career ducks, a fixture of the Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens) also trudged back against Headcorn CC without scoring. Some grounds simply seem to attract the affliction on this date. 📅 Sunday — Quiet on the Duck Front The second fixture of the weekend passed without incident for the Duck Club — no additions, no near misses worth reporting. Even the ducks need a rest day occasionally. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks from two matches, both claimed inside the same innings at Headcorn — a matching pair of dismissals from a middle order that clearly fancied an early bath. Season tally ticks along nicely, and with Ross Brice already boasting a 50% duck rate for the summer, the race for the year's top ignominy looks all but decided. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Ross Brice — 10 innings, 5 ducks (50.0%) - Allan Cooper — 12 innings, 4 ducks (33.3%) - Paul Brattle — 20 innings, 3 ducks (15.0%) - Jon Harris — 24 innings, 3 ducks (12.5%) - A Donovan — 2 innings, 2 ducks (100.0%) A Donovan's perfect record deserves a special mention — flawless consistency of a very particular kind. 🍩 That's the lot for this round. The next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, fresh candidates queuing to bow before the Duck Club's exclusive zero club. See you all at the crease — briefly, if history is any guide. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 28–31 July 1979

7 ducks
Week 14 · 28 Jul 1979 – 31 Jul 1979
A short, sharp period on the calendar — just two matches, but Margate's bowlers made sure the Duck Club's books stayed busy. 🏏 Saturday 28th — Sibton Park CC (away), 1st XI Batting at 11, Clive Stacey introduced himself to Linton Park cricket in the purest way the club recognises — caught and bowled by Nick Guy without so much as unsettling the scorebook, and straight onto the Debutante Duck honours board for his trouble.
