Sun 25 Sep 2022 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv Crusaders CC · HomeformLWWWW

Sunday 25 September saw Crusaders CC roll into Linton Park on a pleasant, grey afternoon, and I duly lost the toss — leaving us to field first and, we hoped, keep things respectable. That hope survived about as long as Sanam Pannu's innings, which is to say not very long at all. His unbeaten 78 anchored a Crusaders total of 269/4 from their 40 overs, with Jin Singh (53) and Chand Channa (42) piling on further misery. To our credit, the bowling wasn't without its bright spots — Junaid Mufti was the pick of the attack with 1-19, and Nolan Wilde (1-30) and Sam Boyns (1-39) chipped in gamely, though against that kind of scoring rate it rather felt like bringing a teaspoon to bail out a rowing boat. Poor Aaron Hughes, meanwhile, had a torrid time behind the stumps, shipping 13 byes for his troubles.

Chasing 270 was always going to want something special from the bat, and instead we mustered 120 all out in 32.5 overs, with Theo Clements and John Karassellos both departing for golden ducks to set the tone. It was, in short, not our day — beaten by 149 runs, comprehensively and without much argument.

Still, the season ticks on: Jordan Austin continues to lead our run charts with 421 for the year, and Sam Boyns sits fourth in the wickets list with 11. As for last Sunday — heads up, lessons noted, and back to the nets we go.

Sat 24 Sep 2022 🧢 1st XIv The Mote CC · HomeformWLLLL

Saturday the 24th of September, and The Mote rolled up under a damp, mild sky that promised more than it delivered. I lost the toss — no change there — and they had no hesitation in sticking us in.

It was a funny sort of knock. I found some rhythm early and made 47, the innings that held the thing together, with Alex Wade chipping in a tidy 20 alongside me. But cricket has a habit of humbling you just when you're feeling pleased with yourself, and from a respectable 56 for 3 we contrived to stumble to 62 for 6 in the time it takes to put the kettle on. The Duck Club secretary was rubbing his hands: Nolan Wilde, Theo Clements and Sam King all took that long, lonely walk back for nought apiece. Only the extras column kept its nerve, quietly amassing 25 while the rest of us panicked, and we were all out for 132 in 39.3 overs.

Out we went to defend it, chests puffed, plans laid — and got precisely 0.4 of an over in before the heavens, or the umpires, or simply the afternoon itself decided enough was enough, with The Mote on 4 for none. Not nearly time to find out who'd have won.

So honours shared, a draw manufactured more by the clock than by either side's cricket. Frustrating, but there are worse ways to spend a Saturday than watching your own 47 go begging for a result.
