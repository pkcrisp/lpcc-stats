🦆 Duck Report – Sunday 24 September 2016

2 ducks
Week 23 · 24 Sep 2016
Season's back end in sight, and Stowting CC's visit to Linton Park proved there was still sport to be had in the art of not scoring. Just the one match on the books this week, but it delivered a tidy little brace for the exclusive zero club — both compliments of the same bowler, no less. 🏏 1st XI v Stowting CC (home) First to the crease and first to depart was Luke Welsman, undone for 2 balls by Matthew Cox at number 3. It's the 9th duck of Luke's season and the 20th of a career now standing at 79 innings — good enough for 68th on the all-time list of 1,447. Luke is a long-serving pillar of this institution: a card-carrying member of the Debutante Duck club (his very first senior innings was, fittingly, a blob), a Duck of the Year in 2015, and — with the season charts as they stand — very much still wearing that crown for 2016 too. He also sits agonisingly one position shy of the Full House nearly-man club, having ducked in 10 of the 11 batting slots. On this very day back in 2003, Brian Piper (1973–2013) was doing much the same thing against Penshurst Park CC, en route to 23 career ducks of his own. Six down and in walked Martin Harris at 7, who lasted the same 2 balls before also falling to Matthew Cox — the Duck Club would like to formally note the bowler's fine afternoon's work. It's Martin's 2nd duck of the season and 23rd of his career from 127 innings, good for 59th all-time, and adds another line to his Duck of the Year 2011 entry. He now sits level on 23 with Brian Piper (1973–2013, 444 innings) — kindred spirits in nought, separated by four decades. A small family footnote too: Martin's relative Jon Harris carries a formidable 93 career ducks of his own, so the aptitude for the graceful walk-back clearly runs in the blood. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks, one match, one bowler doing all the damage — Matthew Cox claimed both scalps and can consider himself an honorary Duck Club patron for the week. No pairs, no family doubles, no golden ducks — just two solid, professional noughts from senior members going about their business. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard (Top 5) 1. Luke Welsman — 9 ducks / 42 innings (21.43%) 2. Darrell Houghton-Judge — 6 ducks / 32 innings (18.75%) 3. Tobey Castle — 5 ducks / 31 innings (16.13%) 4. Anton Bourdillon — 3 ducks / 5 innings (60.0%) 🍩 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks / 16 innings (18.75%) Luke Welsman holds a clear grip on the 2016 crown, but with the season drawing to a close, anyone fancying a late raid on that top spot had better find some poor form fast. As ever, the Duck Club extends a warm invitation to next weekend's fixtures — someone, somewhere, is due to add their name to the list. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 17-18 September 2016

2 ducks
Week 22 · 17 Sep 2016 – 18 Sep 2016
A quieter weekend for the exclusive zero club, but two fresh names duly presented themselves for consideration. Two matches, two ducks — modest pickings, but never let it be said the harvest was skipped entirely. 🏏 Saturday — 2nd XI v Margate CC Martin Harris did what he could, surviving three balls at number eight before feathering one through to the safe hands of Joshua Canham off Neil Canham's bowling — no golden duck here, just a slow and dignified surrender. It's Martin's 1st of the season and his 22nd career nought, lifting him to 64th of 1447 in the all-time charts, a lofty position for a man who already holds Duck of the Year 2011 on his mantelpiece. He now sits level with Alan Boyns, the long-serving 1986–2016 stalwart who reached the same 22 in 219 innings and owns a Diamond Duck from 1990 for good measure. Martin's kin Jon Harris, incidentally, presides over the whole club from the very top of the standings with a staggering 93 — clearly the ducking gene runs strong in that family. 🌱 Sunday — 1st XI v Tenterden CC Jordan Austin, opening at number two, departed caught Guy Day bowled Sam Jones — the ball count has been lost to history, so no comment on how long he lasted, only that it wasn't long enough. This is Jordan's 2nd duck of the season and his 16th for the club overall, good enough for 83rd of 1447 and a seat at the table alongside two respectable honours-board entries, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck, both top ten finishes in their own right. He draws level on 16 career ducks with Iain Rawson, who compiled his tally between 2006 and 2013 in 135 innings. Jordan's duck-collecting habit is a family trait too — sibling Benjamin Austin carries 6 of his own, while father Mark Austin has 1 to his name, a modest start that Jordan may yet help extend. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, no pairs and no family ducks to report this time round — the Austins and the Harrises will have to wait for their multi-generational moment. One golden opportunity for a truly instant duck was on the cards with Jordan's unknown ball count, but we can't confirm it either way. 🦆 Duck Report — 3-4 September 2016 🦆

2 ducks
Week 20 · 3 Sep 2016 – 4 Sep 2016
Four fixtures went to the middle across the weekend and, in truth, the ducks mostly stayed in hiding. Only one nest was raided — but what it lacked in volume it made up for in pedigree. 🏏 Sunday 2nd XI at Little Chart CC The only away-day casualties came in a single innings at Little Chart, where two Duck Club stalwarts were relieved of their stumps in short order. Nat Presneill, at five, was snapped up — caught Thom Williams, bowled Adam Stuttle — for his 3rd duck of the season and 19th of an increasingly distinguished career, good enough for 69th on the all-time list. Nat is a paid-up member of several honours boards already: the Debutante Duck club from his very first innings, a Duck of the Year gong back in 2013, and the rare double distinction of Quack-to-Quack, having ducked on both arrival and presumed farewell. He also carries it in the blood — kinsman Kevin Presneill has quietly banked 24 career noughts of his own, so the family name is well represented in the club ledger. Today's effort draws Nat level on 19 with none other than current-season pacesetter Luke Welsman (2012–2016, 74 innings), who — fittingly — sits top of this year's Duck Hunting leaderboard below. Coming in at 10, Tim Thirkell followed him back — caught Steve Rock, bowled Steve Baggs — for his 2nd of the season and a staggering 93rd of his career, moving him into a virtual tie for top spot on the entire all-time chart, level in tally with the great Jon Harris (1966–2016, 693 innings) who currently heads the list. Tim's own honours cabinet barely has room left: Duck of the Year 1972, a Full House (a duck at every position 1 through 11 — genuine completism), and top-ten billing in the Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road categories. He also does it in style as one half of Quacking Together with Jim Hall. And the surname is doing some heavy lifting elsewhere in the record books too — parent John Thirkell (90), siblings Nigel (60) and Simon (59), plus Christopher, Daniel, Will, Tom and Stuart Thirkell besides, giving Linton Park what may be the most prolific duck-laying dynasty in club history. 📅 Elsewhere The other three fixtures across the weekend passed off entirely duck-free — a rare weekend where most of Linton Park kept the scorers honest. Every fowl deed of note came from one Little Chart scorecard alone. 🦆 Week in summary 2 ducks from 4 matches, both at Little Chart CC, both from the Sunday 2nd XI. A quiet weekend by the club's usual standards, but a heavyweight one — Tim's effort alone nudges him to the very top of the all-time table. Elsewhere across the three fixtures, batters kept their heads down and their bats straight — no further additions to report, which either speaks to improving technique or a temporary drought in duck fortune.
