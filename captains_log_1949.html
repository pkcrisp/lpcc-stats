🗓️ 17 Sep – 23 Sep 1949 · 1949 — 2 matches

Sun 18 Sep 1949 🧢 1st XIv BM British Legion Boughton Monchelsea · HomeformWDLWW

The captain's pen finds him in reflective mood this week, for Sunday the eighteenth of September brought us British Legion Boughton Monchelsea, and a fine, sporting contest it proved to be. Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in sending our men in first, trusting the wicket would play kindly to good batting, as indeed it did.

We closed our innings at 160 for nine from thirty-four overs, a total built chiefly upon John Larking's fine fifty, 52 well-earned runs that formed the backbone of the innings. He found willing company in Richard Harris, whose 23 helped fashion a second-wicket stand worth 55 and set the innings on a sound footing, while G Breden's 21 added further substance further down. Not every man enjoyed such fortune, poor Gordon Fenner making the sorry walk back for a duck, though such things happen to the best of us and are borne, as ever, with good humour.

In the field the Legion batted with resolution, losing an early wicket with but 5 runs on the board, a nervous beginning from which they steadied themselves through Arthur Cradduck's 33 and Des Latham's unbeaten 23, reaching 105 for five from their thirty-four overs. Tubb bowled splendidly for his 3 for 35, well supported by George Winter and Ray Tippen, the latter delivering four tidy maidens among his figures of 1 for 17. Bob Arnold had a busy afternoon behind the stumps, thirteen byes testifying to some lively bowling. I note with quiet satisfaction that my own season's efforts have carried me to third in the run-making table with 160 to my name, while Tippen sits fourth among the wicket-takers with eleven.

Honours were shared in the end, a draw that flattered neither side unduly, and a thoroughly agreeable afternoon's cricket against opponents who played the game exactly as it ought to be played.

Sat 17 Sep 1949 🧢 1st XIv EO Expense Account Officers · HomeformDWDLW

Wearing Officers of the Expense Account down to a stand at Linton Park, we welcomed them for a Saturday's cricket on the 17th of September, and a right good afternoon it made.

Winning the toss, I put the visitors in, and though X May batted with real application for his 43, the innings never quite found its feet, a stumble at 5 for 1 setting the tone. Once we had them from 65 for 4 to 73 for 7 the game was as good as won, Tipple and Little both departing without troubling the scorers. It was Bill Hall's day through and through — six for 47, with six overs given up as maidens, a spell of the sort a captain dreams of and a bowler richly deserves. I chipped in with two for 40 myself, and Ray Tippen took one for 14, but the honours were all Hall's.

Set 120 to win, we made handsome work of it. The openers gave us the platform we wanted, putting on 60 for the first wicket before the breakthrough came, and Basil Sears built handsomely upon it with a fine half-century of 54, well supported by Tom Osborn's 29 and Ray Tippen's 20, bringing us home at 121 for 4 with overs in hand. It was pleasing, too, to see Sears's innings carry him past Gordon Fenner into 23rd on the club's all-time list of run-scorers, a fitting reward for a summer in which he leads our run charts. A fine win by six wickets, and in the best traditions of the club — onwards we go.

🗓️ 10 Sep – 16 Sep 1949 · 1949 — 1 match

Sat 10 Sep 1949 🧢 1st XIv MC Maidstone YMCA CC · AwayformWDWDL

The road to Maidstone found us in good heart, and winning the toss I had no hesitation in sending us in first upon what looked, at the outset, a fair enough wicket for batting. It did not prove so kind for long. We lost a man at 1 for 1 and never quite recovered our composure, three of our number — Basil Sears, Bert Buckoke and C Horton — returning to the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and the whole side was dismissed for 87 in 17.5 overs. Not a total to inspire great confidence, though I fancy there is no shame in a modest score honestly come by against useful bowling.

Yet cricket has its own justice, and so it proved. Maidstone YMCA began briskly enough but fell away sharply from 14 for 1 to 21 for 4, their opener Smart accounting for nought, and thereafter the innings simply crumbled before us. C Horton, having had no fortune with the bat, made handsome amends with the ball, running through the order for 6 for 21, while Bill Hall bowled with admirable control for 4 for 23, two of his overs going entirely unscored. Maidstone were all out for 51 in 18.3 overs, and the match was ours by 36 runs.

A satisfying afternoon's work, and one that leaves me sitting fourth in the season's run charts with 128 to my name, while Horton's fine display lifts him to fifth among the wicket-takers. A sporting contest, played in the right spirit throughout, and a thoroughly earned two points for the club.

Frank Taylor's Skipper's Log, Saturday 27th August 1949 — Away to Hunton Wanderers CC

We won the toss at Hunton and, the pitch looking a fair one for batting, I had no hesitation in taking first strike — a decision that repaid us handsomely before the day was out. The eleven set about their work in businesslike fashion, and though wickets fell at intervals to keep us honest, we closed our innings at a healthy 164 for 6 from 39 overs. The innings owed much to D Simmons, whose fifty was a model of good sense and good timing, and I was glad to lend him support with 48 of my own, the two of us putting on 75 for the second wicket that rather set the tone of the afternoon. Poor Richard Harris, it must be said, departed without troubling the scorers, but such is cricket, and the rest of the order carried the total to a respectable declaration of intent.

If our batting had been sound, our bowling that afternoon bordered on the merciless. Hunton Wanderers never truly settled, losing a wicket without a run on the board and finding themselves at 17 for 5 before they had fairly begun. D Drury batted with some spirit for his 27, but there was precious little else to cheer the home supporters, three of their number failing to trouble the scorers at all. I confess to a happy afternoon with the ball myself, finishing with 8 for 30 and six maidens into the bargain, while H Newport chipped in usefully with a wicket of his own. Hunton were all out for 77, and we came away with a fine win by 87 runs — two points thoroughly earned, and a day the whole eleven may look back on with quiet satisfaction.
