🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 2011 · 2011 — 2 matches

Sun 25 Sep 2011 🧢 1st XIv CX Chairman's XI · HomeformLLWDL

We hosted the visitors on a fine Sunday, 25 September, and having called correctly at the toss, I had no hesitation in setting us in — the sort of decision that looks either shrewd or daft depending entirely on what follows, though for once I'll claim the credit.

The lads gave the bowlers a proper afternoon of it. Daniel Thirkell led from the front with a fine 83, well supported by Will Thirkell's 60 and Ryan Boyns' 50, the two of them putting on a crucial 79 for the third wicket while I fretted happily from the boundary. Poor Thomas Beeney was the one blot on an otherwise handsome copybook, done for a duck, but with 26 useful extras thrown in we posted a formidable 290/8 from our forty overs.

Defending it, we made early inroads and never quite let go, bowling the visitors out for 239 in the 39th over — Tom Davey's 71 the chief obstacle, with Adrian Samson and Adrian Moll chipping in, while Paul Davey and Ray Dixon both departed for ducks at our hands. Daniel Thirkell was superb with the ball too, 3-14, Martin Harris toiled manfully for 3-46 with two tidy maidens, and Stuart Cheesman took 1-5 as well as cracking 37 runs to move past Jim Hall into 21st on the club's all-time run-scorers list — no small feat. Ryan Boyns had a trickier time behind the stumps, 10 byes going astray.

And yet, for all that, we finished up on the wrong end of it, beaten by 51 runs. Not our day. Heads up, lads — back to the nets.

Sat 24 Sep 2011 🧢 1st XIv OC Offham CC · HomeformWLLWD

I called it right at the toss and, with the sun still doing its best impression of summer, elected to bat — a decision that looked briefer than I'd have liked. We were bowled out for 98 in 31.4 overs, and it was only my own 20 that gave the innings any shape at all, dragging us along while wickets clattered from 27-1 to 33-4 in the sort of rush that has grown men reaching for the tea urn early. Poor Will Thew had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, ten byes escaping his gloves like eels.

Richard Piper continues to sit fourth in the season's run charts with 294, and I find myself leading the wicket-takers on 28 — small consolations on a day we lost by seven wickets. Not our finest hour; heads up, and back to the nets.

Wed 14 Sep 2011 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDDDLW

Skipper's Log — Wednesday 14 September 2011. Away at Penshurst Park CC, and the coin let us down again, so it was pads off, boots on and out into the field first.

Penshurst set about building a total, and while nobody quite ran away with it, Andy Eames' composed 52 gave their innings its backbone, well supported by John Boyden (39) and Graham Nicholls (34) — that eighth-wicket stand of 61 doing most of the damage to our afternoon. I can't say I was unhappy to see Mark Lower back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and pleasingly I found some rhythm myself with the ball, finishing with 3-37 to lead the attack. Darren Styles bowled beautifully in support — 2-15 with five maidens is the kind of miserly spell that makes a captain's job easy — while Tim Thirkell chipped in with 2-29. All out for 198 in 39.2 overs felt like a fair result for a good shift in the field.

Chasing it down was never going to be straightforward, but Stuart Cheesman played the innings of the day, an assured 54 that also nudged him past Jim Hall into 21st on the club's all-time run-scorers list. Craig Sands (43 not out) and Ryan Boyns (38) built around him — Boyns' contribution doubling as the milestone that took him past 2,000 career runs for the club — with a 55-run third-wicket stand the platform for victory. Michael Beeney's duck was the one blemish on an otherwise tidy card.

Three wickets in hand at the end, 201/7, and the win in the bag. A proper team effort, milestones and all — a very good day for the club.

But it was Sam Boyns who did the real damage, tearing through with 2 for 14 and four maidens that strangled the middle overs, well backed up by Joshua Knowler's 2 for 20 — and I'll allow myself one line of self-congratulation for a tidy 1 for 20 of my own.

Chasing 180, we made it look rather more comfortable than these things usually are. Kevin Presneill played the innings of the day, a well-crafted fifty worth 60, with Craig Sands unbeaten on 45 and Richard Piper adding a brisk 20; a generous 30 extras from the visitors didn't hurt either. We strolled home by 7 wickets with 31.3 overs on the clock.
