🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1990 · 1990 — 1 match

Sat 29 Sep 1990 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformWWDWD

Saturday 29 September saw Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC roll up for what promised to be the last real cricket of the season, and having called correctly at the toss I didn't so much as pause for thought before opting to bat — the sort of decision that either marks you out as a shrewd tactician or a man who simply likes batting first, and I shall let the reader decide which.

We made a fine fist of it. The openers put on 63 before the first wicket fell, and from there Piers McDonald simply refused to come back in, compiling a majestic 127 not out — three figures, no less, and worth every ripple of applause from the boundary. Peter Whyte chipped in nicely with 26, and the third-wicket pair added a further 73 for good measure. By the time we declared our innings closed at 212 for 3 off 43 overs, I confess I allowed myself a small smile in the pavilion, and I was pleased enough with my own contribution to the run charts this season without needing to dwell on it further here.

Defending that total, we found Hastings watchful rather than reckless, limping to 10 for 1 from their three overs before bad light or good sense — I forget which — brought proceedings to a close. Matt Large struck early with figures of 1 for 8, a timely reminder of why he leads our wicket charts this year.

Honours shared, then. Not the win our batting deserved, but on a golden autumn afternoon like that, nobody in whites or blazers seemed too troubled by the scorebook.

Wed 26 Sep 1990 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWWLWL

Wednesday last saw us venture to Penshurst Park, and for once the coin obliged me — I won the toss and, with the pitch looking full of runs, had no hesitation in batting first. Tim Thirkell was in fine form for his 68, well past his fifty, while Trevor Back chipped in a handsome 48 and Piers McDonald finished unbeaten on 42, the pair sharing a priceless stand of 69 for the fifth wicket. We closed on 241 for 8 from 39.1 overs — enough, even to my ever-nervous captain's eye, to be going on with.

So it proved. Penshurst's T Bones played a lone hand of real quality, 72 before he too passed his fifty, supported by P Grayland's 26, and the pair put on 85 for the second wicket that briefly had me regretting my earlier confidence. But we hauled them back, tumbling from 103 for 2 to 115 for 5 in the blink of an eye, poor David Smith sent packing without troubling the scorers. I found some rhythm with the ball myself, finishing with 3 for 54 and, rather pleasingly, easing past 1,700 career wickets along the way — one for the mantelpiece. Martin Larkin bowled beautifully for 2 for 34 with three miserly maidens, and Alan Boyns chipped in with 1 for 18. Penshurst were all out for 143.

A handsome win by 98 runs, then, and with Piers McDonald still topping our run charts and my own tally ticking along at 35 wickets for the season, spirits in the Linton Park camp remain thoroughly high. Onwards.

Sun 23 Sep 1990 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLWWDW

Sunday 23rd September saw us welcome Anchorians CC, and having called correctly at the toss I had no hesitation whatsoever in sending us in to bat — a decision I stand by in principle, if not entirely in outcome. What followed was twenty overs of what I might charitably call "character-building" batting, the innings folding for 69 all out. It was not, I confess, a total to strike fear into anyone, and there were one or two shots played that will be quietly revisited over a pint before they're revisited again in the retelling.

Credit where it's due, though — Peter Whyte stood firm throughout, carrying his bat for a splendid 36 not out while wickets fell around him like autumn leaves. Without him we'd have been discussing something considerably more embarrassing than 69, and the club owes him a round for services rendered.

With such modest fuel in the tank, containing Anchorians became the order of the day, and to the lads' great credit that's precisely what we did — a draw secured through grit, guile, and no small measure of stubbornness in the field.

Elsewhere in the season, it's worth noting Paul Gibson continues his fine form with the bat, sitting fourth in the 1990 first XI run charts on 591 — a reminder that not everything this season has been quite so scratchy.

Verdict: a scrappy 69 rescued by Whyte's defiance and a team effort in the field — no classic, but a draw earned is a defeat avoided, and we'll gladly take it.

Five wickets in hand and overs to spare — I'll happily take that. With 1360 runs now to my name atop the season's run charts, and Jon Harris sitting second among the wicket-takers with 50, the old firm looks in decent shape as autumn draws in. A thoroughly good day for the club.
