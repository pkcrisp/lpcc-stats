🦆 Duck News — 18–22 September 1999

4 ducks
Week 22 · 18 Sep 1999 – 22 Sep 1999
Four ducks shuffled out of the reeds this week across three fixtures, and with the season drawing towards its close, every one of them carries real weight in the race for Duck of the Year. 🏏 Saturday 18th — 2nd XI v Elliotts CC Jon Harris, walking in at number 10, was caught by Mike Ling off David Hales without troubling the scorers — his 4th duck of the season and a frankly staggering 79th of his LPCC career from 577 innings. That keeps him anchored at #2 of 1246 in the all-time charts, a position from which he surveys the club's Duck of the Year honours board of 1976, 1980, 1981 and — topically — 1999 itself, four crowns and counting. He's also a Full House man (a duck at every position 1 to 11), a fixture of the Homebody, Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and shares a Quacking Together entry with fellow victim this week Trevor Back, though history, not this week, ties that particular knot. On this very day in 1960, Cliff Anscombe made his own duck against Tilling Steven, one of 25 in a career running 1957 to 1975. And for those keeping track of duck bloodlines, Jon's father Richard Harris sits proudly on 36 of his own. 🏏 Sunday 19th — 1st XI v Anchorians CC Richard Piper, at five, was run out for nought — the umpire's report simply says "Unsure," which feels appropriate for a dismissal that adds nothing to the scoreboard. It's his 4th of the season and 19th career duck from 94 innings, good for 50th of 1246 all time, and another notch for a man already fixed on the Ducks on the Road, Full House, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. Splendidly, on this exact day in 1982, David Iliffe also fell for a duck against these very same Anchorians — one of 31 in a career that ran 1976 to 1988, and a season in which he was crowned Duck of the Year. Richard's father Brian Piper, incidentally, already carries 20 career ducks of his own — clearly a family with a gift for the art of not scoring. 🌱 Midweek, 22nd — Midweek XI v Penshurst Park CC A double dose at Penshurst. Peter Whyte, opening the innings, was caught by Michael Meade off Cox — his 2nd duck of the season and 20th of his career from 222 innings. Down at four, Trevor Back followed him back, caught off Cox again, his 4th of the season and 52nd career duck from 434 innings, keeping him a lofty 8th of 1246 all time and a proud member of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody and Lone Duck top tens. Remarkably, both men echo the very same historical duck: on this day in 1977, none other than John Thirkell — the club's all-time #1 with 90 career ducks — also fell against West Kent Wanderers CC. Two Linton men, one Wednesday, one shared ghost from 1977. Peter's sibling Tom Whyte, meanwhile, holds 2 career ducks of his own — the Whyte name is not unfamiliar to this club. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks, three matches, and not a single golden duck among them — everyone at least troubled the bowler before troubling the club's records. Jon Harris edges ever closer to 80, Trevor Back consolidates his top-ten spot, and the Duck of the Year race tightens with the season's end in sight. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 4 ducks / 14 innings (28.57%) 2. Richard Piper — 4 ducks / 20 innings (20.00%) 3. Trevor Back — 4 ducks / 25 innings (16.00%) 4. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks / 28 innings (14.29%) 5. Stewart Morrison — 4 ducks / 29 innings (13.79%) The exclusive zero club never sleeps, and neither do the bowlers waiting to add to it. Roll on the next fixture — the ducks won't catch themselves. 🦆🍩

🦆 LPCC Duck Report — 11th & 12th September 1999 🦆

4 ducks
Week 21 · 11 Sep 1999 – 12 Sep 1999
Three fixtures, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomed its latest inductees over the weekend. Nothing spectacular in volume, but plenty in flavour. Saturday 11th September 🏏 1st XI v Macknade 2nd (home) P- Martin strode in at number 5 and was run out for nought, his 1st duck of the season and, remarkably, his 2nd in just 2 career innings - a flawless, if unwanted, 100% start to his LPCC account. He's already inscribed on the Retirement Duck honours board from some past farewell performance, though today's effort was very much a beginning, not an end. On this very date in 1991, club legend Fred Cheesman (1980-1999, 34 ducks, 20th all-time, twice Duck of the Year, Full House honoree) was also dismissed for nought against The Mote CC. Good company to keep. Richard Piper followed at 6, caught behind off P- Thompson, for his 3rd duck of the season and 18th of a career spanning 91 innings, good enough for 52nd on the all-time list. Richard's name already decorates the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck boards, plus the coveted Full House (a duck at every position, 1 through 11) - so number 6 was simply revisiting old ground. A chip off the old block, too: kinsman Brian Piper carries 20 career ducks of his own. That same 1991 date claimed Fred Cheesman once again for company. 🏏 2nd XI v Betsham CC (away) Darren Styles fell for nought at number 9, caught off Paul King, his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of his career. Little did he know it was only the opening half of a weekend double. Sunday 12th September 🏏 1st XI v Northend Sunday (home) Darren Styles was back at the crease, this time at 10, run out without scoring - his 3rd duck of the season, 5th of his career, and with it a grim pair completed for the weekend. Two innings, two ducks, zero complaints from the Duck Club. On this day in 1959, S Lusher did likewise against Bowaters CC. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from three matches, with poor Darren Styles doing the double, the only man to feature twice this week. The 1st XI contributed three of the four, Macknade 2nd proving a particularly unwelcoming host. No golden ducks recorded - everyone at least troubled the scorers before departing empty-handed. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Piers McDonald - 4 ducks / 26 innings (15.4%) - Stewart Morrison - 4 ducks / 29 innings (13.8%) - Jon Harris - 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.1%) - Darren Styles - 3 ducks / 17 innings (17.6%) - Dave Charlton - 3 ducks / 17 innings (17.6%) Darren's weekend pair vaults him straight into the season's top five - one to watch as the race for Duck of the Year hots up. 🍩 Same time next fixture, ladies and gentlemen. The zero club never sleeps - who's joining the honours boards next?

1 duck
Week 20 · 4 Sep 1999 – 8 Sep 1999
A quiet week in the coppice, ducks. Three matches played, and the club's most exclusive establishment recorded just a single new arrival at the door. Rare is the week the zero club goes so lightly staffed — savour it, because normal service always resumes. 🏏 Midweek XI Monday's solitary intake came courtesy of Roly Edwards, undone lbw b E. Brassey against Lord Cornwallis XI, departing from his post at number 7 without troubling the scorers. This is Roly's 2nd duck of the 1999 campaign and the 14th of a career now standing at 113 innings — a body of work that plants him a solid 61st of 1245 in the all-time table. Consistency of a very particular kind. And rather splendidly, on this very day back in 1981, Richard Ashby was doing precisely the same thing against Paul CC — one of the five-a-side stalwarts on the club's "Ducks in a Row" board and a fixture of the Tourist Duck top 10, Ashby played from 1962 to 1995 and finished with 28 ducks to his name, good for 30th all-time. Nice to know the calendar has form. 📊 Week in Summary One duck. Three matches. A remarkably restrained showing by Duck Club standards — either the bowling attacks were merciful or the batting was, for once, doing its actual job. Don't get used to it. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 - Jon Harris — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) - Stewart Morrison — 4 ducks in 28 innings (14.29%) - Piers McDonald — 4 ducks in 26 innings (15.38%) - Dave Charlton — 3 ducks in 17 innings (17.65%) - Trevor Back — 3 ducks in 23 innings (13.04%) Jon Harris leads the percentage race by some distance, but with the season entering its final furlongs, Stewart Morrison and Piers McDonald's raw tallies of 4 keep this one very much alive. 🍩 Next fixture awaits, and with it the inevitable fresh recruits to the exclusive zero club. The nets are down, the bowlers are sharpening up, and somewhere out there this week's golden duck is already being measured for its board. See you at the crease — or, more likely, on the walk back to it.

0 ducks
Week 19 · 28 Aug 1999 – 3 Sep 1999
A rare and troubling week for the Duck Club faithful. Seven fixtures played, seven fixtures completed, and not a single naught to show for it. The exclusive zero club, so reliably populated most weekends, sat entirely empty this time round — eleven ducklings' worth of opportunity, squandered by batsmen actually scoring. Somewhere out there, bats were middled, singles were nudged, and not one soul walked back for a duck. Frankly it's the kind of form that gets a man talked about in the wrong sort of way at the clubhouse bar. The art of not scoring, it seems, took the week off. We can only assume the ducks themselves saw the fixture list, had a quiet word among themselves, and decided Linton Park wasn't worth the trip. They'll be back. 🦆 Duck News — Saturday 21 August 1999

5 ducks
Week 18 · 21 Aug 1999
A Meopham CC double-header proved fertile ground for the Duck Club this week, with both senior sides finding the exclusive zero club open for business. Five ducks in two matches, and one for the family scrapbook. 🏏 1st XI at Meopham (away) Piers McDonald departed lbw to Jimmy Ward without scoring — his 4th duck of the season and 45th for the club, good enough for 11th on the all-time list of 1245. A two-time Duck of the Year (1997 and 1998) and a permanent fixture of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, Piers can at least reflect that misery loves historical company: on this very day in 1955, John Ames — who would go on to retire with 49 club ducks, 9th all-time — was doing precisely the same thing against Halfway House. 🥇 2nd XI vs Meopham (home) Meopham's bowlers did rather better business back at home. Jon Harris edged behind off Paul Lamb for his 3rd of the season and 78th of an extraordinary career, now just one duck shy of top spot — 2nd of 1245 and closing fast. The reigning Duck of the Year for 1999 and a Full House man (a duck at every position going), Jon shares the "Quacking Together" honour with clubmate Trevor Back — fittingly, the pair obliged together in the very same innings. A chip off the old block too, with son Richard Harris already carrying 36 career noughts of his own. Trevor followed moments later, bowled by Paul Lamb for his 3rd of the season and 51st for the club — 8th all-time and a fixture of the Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. Like Piers, Trevor's nought landed on the very anniversary of John Ames's effort against Halfway House in 1955. Paul Lamb wasn't finished. At number ten, Christopher Thirkell became his third victim of the innings, bowled for his 1st career duck in only his 3rd senior knock. But the real story was two spots higher, where father Simon Thirkell — Duck of the Year back in 1983 and a card-carrying member of the 50 Before a 50 club — had already been strangled out caught by "Unsure" off Darren Deeks, his 1st duck of the season and 35th of his career, 18th all-time. Father and son, back in the shed without scoring in the same innings — a proper Duck and Ducklings entry for the honours board, with siblings Tim (68) and Nigel (54) Thirkell surely watching the family tally with pride. And as it happens, both Thirkells shared their nought with John Ames, who was doing the very same thing against Halfway House on this date in 1955. 📊 Week in Summary 5 ducks from 2 matches, both against Meopham CC, with Paul Lamb claiming three of them in a single 2nd XI innings — a fine spell of ceremonial destruction. The undoubted highlight, though, is the Thirkell double: not every Saturday gives you a Duck and Ducklings entry. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🏆 - Piers McDonald — 23 inns, 4 ducks (17.4%) - Stewart Morrison — 24 inns, 4 ducks (16.7%) - Jon Harris — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.3%) - Dave Charlton — 15 inns, 3 ducks (20.0%) - Trevor Back — 19 inns, 3 ducks (15.8%) 🍩 The season's not done yet — plenty of time for Jon Harris to complete his march to the summit, and plenty more Thirkells waiting in the wings. See you all at the next fixture, bats and low expectations at the ready. 0️⃣🦆

Week in summary Two ducks from four matches — the herd was evidently well hidden this week, with 1st XI and Midweek XI each contributing one apiece and the 2nd and 3rd XIs escaping entirely unscathed. A rare weekend of restraint. It's Dave's 3rd duck of the season and 21st for the club, good for 44th on the all-time list of 1245 - a tally that conveniently lands him 2nd on this week's Duck Hunting Season leaderboard, see below. On this very day back in 1996, Gareth Thomas was doing much the same thing against Ipswich Greyhounds - it turned out to be his final innings for the club, a Retirement Duck for the ages. Dave, mercifully, has runs ahead of him yet. Worth noting: sibling Sean Charlton already carries 4 career ducks of his own, so the trait, such as it is, runs in the family. Sunday 1 August Sunday NVKC away at Eversholt CC A grim day at the crease produced a brace for the visitors. Clive Newman was run out without scoring, his 1st duck of the season and 10th of an increasingly distinguished career spanning 216 innings, good for 83rd all time. On this same date in 1994, Roly Edwards was busy doing the exact same thing. Moments later Stuart Cheesman was bowled by Dave Turner for nought, his own 1st duck of the season but 28th overall, ranking him 31st in club history. Stuart is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having ducked on his actual debut, a two-time Duck of the Year (1994 and 1996), and holder of the Full House honour - a duck at every position 1 to 11, a genuinely thorough body of work. Stuart shares Clive's on-this-day parallel exactly - Roly Edwards, 1994. The Cheesman name runs deep in these pages too: parent Fred carries 34 career ducks and sibling Steve another 13, so Stuart had excellent tutelage in the art of not scoring. Tuesday 3 August Midweek XI at home to Bexley CC Michael Beeney was caught by John Taylor off the bowling of Christopher Allbut, his 2nd duck of the season and 11th career, 77th on the all-time list. Michael already holds Only Two Quacks (with Ian Brattle) and, from a past farewell innings, a presumptive Retirement Duck - a man well acquainted with this territory. On this day in 1952, Gordon Fenner, eventual owner of 34 career ducks, was going through much the same ritual. Week in summary: four ducks from four fixtures, with Sunday NVKC contributing two from a single innings at Eversholt. 🦆 Duck News — 17–18 July 1999

2 ducks
Week 13 · 17 Jul 1999 – 18 Jul 1999
Slim pickings this week, but the two ducks recorded were of properly ceremonial quality. Saturday — 1st XI at Sheldwich CC Robert Quirk walked to the crease at number 9 and departed without so much as troubling the scorer, caught and bowled by David Knight. The twist: this was his very first senior innings for the club — a duck on debut, sealing his place on the Debutante Duck board before he'd even got his eye in. With career figures reading a pristine 1 duck from 1 innings, he's also straight onto the One and Done honours list for 1999 — a career, so far, of magnificent singular focus. Whether the Retirement Duck board beckons too remains to be seen — the club sincerely hopes not, and awaits his second innings with interest. He slots into the all-time list at 534th of 1244, and — fittingly — on this very day in 1993, Nick Froom (1989–1994) also made a duck, against Kennington CC, Kent. Some things are written in the fixture list. Sunday — NVKC away at Hursley Park CC Dave Charlton, bowled by Ross Cambray for nought at number 6, notched his 2nd duck of the season and 20th of an increasingly distinguished career — one from every 9 or so trips to the crease across 184 innings. That's good enough for 46th of 1244 in the all-time table, a rarefied neighbourhood indeed. Charlton family watchers will note brother Sean is himself a paid-up club member with 4 career ducks — clearly something in the blood.
