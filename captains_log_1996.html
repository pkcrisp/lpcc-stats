Sat 28 Sep 1996 🧢 2nd XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWWWW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 28 September 1996

Won the toss against Linton Park and didn't so much choose to bat as feel the sun on my face and decide the decision had already made itself. Batting first, on a day like that, felt less like tactics and more like manners.

And how the lads repaid the faith. Stuart Cheesman cruised past his fifty and on to 56, batting with the unhurried confidence of a man who knows the tea urn will still be warm whenever he gets round to finishing. Dave Todd chipped in a brisk 48 and Richard Piper 36, the pair of them stacking bricks while Cheesman laid the foundations. I managed 27 myself, more workmanlike than watchable, but every run counts when you're building something worth defending. All told we posted 218 for 4 off our 37, a total with a bit of swagger to it.

Elsewhere in the club almanac, Clive Newman continues his quiet march up the 1996 First XI run charts, now sitting fourth with 778 for the season, while Trevor Back holds fourth in the Second XI standings on 321 — good men both, doing good work while the rest of us grab the headlines.

We closed it out by 5 wickets, two points banked and thoroughly deserved. A proper afternoon's cricket, capped off in the manner every captain dreams of and precious few actually get: comfortably, cheerfully, and with enough left over for a second slice of cake.

Wed 25 Sep 1996 🧢 Midweek XIv P Penshurst · HomeformDDDWW

Wednesday last, we welcomed Penshurst to the ground, took first use of the ball, and asked them to bat under skies that behaved themselves for once. They compiled a respectable 185 for 6 from their 45 overs, built chiefly around H. Zaltzman's well-made 57, with N. Keest's 30 and D. Smith's unbeaten 20 filling in the gaps around him. Darren Styles was pick of the attack with three for 26, while Stuart Cheesman chipped in with 2-12 and Trevor Back bowled with real discipline for 1-7, three maidens among them — not a run given away lightly.

In reply, the lads made light work of the target, racing to 189 for 3 in just 26 overs. Peter Whyte was in imperious touch with 86, well supported by Mike Elworthy's fine 67, and I chipped in with 16 of my own down the order. Peter did have a trickier afternoon of it behind the stumps, with 30 byes slipping past for the purists among you to wince at, but nobody was minded to hold that against him after an innings like his.

There was cause for wider celebration too. Elworthy's 67 eased him past Cliff Anscombe into 23rd on the club's all-time run-scorers list, while David Castle continues to sit 2nd in this season's Midweek XI run charts on 138 and Stuart Cheesman 3rd in the wicket charts with 9. A fine win by 7 wickets, then, and a thoroughly satisfactory afternoon's work all round.
