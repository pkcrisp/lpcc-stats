Sun 22 Sep 1991 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWLWDD

Sunday last saw us welcome Anchorians CC, and losing the toss meant an afternoon in the field to open proceedings — never my favourite way to spend a Sunday, but there it is. Keith Wooding had other ideas about our afternoon, carving out a superb unbeaten 122 that anchored their innings from start to finish, with Graham Siveyer (29) and Ray Willis (22) chipping in either side of a stubborn 58-run second-wicket stand. We chipped away where we could — Rob Doick the pick of the attack with 1-29, Jon Harris weighing in with 1-36, and I managed to prise one out myself for 38 runs, which felt like fair reward for an honest afternoon's toil. Matt Large, meanwhile, bowled with the miserliness of a man counting out his own tea money, sending down two maidens. They closed on 215/4 from their 41 overs — a target that looked, from the boundary rope at least, rather more imposing than it turned out to be.

Because what followed with the bat was as good as anything we've produced all season. David Castle and Piers McDonald put on 124 for the first wicket before Anchorians so much as glimpsed a breakthrough, and from there the platform was simply too good to waste. Castle went on to a fine 62, McDonald a well-made 53, and Peter Whyte added a classy 52 to see us home with 217/4 in 38.1 overs.

A six-wicket win, then, built on a partnership for the ages and bowling tight enough to keep the target honest. Onwards indeed — and long may David Castle's charge up the season's run charts continue.

Sat 21 Sep 1991 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWWLWD

Saturday 21 September 1991, and West Farleigh CC came calling at Linton Park. I won the toss and, with barely a flicker of hesitation, sent us in to bat.

What a platform we built. The openers put on 99 for the first wicket before the breakthrough came, and from there David Castle simply refused to leave, compiling a glorious unbeaten 103. Paul Bowles chipped in handsomely with 62, and Dave Charlton's 38 rounded out a total of 223/3 from our forty overs that had the tea urn rattling with anticipation.

Defending it proved rather more of a grind. Andy Golding gave us an early sniff with a wicket for just 8 on the board, but Golding himself then dug in for 84 and, with Micky Back's 60 alongside him, the pair added a chastening 129 for the second wicket. Dave Latter's 23 kept the total ticking, and D Todd departed for a duck without troubling the scorers, but by the close they'd reached 210/5 from 39 overs. Ian Brattle bowled his heart out for 3-57, with two tidy maidens thrown in, and Paul Bowles backed him up with 2-54 — though poor Roly Edwards had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, twelve byes finding their way past him.

In the end honours were shared, neither side quite able to force the door open. A draw, then — fair reflection of a match that gave rather more than it settled, and no bad way to spend a Saturday.

Sun 15 Sep 1991 🧢 1st XIv NS Northend Sunday · HomeformLWWLW

Sunday 15 September brought Northend Sunday to Linton Park, and having won the toss I never seriously considered bowling first – on a day like that, you bat. It proved a shrewd, if entirely unsurprising, decision. I found some early form and it simply refused to leave, carrying me all the way to a hundred, while Piers McDonald played beautifully alongside me for 72 as we put on 172 for the second wicket – the kind of stand that makes a captain's job embarrassingly easy. We closed on 217/3 from our 41 overs, and I allowed myself a quiet, entirely un-English moment of satisfaction at bringing up three figures.

In reply, Northend never quite got away from us, though J Bolding gave it a fair go with a well-made 52, helped by N Twiddy (24) and D Abernathy (22) in an opening stand of 54. Jon Harris was the story of the innings, though – six for 55 is the sort of spell you buy a man a pint for, several pints in fact, and richly deserved. Paul Gibson (1-1) and Matt Large (1-22) chipped in, Alan Boyns bowled three miserly maidens, and poor N Wood departed without so much as troubling the scorers. Northend limped to 158/9 from their 49, never threatening to chase us down but never quite folding either.

So honours shared, a draw when neither side could force the issue – not the result the scoreline suggested we deserved, but cricket has a way of humbling even a captain having a rather good day.

Sat 14 Sep 1991 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · HomeformLLWWL

We won the toss on Saturday 14 September 1991, and with Southborough CC visiting on a good drying pitch I had no hesitation putting them in the field first. Simon Fisher's fifty was the lone bright spark for the visitors, but an early wobble to 7 for 1 set the tone, and the slide from 100 for 7 to all out for 109 in 37.5 overs told its own story — G Lanham and Steven Thomas both departed without troubling the scorers. I contributed a modest 17 with the bat and rather enjoyed watching from the field as Ian Brattle's two wickets nudged him past Tony Snell into 17th on the club's all-time list.

A comfortable 64-run win, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round. Onwards we go.

Wed 11 Sep 1991 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformDDLDW

Not out yet, but the perfect example of a Wednesday in club cricket. Wednesday 11 September had us on the road to The Mote, and having won the toss my instinct was to bowl first — sorry, bat first, I put us in. We got to 141 all out in 42.5 overs, which felt like scratching a living from unpromising soil after slipping to 3-1 with the ink barely dry on the scorebook. David Castle (42) and Jeremy Chapman (41) dragged us back into respectability with a third-wicket stand of 67, while poor Fred Cheesman's golden duck reminded us all that cricket remains a cruel game played by optimists.

Defending 141 always demands nerve rather than method, and for a good while it looked beyond us as The Mote's S Patel (37) and N Barr (30) threatened to bat us out of the gazette entirely. But cricket has a way of turning on an afternoon, and we ran through the middle and lower order — 123-6 became 132-9 almost before the tea urn had cooled, Malcolm Bristow and Julian Taylor both dismissed without troubling the scorers. I found some rhythm with the ball and finished with 5-42, a five-for I shan't pretend not to be pleased with, well supported by Ian Brattle's 4-50 and four miserly maidens from C Lambert that gave the batters nothing to work with.

Five runs. That was the final margin, and I'll take it with both hands and a grateful heart. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a timely reminder that this Midweek XI — with Chapman's bat and, I'm proud to say, my own bowling both flying at the top of the season's charts — knows how to make a match of it.

Sat 07 Sep 1991 🧢 2nd XIv PC Petts Wood CC · HomeformWDLLD

Skipper's Log — Saturday 07 September 1991

There are days when you win, days when you lose, and days when the cricketing gods simply shrug and leave both sides to argue it out over the teas. Saturday was very much the latter, as we welcomed Petts Wood CC for a match that had a bit of everything and, in the end, decided precisely nothing.

Batting first, we crawled and occasionally sprinted to 163 for 8 from our 45 overs, a total that owed most of its respectability to Roly Edwards, whose 33 was the backbone of the innings, with Fred Cheesman (25) and Malcolm Pay (23) chipping in handsomely around him. I contributed a workmanlike 22 myself, more graft than grace, but every run counts when you're stitching together a defendable total from limited materials.

In the field we made the sort of start captains dream of, reducing Petts Wood to 4 for 1 with poor Eddie Sharr back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. But cricket has a habit of punishing early optimism, and Neil Speers settled in to compile a fine unbeaten 52, marshalling their tail admirably as they closed on 128 for 9 from 39 overs. Tim Thirkell was superb with the ball, finishing with 4 for 43, while Rob Doick (3 for 30, and stingy enough to bowl three maidens) and Colin Bentwood (1 for 21) kept the pressure on throughout.

In the end, neither side could quite find the finishing move, and honours were shared. A fair result, if I'm honest — though the lads will fancy we had the better of it.
