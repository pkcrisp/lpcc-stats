🦆 Duck News — 26th–29th September 1982

3 ducks
Week 23 · 26 Sep 1982 – 29 Sep 1982
A season-closing flurry at home to Linton Park CC saw the zero club welcome three new arrivals in a single unhappy innings, each dispatched inside the paperwork stage of their visit. Sunday 26th September – vs Linton Park CC (home) David Cheale, in at six, fell caught Simon Thirkell bowled Mick Filmer without scoring — his 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from just 8 innings, good enough for 141st of 1038 in the all-time list. He's a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, and keeps pace with brother Steve, who sits on an identical 3 career ducks — evidently a family aptitude for the art of not scoring. On this very day back in 1965, Roy Clarke (1957–1966, 40 ducks, 9th all-time, Duck of the Year 1959) was also dismissed for nought against Linton Park CC — some anniversaries never lose their shine. Simon Thirkell, fresh from pouching Cheale's catch, then walked out at five and was run out for nought himself — his 2nd of the season and 5th of his career from 29 innings, 106th of 1038. A card-carrying member of the 50 Before a 50 club (59 ducks, high score 48) and a fixture of both the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens, Simon plainly travels well for all the wrong reasons. The Thirkell name is a duck dynasty in its own right — father John on 90 career noughts, brothers Tim on 50 and Nigel on 30 — so today's zero keeps very distinguished company. Roy Clarke, same date, same opposition, 1965, sends his regards for a second time. David Iliffe, number 11, was last man out — caught Mike Elworthy bowled Mike Iliffe for nought, undone by his own kin. That earns him a fresh line on the Quacked by Kin board, dismissed by a relative in 1982 itself, a genuinely rare family ceremony. It's his 7th duck of the season and 19th of his career in 92 innings, 33rd of 1038 all-time, and keeps him handsomely clear at the top of this year's Duck of the Year race. Roy Clarke, 1965, vs Linton Park CC once more — the third and final sighting of him today. Week in summary: two fixtures, three ducks, the lot condensed into one unfortunate innings at home to Linton Park CC — headlined by cricket's rarest party trick, a wicket taken by one relative and endured by another. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. David Iliffe — 7 ducks, 36 innings (19.44%) 2. Fred Cheesman — 6 ducks, 29 innings (20.69%) 3. Malcolm Pay — 6 ducks, 37 innings (16.22%) 4. Sean Cook — 4 ducks, 8 innings (50.0%) 5. Jim Hall — 4 ducks, 27 innings (14.81%) Plenty of season left for the chasing pack to close the gap on Iliffe. Same time next fixture — bring your bat, or don't bother 🍩🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 18–23 September 1982

3 ducks
Week 22 · 18 Sep 1982 – 23 Sep 1982
Four fixtures, three ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. Modest pickings by Linton standards, but quality over quantity, as ever. Saturday 18 September 1st XI at home to West Farleigh CC — Brian Piper strode in at four and departed without troubling the scorers, bowled by Norman Underdown. His second duck of the season nudges him to a round dozen for his career, good for 46th of 1035 in the all-time roll. Spookily, on this very date in 1960 Cliff Anscombe (1957–1975, 25 ducks, 24th all-time) was doing precisely the same thing against Tilling Steven. Some anniversaries you don't choose. Sunday 19 September 1st XI v Anchorians CC — David Iliffe, in at two, was caught and bowled by G Cooper for the sixth time this season, taking him to 18 career ducks and 35th of 1035 all-time. That sixth duck draws him level at the very top of this year's Duck Hunting Season charts, and he arrives there as a live Duck of the Year 1982 contender — a title he's already chasing hard. He's also no stranger to the Quacked by Kin honours, having shared a nought with relative Mike Iliffe (43 career ducks himself) earlier this campaign — a proper family trade. And on this day in 1959, G Martin (1958–1959, 3 ducks) was falling the same way against Bearsted CC. Thursday 23 September Midweek XI v Withnell Fold CC — Jon Harris, promoted to eight, was caught by T Franks off Robinson for his third duck of the season. Ordinarily unremarkable, except Jon now sits on 56 career ducks — second of all 1035 names ever to pull on an LPCC shirt. A three-time Duck of the Year (1976, 1980, 1981), the only man to complete the Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), and a fixture of the Lone Duck, Tourist Duck and Homebody Duck top tens besides. Fittingly, on this day in 1972 club great Tim Thirkell (1967–1982, 50 ducks) was also walking back for nothing, against West Farleigh CC. The week in brief Three ducks from four matches — one fixture, mercifully or not, got away entirely fowl-free. The Iliffe household and the Harris family both add further weight to already groaning honours boards, proof once again that in this club the art of not scoring is a hereditary trait as much as a skill. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Fred Cheesman — 27 inns, 6 ducks, 22.2% - David Iliffe — 34 inns, 6 ducks, 17.6% - Malcolm Pay — 35 inns, 6 ducks, 17.1% - Sean Cook — 6 inns, 4 ducks, 66.7% - Jim Hall — 24 inns, 4 ducks, 16.7% Three names locked together at the summit on six apiece — the Duck of the Year race is wide open. Sean Cook's strike rate remains a thing of terrible beauty. Next match, next chance. The zero club never closes. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 11 September 1982

4 ducks
Week 21 · 11 Sep 1982 – 15 Sep 1982
Only three fixtures on the card this week, but the zero column still found its way into two of them. Four fresh entries for the ledger — read on. Sunday 12 September — 1st XI v Northend Sunday (h) The 1st XI's lone contribution came from David Iliffe, tidily held by K Stapley off T- Pearce at number five. It's his 5th duck of the season and 17th for the career — 89 innings in, 37th on the all-time list of 1034, and currently sat pretty atop this year's Duck of the Year table, a crown he's very much still wearing. Keeping it in the family, his father Mike Iliffe is already a fixture of the honours board on 43 career ducks of his own — the "Quacked by Kin" entry between them dates from earlier this season, so today's effort is rather on brand. Historical company arrives via the on-this-day file too: back in 1959, S Lusher made a duck on this exact date against Bowaters CC, a lone entry on 1 for the career — glad to have some 1982 company, no doubt. Wednesday 15 September — Midweek XI v The Mote CC (a) The Mote were considerably less hospitable, prising out three Linton men for nought in the same innings — a proper cull. Trevor Hales went first, bowled Glyn Mitchell at four, his 1st duck of the season and 2nd of a still-fledgling career (4 innings). Trevor already carries a "Retirement Duck" entry on the honours board from a presumed-past farewell, which today rather cheerfully undermines — reports of his retirement, evidently exaggerated. Family runs deep here too, with Dave Hales and Andrew Hales both quietly ticking along on 2 career ducks apiece. Next, Jim Hall — caught Richard Chapman, bowled Glyn Mitchell — his 4th of the season and a staggering 51st of the career, good for 3rd on the entire all-time list out of 1034 names. Jim's board reads like a small pamphlet: Duck of the Year in both 1970 and 1977, permanent residence in the Lone Duck, Tourist Duck and Homebody Duck top tens, and the cruellest entry of all — "Full House nearly-man", denied a duck at all eleven positions by a single stubborn slot. And completing the set, Bob Dyer, bowled W- Beg, for a duck on debut — his very first senior innings, and it's a blank. Instant, uncontested membership of the Debutante Duck board, and a flawless 100% career duck-rate to open his account. Welcome to the club, Bob — nowhere to go but up. Fittingly, all three Mote victims share the same echo down the years: Mike Iliffe — David's own father — made a duck against Southborough CC on this very date back in 1973. Three Linton men, one shared ghost of ducks past. Week in brief 4 ducks from 3 matches, with the third fixture escaping entirely duck-free. Wednesday did the damage, with The Mote claiming three in a single innings. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Fred Cheesman — 6 ducks, 27 inns, 22.2% - Malcolm Pay — 6 ducks, 33 inns, 18.2% - David Iliffe — 5 ducks, 33 inns, 15.2% - Sean Cook — 4 ducks, 6 inns, 66.7% (a rate that deserves its own trophy cabinet) - Jim Hall — 4 ducks, 22 inns, 18.2% The season's winding down but the ducks show no sign of migrating early. See you all at the next fixture — bats, and inevitably a few more noughts, at the ready. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Club Dispatch — 5–8 September 1982 🏏

5 ducks
Week 20 · 5 Sep 1982 – 8 Sep 1982
The Cornwall Tour rolled on this week, and the Duck Club's touring wing was busy: five noughts posted across four matches against Beacon, Grampound Road, Paul and Troon. The exclusive zero club, it turns out, travels rather well. Sunday 5th, v Beacon CC, Cornwall Mike Elworthy departed early, caught behind off John Hutchins. His 2nd duck of the season is also his 2nd career duck from 20 innings, enough to earn him a spot in the Tourist Duck top 10 — a man who saves his best failures for away soil. On this very date in 1970, David T Lowen was doing much the same against A.Trembeths. Monday 6th, v Grampound Road CC David Iliffe fell caught, his 4th duck of the season and 16th of a career now 87 innings deep, 38th on the all-time list. 1982 is shaping into quite a year for him — he already wears the Duck of the Year 1982 sash and was Quacked by Kin earlier this season alongside relative Mike Iliffe, who carries 43 career ducks of his own. Back in 1967, Roger Craddock marked this same date with a duck against Birchington. Tuesday 7th, v Paul CC A double blow for the tourists. Allan Cooper was caught for his 1st duck of the season, 26th of his career from 175 innings and 21st all-time, a serial member of the Only Two Quacks club, previously paired with Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back. Fred Cheesman followed him back, stumped for his 6th of the season and 9th career duck, a Full House honouree who has now failed at every position in the order. Both men share a calendar twin in Tim Thirkell, whose own duck fell on this exact date in 1977. Wednesday 8th, v Troon CC And then, arrival and ignominy in the same breath. Kevin Riley marked his LPCC debut bowled for nought — a fresh face straight into the Debutante Duck club, all-time rank 390 of 1033 and, we trust, only just getting started despite the club's fondness for filing new arrivals under One and Done. In 1981, Richard Ashby made his own duck against Paul CC on this very date. Five ducks, four matches, one tour — Cornish bowling proving no gentler than the home attacks. Fred Cheesman's efforts also keep him handily placed in the season race below. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Fred Cheesman — 6 ducks, 25 innings (24.0%) 2. Paul Bowles — 4 ducks, 48 innings (8.33%) The tour rolls on, and so, inevitably, will the ducks. See you at the next fixture 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 28 August to 3 September 1982

3 ducks
Week 19 · 28 Aug 1982 – 3 Sep 1982
The August Bank Holiday and a tour fixture in Cornwall conspired to add three fresh entries to the club's exclusive zero club this week. Modest numbers, but one of them is a genuine slice of history. 🏏 Bank Holiday Monday, 30 August — 1st XI v Bearsted CC (home) Paul Bowles opened the innings and departed just as promptly, edging behind c Peter Mills b Richard Campion for nought — his 4th duck of the summer and 42nd of a heavily decorated Linton career, good enough for 8th on the all-time table of 1,030 hopefuls. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966 and 1968) and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top ten, Paul continues to demonstrate that scoring nothing away from home is very much its own discipline. Neatly enough, on this same date back in 1965 Frank Brattle (1937–1971, 38 career ducks) also fell for a duck against Bearsted — some things about this fixture never change. Down at number 7, Jim Hall was bowled by Ian Lambert without so much as a murmur — his 3rd duck of the season, but far more significantly his 50th career duck for the club. Fifty. A round, resounding nothing, and it hoists Jim to 3rd on the all-time chart of 1,030, an eyelash off top spot. His honours board already reads like a duck almanac — Duck of the Year 1970 and 1977, Lone Duck top ten, Homebody Duck top ten, Tourist Duck top ten, and no fewer than three "Quacking Together" partnerships (Jon Harris, Martin Mears, Tim Thirkell). Fittingly, Jim shares his date with the same Frank Brattle vintage of 1965 — two Linton ducks, one Bearsted attack, one long-retired bowler getting the credit twice over. 🚢 Friday 3 September — Tour XI v Falmouth CC (away) Out on tour, David Iliffe was bowled by Mike Hoban at number 4 for nought — his 3rd duck of the season and 15th of an 84-innings career, 39th on the all-time list. 1982 has been quite the vintage for David: he currently holds the Duck of the Year 1982 title, and earlier this season was memorably "Quacked by Kin", dismissed by relative Mike Iliffe (43 career ducks — clearly a family with an eye for the ignominious). On this very date in 1972, Benny Bell (1966–1975, 30 ducks) was performing precisely the same trick against Staplehurst CC — the calendar has long had a soft spot for early-September nothings. 📋 Week in summary Four matches, three ducks — a quiet week in volume but a genuinely significant one in substance, with Jim Hall's half-century the headline. No pairs, no family affairs, just honest, old-fashioned failure to score. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Malcolm Pay — 6 ducks in 32 innings (18.75%) - Fred Cheesman — 5 ducks in 21 innings (23.81%) - Sean Cook — 4 ducks in 6 innings (66.67% 🍩) - Paul Bowles — 4 ducks in 45 innings (8.89%) - Steve Cheale — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.86%) Malcolm still leads the chase, but Sean Cook's strike rate for nothing remains the most alarming statistic in the club. 🦆 Next fixture awaits — pads on, guard taken, destiny unknown. The Duck Club never sleeps.

🦆 Duck News — 21–25 August 1982

1 duck
Week 18 · 21 Aug 1982 – 25 Aug 1982
Three matches in the book this week and the ducks proved remarkably shy — just the one addition to the exclusive zero club. Quality over quantity, evidently. Saturday — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC Malcolm Pay, so often a fixture at the crease, found himself back in the pavilion rather sooner than planned, caught B Neale off the bowling of P Wilson at number five. Not a golden duck, just the ordinary business of getting out for nought. It's his 6th of the season and 22nd for the club in 170 innings, keeping him a solid 31st in the all-time standings — a chip off the old block too, with sibling Colin Pay a fellow, if less prolific, member of the club on one career duck. On this very day back in 1953, J Barclay was up to much the same thing, also falling for nought. Barclay went on to be crowned Duck of the Year that season and would eventually bow out of his Linton career on a duck of his own — Malcolm, three decades on, treads a well-worn path. The other two fixtures of the week passed off disappointingly duck-free — a rare weekend of restraint from the rest of the club. Week in summary One duck from three matches is a lean return by Linton standards — the sort of week that sends a correspondent reaching for the record books rather than the honours board. Still, Malcolm's kept the tally ticking over nicely, and with the season table so tightly packed, one man's quiet Saturday is another's opportunity. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Malcolm Pay — 30 inn, 6 ducks (20.0%) 🥈 Fred Cheesman — 19 inn, 5 ducks (26.32%) 🥉 Sean Cook — 6 inn, 4 ducks (66.67%) 4. Steve Cheale — 7 inn, 3 ducks (42.86%) 5. Paul Bowles — 42 inn, 3 ducks (7.14%) Sean Cook's rate remains eye-watering — two trips in every three ending in a nought. Malcolm holds top spot for now, but Fred Cheesman is lurking with menace and the summer isn't done yet. 🍩 Fixtures continue next week — no doubt the ducks will return in greater numbers. Get down to the ground and see who's next to join the club. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 14–15 August 1982

2 ducks
Week 17 · 14 Aug 1982 – 15 Aug 1982
A lean weekend for the Zero Club, but what it lacked in volume it made up for in ceremony: one man, two days, one immaculate pair. 🏏 Saturday 14th August — 1st XI v St Lukes Maidstone CC (home) Malcolm Pay strode out at first drop and departed for nought, caught R Aiston bowled R Moon. His 4th duck of the campaign and 20th of an increasingly distinguished career spanning 168 innings — good enough for 31st on the all-time list of 1,030 noughts. On this very day in 1893, A Kent was doing much the same against Royal Engineers CC, en route to 3 career ducks and the 1893 Duck of the Year crown. Eighty-nine years apart, same script. 🌱 Sunday 15th August — 1st XI v Whitstable CC (home) Malcolm wasn't finished. Back out at number four the following day, he fell again — caught T Robertson bowled C Hadler — for his 5th duck of the season and 21st of his career, holding steady at 31st all-time. File the weekend under Pair: two ducks, back to back, the sort of consistency the club exists to honour. Fittingly, on this same day in 1964, the legendary John Thirkell — Linton's all-time leader with 90 ducks across 1943–1982 and a five-time Duck of the Year — was also walking back, against Bowaters CC. Malcolm keeps company he probably didn't ask for. For the record, Malcolm's sibling Colin Pay carries a single career duck of his own — the Pay family evidently prefers quality over quantity, except this particular weekend. 📊 The Week in Brief Just two matches, and only one man among the fallen — but he made absolutely sure to double up. The rest of the club batted through undisturbed this time, no drama at the 2s, 3s or beyond. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Fred Cheesman — 5 ducks in 18 innings (27.78%) - Malcolm Pay — 5 ducks in 28 innings (17.86%) - Sean Cook — 4 ducks in 6 innings (66.67%), form of a lifetime - Steve Cheale — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.86%) - Paul Bowles — 3 ducks in 39 innings (7.69%) Malcolm's weekend brace hauls him level with Fred Cheesman at the summit of the 1982 race — Duck of the Year is very much up for grabs. Next fixture, who fancies joining them at the top? The ducks are out there. We'll be watching. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 7-8 August 1982

0 ducks
Week 16 · 7 Aug 1982 – 8 Aug 1982
Two matches, two results, and — brace yourselves — not a single duck to show for it. The full 1982 top order marched out, faced their overs, and every last one of them troubled the scorer. A blank weekend for the exclusive zero club, and a rare one at that. We won't pretend this isn't a little disappointing. The art of not scoring is a proud Linton tradition and this weekend it simply wasn't practised. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to report — just eleven men a side quietly getting off the mark like it's the most natural thing in the world. Frankly, unnatural. Still, a fallow weekend for the ducks is not a fallow weekend for the season. The race for Duck of the Year rumbles on regardless, and the current pecking order makes for interesting reading. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Sean Cook — 4 ducks from just 6 innings, a scarcely believable 66.67% strike rate. At this pace he may retire the trophy before September. 🥈 Steve Cheale — 3 from 7, ticking along at 42.86%. Every third visit to the crease is apparently optional. 🥉 Fred Cheesman — 5 ducks from 17 innings and 29.41%, the current volume leader even if the percentage boys are chasing him down. 4️⃣ Malcolm Pay — 3 from 26, a more modest 11.54%, playing the long game. 5️⃣ Paul Bowles — 3 from 37, 8.11%, the elder statesman of the group proving that consistency of a very particular kind takes patience. Sean Cook holds the raw pace, Fred Cheesman the raw total — plenty of season left for either to pull clear, and plenty of time for the rest of the chasing pack to come good. 🏏 So, an uneventful weekend for the collectors among us. The ducks evaded capture this time, but nobody should get comfortable — form like this rarely lasts, and Linton's middle order has let the club down (in the best possible way) too often for anyone to relax. Next fixture, normal service will surely resume. See you at the crease. 🦆

The name should sound familiar — son of club legend John Thirkell, all-time number one with 90 ducks, and brother to Tim Thirkell 50 and Nigel Thirkell 30. A dynasty built entirely on not scoring. Cruttenden salutes a fourth time. 🏏 Staplehurst CC (home) Mike Elworthy became properly acquainted with the club, caught unsure bowled Hotson, his very first career duck in ten innings. He joins the Tourist Duck top 10. On this day in 1964, Basil Sears was doing exactly the same against Headcorn CC, en route to 33 career ducks. 🌙 Monday 4th — Midweek XI vs Maidstone Police Sean Cook was bowled by B Bradley for his 4th duck of the season, level with his 4th career duck from just six innings — a duck rate needing no further comment. Sean is a Debutante Duck club member from way back, having ducked on his actual first-ever knock. On this day in 1976, Robert Chance did likewise against Sennocke CC. 📋 Week in Summary Six ducks, three matches, one thoroughly demolished top order at Bromley. The ghost of Ernest Cruttenden, 1895, was busy — popping up four times in one afternoon. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Fred Cheesman — 5 ducks/16 inns (31.25%) 2. 🦆 Duck News: 24th to 27th July 1982 🦆

3 ducks
Week 14 · 24 Jul 1982 – 27 Jul 1982
Only one team troubled the zero column this week, but the 1st XI made sure the exclusive club kept its books open with three fresh entries across the weekend. Saturday 24th July, away at Newchapel & Horne Fred Cheesman departed at number six, caught Saffery bowled Arthur, for his 4th duck of the season and 7th of a career now standing at 28 innings, good enough (bad enough) for 79th of 1030 in the all time roll. Fred is a fully paid up Full House man, with a duck at every position from 1 to 11 to his name, a member of Only Two Quacks with Adam Pooley, and a regular face in the Tourist Duck top 10, proof that the art of not scoring travels well. On this very day in 1960, Frank Brattle, who played from 1937 to 1971 and finished with 38 ducks, now 10th all time, also trudged off for nought, against Folkestone CC. Sunday 25th July, away at The Mote CC Richard Chapman had himself an afternoon, accounting for two Linton batsmen in short order. Trevor Back went first, caught Trigg, for his 2nd duck of the season and 19th of his career, 34th of 1030 all time from 129 innings. Trevor's honours cabinet groans under placings in Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top 10, an Only Two Quacks partnership with Allan Cooper, and a Quacking Together entry alongside Jon Harris. Martin Larkin followed at ten, bowled by the same Chapman, his 2nd of the season and 5th career duck, 103rd of 1030 all time, and a paid up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell. Both men were shadowed on the calendar by W Turner, whose solitary career duck for the club came against Bearsted CC on this very day in 1925. Week 1 · 24 Apr 1982 – 25 Apr 1982
Season 1982 gets off the mark in the worst possible way for four unlucky souls, as the Duck Club's books open for another year of dubious distinction. 🏏 Saturday 24th — Wadhurst CC (away) The 1st XI's trip to Wadhurst yielded a brace for the home bowling, both credited to the impressively named T. Nelson. Steve Gower fell at five, caught Plumley bowled Nelson, for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of a growing career — 165th on the all-time roll of dishonour. Steve remains a card-carrying member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first knock, and on this very day back in 1965 club legend John Thirkell — a four-time Duck of the Year and the all-time record holder on a frankly obscene 90 career ducks — was also walking back for nought, albeit against West Farleigh. Two down at seven came Fred Cheesman, bowled Nelson for his own 1st of the season and 4th career quack, 116th all-time. Fred already holds the rare Full House honour (a duck at every position from 1 to 11 — a completionist's nightmare) and sits among the club's Tourist Duck top 10 for his away-day form. Like Steve, he shares the 1965-on-this-day echo of John Thirkell's own nought. 🏏 Sunday 25th — Chatham Nomads CC (home) Sunday's home fixture against Chatham Nomads brought two more names to the ledger, and a rather fitting reunion. Jim Hall departed at four, caught Barry Martin bowled Peter Pitt, his 1st duck of the season but a colossal 48th of his career — 5th on the all-time list and edging ever closer to the exalted Hall of Fame threshold of 50. Jim's honours board reads like a small library: two Duck of the Year titles (1970, 1977), places in the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, and no fewer than five separate "Quacking Together" and "Only Two Quacks" partnerships. On this day in 1976, Trevor Back was performing his own vanishing act against Aylesford Paper Mills. Batting at seven, Martin Mears was bowled by R. Bell for his 1st duck of the season and 13th of his career, 43rd all-time. Fittingly, Martin's "Quacking Together" board partner is none other than Jim Hall himself — and with both men back in the pavilion in the very same match, the pairing has rarely felt more apt. Martin shares Jim's echo of Trevor Back's 1976 duck on this same date. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, all falling to the 1st XI, and all in the very first week of records for this period. Wadhurst's Nelson can claim a tidy brace, while Chatham Nomads shared the spoils between two different bowlers. Jim Hall's tally now looms large over the season — watch this space. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 1 inn, 1 duck (100%) 2. Martin Mears — 1 inn, 1 duck (100%) 3. Steve Gower — 1 inn, 1 duck (100%) 4. Fred Cheesman — 2 inn, 1 duck (50%) Four names, four perfect starts to the season's least wanted competition. Plenty of cricket left to separate them — or to see who else joins the queue. Next fixture, the ducks await. 🦍🏏0️⃣
