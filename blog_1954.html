Week 22 · 25 Sep 1954 – 26 Sep 1954
A quiet weekend for the hunting fraternity, and not the good kind of quiet. Two fixtures came and went across the closing weekend of the 1954 season and, remarkably, not a single soul walked back for nought. Every batsman who took guard troubled the scorer at least once — a state of affairs the Duck Club can only regard with deep suspicion. Bats were middled, singles were nurdled, and not one single dismissal for a duck presented itself for the honours board's consideration. Whether this is due to a sudden and alarming outbreak of competence, or simply two matches where the ducks saw the beaters coming and scattered for the reeds, we cannot say. What we can say is that the season's tally sits exactly where it did last week, and the regulars atop the leaderboard will have enjoyed the rare pleasure of a weekend off from scrutiny. Let it be noted for the record: 0 ducks, from 2 matches, is itself a result of a very particular kind — the art of not scoring nothing at all. Fleeting, no doubt. 🦆 Duck Report — 5 September 1954

4 ducks
Week 19 · 5 Sep 1954
A single fixture on the books this week, away trade forgotten, as the 1st XI hosted Gillingham CC, Kent — and Gillingham's bowlers found the home dressing room in generous mood. Four ducks recorded, three of them falling in a tidy little cluster. 1st XI v Gillingham CC, Kent (home) John Larking opened up and didn't last, bowled by J Tansley for his very first LPCC duck — a debut entry on the nought column in just his 8th innings, landing him 215th of 650 in the all-time list. Curiously, this very date has previous: on 5 September 1953, John Thirkell was also trudging off for nought, that time against Yalding CC. History, it seems, likes this fixture week. Then O Barden got to work, and got to work thoroughly. Number six John Thirkell fell to him for a 7th duck of the season, his 26th in an LPCC career now 150 innings deep — a total that keeps him imperiously parked at #1 on the all-time chart. Thirkell already carries Duck of the Year honours from 1952 and, on this season's evidence, is mounting a serious defence of the 1954 crown too. He's a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens besides, with shared quacking history alongside Bob Arnold and William Buck. On this same day in 1953, J Barclay was likewise dismissed for nought against Yalding — Barclay finished his short career on exactly 7, precisely where Thirkell now sits for the season alone. O Barden wasn't done. Richard Harris, in at seven, followed his captain back for a 5th duck of the campaign, taking him to 19 career ducks from 96 innings and into 2nd place all-time — Thirkell's shadow lengthens behind him. Like Larking, Harris finds company on this calendar date in Thirkell's 1953 effort at Yalding. And to complete the set, John Abnett went at eight, O Barden's third scalp in a row, for his 1st career duck in only his 2nd senior innings. He arrives with a "Ducks in a Row" board credit already to his name for a run of four in swift succession — a man clearly not afraid of type-casting. Another entry for the 5 September file, joining Thirkell's 1953 quack at Yalding. Week in brief: one match, four ducks, and O Barden helping himself to three victims on the spin — Thirkell, Harris and Abnett walking back in convenient single file. A tidy afternoon's work for Gillingham, a less tidy one for the LPCC top order. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Thirkell — 23 inns, 7 ducks, 30.43% - Richard Harris — 19 inns, 5 ducks, 26.32% - Bob Arnold — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% - William Blight — 20 inns, 4 ducks, 20.0% - Gerald Wright — 7 inns, 3 ducks, 42.86% Thirkell holds the top spot but Gerald Wright's strike rate should worry him. Next fixture beckons — the zero club never closes its books for long. 🥇🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 21st to 22nd August 1954

3 ducks
Week 17 · 21 Aug 1954 – 22 Aug 1954
Only two matches on the books across this short midweek stretch, but one of them made up for the other's silence in some style. 🏏 Saturday — v Charles Arkcoll (home), 1st XI Gordon Fenner did most of the damage here. First to fall was John Thirkell, in at four, stumped by Humphrey Morris off Fenner's bowling — his 5th duck of the season and 24th of an already storied career, which sits, remarkably, at number 1 of 649 in the all-time Linton Park charts. Nobody in the club's long history has mastered the art of not scoring quite so often. Thirkell arrives garlanded besides — Duck of the Year back in 1952, with this season's 1954 title already pencilled in against his name too — and a fixture on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, plus Quacking Together honours shared with clubmates Bob Arnold and William Buck from outings gone by. Fenner struck again at number eight, bowling Michael Morton for nought — his 2nd duck of the season and 5th overall from 30 innings, edging him to 31st on the club's all-time roll. Last man in was Gerald Wright, bowled by John Ames without scoring — a bowler who, if the honours board is to be believed, has been troubling Duck Club members since at least 1949. It's Wright's 3rd of the season and 9th of his career from just 25 innings, over a third of his trips to the crease ending in nought, good enough for a lofty 11th all-time. Curiously, all three shared their moment with history: this very date in 1949 also saw Stan Wratten (1943–1954, 8 career ducks, 13th all-time) fall for nought against The Mote CC. Three quacks in 1954, one echo from 1949. The period's other fixture, meanwhile, passed off as a rare vegan affair — no fowl found. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, all delivered in a single innings at Charles Arkcoll. Gordon Fenner claims a brace of scalps for his figures, and the 1st XI's middle-to-late order duly obliged. A tidy little collapse, by the club's own dubious standards. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Thirkell — 21 innings, 5 ducks (23.81%) - Bob Arnold — 14 innings, 4 ducks (28.57%) - Gerald Wright — 6 innings, 3 ducks (50.0%) - Peter Weedon — 6 innings, 3 ducks (50.0%) - Richard Harris — 17 innings, 3 ducks (17.65%) 🥇 Thirkell leads the chase for Duck of the Year 1954, but with Wright and Weedon lurking on a fearsome 50% strike rate, nobody's safe yet. 🦆 Duck News — 31 July to 2 August 1954

6 ducks
Week 14 · 31 Jul 1954 – 2 Aug 1954
Three matches shared out this week's fixtures, but only one club produced the goods for the exclusive zero club — the 1st XI, twice over, with six ducks between two outings. Consistency of a very particular kind. Saturday 31 July — Aylesford Paper Mills CC (away) The top order held but the tail went quietly. John Thirkell was first to go, bowled by M Burton for his 3rd duck of the season and 22nd of a career that already tops the all-time charts — he sits at a commanding #1 of 648 in the club's roll of dubious honour, a two-time Duck of the Year (1952 and 1954) and permanent resident of the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. Fittingly, on this very day in 1895 Ernest Cruttenden also fell for nought, against Headcorn CC, on his way to a career 10 and 9th place all time. Bob Arnold followed, caught by Alfie Pullen off Bobby Brigden, his 4th duck of the season and 13th of his career, good for 4th on the all-time table and mid-stride through a run of four consecutive ducks. He too shares his fall with Cruttenden's 1895 vintage. Brian Relf edged behind off M Burton for a modest 1st duck of the season and just his 2nd career nought. Relf is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck and Retirement Duck honours from past chapters of his innings, though this particular failure was neither — simply an honest addition to the ledger, and further company for Cruttenden. Gerald Wright rounded things off, bowled by M Burton, for his 2nd duck of the season and 8th of his career, 12th all time. Same match, same 1895 echo — clearly a strong year for the art of not scoring. Monday 2 August — Lord Cornwallis XI (home) Stan Wratten was caught behind off a- Milsted for his 1st duck of the season and 8th career nought, 13th on the all-time list, keeping fine company with John Ames in the Quacking Together honours. On this day in 1944, E- Humphreys made a duck against the National Fire Service Overseas Contingent — a suitably wartime parallel. And John Thirkell was back at it, caught behind off J- Clark for his 4th duck of the season and 23rd career duck — a grim pair completed across the weekend, out for nought in both matches without so much as a change of scenery. E- Humphreys' 1944 shadow follows him here too. Week in Summary Six ducks, three matches, and the 1st XI shouldering the lot. Thirkell's weekend pair is the standout feat — twice out, twice for nothing, and still comfortably the club's most prolific collector of noughts in 648 recorded ducks. 1895 and 1944 both got a nostalgic name-check from the archives along the way. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Bob Arnold — 12 innings, 4 ducks, 33.33% - John Thirkell — 18 innings, 4 ducks, 22.22% - Peter Weedon — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60.0% - William Blight — 15 innings, 3 ducks, 20.0% - Gerald Wright — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.0% Arnold leads on percentage but Thirkell, the all-time #1, is never far behind. Plenty of season left for the leaderboard to change hands. Pads on, guard taken — the ducks will keep coming. See you all at the next fixture. 🦆

Two fixtures, three ducks, and the 1954 season ledger creaks a little further into the red. The exclusive zero club welcomes fresh entries this week — a place of dubious distinction, as ever, open to all. Saturday 10th — vs A.Trembeths (Home) The top order held firm but the tail found its usual trouble. Robert Muir, in at eight, was caught by Jessop off the bowling of Chance — his 2nd duck of the campaign and 3rd of a 26-innings career, planting him at a solid 63rd of 648 in the all-time ledger. A card-carrying member of the Retirement Duck honours, though we stress his career carries on regardless of this week's efforts. Curiously, on this very day back in 1869, J Amies was doing exactly the same thing against 1st Innings Hayle Place CC — some traditions simply refuse to die. In at nine came Bob Arnold, trapped lbw by the same relentless Chance. This was Arnold's 3rd duck of the season alone — a genuinely industrious rate of failure — and his 12th career nought from 59 innings, vaulting him to a rarefied 6th of 648 all time. Arnold's honours cabinet is already groaning: Ducks in a Row (four consecutive, a feat of remarkable consistency of a very particular kind), Only Two Quacks alongside Alan Wilkes, and Quacking Together with John Thirkell. And yes — by pleasing coincidence, 1869's J Amies strikes again as this day's historical companion in futility. Sunday 11th — vs Selstead (Away) At Selstead, R- Waters departed bowled by H- Denham, batting at three. This was his 1st duck of the season and, notably, his 1st career duck from just 3 innings — the art of not scoring, freshly discovered. He lands at 247th of 648 in the all-time standings, and joins the Retirement Duck ranks as a standing club honour, entirely unrelated to any suggestion he's finished playing. On this same day in 1943, Stan Wratten was falling for nought against the Royal Artillery Wing — a fellow member of the Quacking Together honours, paired with John Ames.
