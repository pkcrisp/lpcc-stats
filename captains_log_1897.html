🗓️ 21 Aug – 27 Aug 1897 · 1897 — 1 match

Sat 21 Aug 1897 🧢 1st XIv SC 1st Innings Maidstone Conservative Club CC · HomeformDWLLD

Saturday last, the twenty-first of August, brought Maidstone Conservative Club CC to our ground as visitors, and I confess some satisfaction in winning the toss and electing to take first use of the wicket, a decision that answered handsomely enough. G Knight gave a truly magnificent display, compiling a century of 121 that stood as the innings' chief ornament, and he found willing company in H G Smith, whose 37 was a valuable knock, while I was glad to remain unbeaten on 39 when our innings closed. F Smith, I am sorry to relate, departed for nought, though the extras column, swelled to 30, was itself no mean contributor to the total.

Our visitors came to the wicket knowing a stern task lay before them, and so it proved. F Baker, J Bransome and E Taylor were each sent back without troubling the scorers, and the trundling that afternoon fell chiefly to my own lot, for I found the timbers to my liking and returned figures of nine wickets without cost, well supported by H G Smith's solitary wicket. The innings closed in short order, and we carried the day by an innings and two hundred runs, a margin as decisive as any I can recall.

I ought to add that G Knight's fine century has carried him past J N Tonge into seventh place among the club's run-scorers of all time, a distinction thoroughly deserved. A most satisfactory afternoon's cricket, and I trust our visitors will find sterner opposition from us again before the season is out.

🗓️ 07 Aug – 13 Aug 1897 · 1897 — 1 match

Wed 11 Aug 1897 🧢 1st XIv Town Malling CC · AwayformDDWLL

Wednesday last, the eleven took the road to Town Malling, there to try conclusions with a side ever ready to give us a sporting game. The toss went against us, and our opponents asked that we take the field first, a decision they may since have reflected upon. Richardson gave their innings its backbone with a fine 89, soberly compiled, while Cooper added 23 and Todman 20 in useful support. Viner, less fortunate, was sent back to the pavilion without troubling the scorers. For my own part I found something in the wicket that suited my spin, and had the satisfaction of taking five wickets without cost, Smith bowling with great steadiness for four and Pile chipping in with one.

Our own innings owed most to my bat, forty-five runs that proved the making of the total, with Laurence following handsomely for 44 and Fryer for 39. It was, I confess, a pleasant day all round, for those forty-five runs carried me past Arthur Cornwallis to the head of the club's list of run-getters, while Smith's four wickets saw him pass W Crittenden into sixteenth place among our bowlers of all time. The season continues kindly besides, for I find myself presently leading both the run-scoring and wicket-taking charts for the first eleven this year, with 307 runs and 36 wickets to my name.

Neither side, in the end, could force a conclusion, and honours were shared in a draw that reflected fairly enough the even tenor of the contest. Town Malling proved generous hosts and keen opponents, and we came away well content with an afternoon's cricket played in the right spirit.
