Sat 21 Aug 1897 🧢 1st XIv SC 1st Innings Maidstone Conservative Club CC · HomeformDWLLD

Saturday 21st August 1897 dawned fair over Linton Park, and Maidstone Conservative Club CC were our visitors — a fixture I'd been rather looking forward to, if only to remind myself which end of the bat to hold. Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in electing to bat, and for once my judgement was rewarded handsomely. G Knight played an innings of real substance, a magnificent 121, while H G Smith chipped in a sound 37. I found some rhythm of my own with 39 not out, though I shan't pretend it troubled Knight's effort for column inches. Poor F Smith departed for a golden duck, bless him, but the extras column did its patriotic duty with 30, and we closed all out for a total that felt, at the time, thoroughly defendable.

How wrong a captain can be. Whatever we'd scored, Maidstone simply declined to be dismissed in the ordinary sense of the word — they finished unbeaten, and while F Baker, J Bransome and E Taylor all went back without troubling the scorers, and I managed to run riot with 9 wickets for none, ably backed by H G Smith's 1-0, it counted for precious little in the end. Beaten by an innings and 200 runs is not a scoreline one dresses up.

There was, at least, one thing to toast in the wreckage: Knight's 121 lifts him past J N Tonge to 7th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — a fine achievement amid the rubble. We'll be better for it next week, I'm sure, though I confess I shall need convincing.

Wed 11 Aug 1897 🧢 1st XIv Town Malling CC · AwayformDDWLL

The road took us over to Town Malling on a fine Wednesday in August, and having lost the toss and been asked to chase, we found ourselves with the ball in hand first — which, on reflection, is not the worst way to begin an away fixture with the sandwiches still unpacked. Town Malling's innings never quite escaped us. H B Richardson played the innings of the day, a well-made fifty that carried him to 89, with W H Cooper (23) and J Todman (20) offering support, while poor G Viner departed for a duck at our hands. I found some rare purchase off the pitch and finished with 5 for 0, spinning through their order in a manner I shall be dining out on for weeks, with H G Smith (4 for 0) and H Pile (1 for 0) mopping up admirably around me.

With the bat we were bowled out in turn, though not before putting up a proper fight — F G Laurence made a stylish 44 and Jack Fryer 39, while I chipped in with 45 of my own, the sort of innings that felt rather better than it looked from the boundary.

Two small pieces of history along the way: those 45 runs lifted me past Arthur Cornwallis to the top of the club's all-time run-scorers, and H G Smith's four wickets nudged him past W Crittenden into 16th on the all-time list — not bad for an afternoon's work.

Neither side could force the issue, and honours were shared. A draw, then, but a thoroughly entertaining one — we'll happily take that.

Small consolations amid the wreckage: the season's run and wicket charts both still have my name at the top. But a captain's job is to lift the side, not his own averages, and on this evidence we've plenty of work to do before we're worth Headcorn's trouble again.
