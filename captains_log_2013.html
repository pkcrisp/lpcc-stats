Sun 22 Sep 2013 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · HomeformWWWLW

Skipper's Log — Sunday 22 September 2013. Tenterden CC at home, and having lost the toss we were put into the field, which on a fine September afternoon felt less like a hardship than an invitation to enjoy the sunshine before the real work began.

Work it duly was. We slipped to 2-1 early doors, the sort of stumble that has grown men reaching for the biscuit tin, but James Piper had other ideas, compiling a magnificent unbeaten century while Will Thirkell chipped in a fine fifty and Richard Knowler weighed in with 22. Their fifth-wicket stand of 125 was the innings' backbone, and 229/6 from 41.1 overs felt like a total worth defending — even after I contributed a duck to proceedings, a blot on the card I shall gloss over quickly.

Tenterden made a decent fist of the chase, Ben Price's fifty and useful knocks from James Rand and Tom Richards keeping them in it, but once the middle order folded from 93-3 to 99-6 the game was as good as ours. I found some rhythm with the ball to finish with 3-12, well supported by Thirkell's 3-16 and Beeney's 3-48, with Knowler tying an end down for two maidens. Nine byes gave poor Kevin Presneill a torrid afternoon behind the stumps.

A golden blob for the skipper, then, but a win by 41 runs for the side. I'll take that trade every time.

Wed 18 Sep 2013 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWWLDL

It was fielding first for me on Wednesday 18th, when we made the trip out to Penshurst Park CC — and I'll admit I rather fancied our chances putting them in. The right call, as it turned out. Penshurst crawled to 120/8 from their forty overs, with only Sean Axtell (34 not out) offering any real resistance. Tom Fordyce and Mike Croft were sent back for ducks apiece, and the real story was with the ball: Thomas Beeney was a menace, mopping up 3-13, with James Piper (1-13) and the ever-parsimonious Matthew Carter (1-15, three maidens and not a run given away lightly) squeezing the life out of the innings around him.

Chasing 121 should never have been the formality it looked, but Richard Piper's 30 set us on our way, and Peter Whyte (26 not out) and James Piper (24 not out) saw us home in some comfort — 121/3 in 31.4 overs, seven wickets to spare. Poor Will Thew will want to forget his contribution, bagging a golden duck, but nobody remembers the blot when the job's done.

A nice personal landmark too: James Piper brought up his 50th career wicket, and with it further burnished his claim to top spot in this season's Midweek XI run charts, while Thomas Beeney's 3-13 nudges him along nicely in the wicket column. All told, a thoroughly good day at the office — professional with the ball, comfortable with the bat, and the sort of win that makes the drive home feel a good deal shorter.

Sun 08 Sep 2013 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tyler Hill CC · HomeformWWWWL

As tricky an afternoon as a losing toss can promise turned into a rather satisfying one for Linton Park. Put in to field on a Sunday when the coin, as ever, ignored our calls entirely, we made hay with the bat: 196/7 from our 40 overs, built around a lovely unbeaten fifty from Craig Sands (54 not out) and fine support from Mark Christmas (40), with the extras column chipping in a generous 26 of its own — proof, if any were needed, that Tyler Hill's bowlers were as generous with their line as our lads were ruthless in punishing it.

Defending it should have been the nervy part, but 159/9 tells its own story. Richard Keir batted beautifully for his 57 and Dan Savage's 37 kept the required rate honest for a while, but James Dash departed without so much as a run to his name, and the middle order never quite got the innings purring. Mark Christmas was the pick with the ball, 3-25, backed up handsomely by Will Thirkell (3-39) and Joshua Knowler (2-32) — a genuine team effort with the cherry. Poor Kevin Presneill had a torrid time behind the stumps, ten byes escaping his gloves, though he'll no doubt claim the pitch was doing more than the scorebook lets on.

The day belonged in part to Mark Christmas, who brought up 6,000 career runs for the club and, with his 40, leapfrogged Jim Hall into 23rd on the all-time run-scorers list — and he now leads our 2013 Sunday charts with 386 runs, with Thomas Beeney topping the wickets on 24. A 37-run win, two points banked, and a thoroughly deserved pint at the bar afterwards.

Sat 07 Sep 2013 🧢 2nd XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWWLLW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 07 September 2013, West Farleigh CC at home.

Won the toss and, in a fit of confidence I can only put down to the tea urn being on early, stuck them in. It came off rather nicely. Nine wickets, two points, and a thoroughly satisfying day's work. We'll take that all season long.

A losing evening, then, but one with too much to be cheerful about to sulk for long. We'll be better for it next week.

Sat 31 Aug 2013 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformWWWWD

Kevin Presneill's Skipper's Log, Staplehurst CC, Saturday 31 August 2013

Won the toss, and against all my better instincts I chose to bowl first — sorry, bat first. The old grey cells still work occasionally. And how the lads repaid the faith: 245/7 from our 45 overs, built on a fine, well-crafted fifty from Lewis Burnham (63), with Will Thirkell (34) chipping in nicely. I managed 47 myself, which I mention now and shall resist mentioning again, on pain of being accused of self-promotion in my own newsletter. Even the extras column pitched up with 27, which at Staplehurst passes for a top-order contribution.

Defending it looked a tall order once Graham Ward decided he fancied three figures — 116 of pure, unanswerable class, with Colin Breed (26) lending support. For a while it felt like we'd handed him the keys to the ground. But cricket has a way of rewarding patience, and ours came in the shape of Richard Knowler, who simply ran riot — 6-32 of the highest quality — while Will Thirkell (2-48) and the miserly Joshua Knowler (1-35, three maidens and not a run given away lightly) squeezed from the other end. Aaron Davey and Matthew Collard departed without troubling the scorers, and Staplehurst were bowled out for 224.

Twenty-one runs the right way, two points banked, and — quietly, for the record books — my 47 nudged me up to 25th on the club's all-time run charts. Not bad for a Saturday that started with me getting the toss decision monumentally overthought.
