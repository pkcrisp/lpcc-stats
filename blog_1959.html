🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 26–27 September 1959 🦆

4 ducks
Week 22 · 26 Sep 1959 – 27 Sep 1959
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the archives creaking under the weight of history — a proper vintage weekend for the exclusive zero club. 🏏 Saturday 26th — 1st XI v A.Trembeths Robert Wood battled to the crease at seven, dug in gamely, and was eventually undone c P. Rye b C. Rye — the Rye family clearly operating a closed shop that afternoon. It's his 5th duck of the season and 7th of an illustrious career, good enough for 31st on the all-time list of 726. Robert is also a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from a duck-on-debut long since consigned to history — not this one, but the badge stands. He now joins the Victorian great A Startup on exactly 7 career ducks — a man who played 1868–1963... sorry, 1868–1882, took Duck of the Year honours three separate times, and once strung five ducks in a row. Illustrious company for a Saturday afternoon dismissal. 🌱 Sunday 27th — the "Unknown XI" v Linton Park CC Whoever they were, the archives have lost their name — but not, mercifully, their ducks. Three fell in this one innings alone, and two of them were old sparring partners reunited. Alan Wilkes opened and departed first ball region c John Abnett b Terry Whittaker — his 3rd of the season and 11th of a career now sitting a lofty 15th of 726 all time. Alan is a double Only Two Quacks honouree, paired separately with Bob Arnold and John Ames — both of whom, delightfully, ducked in this very same innings, reuniting all three on the honours board in the flesh. His dismissing bowler, Terry Whittaker, has previous form too — on this very day in 1958 he also walked for nought against A.Trembeths. Bob Arnold, at three, fell b Tony Tipples for his 5th of the season, moving to a barely believable 31 career ducks — 2nd of all 726 in club history, second only to the legendary Tim Thirkell-tier company. Bob owns Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive) and shares Only Two Quacks with Alan Wilkes, his batting partner in ignominy this very afternoon. His own executioner, Tony Tipples, has a habit of this fixture — he too made a duck against Linton Park CC on this day back in 1953. John Ames, two down, fell b Alan Wilkes for his 3rd of the season and 20th career — 4th of 726 all time, and a Homebody Duck top-tenner to boot. He shares Quacking Together honours with none other than Roy Clarke, who currently tops this season's leaderboard — proof that good company follows a man even into the zero column. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, three of them arriving in a single Sunday innings — a rare cluster that would have made fine viewing for the purists. Special mention to catcher John Abnett, who claimed Wilkes's wicket this week while quietly sitting 5th in the season charts himself — a man evidently more comfortable behind the stumps than in front of them. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Roy Clarke — 9 ducks, 28 innings, 32.14% 2. Tony Snell — 5 ducks, 8 innings, 62.5% 3. Bob Arnold — 5 ducks, 22 innings, 22.73% 4. Robert Wood — 5 ducks, 30 innings, 16.67% 5. John Abnett — 4 ducks, 6 innings, 66.67% Roy Clarke holds firm at the summit, but with three names locked on five apiece the chase is tightening nicely. 🍩 As ever, the crease waits for nobody. See you next weekend, when the ducks — mysterious XI or not — will doubtless return for more.

🦆 Duck News — 19–20 September 1959

3 ducks
Week 21 · 19 Sep 1959 – 20 Sep 1959
A quiet couple of days for most of the club, but the 1st XI found the special sort of quiet that only a trio of ducks can bring. Three additions to the ledger from two fixtures — modest in number, mighty in pedigree. 🏏 Saturday, away at Bearsted CC G Martin fell to Bryan Elswood without scoring, his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of his career in just 14 innings — rank 78 of 726 in the all-time reckoning. Martin is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his account with a nought on debut some innings back, so this one at least had company. On this very day in 1953, Richard Harris (1940–1959, 29 career ducks, all-time 3rd) was likewise dismissed for nought against Bearsted — the Green clearly has form. 🏏 Sunday, home to Tilling Steven Eddie Lawrence was trapped lbw by Bailey for nought, his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from 12 innings, nudging into the all-time top 110. On this day back in 1958, Tony Snell — that year's Duck of the Year and owner of four ducks in a row — also walked back for nought, against Bearsted CC of all sides. And then the main event. John Thirkell, bowled by Baker, recorded his 2nd duck of the season — but his 42nd of a monumental career spanning 307 innings, which keeps him exactly where he has long resided: 1st of 726 in Linton's all-time duck standings. A three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955), a fixture of the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and a Quacking Together veteran alongside both Bob Arnold and William Buck, Thirkell remains the undisputed emperor of the zero club. Like Lawrence before him, his on-this-day parallel is Tony Snell's 1958 nought. 📅 Week in summary Three ducks, two matches, one team doing all the damage — the 1st XI cornered the market this week while everyone else kept their average intact. Two of the three fell at home to Tilling Steven, so those bowlers will fancy a repeat. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Roy Clarke — 9 ducks in 27 innings (33%) 2. Tony Snell — 5 ducks in 8 innings (63%) 3. John Abnett — 4 ducks in 5 innings (80%) 4. Brian Mills — 4 ducks in 8 innings (50%) 5. Bob Arnold — 4 ducks in 20 innings (20%) Watch Bob Arnold up at 5th — he already shares a Quacking Together honour with this week's headline man, Thirkell himself. Whether anyone catches Abnett's eye-watering 80% strike rate before season's end remains to be seen. Next fixture, next chance — the zero club never sleeps. Pad up, or don't, and we'll see you in the ledger. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 12th–13th September 1959

5 ducks
Week 20 · 12 Sep 1959 – 13 Sep 1959
Two fixtures at Linton Park this weekend, and the zero club welcomed five new entries to its ledger, all from the 1st XI. A tidy reminder that late September shows no mercy to the specialist blocker. 🏏 Saturday 12th September — v Bowaters CC (home) S Lusher, in at seven, was stumped by John Woollett off Bernard Collins to open his LPCC duck account. It is duck number one of the season and number one of a career just five innings old, placing him 296th of 726 in the all-time table. He arrives alongside - Dampier, whose sole Linton duck came in 1952 and came, rather ominously, with a Debutante Duck, a One and Done, and a presumptive Retirement Duck all in the same afternoon. Lusher, we trust, has a rather longer stay in mind. 🏏 Sunday 13th September — v Tunbridge Wells CC (home) A grim afternoon in the middle order and beyond, as four Linton batsmen queued up for the walk back without troubling the scorers. John Ames was stumped by Don Glanvill off John Piper for his 2nd duck of the season and 19th of a long and distinguished career, good enough for 4th on the all-time list. A fully paid-up member of Ducks in a Row and the Homebody Duck top ten, Ames also extends his Quacking Together partnership with Roy Clarke, who joined him in the pavilion in this very innings. Robert Wood fell lbw to Mike Willard, his 4th of the season and 6th of a career that began, fittingly, with a Debutante Duck some years back. He draws level on career ducks with D Sells (1943-44), who reached six in just 14 innings and wore the Duck of the Year crown in 1943. Roy Clarke, caught and bowled by Mike Willard, brought up his 9th duck of the season, a tally already leading the current Duck Hunting Season table by some distance, and his 16th career duck, 6th on the all-time roll of dishonour. 1959 is officially Clarke's year: he holds the Duck of the Year 1959 title, and duly proved it by quacking together with John Ames for the second time in the innings. He is level on career ducks with the mighty Stan Wratten (1943-59, 172 innings), and joins a small dynasty of Clarkes, sibling H Clarke having chipped in two career ducks of his own. John Abnett completed the rout, bowled by Mike Willard for his 4th duck of the season and 14th of his career, good for 11th all-time. A charter member of Ducks in a Row, Abnett is now level with Frank Sutton (1925-49), twice Duck of the Year in the 1940s, who took 62 innings to reach the total Abnett has managed in 36. 📋 Week in Brief Five ducks from two matches, four of them arriving in a single Tunbridge Wells innings. Mike Willard alone accounted for three, a spell the Linton dressing room will not remember fondly. Bowaters, by contrast, got away with contributing just the one. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Roy Clarke — 26 inns, 9 ducks (34.62%) 2. Tony Snell — 7 inns, 5 ducks (71.43%) 3. John Abnett — 5 inns, 4 ducks (80.00%) 4. Brian Mills — 7 inns, 4 ducks (57.14%) 5. Bob Arnold — 19 inns, 4 ducks (21.05%) With Clarke setting the pace and Abnett breathing down his neck, the race for the 1959 crown is very much alive. Next fixture, same rules apply — middle stump exposed, bat raised, ledger open. 🦆

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 5th & 6th September 1959

0 ducks
Week 19 · 5 Sep 1959 – 6 Sep 1959
A rare and troubling bulletin from your correspondent this week: two fixtures played, not a single duck recorded. The Club can only assume the entire Linton Park batting order suddenly remembered how a bat works. 🏏 The Weekend That Wasn't Both matches came and went without so much as a nervous nick to the keeper. Eleven men per side, twenty-two per match, forty-four opportunities for ignominy in total — and the zero club processed not one new application. Somewhere, the honours board gathers dust and mutters darkly about complacency. It happens. Even the most dedicated poultry farmer has a fallow week. Consider it a temporary ceasefire rather than a retirement — the ducks have merely gone quiet, not extinct, and normal service will surely resume the moment somebody gets ideas above their station against a moving new ball. 0️⃣ A Note on the Art of Not Scoring Let it be recorded that "not scoring" and "not getting out for nought" are two entirely different pursuits, and this weekend the Club regrettably excelled at neither in the celebrated sense. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to toast. A quiet weekend for the dishonours board — savour it, because it won't last. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — 1959 1. Roy Clarke — 8 ducks from 25 innings (32.0%) 2. Tony Snell — 5 ducks from 7 innings (71.43%) 3. Brian Mills — 4 ducks from 7 innings (57.14%) 4. Bob Arnold — 4 ducks from 18 innings (22.22%) 5. John Abnett — 3 ducks from 4 innings (75.0%) Tony Snell and John Abnett remain a genuine menace at the crease with strike rates for failure most bowlers would sell their whites for, while Roy Clarke leads on sheer volume — consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. 📅 Looking Ahead Rest assured, the Duck Club selectors remain vigilant and fully expect the drought to break at the earliest opportunity. Get down to the next fixture, take guard with appropriate caution, and remember: the zero club never truly sleeps, it simply waits. 🦆🍩

0 ducks
Week 18 · 29 Aug 1959 – 30 Aug 1959
A rare and faintly embarrassing dispatch this week, members: the Duck Club draws a blank. Two fixtures were played across the 29th and 30th of August, and by whatever conspiracy of application, luck or simply keeping bat vertical, not a single Linton batsman troubled the scorers for nought. Eleven-plus willow wielders per side, twice over, and the zero club gained not one new entry. 🏏 It happens. Even the most committed practitioners of the art of not scoring must occasionally rest on their laurels — or, in this case, fail entirely to add to them. Roy Clarke, Tony Snell and company will no doubt be disappointed to have missed the opportunity to extend their leads, but the season is young yet and there remain fixtures aplenty in which to resume normal service. Consider this weekend less a defeat for the Duck Club and more a well-earned bye. The ducks, it seems, went into hiding. They will not stay hidden for long. 🦆 Duck Report — 22–23 August 1959

3 ducks
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1959 – 23 Aug 1959
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, but only one troubled the scorers in the most spectacular way — the 1st XI's trip to Charles Arkcoll delivered a full house of ignominy, three ducks in the same innings. Eerily, on this very calendar date back in 1953, J Barclay (1953–54) also made a duck against these same opponents. Charles Arkcoll, it seems, has form for extracting noughts from Linton Park. 🏏 1st XI (away) v Charles Arkcoll At number 7, Roy Clarke was caught by Randolph Thompson off Stan Wratten for his 8th duck of the season and 15th of an increasingly storied career — good enough for 8th on the all-time list of 723. Roy currently sits proudly atop the 1959 Duck of the Year table, and with sibling H Clarke already a paid-up member on 2 career ducks, the art of not scoring plainly runs in the family. His dismissal echoes J Barclay's own duck against Charles Arkcoll on this exact day in 1953. In at 10, Alan Wilkes fell c & b John Ames — his 2nd of the season but a tidy 10th career duck, 18th on the all-time honours board. Alan is a two-time inductee of the "Only Two Quacks" club, having shared the dubious distinction with both Bob Arnold and, somewhat awkwardly, his own bowler on the day. Like his captain before him, Alan's nought landed on the very date J Barclay made his in 1953. And rounding off the collapse at number 11, Tony Snell was caught by Sean Carter, again off John Ames — a hat-trick of victims to the same bowler. It's his 5th duck of the season and 18th career, propelling him to a mighty 5th on the all-time list. Tony already carries the 1958 Duck of the Year crown, a "Ducks in a Row" honour for four on the bounce, and the fearsome pedigree of a Runaway Duck season in 1958 without a single run to his name. And yes — his fall too landed on J Barclay's 1953 anniversary. Charles Arkcoll clearly keep a diary. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, three ducks, one bowler — John Ames — doing for two of the three, and the entire haul confined to a single 1st XI innings. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Roy Clarke — 24 inns, 8 ducks (33.3%) 2. Tony Snell — 7 inns, 5 ducks (71.4%) 3. Brian Mills — 7 inns, 4 ducks (57.1%) 4. Bob Arnold — 15 inns, 4 ducks (26.7%) 5. John Abnett — 4 inns, 3 ducks (75.0%) Roy leads on volume, but Tony and John Abnett are quietly setting the pace on percentage — nobody atop this table is in what you'd call form. The season rolls on, and Charles Arkcoll remain stubbornly unlucky opponents for LPCC bats. Same time next week, same ceremonial silence at the crease. 🦆🍩

Week 16 · 15 Aug 1959 – 16 Aug 1959
Saturday's fixture passed by disappointingly fowl-free — not a single nought to report, the sort of afternoon that leaves the Duck Club scratching its head. But patience is a virtue, and Sunday's clash with Mote A more than made amends. 🏏 1st XI v Mote A (Home) Eddie Lawrence opened the innings and, sadly, opened the Duck Club's account for the weekend too — trapped lbw by S. Kitchin. It's his first duck of the 1959 season and just his second visit to the crease that's ended in nought across 9 career innings, leaving him a tidy 203rd of 723 in the all-time reckoning. Rather splendidly, on this very day back in 1953, none other than John Ames also fell for a duck against Mote A — the same fixture, the same calendar date, six years apart. Ames finished with 18 career ducks and sits 4th on the all-time list, a name attached to no fewer than five honours — Ducks in a Row, Homebody Duck, and Quacking Together partnerships with both Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. Good company to keep, even in ignominy. Barely had the scorers reset before Pat Hardwick strolled to the crease at first drop and departed just as swiftly, caught by a substitute off I. Milsted. A second duck for the 1st XI inside the same innings — the club's very own back-to-back tribute act. It's Pat's 2nd duck of the season and career, from 15 innings, ranking 134th of 723. And wouldn't you know it — Pat too finds himself standing alongside John Ames, who made his own duck against Mote A on this same date in 1953. Two Linton batsmen, one bowling side, one historical ghost looking on approvingly from 1953. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both delivered by the same fixture against Mote A — proof that some oppositions simply have the Duck Club's number. Saturday, by contrast, was a monastic affair of pure restraint. A quiet week by recent standards, but quality over quantity, as they say — or don't, in this case. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. John Abnett — 4 innings, 3 ducks (75.0%) John Abnett's 75% strike rate remains a thing of terrifying consistency, while Roy Clarke leads on volume alone. Plenty of season left for the order to be shuffled. 🍩 Onwards to next weekend, where the Duck Club's talent scouts will, as ever, be watching every LBW shout and thick edge with keen anticipation. The zero club never sleeps.

Week 15 · 8 Aug 1959 – 9 Aug 1959
A modest but perfectly formed weekend for the exclusive zero club, with three fresh names inked into the 1959 ledger across two matches. Headcorn on Saturday, Harrietsham on Sunday — the art of not scoring travelled well. 1st XI Saturday, away to Headcorn CC — Bob Arnold, batting at 8, was caught and bowled by J Maybourne for his 4th duck of the season and 30th of a long and distinguished LPCC career, a tally that plants him at a mighty 2nd in the all-time standings of 723. Bob is a long-standing resident of the Ducks in a Row honours board and of Only Two Quacks and Quacking Together besides, company he keeps with Alan Wilkes and John Thirkell respectively. On this very day in 1892, LPCC's Jack Fryer (1881–1906, twice Duck of the Year and 12 to his name) was doing exactly the same thing against Royal Engineers CC. Some traditions simply refuse to die. Sunday, home to Harrietsham CC — a rare double helping, with Keith Chaney doing the damage twice over. Robert Wood departed at number 9, bowled, for his 3rd duck of the season and 5th of his career, rank 47 of 723, and a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his actual first-ever innings. Moments later John Abnett followed at 10, caught by J Wisden off the same bowler, his 3rd of the season and 13th career duck, good enough for a startling 12th on the all-time list and continued membership of the Ducks in a Row board. Fittingly, this very fixture and date carries history of its own — on 9th August 1904, Jack Fryer made yet another duck, that time against the Leeds Castle CC House Team, a name determined to turn up on every page of this almanac. Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, Keith Chaney the pick of the executioners with a brace to his name, and top honours of the weekend to Bob Arnold's continued assault on the all-time podium. A quiet week for most of the club, but the 1st XI more than made up the shortfall with four ducks in two matches — three of them coming off the same over-worked shoulder of one Aylesford bowler. 🏏 Saturday — away to Aylesford Paper Mills CC The rot set in at number seven, where Roy Clarke was bowled by J Lee without scoring. It's his 7th duck of the season and 14th of an increasingly distinguished career in 67 innings, keeping him at a lofty 10th of 723 in the all-time charts and squarely atop this year's Duck of the Year standings — a crown the honours board already has his name pencilled against for 1959. He has previous company on the day itself: on this very date back in 1910, a Rev F A Leveson-Gower was also dismissed for nought, against Maidstone Conservative Club CC no less. Clarke also has a sibling in the family firm, H Clarke, quietly amassing 2 career ducks of his own — the art of not scoring, it seems, runs in the blood. J Lee wasn't finished. Numbers ten and eleven, Terry Whittaker and Tony Snell, both fell in near-identical fashion — caught Cliff Anscombe, bowled J Lee — handing the bowler a tidy brace of tail-enders and Anscombe two safe catches for his trouble. Whittaker moves to 2 for the season and 3 for his career in 21 innings, 93rd of 723 all-time, and carries paid-up membership of both the Quacking Together board (with Brian Relf) and the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell. Snell, meanwhile, is quietly building a formidable CV — his 4th duck of the season lifts him to 17 career ducks in just 50 innings, a startling 5th of 723 all-time, backed by a Duck of the Year gong from 1958, a "Ducks in a Row" honour, and the small matter of a Runaway Duck season with no runs at all to his name that year. Both tail-enders share the Leveson-Gower coincidence with Clarke — quite the reunion on Maidstone Road, forty-nine years apart. 🌱 Sunday — home to Newington CC Pat Hardwick was run out for nought at first drop, his opening entry to the Duck Club — 1st of the season, 1st of his career in 11 innings, 278th of 723 all-time. On this same day in 1944, one E- Humphreys was likewise dismissed without scoring, against the splendidly named National Fire Service Overseas Contingent. Some coincidences transcend the decades. 📋 Week in summary Four ducks, two matches, and J Lee can fairly claim to have run a one-man cull through the Aylesford innings. No pairs, no family affairs, and nothing from the 2nds, 3rds or 4ths this week — they'll want to pull their weight before the Duck of the Year race gets away from them. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Roy Clarke — 22 inns, 7 ducks (31.8%) - Tony Snell — 6 inns, 4 ducks (66.7%) - Brian Mills — 7 inns, 4 ducks (57.1%) - Michael Fagg — 5 inns, 3 ducks (60.0%) - William Buck — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.3%) Snell's strike rate for failure is now genuinely alarming — two in three innings ending in nought. Next weekend's fixtures await, and the exclusive zero club, as ever, keeps its doors wide open. 🍩

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 25-26 July 1959

4 ducks
Week 13 · 25 Jul 1959 – 26 Jul 1959
A quieter weekend by Duck Club standards, but no less rich in ceremony for it — four fresh entries to the ledger across two 1st XI outings, headlined by a proper family occasion. 🏏 Saturday — Tovil (Home) The Clarke household supplied both of Saturday's casualties. Roy Clarke, stumped down the leg side off D- Hitchin, moves to 6 ducks for the 1959 season and 13 for his career from 66 innings — good enough for 11th on the all-time list of 723, and a season tally that currently leads the entire club. "Duck of the Year 1959" already sits on his honours board, which rather suggests this is a title being defended, not merely chased. He also keeps rare company as one half of "Quacking Together" alongside club great John Ames. Next in was sibling H Clarke, bowled by J- Morrison for a second duck of the summer, bringing up 2 career ducks from just 9 innings and 115th of 723 — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from a first-ever knock long past. Together the Clarkes complete a rare "Two of a Quack" — sibling following sibling back to the pavilion in the same innings against Tovil, precisely the shared misfortune this club exists to honour. Fittingly, both share a footnote in history too: on this very date in 1925, W Turner also made a duck, against Bearsted CC no less. 🌱 Sunday — Folkestone CC (Away) Robert Wood departed for nought at number 8, bowled by Gerald Jeanes — his second duck of the season and fourth of a career now standing at 28 innings, 65th on the all-time roll and, like the Clarkes, a graduate of the Debutante Duck honours.
