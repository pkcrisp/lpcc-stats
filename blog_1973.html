🦆 Duck Report — 29 September 1973 (v Bexhill CC)

1 duck
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1973
A single fixture, a single duck — but a quality one, so let's not skimp on the ceremony. 1st XI Nigel Thirkell strode in at four and departed without troubling the scorers, caught Michael Waghorn off the bowling of N Bennett. It's his 5th duck of the summer and the 12th of a career now standing at 73 innings — enough to install him at 38th of 899 in the all-time table of ignominy. Nigel is no stranger to the honours boards either: a paid-up member of both the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and half of the exclusive Only Two Quacks arrangement with Jim Hall. The failing clearly runs in the blood — his parent-child kin John Thirkell has quietly amassed 81 career ducks of his own, with sibling Tim on 20 and Stuart contributing a solitary entry to keep the family name on the board. Fittingly, on this very day in 1957 Stan Wratten also walked back for nought against this same opposition, en route to 31 career ducks and 9th place all-time — a founding member of the Quacking Together society alongside John Ames. Some traditions, it seems, are worth repeating on the calendar as well as the scorecard. Week in Summary One match, one duck — a modest harvest by Duck Club standards, but every nought counts toward the ledger. Bexhill CC can consider themselves honorary contributors to the 1973 tally. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🏆 1. Colin Dicker — 7 ducks, 15 innings (46.67%) 2. Mike Iliffe — 6 ducks, 26 innings (23.08%) 3. Jon Harris — 6 ducks, 40 innings (15.00%) 4. Paul Brattle — 6 ducks, 41 innings (14.63%) 5. Nigel Thirkell — 5 ducks, 34 innings (14.71%) Colin Dicker sits in commanding form at nearly one duck in every two visits to the crease — truly the art of not scoring, perfected. 🦆 The season isn't done yet. Next fixture brings fresh opportunity for glory of the wrong kind — bats at the ready, and may fortune continue to desert the deserving. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 22-23 September 1973 🦆

0 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1973 – 23 Sep 1973
A rare and troubling weekend for the Duck Club faithful: two fixtures played, both grafted through without a single nought to show for it. Every bat that walked to the crease walked back having laid at least a claim to some runs, however modest. The zero club's doors stood open all weekend and, scandalously, nobody came knocking. 0️⃣ Ducks recorded: none. Not a golden one, not a pair, not so much as a nervy single stopping a king pair in its tracks. The hunting season carries on unblemished this week, which is either a triumph of application or simply everyone remembering, for once, to look at the ball. We shan't dwell on it. The greats did not become great overnight, and a quiet weekend is merely the séance before the storm. Somewhere out there a batsman is shouldering arms to one that's straightening back in, and we will hear about it in due course. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1973 1. Colin Dicker — 7 ducks in 15 innings (46.67%) — comfortably the season's most committed non-scorer, closing in on half his innings ending in a blob 2. Mike Iliffe — 6 ducks in 25 innings (24.0%) — quietly consistent in his inconsistency 3. Jon Harris — 6 ducks in 39 innings (15.38%) — proof that quantity of innings is no defence against quality of failure 4. Paul Brattle — 6 ducks in 40 innings (15.0%) — level with Harris on ducks, just working harder for the privilege 5. John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 14 innings (28.57%) — the nearly-man of the top five, and the one with the highest strike rate for going nowhere Colin Dicker sits well clear at the summit with a duck rate the rest of the club would call an affliction and we call an achievement. The chasing pack of Iliffe, Harris and Brattle remain locked together on six apiece, a proper scrap for the runner-up spot as the season winds toward its close. 🏏 Onward to the next fixtures, where normal service — a bat beaten all ends up, a leading edge ballooned to gully, an LBW shout upheld a fraction early — will surely resume. The Duck Club never sleeps for long. Get out there and give us something to write about. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 15-16 September 1973

4 ducks
Week 21 · 15 Sep 1973 – 16 Sep 1973
A quiet couple of days for the Linton willow but a productive one for the Club — four fresh entries to the ledger, and one very familiar name doing most of the damage. 🏏 Saturday 15th — Southborough CC (away) The 1st XI's trip to Southborough proved fertile hunting ground, yielding two additions inside a single innings. Mike Iliffe departed for nought, bowled by Richard Russell at number eight — his 6th duck of the season and 28th of an increasingly distinguished Linton career, good enough for 11th on the all-time list of 899. On this very day back in 1963, Norman Mattingley was doing much the same against Staplehurst CC, one of five ducks in a career running 1963-1965. Colin Dicker followed him back, bowled by Alan Honess at number ten, for his 7th duck of the season — taking him to 8 career ducks in a mere 17 innings, a strike rate for failure most of the club can only envy. Colin already carries the Duck of the Year 1973 honour, and with the leaderboard below confirming he's out in front by some distance, the rest of the field have their work cut out. He too keeps company with Norman Mattingley's 1963 vintage on this same date. 🏏 Sunday 16th — Mersham Hatch (home) Back on home soil, the ducks kept coming. Allan Cooper fell at number eight, bowled by F-H- Ames — his 4th of the season, 6th of his career, and 66th on the all-time roll. Allan is a three-time paid-up member of the exclusive Only Two Quacks fraternity, sharing duck-pairs history down the years with Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back. On this day in 1962, Richard Ashby was suffering identically against Staplehurst CC, on his way to 24 career ducks. Ron Hickman brought up the weekend's fourth and final duck, lbw to B. Campbell at number ten — his 3rd of the season, 22nd of his career, and a lofty 20th all-time. Ron's Runaway Duck honour still stands from a chastening 1965 season without troubling the scorers, and like Allan he finds historical fellowship with Richard Ashby's 1962 nought. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, and in both games it was the lower order — numbers 8 and 10 falling each time — doing the honours. The top order will be quietly relieved. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Colin Dicker — 7 ducks / 14 innings (50%) 2. Mike Iliffe — 6 ducks / 24 innings (25%) 3. Jon Harris — 6 ducks / 38 innings (15.79%) 4. Paul Brattle — 6 ducks / 38 innings (15.79%, tied with Jon) 5. John Thirkell — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.77%) Colin Dicker sits handsomely clear at the top, right on course for the Duck of the Year 1973 crown he already wears. Roll on next weekend — the exclusive zero club never stays quiet for long. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 8 to 9 September 1973

2 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1973 – 9 Sep 1973
Two fixtures out on the road that weekend, but it was the away day at Bexhill CC that did the damage, handing the 1st XI a brace of noughts and keeping the exclusive zero club nicely ticking over as the 1973 season heads for home. 1st XI — Bexhill CC (away) John Ames set the tone at first drop, lbw to Robert Saxby for a duck that's now his 1st of the season and 45th of an extraordinary career spanning 364 innings, good for 3rd on the all time list and one of the true grandmasters of the art of not scoring. Ames is a fixture of the Ducks in a Row board, four on the spin, a feat few would attempt on purpose, and turns up twice more in the Quacking Together registers alongside Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten, plus an Only Two Quacks entry shared with Alan Wilkes. On this very day back in 1961, John Crellin (1961 to 1965, 9 ducks, Duck of the Year that season) was performing the identical ceremony against St Lukes Maidstone CC. The calendar has a long memory. Two down the order, Nigel Thirkell fell to Saxby's second victim of the innings, caught behind by Michael Waghorn. It's his 4th duck of the season and 11th of a career built across 69 innings, 40th on the all time chart, with Homebody Duck, Tourist Duck and an Only Two Quacks pairing with Jim Hall all decorating his honours board. Ducking evidently runs in the family, father John Thirkell carries 81 career noughts of his own, brother Tim a further 20, and kinsman Stuart 1, and rather neatly John himself pops up down in the season charts below. Like Ames, Nigel shares his on this day duck with John Crellin's 1961 vintage, a matched pair 12 years and two very different Thirkells apart. Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one bowler doing all the damage. Robert Saxby claimed both scalps for Bexhill, an lbw and a catch behind, a fine afternoon's poultry farming. The other fixture escaped entirely unscathed. Quality over quantity this week, but the Duck of the Year race grinds on regardless. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 - Colin Dicker — 6 ducks in 13 innings (46.15%) - Paul Brattle — 6 ducks in 36 innings (16.67%) - Jon Harris — 6 ducks in 37 innings (16.22%) - Mike Iliffe — 5 ducks in 23 innings (21.74%) - John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) Colin Dicker leads on sheer percentage alone, though Brattle and Harris are matching him duck for duck further down the table. Plenty of season left yet for a late surge to the top of this most dubious of tables. Next up, another round of fixtures and no doubt further contributions to the cause. The zero club never sleeps, see you all next week for the inevitable additions. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 1-2 September 1973

5 ducks
Week 19 · 1 Sep 1973 – 2 Sep 1973
A vintage weekend for the exclusive zero club, with Linton's top order queueing up at Whitstable before honours resumed down at Staplehurst. Five ducks in two days is a proper harvest for early September — read on. Saturday 1 September — Whitstable CC (away) Openers are meant to set the tone, and Tim Thirkell did exactly that, trudging back lbw to John Robertson without troubling the shine. His 3rd duck of the season and 20th for the club sees him hold at 22nd on the all-time list of 899 — a list he already adorns as Duck of the Year 1972 and a fully paid-up Full House member, having now ducked at every position from 1 to 11. On this very day in 1968, Paul Bowles (1965–1973) was completing the identical ritual against Staplehurst on his way to 23 career ducks. The Thirkell name runs deep in club folklore too, with kin John (81 career ducks), Nigel (10) and Stuart (1) quietly building tallies of their own. 0️⃣ Alan Cradduck followed at six, bowled by S. Tritton for his 4th duck of the season and 15th of a career now 30th all-time — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own first-ever innings, and no stranger to the Ducks in a Row honour either. Paul Bowles, naturally, was doing the same thing on this day in 1968. Terry Donovan continued the procession at eight, lbw to Tritton for his 3rd of the season and 17th overall, 28th all-time — a Duck of the Year alumnus from 1964, and just one position shy of completing his own Full House. Same day, same Bowles, 1968. But the day belonged to Ian Brattle, who marked his first-ever senior innings for the club with a first-ball retreat — bowled by Tritton for career duck number one of one, a genuine Debutante Duck earned the hard way. Brother Paul Brattle is no stranger to the honours board either, sitting on 11 career ducks and currently propping up the season's top five. 🍩 Sunday 2 September — Staplehurst CC (away) Jon Harris made it five for the weekend, bowled by J Munn for his 6th duck of the season and 18th of an extensively decorated career, 26th all-time. Jon's honours board reads like a small library — Full House, Lone Duck top ten, Homebody and Tourist Duck top tens both, plus Quacking Together doubles alongside both Jim Hall and Trevor Back. Paul Bowles was at it again on this day in 1968, this time against Bearsted. Kin Richard Harris (36 career ducks) keeps it in the family. Five ducks, two matches, one thoroughly efficient top order. Jon's sixth of the season also cements him at 3rd in the current standings — right behind Ian's brother Paul. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Colin Dicker — 12 inns, 6 ducks (50.0%) 2. 🦆 Duck News — Week of 25 August 1973

4 ducks
Week 18 · 25 Aug 1973 – 29 Aug 1973
Four matches, four ducks, and a proper cluster of them from the Bearsted fixture. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. 🏏 1st XI Tuesday 26th, away to Mersham Hatch — Paul Brattle departed early, caught J. Smith off W. Jeanes, for his 5th duck of the season and 10th of his career from 80 innings, sitting 40th of 898 in the all-time roll. On this very day in 1962, LPCC great John Ames also strolled back for nought, against Sevenoaks Vine CC, on his way to a mighty 44 career ducks and 3rd place all-time. Brattle wasn't finished. Wednesday 27th, away to Bearsted CC, he opened the batting and was promptly bowled by Clive Britcher — duck number 6 of the season, 11th of his career, and a grim back-to-back pair to close the week. Fittingly, on this day in 1949 Richard Harris was doing exactly the same against Hunton Wanderers CC, en route to 36 career ducks and 6th on the all-time list. Brattle remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own first-innings blob years ago, and keeps distinguished company off the field too — his forebear Frank Brattle carries 38 career ducks, with Percy Brattle a further 16 behind him. Clearly a family with a gift for the art of not scoring. The same Bearsted innings claimed a second victim. Batting at 10, Colin Dicker was bowled by Fred Ovey without troubling the scorers — his 6th duck of the season, drawing him level with Brattle at the top of the pile, and his 7th career duck from a mere 15 innings, a startling strike rate that has already earned him the Duck of the Year 1973 crown. Rank 62 of 898 and rising fast. Like Brattle's second effort, this one echoes Richard Harris's Hunton Wanderers duck of 1949 — two Linton men, 24 years apart, both undone on the same August afternoon in history. 🌳 Midweek XI Thursday 29th, at home to Gravesend CC, Mike Iliffe was run out — the fielder unconfirmed but the outcome painfully clear — for his 5th duck of the season. It's his 27th career duck from 171 innings, keeping him a lofty 12th of 898 on the all-time list, a testament to a very particular kind of consistency at the crease. On this day back in 1954, Stan Wratten made his own duck against D Hambrook XI, one of 31 he'd amass across a three-decade Linton career. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from four matches, three of them clustered into a single grim Wednesday — Bearsted CC clearly fancied their chances with the ball. Brattle's pair was the week's headline abjection, but Dicker's economy of innings deserves its own round of applause. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Colin Dicker — 12 innings, 6 ducks, 50.0% 2. Paul Brattle — 32 innings, 6 ducks, 18.75% 3. Mike Iliffe — 22 innings, 5 ducks, 22.73% 4. Jon Harris — 34 innings, 5 ducks, 14.71% 5. John Thirkell — 10 innings, 4 ducks, 40.0% Dicker and Brattle share top spot on ducks but couldn't be further apart on rate — triumph through adversity, wherever you sit on that table. The season's not done yet, and there's still time for the standings to turn. See you all at the next fixture — the ducks certainly will be.

🦆 Duck News — Week of 18–22 August 1973

4 ducks
Week 17 · 18 Aug 1973 – 22 Aug 1973
A short week and a sharp one — just three fixtures, but the exclusive zero club still found time to induct four new members, all of them, as it happens, wearing 1st XI colours. 🏏 1st XI — Bobbing Court CC (Saturday 18th) A miserable middle-and-lower order for the 1sts saw three ducks fall in a single innings. Paul Brattle went first, bowled by Richard Witts, his 4th duck of the season and 9th of an increasingly seasoned career — 76 innings in, rank 50 of 897, and level company with B Westover, David Gale, Gerald Wright and John Crellin on the all-time ledger. He's long been a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, and the art of not scoring evidently runs in the blood — father Frank Brattle carries 38 career ducks, kinsman Percy Brattle another 16. Splendidly, on this very date in 1963, club legend John Thirkell was doing exactly the same thing against The Mote CC. Next man in fared no better. Nigel Thirkell was caught behind off Roger Mills for his 3rd of the season and 10th career duck in 64 innings, good for 44th all-time and a fixture on both the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, plus an Only Two Quacks pairing with Jim Hall. His parallel is a family affair with extra spice — that very 1963 nought belonged to none other than his own father, John Thirkell, still the club's all-time duck monarch on 81. Brother Tim, on 19 career ducks, keeps the tradition thoroughly alive. Last man Colin Dicker completed the collapse, caught off Roger Mills, for his 5th duck of the season — a remarkable return from just 14 career innings, taking him to 6 overall and 66th on the all-time list. More pressingly, the board reads Duck of the Year 1973, and with the season numbers below, that title looks increasingly like his to lose. He too found an echo of John Thirkell's 1963 nought waiting for him down the years. 🏏 1st XI — The Mote CC (Sunday 19th) Mike Iliffe carried the family name for solitary suffering the next day, bowled by Phil Briant for his 4th duck of the season and 26th of a genuinely formidable career — 169 innings, and a top-twelve all-time placing at 12 of 897 that few will ever trouble. History rhymed neatly here too: on this same date in 1956, John --'s Taylor made a duck in this very fixture against Mote A, one of 15 in a career running 1949 to 1960. The third fixture of the week, mercifully for someone, proved a vegan affair entirely free of fowl. 📋 Week in brief Four ducks from three matches, and a rather eerie thread running through them — three of this week's four duckers each found their historical twin in club legend John Thirkell's own nought, struck on this exact date a decade earlier in 1963. For one of them it wasn't just a coincidence but the family record book. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Colin Dicker — 5 ducks in 11 innings (45.45%) - Jon Harris — 5 ducks in 31 innings (16.13%) - John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 10 innings (40.0%) - Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks in 20 innings (20.0%) - Paul Brattle — 4 ducks in 28 innings (14.29%) Colin Dicker sits proudly atop the pile, but with Jon Harris level on ducks and John Thirkell posting an eye-watering 40% strike rate in limited innings, the Duck of the Year race is far from settled. Roll on next weekend — the ducks, as ever, will be waiting. 🦆🍩0️⃣

Quite the calendar company to keep. Brian's sibling David Piper is no stranger to these pages either, with 4 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring may run in the blood. At the other end of the order, last man Tony Gauna fell b Ian Fordham for his second duck of the season, moving to 5 career ducks from 33 innings and 86th of 895 all time. Remarkably, the same date-in-history quirk applies to Tony as it did to Brian — 1971, Sibton Park CC, John Thirkell strolling back for nought. Two Midweek batsmen, numbers 1 and 11 on the card, both keeping company with the same great man on the same calendar day, 52 years apart. Consistency of a very particular kind. 📅 Elsewhere The second fixture of the period proved a rather more abstemious affair for the Duck Club — no additions to report, the ducks evading capture entirely. Can't win them all, or rather, can't lose them all. A quiet week in the counting house, then: 2 ducks from 2 matches, both arriving in the same innings, at opposite ends of the batting order, on the same page of the history books. The art of not scoring, alive and well. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 1. John Thirkell — 4 ducks from 8 innings (50%) 2. Jon Harris — 4 ducks from 26 innings (15.38%) 3. The rest of the chasing pack will need to find some rather more committed failure if they're to catch him before the season's out. 🦆 Same time next fixture, ladies and gentlemen — the ducks are out there, and Linton Park will be doing its level best to find them for you. Pads on, bats up, and may fortune desert you gloriously.

🦆 Duck News — 28–29 July 1973

3 ducks
Week 14 · 28 Jul 1973 – 29 Jul 1973
Two fixtures, three ducks, and the exclusive zero club took in a couple of new deposits and one very familiar face indeed. Saturday 28th July — away at Sibton Park CC The middle order took a battering at Sibton Park, with Rawlings doing the damage twice in short order. Colin Dicker went first, caught by Tim Wren off David Rawlings at number eight, for his third duck of the season and a career tally that now stands at 4 in just 12 innings — a strike rate for nought that keeps him comfortably in the current Duck of the Year 1973 conversation. Week 3 · 12 May 1973 – 14 May 1973
Three matches, four ducks, and one appearance from bona fide cricketing royalty. The 1973 season continues to deliver handsomely for connoisseurs of the naught. 🏏 Saturday 12th — 1st XI away at Rumwood CC Brian Fuller, walking to the crease at number 11 with little left to lose, was undone by S- Simmonds and departed for his 1st duck of the season. It's his 7th for the club from 29 innings, putting him 59th of 884 in the all-time table. On this very day back in 1906, D H Luck was doing precisely the same thing against Town Malling CC — some traditions refuse to die. 🦆 Sunday 13th — NVKC at home to Harvel CC Hold the front page. John Thirkell, caught by Hanks off J- Howe, has added to what is already an unassailable all-time collection — his 78th career duck from 710 innings, still comfortably 1st of 884 in the club's ignominious history books. Five-time Duck of the Year (1952, '54, '55, '67 and '71), permanent resident of the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens, and a man who has turned ducking-together into an art form alongside both Bob Arnold and William Buck. This is only his 1st of the current season — the King rests, briefly, before no doubt reigning again. A chip off the old block runs in the family too, with sons Tim (17 career ducks) and Nigel (7) carrying the tradition forward. On this day in 1961, William Agate — on debut, no less — was enjoying his own small taste of the same fate against Charles Arkcoll. 🏏 Monday 14th — 1st XI at home to Boughton Malherbe CC A costly middle-order collapse of the scoring variety. Ken Webb, caught and bowled by X Pryce at number 2, picked up his 1st career duck, from just 2 innings so far — early days, but the sample size is not encouraging — now 331st on the all-time list. Moments later Allan Cooper followed, stumped off X Morley for his own 1st duck of the season and 3rd career duck in 36 innings, good enough for 109th all time and a proud paid-up member of the Only Two Quacks club alongside Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back. Jon arrives with credentials most duckers only dream of: a fully paid-up Full House man (a duck at every position, one to eleven — a feat of thoroughness), regular resident of the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, no stranger to home comforts either courtesy of a Homebody Duck placing, and co-owner of not one but two Quacking Together entries, alongside Jim Hall and Trevor Back, plus an Only Two Quacks partnership with Allan Cooper.
