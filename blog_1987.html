🦆 Duck Report — 26–30 September 1987 🦆

5 ducks
Week 23 · 26 Sep 1987 – 30 Sep 1987
Five fresh entries to the exclusive zero club this week, every one of them courtesy of the 1st XI across two testing fixtures. A properly sodden week for the top order. 🏏 1st XI Saturday at Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC brought early sorrow for Peter Whyte, lbw Keith Turk for nought at number four — his 1st duck of the season and 1st of his career from 18 innings, good (or bad) enough for 245th on the all-time list. Roy Clarke sits on that same landmark tally, having made his own duck against Linton Park CC on this very date back in 1965. Whyte wasn't finished. Opening the very next day against Lord Cornwallis XI, he fell lbw to S Ross without scoring — his 2nd duck of the season and career, completing a grim weekend pair. Consistency of a very particular kind. Robert Murray was doing exactly the same thing on this date in 1964. S Ross struck again in that innings, removing Adrian Simmonds caught by M Estorick for nought at six — his 2nd duck of the season, 3rd of his career from just 11 innings, 146th on the all-time list and level with Robert Murray for the date. Adrian already carries the Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack honours from earlier chapters of his Linton story. At eight, Matt Large fell caught Jefferson bowled S Ross for his 6th duck of the season and 13th of his career, 48th on the all-time list and matching Derek Pantony (1947–1963) and Sean Cook (1982–1987). Matt reigns as this season's Duck of the Year and holds Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top-ten status besides — the complete package, and triumph through adversity personified. Rounding things off, Nigel Thirkell was caught Jefferson bowled Dilip Doshi at nine for his 1st duck of the season but a colossal 39th career duck from 604 innings — 10th on the all-time list. He hails from proper Duck Club stock: father John on 90 career ducks, brothers Tim and Simon on 59 and 18, relative Stuart on 1. Robert Murray keeps him company on the date front too. 📊 The Week Five ducks in three matches, all in the 1st XI, with S Ross claiming three of the five himself. The top order will want a word with itself before the season's out. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 6 ducks, 22 innings (27%) 2. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks, 8 innings (38%) 3. Stewart Morrison — 3 ducks, 8 innings (38%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks, 20 innings (15%) 5. David Castle — 3 ducks, 46 innings (7%) Next fixture awaits — plenty of time yet for one more name to join the club. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 19–20 September 1987

3 ducks
Week 22 · 19 Sep 1987 – 20 Sep 1987
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, but it was the 1st XI's meeting with Anchorians CC that did all the damage for the exclusive zero club. One bowler, Don Wykes, claimed all three ducks in the same innings, with S Grubb doing the honest work of pouching two of them behind the stumps. The other fixture, disappointingly, stayed entirely vegan. 🏏 1st XI vs Anchorians CC (home) First to go was Simon Thirkell, bowled Wykes at number four, for his 1st duck of the season and 18th of his LPCC career — good enough for 38th on the all-time list of 1,093. Simon already sits on the 50 Before a 50 board, a top score of 48 his personal best, alongside a Duck of the Year 1983 credit and spots in the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens. On this very day back in 1959, Eddie Lawrence (1956–1967) was making his own duck against Tilling Steven — some things never change. Next in, and next out, was his brother Tim Thirkell, caught Grubb bowled Wykes at five, for his 3rd duck of the season and 59th of an extraordinary career that sits him 4th on the all-time roll of 1,093 — with a Full House, a duck at every position from one to eleven, already safely banked. Tim's third of the campaign also lifts him to 4th on the current season standings. Eddie Lawrence keeps him company too, obliging with a duck on this same date in 1959. And with both Thirkell boys back in the pavilion inside the same innings, the honours board records a proper Two of a Quack — siblings, both undone without scoring, a feat the family name wears well given patriarch John Thirkell's all-time-leading 90 career ducks and brother Nigel's quiet 38 in the background. Completing Wykes' clean sweep was Ian McKechnie, caught Grubb at number nine, for his 1st LPCC duck in just 3 career innings — 378th on the all-time list of 1,093. Ian already carries the Retirement Duck honour from some past swansong, though today's effort is very much business as usual. Eddie Lawrence, 1959, rounds off his hat-trick of company for the day. 📋 The Week in Brief Three ducks, one match, one bowler responsible for the lot — Don Wykes can retire the ball on this one. Fittingly, all three fell to the identical growth curve, with 1959 echoing back at every one of them. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 5 ducks in 21 innings (23.81%) 2. Stewart Morrison — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.86%) 3. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks in 8 innings (37.5%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 3 ducks in 18 innings (16.67%) 5. David Castle — 3 ducks in 43 innings (6.98%)

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 5th to 9th September 1987 🦆

0 ducks
Week 20 · 5 Sep 1987 – 9 Sep 1987
A quiet week in the exclusive zero club, ducklings. Three fixtures came and went across the period and, remarkably, not a single Linton bat found its way back to the pavilion for nought. The Duck escaped the net entirely. Whether this represents a sudden and alarming outbreak of competence, or simply everyone having an unusually good week with the willow, the correspondent cannot say. Either way, the honours board gathers dust for once, no fresh names troubled the Debutante Duck club, no pairs, no kings, and certainly no Family of Ducks ceremonies to report. The scorers, frankly, have had it easy. Do not mistake this for the Duck Club closing its doors, however. A blank week is merely the calm before the next collapse — as any student of the art of not scoring knows, form is temporary, but the zero is eternal. Somewhere out there a golden duck is being quietly, patiently prepared for someone. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1987 1. Matt Large — 5 ducks from 20 innings (25%) 2. Stewart Morrison — 3 ducks from 7 innings (a startling 42.86%) 3. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks from 8 innings (37.5%) 4. David Castle — 3 ducks from 41 innings, the model of grim consistency at 7.32% 5. Steve Cheale — 2 ducks from just 2 innings, a flawless 100% strike rate for those keeping score Stewart Morrison and Ian Brattle remain locked in a fine arm-wrestle for second, while Steve Cheale's perfect record continues to loom over proceedings like a man who has simply decided batting isn't for him. Matt Large sits proudly atop the pile — a genuine contender for Duck of the Year 1987 if this rate of attrition keeps up. 📅 Onwards to the next fixture, where normal service — one hopes — will resume. The ducks are out there somewhere, biding their time. Pad up, and good luck to all. 🦆 0️⃣ 🏏

🦆 Duck Report — 29-31 August 1987 🦆

5 ducks
Week 19 · 29 Aug 1987 – 31 Aug 1987
A vintage bank holiday weekend for the exclusive zero club, with the 1st XI shouldering the entire burden — five ducks across three matches, and not a single other team troubling the honours board. Consistency of a very particular kind. Saturday 29th — v Yalding CC (h) Paul Gibson opened the innings and the scoring stayed shut, bowled by Alan Snowden for his 2nd duck of the season and 11th of a 122-innings career — 58th on the all-time list of 1,093. On this very day in 1954, Stan Wratten (1943–1984, 35 ducks, a member of Quacking Together with John Ames) was doing exactly the same thing against D Hambrook XI. Some traditions simply refuse to die. Sunday 30th — v Brighton & Hove CC (a) A proper cluster at the seaside. Brian Piper went lbw to Tony Payne at four, his 15th career duck from 238 innings, rank 42 overall — a chip off the old block, with sibling David Piper sat on 7 of his own. Frank Brattle (1937–1971, 38 ducks) was last seen doing likewise on this date back in 1965. Then A Hansford got to work and simply wouldn't stop. Huckstep, on senior debut, fell for nought at number 8 — an instant and complete Duck Club career, opening the account with Debutante Duck, One and Done (1987), and a presumptive Retirement Duck all in a single ball. A baptism the honours board will remember fondly even if the scorebook won't. One place later Alan Boyns joined the same bowler's tally, his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of a 26-innings career, rank 120 — the same fixture, the same wrecking ball, the same fate as Huckstep, both keeping Frank Brattle's 1965 company. Monday 31st — v Bearsted CC (h) The headline act. Matt Large, caught and bowled by Steve Dudhill off just two balls at six, brings up his 5th duck of the season — and with it, retains top spot in the current Duck Hunting Season standings. His 12th career duck from 49 innings puts him level with Bert Buckoke (1928–1953), Jack Fryer (1881–1906) and Len Conway (1954–1965), rank 52 of 1,093. The board is groaning under the weight of his season alone: Duck of the Year 1987 confirmed live, plus entries in Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck's respective top tens, and agonisingly one position shy of the full set at Full House nearly-man. Back in 1980 Allan Cooper — a veteran of three separate Only Two Quacks pairings — was doing his own version of this against Staplehurst. Week in brief Five ducks, three matches, one long-suffering XI. Brighton & Hove alone conceded three of them, with A Hansford claiming a fine brace. Not a bad advert for the art of not scoring. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 20 inns, 5 ducks (25%) 2. Stewart Morrison — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.86%) 3. Ian Brattle — 8 inns, 3 ducks (37.5%) 4. David Castle — 39 inns, 3 ducks (7.69%) 5. Steve Cheale — 2 inns, 2 ducks (100%) Matt Large sits pretty at the top for now, but the season's not done yet. Next fixture, someone's number is surely up — see you at the crease. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 15-19 August 1987

2 ducks
Week 17 · 15 Aug 1987 – 19 Aug 1987
Three matches out on the paddock this week and the Duck Club harvested a modest but perfectly formed brace, both arriving in the same innings against Whitstable CC. Slim pickings, but quality over quantity, as ever. 🏏 1st XI v Whitstable CC (16 August, home) Trevor Back departed at number five, caught Andy Robertson bowled Duncan Hardy, for his 2nd duck of the season and 28th of an increasingly distinguished career - good enough for 22nd on the all-time list of 1092. Trevor arrives with a well-stocked mantelpiece already: top ten finishes in both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck charts (rare to manage both), a place in the exclusive Lone Duck top ten, and shared honours in Only Two Quacks with Allan Cooper and Quacking Together with Jon Harris. On this very day in 1953, John Ames - he of 49 career ducks and a name permanently etched into the Ducks in a Row and Homebody Duck honours boards - was doing much the same thing against Mote A. Some traditions never die. One place down the order, David Iliffe followed him back to the pavilion, caught I Hadfield bowled Duncan Hardy, for his own 2nd of the season and 31st career duck, edging him to 17th all-time. David is a paid-up Duck of the Year alumnus from 1982, a season that also saw him inducted into the rather more unusual Quacked by Kin board, dismissed by relative Mike Iliffe - himself no stranger to nought, with 43 career ducks of his own. And, extraordinarily, the very same 16th of August 1953 also claimed John Ames against Mote A - so David finds himself keeping historical company with Trevor this week, both linked to a duck laid down 34 years earlier almost to the day. 📅 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches, both from the same top order, in the same over of misfortune against Whitstable. The other two fixtures of the week passed off suspiciously duck-free - either good batting or good luck, we make no judgement. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large - 4 ducks from 16 innings (25%) 2. Ian Brattle - 3 ducks from 7 innings (42.86%) 3. David Castle - 3 ducks from 38 innings (7.89%) 4. Steve Cheale - 2 ducks from 2 innings (100%) 5. David Iliffe - 2 ducks from 3 innings (66.67%) David climbs straight into the top five off the back of this week's effort - one more and he'll be giving Ian Brattle something to think about. As ever, the season rolls on and the Duck Club awaits its next intake with open arms. Get out there, get bowled, and give us something to write about next week. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 8-9 August 1987

4 ducks
Week 16 · 8 Aug 1987 – 9 Aug 1987
Only two of the three fixtures troubled the scorers this weekend, but between Headcorn and Benenden the exclusive zero club took in four fresh members with the usual mix of ceremony and consolation. Saturday 8th August — 1st XI v Headcorn CC Matt Large departed at number 10, caught Alastair Lee off John Phipps, for his 4th duck of the season and 11th of a growing career — 44 innings in, now 56th in the all-time table. That's four in 1987 already, and with the season standings showing him out in front, Matt is the man currently wearing the (thoroughly unwanted) Duck of the Year crown. He remains a nearly-man on the Full House board, one lonely position short of having ducked everywhere from 1 to 11 — the completists among us live in hope. On this very day back in 1979, Paul Brattle made a duck of his own against Maidstone police, so Matt keeps good historical company. Following him back was Jon Harris, bowled John Phipps at number 11 for his 1st duck of the season but a startling 62nd of an illustrious career — 434 innings and 3rd on the all-time chart, a rung below immortality. Jon is a fully paid-up Full House member, having ducked at every position the order offers, and carries a small constellation of shared-quack honours with Allan Cooper, Jim Hall and Trevor Back for company. A chip off a duck-prone block too — his relative Richard Harris carries 36 career noughts of his own. Jon shares Saturday's on-this-day parallel with Matt: Paul Brattle, Maidstone police, 1979. Sunday 9th August — 1st XI v Benenden CC Bob Lines went for nought at first drop, bowled D Marshall, his 2nd duck of the season and 5th career, 100 innings into his Linton life and 108th in the all-time reckoning. His relative David Lines is himself on 4 career ducks — clearly a family with an eye for the art of not scoring. On this day in 1964, John Thirkell — the club's all-time duck emperor on 90 — made a nought of his own against Harrietsham CC. And speaking of the Thirkell dynasty — Tim Thirkell was run out for nought at number 5, his 2nd duck of the season and 58th career, 480 innings deep and sitting proudly 4th in the all-time standings. Tim is another Full House graduate and keeps a Jim Hall pairing on his honours shelf besides. The duck ducks don't fall far from the tree here: father John Thirkell (90, the all-time record holder), siblings Nigel (38) and Simon (17), and cousin Stuart (1) round out a genuinely fearsome family collection. Fittingly, Tim's own on-this-day parallel is his father — John Thirkell's 1964 duck against Harrietsham CC, made on this very date. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, both falling to Linton's top order and tail in roughly equal measure — Headcorn claimed a pair via John Phipps' opening burst, Benenden helped themselves to one bowled and one run out for good measure. The 3rd fixture in the period stayed refreshingly, or disappointingly, fowl-free. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 15 inns, 4 ducks (26.67%) 2. Ian Brattle — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.86%) 3. David Castle — 35 inns, 3 ducks (8.57%) 4. Steve Cheale — 2 inns, 2 ducks (100.0%) 5. Stewart Morrison — 3 inns, 2 ducks (66.67%) Matt Large leads the charge, but with Ian Brattle and Steve Cheale both maintaining eye-watering duck rates, the race for 1987's crown is very much alive. Next weekend's fixtures await — same crease, same bowlers, same beautifully cruel game. See you there 🏏0️⃣

Five years almost to the day since that 1982 vintage against the very same opposition. History does not so much repeat as heckle. His 27 career ducks keep him serenely at 24th all-time, comfortably installed on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. Elsewhere on this day in 1940, one J Short was quietly doing his own bit for the cause against Boughton Monchelsea CC. 🏏 2nd XI v Newchapel and Horne (a) Stewart Morrison, lbw to Bennett, chalked up his 2nd duck of the season and, remarkably, his 2nd career duck — in only 3 innings, ranking 257th all-time. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his actual debut, and a "Full House nearly-man" with ducks logged at 10 of 11 batting positions, he's now firmly on this season's watch-list. Stewart Morrison — 2 ducks / 3 inns (66.67%) Steve Cheale still holds a flawless strike rate for futility. Can anyone catch him before season's end? 🍩 See you all at the next fixture — the ducks never sleep, and neither, apparently, does The Mote CC.

🦆 Duck News — 11 to 15 July 1987

2 ducks
Week 12 · 11 Jul 1987 – 15 Jul 1987
A slender harvest this week, but the exclusive zero club never turns away new members, and one arrived in some style. 🏏 1st XI — Two for the Ledger Saturday's trip to Morden Village CC brought the club a fresh face on the honours board. Batting at five, Simon Gibson departed bowled by I Russell to register not merely his first duck of the season but his first duck, full stop, in senior colours. A genuine baptism — Simon steps straight into the Debutante Duck fraternity, and with career_ducks and career_innings both reading 1, he's technically also a paid-up member of One and Done and even carries a place on the Retirement Duck board, a statistical curiosity for a man precisely one innings into his Linton life rather than any hint he's off anywhere.
