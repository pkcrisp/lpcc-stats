🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1989 · 1989 — 2 matches

Sun 01 Oct 1989 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformDDDWW

Here's the report:

Sunday saw us at home to face our visitors, and having called correctly at the toss, I didn't need asking twice before electing to bat first on what looked a decent surface. We were rewarded handsomely: 173 for 6 from our 25 overs, built around a lovely third-wicket stand of 58 between Tim Thirkell, whose 48 was the innings' backbone, and myself, chipping in with 37. Bob Lines finished unbeaten on 27 to give the total real heft, while extras chipped in a generous 30 of their own — the sort of contribution that never gets a commemorative mug but is always gratefully received. Poor Allan Cooper, alas, will want the scorebook to record rather less about his contribution, departing for a golden duck; these things happen to the best of us, and no doubt he'll make amends next time out.

There was a genuinely special moment amid the runs, too: Tim Thirkell brought up 7,000 career runs for the club, a landmark that deserved rather better support from the rest of us on the day than we ultimately gave it. Nigel Thirkell now sits second in the 1989 1st XI run charts with 880, Tim leads the Married run charts, and David Castle tops the Singles table — form lines worth watching as the season matures.

Sadly, 173 proved nowhere near enough, and we were beaten by 8 wickets, our bowling and fielding unable to defend a total that had promised so much. Not our day, then — heads up, and back to the nets we go.

Sat 30 Sep 1989 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformDWLDL

Saturday last, under skies that couldn't quite decide their intentions, we welcomed Hastings & St Leonards Priory to Linton Park. I won the toss and, ever the optimist about our attack, stuck them in.

It looked a shrewd move for a while — until B. Phillips settled in for a proper fifty, 51 of the good, old-fashioned variety, with Tim Booth-Jones (36) and I. Phillips (25) chipping in either side of him. A stand of 53 for the sixth wicket did the real damage to our afternoon, dragging them along nicely before Jon Harris, bowling with real zip, mopped up for 4-40. I managed to find a bit of rhythm myself, taking 4-48 with four maidens thrown in for good measure — not that I'd ever say so twice. They closed on 184/8 from their 49.1 overs, a target that felt gettable and faintly ominous in equal measure.

Our reply had its moments of both comfort and cardiac stress, settling on 175/7 from the full fifty. I found something approaching form with the bat, 95 runs' worth of it, though the rest of the order will insist I hogged the strike. We simply couldn't close the gap in the overs left, and honours were shared.

A draw, then — the great English compromise. With David Castle still ploughing on at the top of our run charts and Jon Harris lurking second among the wicket-takers, the season ticks along nicely. Not a win, but not a defeat either, and on a day like this, I'll happily settle for honest toil and tea in the pavilion.

🗓️ 23 Sep – 29 Sep 1989 · 1989 — 2 matches

Wed 27 Sep 1989 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDDWLW

Wednesday 27 September saw us head down to Penshurst Park, and winning the toss I took one look at the pitch and stuck us in — a decision that, for once, I didn't spend the rest of the day regretting. Andrew Hales set the tone with a fine 53, and when Piers McDonald joined him the pair put on 97 for the second wicket, the kind of stand that makes a captain feel unreasonably pleased with himself for a toss he had nothing to do with winning. McDonald's 48 and Nigel Thirkell's 43 kept the scoreboard ticking, and we closed on a healthy 219/5 from our 42 overs.

Defending it, we had Penshurst in real trouble at one stage, only for I Mitchell to play the innings of the day — 95 of pure defiance — with M Latter's well-made 52 alongside him worth 86 for the sixth wicket. Just when we thought we had them, they wobbled from 204/6 to 211/9 under pressure, with three poor souls collecting golden ducks along the way. Alan Boyns was the pick of the attack, miserly and menacing in equal measure with 3-37 and four maidens, backed up nicely by Martin Larkin and Nigel Thirkell, both 2-19. I chipped in with a wicket or two myself, which happened to bring up 1,600 for my career — nice timing, even if I say so myself. Nigel also passed 21,000 club runs, a landmark worth more applause than the tea-time sandwiches got.

In the end neither side could quite finish the job, and honours were shared. A draw that felt, on the balance of the day's cricket, entirely fair — and rather more entertaining than the scorecard alone lets on.

Sun 24 Sep 1989 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWDWLD

From the captain's chair — Sunday 24 September 1989. We hosted Anchorians CC, and having called correctly at the toss, I had no hesitation in asking the lads to bat first. It felt the right call at the time, and for a good while it looked a very right call indeed.

Trevor Back set about the Anchorians attack with real purpose, finishing unbeaten on a fine 73, and found the perfect foil in David Castle, whose 61 was every bit as handsome. Their third-wicket stand added a crucial 71 and did the heavy lifting of the innings. Elsewhere the card wasn't so kind — poor Piers McDonald departed for a duck — but 185/6 from our 41 overs looked a defendable total on a good pitch.

Tappin's 47 the backbone, well supported by Roberts and Grubb, and a third-wicket stand of 53 threatening to take the match away from us. I'll confess to a quiet satisfaction with my own 3-31, backed superbly by Alan Boyns' miserly 2-29 with three maidens, and Nick Froom's 1-28 — though poor Peter Whyte had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 15 byes going astray.

Beaten by a single wicket in the end — cruel arithmetic for an afternoon that deserved better. We'll be all the better for it next week.

🗓️ 16 Sep – 22 Sep 1989 · 1989 — 1 match

Sun 17 Sep 1989 🧢 1st XIv NS Northend Sunday · HomeformWWDWL

Sunday 17 September, and Northend Sunday made the trip to face us — perfect weather for it, and I fancy the coin knew that, because it fell my way and I had no hesitation in sticking us in.

Batting first has a habit of exposing a captain's judgement, but the lads made it look like the easiest decision I've ever taken. We racked up 208 for 3 off 42.3 overs, built around a second-wicket stand of 162 that rather took the game away from Northend before tea was even a thought. I'll allow myself one boast for the scrapbook — I made it to a hundred, and there are few better feelings than raising the bat to your own side's applause. Trevor Back was every bit my equal in support, cracking a fine 78 and giving the innings the sort of backbone that turns a good total into an imposing one.

With 208 to defend, the ball did its job without quite finishing it. Northend crept to 143 for 7 off their 46 overs, W Creed the chief obstacle with a well-crafted 67 before P Dann saw them home unbeaten on 20. Dave Todd was miserly and menacing in equal measure — 2 for 35 with five maidens, the kind of spell that strangles an innings rather than blows it away — while Stewart Morrison chipped in with 2 for 50 and Ian Brattle 1 for 15.

In the end neither side could force the door open, and honours were shared. A draw feels almost a waste of a century, but cricket rarely asks our permission — I'll take the runs, the sunshine, and the handshakes, and call it a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work.

Wed 13 Sep 1989 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · HomeformWDDWL

We beat The Mote CC by 5 wickets in a run chase on a September Wednesday.

We lost the toss — never a good omen — and were sent out into the field, where The Mote set about compiling a stiff-looking 185/7 from their 45 overs. Mike Cutler was the chief architect with a well-crafted 44, with Dave McInnes (33) and Nigel Mullarkey (33 not out) lending sturdy support, the pair putting on 55 for the 7th wicket just when we thought we had them contained. Credit to the bowlers, though — Alan Boyns led from the front with 2-33, Trevor Back and Ian Barker chipped in a wicket apiece, and Pete Westrop, tight as ever, squeezed out two maidens for good measure.

185 is never a formality on a Mote pitch, but Paul Gibson had other ideas. He and David Castle put on 79 for the second wicket to set the platform, then Gibson simply refused to get out, finishing unbeaten on a magnificent 85. Castle's 51 and Trevor Back's brisk 41 filled in the rest of the picture as we cantered home on 186/2 with overs to spare — five wickets in hand and, frankly, more comfort than the scoreline suggests. A tidy footnote for the scorebook: Gibson's innings nudges him up to 14th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, past Malcolm Pay, while Castle continues to sit 3rd in this season's Midweek run charts and Nigel Morton 2nd among the wicket-takers.

All told, a thoroughly good day out — professional in the field, ruthless with the bat, and home in time for tea.

Sun 03 Sep 1989 🧢 Tour XIv BC Beacon CC, Cornwall · AwayformWDWWW

Sunday, the third of September, and the coach rattled us down to Beacon CC in Cornwall for what turned into a proper cricketing education. Won the toss, and with the sun out and no earthly reason to do otherwise, I put us in — a decision I stand by, whatever came after.

Nigel Thirkell batted like a man who'd had a good breakfast, carrying us to 81 and giving our 168 for 9 some genuine backbone. The rest of the order rather let the side down on that front — Peter Whyte, Mark Stevenson and Mike Bains all contributed the traditional golden duck, which is not a hat-trick anyone wants their name attached to.

With the ball, we huffed and puffed for 37.3 overs while Larry Martin (52) and Clive Thomas (51) put on a stand of 102 for the second wicket that rather took the game away from us, Kevin Thomas seeing them home unbeaten on 26. Bryn Martin and Ralph Williams at least fell without scoring, small mercies. Thirkell was our pick again with the ball, 1 for 15, and I chipped in with 1 for 18 and a couple of maidens for good measure, David Castle picking up 1 for 41. Poor Peter Whyte had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 15 byes escaping his gloves like eels.

Beaten by five wickets in the end, no disgrace in that against a side batting so well. Thirkell continues to pile up the runs for the season and I'm quietly rather pleased with how the wickets column is shaping up. We'll regroup, we'll go again — that's village cricket for you.

Nigel Thirkell's Match Report, 13 August 1989

Skipper's Log — Sunday 30 April 1989. Sheldwich CC were the visitors, and I won the toss and elected to bat, which for once turned out to be the right call rather than the customary act of self-sabotage.

It didn't look it early on, mind. We were 4-1 before some of the lads had finished their tea, and things unravelled further to 12-4 in a rush that had me wondering whether I'd sent the wrong XI out. But cricket has a way of rewarding patience, and a fine stand of 71 for the fifth wicket dragged us back into respectability. Malcolm Pay top-scored with a splendid 46, I chipped in with 29, and Piers McDonald finished unbeaten on 26 to see us home to 128/7 from our 40.5 overs. Paul Bowles and Peter Whyte both fell without troubling the scorers — the sort of ducks best filed under "for the good of the team" and never mentioned again.

With the ball, we were altogether more clinical. Sheldwich slipped to 4-1 themselves and never recovered, bowled out for 96 despite a lively 29 from Jon Shields and 22 from A Creed. Matt Large was superb, ripping through with 3-8, while Alan Boyns bowled with real miserliness — four maidens and 3-25 — and Paul Bowles chimed in with 2-34, en route to a rather splendid personal landmark: 1100 career wickets.

We got over the line by 32 runs. A scratchy start, a proper finish, and a very good day for the club indeed.
