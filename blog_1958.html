Week 23 · 27 Sep 1958 – 28 Sep 1958
27–28 September 1958 Nine ducks from two fixtures as September draws to a close — the zero club signs off the 1958 season in vintage, if scoreless, style. Saturday — 1st XI v A. Trembeths (home) - Terry Whittaker — b J- Mortlock. Season duck #1, and his maiden career nought too (1 in 10 innings, 296th all-time). A lifetime member of Quacking Together alongside Brian Relf — who, funnily enough, turns up further down this report, a day and an opposition apart. Also a Retirement Duck honouree from a past farewell; no danger of that rumour today. - Tony Snell — b v- Chance. Season duck #5, 13th career (44 innings) — a rarefied 9th all-time. Already sporting Ducks in a Row (four on the spin), and now crowned with this year's own Runaway Duck honour: 1958, a whole season without troubling the scorer. On this same date in 1953, Tony Tipples was doing precisely the same thing. Sunday — three LPCC sides entertained Linton Park CC The Unknown XI came off worst, shedding five wickets for nought to the home attack of Alan Wilkes (3) and Gordon Fenner (2): - Len Conway — b Alan Wilkes. Season #1, 4th career (48 innings, 60th all-time) — and, from an innings long consigned to history, a paid-up Debutante Duck. - Ray Howard — lbw Gordon Fenner. Season #2, 6th career (55 innings, 38th all-time). - John Waters — Fenner's second victim, bowled. Season #1, 3rd career (24 innings, 81st all-time). - John Abnett — Wilkes' second scalp, bowled. Season #2, 10th career (31 innings) — a lofty 18th all-time, and a fellow Ducks in a Row man. - John Lea — Wilkes' third, bowled. Season duck #5: 6th career nought in a mere 11 innings, reigning 1958 Duck of the Year, and top of this very season's leaderboard below. (All five share their moment with history — on this same day in 1952, Ray Tippen also made a duck against Linton Park CC.) Married v Linton Park CC - Tony Levick — bowled by none other than Ray Howard, fresh from his own duck for the Unknown XI moments earlier — poacher turned gamekeeper. Season #2, 7th career (111 innings, 31st all-time). Same 1952 echo of Ray Tippen. Singles v Linton Park CC - Brian Relf — b G Martin. Season #3, 5th career (14 innings, 42nd all-time). A stacked cabinet: Debutante Duck, Quack-to-Quack (duck on debut and farewell), Quacking Together (with Terry Whittaker, above), and a Retirement Duck credential from long ago — purely historical trivia. Tippen's 1952 shadow falls here too. Week in summary Nine ducks, two fixtures, Linton Park CC's attack doing the week's heaviest lifting — Wilkes and Fenner sharing all five Unknown XI wickets. Bonus trivia: Ray Howard ducked out one over and bowled out a teammate-turned-opponent the next. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. John Lea — 5 ducks / 7 inns (71.43%) 2. 🦆 Duck News — 20-21 September 1958

4 ducks
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1958 – 21 Sep 1958
A quieter weekend for the Club numerically, but a rich one thematically, with the 1st XI supplying the entire haul and Tilling Steven proving a positively fowl venue for Linton's middle order. 🏏 Saturday 20th — Bearsted CC (away) Batting at 11 with little left to protect, Tony Snell departed caught Ian Lambert bowled Bryan Elswood without adding to the total. It's his 4th duck of the campaign and his 12th in a 43-innings career, good enough for 11th of 710 in the all-time reckoning — and it draws him level on tally with Bert Buckoke, who played 1928–1953 and reached his dozen in 55 innings. Snell also extends his run to four ducks in a row, a streak that would make a hen proud, and with "Duck of the Year 1958" already on his honours board he heads into the run-in as the man to catch this season. The "Runaway Duck (a whole season without a run)" honour, too, sits uncomfortably close should he keep this up. 🌱 Sunday 21st — Tilling Steven (home) If Saturday was a solitary quack, Sunday was a full chorus. Three Linton batsmen fell without scoring in the same innings against Tilling Steven, a genuine gathering of the clan. Stan Wratten went first, run out for nought at number 2 — his 4th of the season and 15th of a 161-innings career, 6th of 710 all-time. On this very day in 1957, Richard Harris (1940–1958, 28 career ducks, 2nd of 710) also strolled back for a duck, so the calendar keeps good company. John Ames followed, lbw to F. Baker at 6, his 5th duck of the season and 17th of the career from 121 innings, 4th of 710 — a lofty position, backed by a place in the Homebody Duck top 10. Ames and Wratten renew their "Quacking Together" partnership in fine style this week, both falling in the same innings as history suggested they might. Roy Clarke completed the set, bowled by the same F. Baker at 7, his 5th season duck and 7th career one from 43 innings, 30th of 710. Clarke, too, shares "Quacking Together" honours with Ames — and true to form, the pair obliged together at Tilling Steven. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every one of them a 1st XI affair, and three of the four arriving in a single unhappy innings at Tilling Steven. Not quite family, but certainly friendly. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Ames — 18 inns, 5 ducks, 27.78% - Roy Clarke — 25 inns, 5 ducks, 20.00% - Tony Snell — 7 inns, 4 ducks, 57.14% - Bob Arnold — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Stan Wratten — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% Next fixture looms and the exclusive zero club never closes its books early. Bring your bats, gentlemen — someone's got to keep the honours board honest. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 13-14 September 1958 🦆

5 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1958 – 14 Sep 1958
Two fixtures at the tail end of the season, but it was Sunday's trip to Tunbridge Wells CC that did the real damage — five ducks in a single innings, a proper cluster of quacks to close out the week. 1st XI v Tunbridge Wells CC (away) - Stan Wratten opened and departed early, caught Woodland bowled Hall — his 3rd duck of the season and 14th of a steadily swelling career, good for 8th of 709 all time and level with both Frank Sutton (1925–1949) and Gordon Fenner (1941–1958). Spookily, this very date in 1957 saw Bob Arnold post a duck of his own against Bowaters CC — September has form. - John Ames, at five, fell to William Isard for his 4th of the season and 16th of his career, climbing to 4th on the all-time list. A card-carrying member of the Homebody Duck top ten and the exclusive Ducks in a Row club, Ames also holds Quacking Together honours with both Stan Wratten and Roy Clarke — and remarkably both obliged him in this very innings, the set completed live in front of the Tunbridge Wells fielders. - Cliff Anscombe followed soon after for his 3rd of the season and 7th career duck, 27th of 709 all time, level with Arthur Cradduck (1950–1953) and Frank Brattle (1937–1958). A Debutante Duck by trade and Duck of the Year back in 1957, Cliff shows no sign of slowing this fine tradition. - Roy Clarke made it four for the season and six for his career, 35th of 709 and level with current-era regulars Tony Levick and Tony Tipples. His own Quacking Together pact with Ames rang true once more, the two men departing within the same innings. - Last man John Lea, on debut, was bowled by Charles Wicker without troubling the scorers — a genuine baptism into the Debutante Duck club, and 1958's newest recruit to the One and Done register. Whether he stays a member of one is entirely up to him. Week in summary: 5 ducks from 2 matches, the entire haul arriving in one innings at Tunbridge Wells. Three of the five — Wratten, Ames and Clarke — share mutual Quacking Together honours that all rang true in the same innings, a rare triangle of shared ignominy 🍩🍩🍩. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - Bob Arnold — 4 ducks, 14 innings, 28.57% - John Ames — 4 ducks, 17 innings, 23.53% - Roy Clarke — 4 ducks, 23 innings, 17.39% - J Lea — 3 ducks, 5 innings, 60.00% - Tony Snell — 3 ducks, 6 innings, 50.00% The race for Duck of the Year 1958 remains wide open, with Ames and Clarke breathing down Arnold's neck. Next fixture, the ducks will no doubt be watching — see you all there 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Saturday 6th & Sunday 7th September 1958

3 ducks
Week 20 · 6 Sep 1958 – 7 Sep 1958
A quiet weekend for the club at large, but the 1st XI kept the zero club ticking over nicely with three fresh entries to the ledger of dubious distinction. 🏏 Saturday — Away at Tovil John Ames opened the weekend's account, lbw to F- Goldsworthy at number 5. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 15th of a storied career, good enough for 5th on the all-time list from 119 innings — a genuine club institution, already carrying "Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive)" and a Homebody Duck top ten spot on his honours board, alongside standing "Quacking Together" pairings with Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. On this very day in 1953, Richard Harris — the club's 2nd-highest all-time duck-getter with 28 across 1940–1958 — was performing precisely the same ceremony against Gillingham CC in Kent. Ames keeps fine historical company. 🌱 Sunday — Home to Anchorians CC Sunday's home fixture proved a productive one for all the wrong reasons, with Anchorians CC claiming a brace. Cliff Anscombe went first, bowled by J Toon, for his 2nd duck of the season and 6th of his career — 29th all time. Cliff is a long-standing Debutante Duck club member, having marked his very first senior innings with a nought, and was Duck of the Year back in 1957 for good measure. Fittingly, it was on this same date in 1957 that Frank Brattle (1937–1958, 7 ducks, 27th all time) was last seen making an identical contribution against Tovil. At the other end of the innings, last man Alan Wilkes was bowled by Don Wykes for his 1st duck of the season and 8th career nought, moving him to 22nd all time. Wilkes' honours board already carries two "Only Two Quacks" partnerships — with Bob Arnold, and with Saturday's own John Ames — proof that the zero club runs in company as much as in solitude. Like Cliff, Wilkes joins Frank Brattle's 1957 vintage on the calendar of shared infamy. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks, two matches, one club — with the 1st XI shouldering the entire burden this week and Anchorians CC doing the double on Sunday. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to report — just solid, dependable failure to trouble the scorers. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 Bob Arnold — 4 ducks, 13 innings (30.77%) 🥈 J Lea — 3 ducks, 5 innings (60.00%) 🥉 Tony Snell — 3 ducks, 6 innings (50.00%) 4. John Ames — 3 ducks, 16 innings (18.75%) 5. Roy Clarke — 3 ducks, 21 innings (14.29%) J Lea's 60% strike rate remains the one to watch — at this rate the season's Duck of the Year race may be settled well before the last ball is bowled. 🍩 Next fixture looms, and with it, further opportunities for enshrinement. Get your ducks in a row, gentlemen — the honours board never sleeps. 🦆

Week 19 · 30 Aug 1958 – 31 Aug 1958
30–31 August 1958 A quiet-looking weekend on the fixture list turned into a genuine cluster at Halfway House, with the 1st XI serving up six ducks across two matches and nobody troubling the "Two of a Quack" board — this time, at least. Saturday — 1st XI v W. Buck's XI (home) - John Abnett — c R. Beale b J. Ames. Season duck #1, 9th career (28 innings, 21st all-time), keeping the "Ducks in a Row" four-in-a-row honour ticking along nicely. On this very day in 1952, John Thirkell — the club's all-time top duck-getter with 40 — was likewise trudging back against Hunton Wanderers. Abnett also sits level on 9 career ducks with both B Westover (1940–45) and Gerald Wright (1952–54). - Tony Snell — c H. Morris b J. Ames, Ames's second scalp of the innings. Season duck #3, 11th career (42 innings, 14th all-time) and a proud holder of the grim "Runaway Duck 1958" honour — a whole season, not a run to show for it. He joins a distinguished trio on 11: Colin Haylor, Michael Morton (Duck of the Year 1956) and Peter Weedon, plus club stalwart William Buck. Sunday — 1st XI v Halfway House (home) - John Ames — turning bowler for the first innings, batsman-out for the second — b Colin Robinson. Season duck #2, 14th career (117 innings, 7th all-time all-time), extending his personal "Ducks in a Row" streak and his cameo on the "Quacking Together" board (with Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten). Level on 14 with Frank Sutton (double Duck of the Year, 1940 & 1946) and Gordon Fenner. - E Childs — b Brian Allsworth, first-ever senior innings. A bona fide debut duck 🍩 — straight onto the Debutante Duck board, and, rather poignantly, straight onto "One and Done" and "Retirement Duck" too, a career completed in a single ball-by-ball entry. Rank 188 of 702, joining a very long queue of one-duck wonders stretching back to A E Leatham in 1897. - Roy Clarke — run out, no easing the pain there. Season duck #3, 5th career (38 innings, 44th all-time), and a fellow member of the Ames "Quacking Together" ledger. Level on 5 with 1957 Duck of the Year Cliff Anscombe and the ill-fated Frank Wright (duck on debut and farewell). - John Lea — b Brian Allsworth, Allsworth's second victim of the innings. Season duck #3 — his third in five innings this season alone — 4th career (just 9 innings), and the reigning Duck of the Year 1958, a title he is doing everything in his power to defend. Level on 4 with Brian Relf (also a duck-on-debut-and-farewell man) and Charles Wycherley, a two-time Duck of the Year himself. Week in summary Six ducks from two fixtures, five of them crammed into Sunday's Halfway House innings alone — Brian Allsworth and John Ames each claiming a brace with the ball, proving misery does indeed love company. Two fixtures, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club took in a full house this weekend. Roy Clarke followed at five, caught P. Jordan bowled A. Olsen, his 2nd of the season and 4th career duck, 61st of 706 — and, like Wratten, a fellow member of "Quacking Together (with John Ames)". Kitchin, taking his season tally to a debut-of-the-week 1 but his career tally to a startling 28 — good enough for 2nd of 706 all-time, agonisingly close to the summit. Arnold's 3rd of the season moves him to 25 career ducks, rank 3 of 706, well inside his own Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive) territory, and formally Quacking Together with Thirkell — a partnership the pair have now honoured twice, on this very afternoon. Fryer's 1904 duck obliges the pattern again. Brian Relf closed the innings out, caught Wilkinson bowled Kemp, his 2nd of the season and 4th career duck, rank 47 of 706. His Debutante Duck and Retirement Duck boards are standing honours from a career with its own bookends, nothing to do with today — alongside Quacking Together with Terry Whittaker. Fryer's 1904 shift echoes once more down the decades. Four ducks, two bowlers — Kemp and Murrell shared the spoils evenly, with Farrance doing the honest work at slip twice over. 🌱 Sunday — 1st XI v Harrietsham CC (h) Roy Clarke was bowled by D La Plain for nought, his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of a 34-innings career, rank 86 of 706. His Quacking Together board names John Ames, a partnership from a different vintage. On this day in 1895, J Lampard also made a duck, against Maidstone CC. 📊 The Week in Brief Five ducks from two matches, all in the 1st XI, all on home soil — Headcorn's attack claimed four of them between just two bowlers.
