🦆 Duck Club Report — 27–28 September 1958 🦆

9 ducks
Week 23 · 27 Sep 1958 – 28 Sep 1958
As the 1958 season draws towards its curtain, the Duck Club staged its own closing ceremony — nine gloriously scoreless departures across two matches. The art of not scoring, it seems, never takes a day off. Saturday 27th — 1st XI v A. Trembeths - Terry Whittaker (no. 9) fell b J- Mortlock for the very first duck of his 10-innings career, 297th on the all-time list — and a lifetime member of the "Quacking Together" honour shared with one Brian Relf, who would renew that partnership in spirit come Sunday. - Tony Snell (no. 11) departed b v- Chance, his 5th duck of the season and 13th of a career that now stands 9th all-time. Runs this season: none whatsoever — his board already confirms a full "Runaway Duck" campaign, and the reigning Duck of the Year 1958 shows no sign of loosening his grip on the crown. Both fell on the very date that, back in 1953, Tony Tipples (1952–58) also quacked against Linton Park CC — the fixture list has a long memory for these things. Sunday 28th — v Linton Park CC (Unknown XI, Married & Singles) - Len Conway (Unknown XI, 2) b Alan Wilkes — season duck #1, 4th of his career, 60th all-time, and a paid-up Debutante Duck club member. - Ray Howard (Unknown XI, 3) lbw b Gordon Fenner — his 2nd this season, 6th career, 37th all-time. - John Waters (Unknown XI, 5) b Gordon Fenner — 3rd career duck, 81st all-time. - John Abnett (Unknown XI, 10) b Alan Wilkes — 2nd of the season, 10th career, up to 18th all-time, and already a name on the honours board for four Ducks in a Row. - J Lea (Unknown XI, 11) b Alan Wilkes — an eye-watering 4th duck of the season, 5th career, 44th all-time — see the leaderboard below, he's earned his place. - Tony Levick (Married, 5) b Ray Howard — proof Ray can dish it out as well as collect it — 2nd of the season, 7th career, 31st all-time. - Brian Relf (Singles, 3) b G Martin — 3rd of the season, 5th career, 41st all-time, a long-standing Debutante Duck alumnus whose "Quacking Together" honour pairs him with none other than Terry Whittaker, out himself just a day earlier — kindred quackers across the weekend, if not the crease. Spookily, this same date in 1952 saw Ray Tippen also fall to Linton Park CC — some fixtures simply have the duck's scent about them. Week in summary: 9 ducks from 2 matches, with Alan Wilkes and Gordon Fenner each claiming a brace — Linton Park CC's attack plainly fancied its chances this weekend. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Tony Snell — 8 inns, 5 ducks (62.5%) 2. John Ames — 19 inns, 5 ducks (26.32%) 3. Roy Clarke — 27 inns, 5 ducks (18.52%) 4. J Lea — 6 inns, 4 ducks (66.67%) 5. Stan Wratten — 16 inns, 4 ducks (25.0%) Tony Snell sits top of both the table and the Duck of the Year race — can anyone reel him in before 1958 draws stumps for good? Same ground, same fixtures, more nought-shaped glory awaits next time out. 🏏🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 20-21 September 1958

4 ducks
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1958 – 21 Sep 1958
A quieter weekend for the Club numerically, but a rich one thematically, with the 1st XI supplying the entire haul and Tilling Steven proving a positively fowl venue for Linton's middle order. 🏏 Saturday 20th — Bearsted CC (away) Batting at 11 with little left to protect, Tony Snell departed caught Ian Lambert bowled Bryan Elswood without adding to the total. It's his 4th duck of the campaign and his 12th in a 43-innings career, good enough for 11th of 710 in the all-time reckoning — and it draws him level on tally with Bert Buckoke, who played 1928–1953 and reached his dozen in 55 innings. Snell also extends his run to four ducks in a row, a streak that would make a hen proud, and with "Duck of the Year 1958" already on his honours board he heads into the run-in as the man to catch this season. The "Runaway Duck (a whole season without a run)" honour, too, sits uncomfortably close should he keep this up. 🌱 Sunday 21st — Tilling Steven (home) If Saturday was a solitary quack, Sunday was a full chorus. Three Linton batsmen fell without scoring in the same innings against Tilling Steven, a genuine gathering of the clan. Stan Wratten went first, run out for nought at number 2 — his 4th of the season and 15th of a 161-innings career, 6th of 710 all-time. On this very day in 1957, Richard Harris (1940–1958, 28 career ducks, 2nd of 710) also strolled back for a duck, so the calendar keeps good company. John Ames followed, lbw to F. Baker at 6, his 5th duck of the season and 17th of the career from 121 innings, 4th of 710 — a lofty position, backed by a place in the Homebody Duck top 10. Ames and Wratten renew their "Quacking Together" partnership in fine style this week, both falling in the same innings as history suggested they might. Roy Clarke completed the set, bowled by the same F. Baker at 7, his 5th season duck and 7th career one from 43 innings, 30th of 710. Clarke, too, shares "Quacking Together" honours with Ames — and true to form, the pair obliged together at Tilling Steven. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every one of them a 1st XI affair, and three of the four arriving in a single unhappy innings at Tilling Steven. Not quite family, but certainly friendly. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Ames — 18 inns, 5 ducks, 27.78% - Roy Clarke — 25 inns, 5 ducks, 20.00% - Tony Snell — 7 inns, 4 ducks, 57.14% - Bob Arnold — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Stan Wratten — 15 inns, 4 ducks, 26.67% Next fixture looms and the exclusive zero club never closes its books early. Bring your bats, gentlemen — someone's got to keep the honours board honest. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 13-14 September 1958 🦆

5 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1958 – 14 Sep 1958
Two fixtures at the tail end of the season, but it was Sunday's trip to Tunbridge Wells CC that did the real damage — five ducks in a single innings, a proper cluster of quacks to close out the week. 1st XI v Tunbridge Wells CC (away) - Stan Wratten opened and departed early, caught Woodland bowled Hall — his 3rd duck of the season and 14th of a steadily swelling career, good for 8th of 709 all time and level with both Frank Sutton (1925–1949) and Gordon Fenner (1941–1958). Spookily, this very date in 1957 saw Bob Arnold post a duck of his own against Bowaters CC — September has form. - John Ames, at five, fell to William Isard for his 4th of the season and 16th of his career, climbing to 4th on the all-time list. A card-carrying member of the Homebody Duck top ten and the exclusive Ducks in a Row club, Ames also holds Quacking Together honours with both Stan Wratten and Roy Clarke — and remarkably both obliged him in this very innings, the set completed live in front of the Tunbridge Wells fielders. - Cliff Anscombe followed soon after for his 3rd of the season and 7th career duck, 27th of 709 all time, level with Arthur Cradduck (1950–1953) and Frank Brattle (1937–1958). A Debutante Duck by trade and Duck of the Year back in 1957, Cliff shows no sign of slowing this fine tradition. - Roy Clarke made it four for the season and six for his career, 35th of 709 and level with current-era regulars Tony Levick and Tony Tipples. His own Quacking Together pact with Ames rang true once more, the two men departing within the same innings. - Last man John Lea, on debut, was bowled by Charles Wicker without troubling the scorers — a genuine baptism into the Debutante Duck club, and 1958's newest recruit to the One and Done register. Whether he stays a member of one is entirely up to him. Week in summary: 5 ducks from 2 matches, the entire haul arriving in one innings at Tunbridge Wells. Three of the five — Wratten, Ames and Clarke — share mutual Quacking Together honours that all rang true in the same innings, a rare triangle of shared ignominy 🍩🍩🍩. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - Bob Arnold — 4 ducks, 14 innings, 28.57% - John Ames — 4 ducks, 17 innings, 23.53% - Roy Clarke — 4 ducks, 23 innings, 17.39% - J Lea — 3 ducks, 5 innings, 60.00% - Tony Snell — 3 ducks, 6 innings, 50.00% The race for Duck of the Year 1958 remains wide open, with Ames and Clarke breathing down Arnold's neck. Next fixture, the ducks will no doubt be watching — see you all there 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Saturday 6th & Sunday 7th September 1958

3 ducks
Week 20 · 6 Sep 1958 – 7 Sep 1958
A quiet weekend for the club at large, but the 1st XI kept the zero club ticking over nicely with three fresh entries to the ledger of dubious distinction. 🏏 Saturday — Away at Tovil John Ames opened the weekend's account, lbw to F- Goldsworthy at number 5. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 15th of a storied career, good enough for 5th on the all-time list from 119 innings — a genuine club institution, already carrying "Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive)" and a Homebody Duck top ten spot on his honours board, alongside standing "Quacking Together" pairings with Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. On this very day in 1953, Richard Harris — the club's 2nd-highest all-time duck-getter with 28 across 1940–1958 — was performing precisely the same ceremony against Gillingham CC in Kent. Ames keeps fine historical company. 🌱 Sunday — Home to Anchorians CC Sunday's home fixture proved a productive one for all the wrong reasons, with Anchorians CC claiming a brace. Cliff Anscombe went first, bowled by J Toon, for his 2nd duck of the season and 6th of his career — 29th all time. Cliff is a long-standing Debutante Duck club member, having marked his very first senior innings with a nought, and was Duck of the Year back in 1957 for good measure. Fittingly, it was on this same date in 1957 that Frank Brattle (1937–1958, 7 ducks, 27th all time) was last seen making an identical contribution against Tovil. At the other end of the innings, last man Alan Wilkes was bowled by Don Wykes for his 1st duck of the season and 8th career nought, moving him to 22nd all time. Wilkes' honours board already carries two "Only Two Quacks" partnerships — with Bob Arnold, and with Saturday's own John Ames — proof that the zero club runs in company as much as in solitude. Like Cliff, Wilkes joins Frank Brattle's 1957 vintage on the calendar of shared infamy. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks, two matches, one club — with the 1st XI shouldering the entire burden this week and Anchorians CC doing the double on Sunday. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to report — just solid, dependable failure to trouble the scorers. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 Bob Arnold — 4 ducks, 13 innings (30.77%) 🥈 J Lea — 3 ducks, 5 innings (60.00%) 🥉 Tony Snell — 3 ducks, 6 innings (50.00%) 4. John Ames — 3 ducks, 16 innings (18.75%) 5. Roy Clarke — 3 ducks, 21 innings (14.29%) J Lea's 60% strike rate remains the one to watch — at this rate the season's Duck of the Year race may be settled well before the last ball is bowled. 🍩 Next fixture looms, and with it, further opportunities for enshrinement. Get your ducks in a row, gentlemen — the honours board never sleeps. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 30–31 August 1958

6 ducks
Week 19 · 30 Aug 1958 – 31 Aug 1958
Two fixtures, six ducks, and the ghost of John Thirkell walking arm in arm with every one of them — the great man (1943–1958, still an imperious 40 ducks, all-time #1) made his own duck on this very date back in 1952, once at Hunton Wanderers, once at A Levick XI. History has a wicked sense of timing. 🏏 Saturday 30th — 1st XI v W. Buck's XI (h) John Abnett departed for nought, caught Beale bowled Ames — his 1st duck of the season and 9th of a career now 21st all-time, a paid-up member of the "Ducks in a Row" club, four on the spin at his peak. Tony Snell followed him back, caught Morris bowled the same J. Ames, for his 3rd of the season and 11th career, 14th all-time. Snell wears the 1958 Duck of the Year sash and, more remarkably, the "Runaway Duck" honour — a whole season so far without a run to his name. Watch the leaderboard below though, where he isn't quite top dog yet. Spare a thought for opposition bowler J. Ames, whose afternoon's work sent both men packing — a surname Linton would hear from again rather sooner than expected. 🏏 Sunday 31st — 1st XI v Halfway House (h) Four fell in this one, headlined by our own John Ames, bowled Colin Robinson for his 2nd of the season and 14th career, 7th all-time — a committed Homebody Duck top-tenner who rarely troubles the away tally. Moments later Roy Clarke was run out for his 3rd of the season, 5th career, 44th all-time — and in doing so completed the "Quacking Together" honour shared with Ames, the pair now officially quacking as one. E. Childs marked his senior debut the only way the Duck Club truly respects — straight onto the Debutante Duck board, career figures reading a perfect 1 from 1. J. Lea, bowled Allsworth, notched his 3rd of the season and 4th career, 57th all-time — sharing his method of dismissal with Childs, both undone by the same probing spell. 📊 Week in Summary Six ducks from two matches, all at home, all 1st XI — a rare weekend where the seconds and thirds kept their heads down and let the top team do the collecting. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Bob Arnold — 4 ducks, 13 inns, 30.8% - J Lea — 3 ducks, 5 inns, 60.0% - Tony Snell — 3 ducks, 6 inns, 50.0% - Roy Clarke — 3 ducks, 20 inns, 15.0% - John Thirkell — 3 ducks, 25 inns, 12.0% Arnold sits imperiously atop the pile, but Snell, Lea and Clarke are queuing up behind him. The race for 1958's crown is far from settled. Next fixture, next chance — the ducks never rest, and nor should you. See you at the crease. 🦆🏏

Either way, the Duck Club committee notes the anomaly with mild suspicion and looks forward to normal service resuming forthwith. 0️⃣ Ducks recorded: 0 🏏 Matches played: 2 🍩 Verdict: the ducks evaded capture entirely — a rare and faintly unsettling weekend of runs all round Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. 🦆 Duck Report — Weekend of 16-17 August 1958

4 ducks
Week 17 · 16 Aug 1958 – 17 Aug 1958
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club took in a full house this weekend. Linton's top order continues to demonstrate that consistency of a very particular kind. 🏏 Saturday 16th — away to Bowaters CC Bob Arnold departed at four, bowled by Eric Wilson, for his 4th duck of the season and 26th of a career now bedding into the all-time top three — 3rd of 706, no less. This one extends his "Ducks in a Row" board to four on the bounce, a feat requiring real discipline to sustain, and keeps him in company on the "Only Two Quacks" honour with Alan Wilkes and "Quacking Together" with John Thirkell. Neat symmetry too: on this very day in 1953, John Ames also made a duck against Mote A — 13 career ducks and 7th of 706 to Ames' name, a fitting shadow to cast. 🌱 Sunday 17th — home to Mote A Mote A's bowlers did brisk business, taking three Linton wickets for nought in a single innings. Stan Wratten fell at three, bowled by S. Kitchin, his 2nd duck of the season and 13th of a career that sits him 8th of 706 — a lofty perch. He shares the "Quacking Together" board with John Ames. On this day back in 1957, John Thirkell was also enjoying the poultry treatment, against Bowaters CC no less — 40 career ducks and still Linton's undisputed number one. Roy Clarke followed at five, caught P. Jordan bowled A. Olsen, his 2nd of the season and 4th career duck, 61st of 706 — and, like Wratten, a fellow member of "Quacking Together (with John Ames)". The same 1957 echo applies: Thirkell ducking against Bowaters on this exact date. Richard Harris rounded things off at nine, also bowled by S. Kitchin, taking his season tally to a debut-of-the-week 1 but his career tally to a startling 28 — good enough for 2nd of 706 all-time, agonisingly close to the summit. Another nod to Thirkell's 1957 vintage duck completes the set. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, three of them arriving in the same Mote A innings — S. Kitchin doing the damage twice over. Bob Arnold quietly extends his consecutive-duck streak while Richard Harris continues his relentless march up the all-time table. The art of not scoring remains alive and well at Linton Park. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Bob Arnold — 13 inns, 4 ducks (30.8%) 2. John Thirkell — 23 inns, 3 ducks (13.0%) 3. C. Haylor — 3 inns, 2 ducks (66.7%) 4. J Lea — 4 inns, 2 ducks (50.0%) 5. Brian Relf — 5 inns, 2 ducks (40.0%) That's the tally for now, though with Bowaters and Mote A both showing form for taking Linton wickets cheaply, the Duck Club expects fresh nominations before the season's out. See you at the crease — or, more likely, on the way back from it. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 9th–10th August 1958

5 ducks
Week 16 · 9 Aug 1958 – 10 Aug 1958
A quiet weekend at Linton Park by match count, but a vintage one for the ledger — five new entries to the zero club, four of them courtesy of a single ruthless Headcorn attack. 🏏 Saturday — 1st XI v Headcorn CC (h) Opening the batting and opening the day's account, John Thirkell fell caught Farrance bowled Kemp without scoring. The club's ledger has been counting on his behalf for years — this is his 3rd duck of the season and takes him to 40 for his career, top of the all-time chart at rank 1 of 706, a record about as secure as records get. He arrives already garlanded with Duck of the Year honours from 1952, 1954 and 1955, plus permanent residency in the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. On this very day in 1904, Jack Fryer made his own duck against Leeds Castle CC's House Team — some traditions run deep. Robert Wood followed, bowled Murrell for nought, his 2nd of the season and 2nd of a career just seven innings young, rank 135 of 706. His Debutante Duck board records that his first-ever senior innings, some years back, was also a blob — today's simply adds to the collection. Jack Fryer, 1904, keeps him company on the calendar once again. Then Bob Arnold, caught Farrance bowled Murrell — the same Farrance who'd already pouched Thirkell, evidently having a fine afternoon in the slips. Arnold's 3rd of the season moves him to 25 career ducks, rank 3 of 706, well inside his own Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive) territory, and formally Quacking Together with Thirkell — a partnership the pair have now honoured twice, on this very afternoon. Fryer's 1904 duck obliges the pattern again. Brian Relf closed the innings out, caught Wilkinson bowled Kemp, his 2nd of the season and 4th career duck, rank 47 of 706. His Debutante Duck and Retirement Duck boards are standing honours from a career with its own bookends, nothing to do with today — alongside Quacking Together with Terry Whittaker. Fryer's 1904 shift echoes once more down the decades. Four ducks, two bowlers — Kemp and Murrell shared the spoils evenly, with Farrance doing the honest work at slip twice over. 🌱 Sunday — 1st XI v Harrietsham CC (h) Roy Clarke was bowled by D La Plain for nought, his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of a 34-innings career, rank 86 of 706. His Quacking Together board names John Ames, a partnership from a different vintage. On this day in 1895, J Lampard also made a duck, against Maidstone CC. 📊 The Week in Brief Five ducks from two matches, all in the 1st XI, all on home soil — Headcorn's attack claimed four of them between just two bowlers. Quality over quantity, as the zero club likes to say. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Bob Arnold — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25%) 2. John Thirkell — 3 ducks, 21 innings (14.29%) 3. C- Haylor — 2 ducks, 3 innings (66.67%) 4. J Lea — 2 ducks, 4 innings (50%) 5. Brian Relf — 2 ducks, 5 innings (40%) See you next weekend, when the zero club will no doubt be recruiting again. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 26th–27th July 1958

5 ducks
Week 14 · 26 Jul 1958 – 27 Jul 1958
Two fixtures graced the calendar this weekend, but only one needed the record book out: Linton's 1st XI capitulated against Tovil on the Saturday for FIVE ducks in a single innings, the kind of collective effort the exclusive zero club exists to celebrate. Fittingly, this very calendar date in 1940 also claimed a Linton batsman for nought — J Short, undone without score against Boughton Monchelsea, proving the 26th of July has form. 1st XI v Tovil William Blight departed at number three, bowled by Hitchin, for his 1st duck of the summer and 10th of a career that now sits 18th on the all-time roll of dubious distinction. Brian Relf followed moments later, also bowled by Hitchin, his 3rd career duck in just 12 innings.
