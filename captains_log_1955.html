🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 1955 · 1955 — 2 matches

Sun 25 Sep 1955 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDDD

Skipper's Log — Sunday 25 September 1955

Winning the toss on a Sunday is meant to feel like a gift, and I duly stuck us in, confident the lads would build something to defend. What we actually built was 101 all out in 40.1 overs, which is less an innings and more a cautionary tale told over several chapters. Stan Wratten was the one shining thread through it, cracking a fine 47, with Tony Levick chipping in a tidy 20 to keep the scoreboard honest. I added 16 of my own down the order, which felt like a triumph purely by comparison.

Elsewhere the ledger was less kind. Frank Brattle, Ray Tippen and Peter Weedon all made that lonely walk back for ducks, three abreast in the club's roll of shame, and I dare say the tea urn heard some choice words about it. Still, cricket has a habit of rewarding stubbornness over style, and somehow 101 proved just enough.

Defending a total like that ought to have been an exercise in nerves and prayer, but the lads held firm and got us home by 8 runs. Two points, and thoroughly earned, even if nobody watching would have called it pretty. Stan's 47 keeps him out in front of the 1955 Married run charts too, so at least someone in the side is having a proper season. A scrappy win, but a win all the same, and I'll take those over style points any Sunday.

Sat 24 Sep 1955 🧢 1st XIv Town Malling CC · HomeformDWDDD

Saturday last, 24 September, saw Town Malling CC roll up the drive, and having called correctly for once in my captaincy career, I had no hesitation in sending us in to bat. It was not, I confess, a vintage batting display — 89 for 6 off 25.3 overs is the sort of total that has opposition bowlers suppressing a smirk over their tea — but I managed 36 to top the card, with Stan Wratten a sterling 25 alongside me. Poor John Carter, meanwhile, was back in the pavilion for nought before his pads had properly warmed up — the walk of shame indeed.

Eighty-nine looked distinctly gettable, and for a good while it was. But cricket, bless it, has a way of rewarding patience over panic, and ours held. Richard Terry, Raymond Hollands and P Hollands all departed without troubling the scorers, and the innings never found its footing. The undoubted hero of the afternoon was J Donovan, whose 6 for 21 was a spell of real quality — a five-for and thoroughly deserved. Stan Wratten, not content with his runs, chipped in with 2 for 20 and five miserly maidens, while Bob Brigden took 1 for 18. Town Malling were bowled out for 72 in 30.4 overs.

A win by 17 runs, then — scrappy with the bat, splendid with the ball, and thoroughly satisfactory for the ledger. Onwards we go.

🗓️ 17 Sep – 23 Sep 1955 · 1955 — 2 matches

Sun 18 Sep 1955 🧢 1st XIv TC Tunbridge Wells CC · HomeformWDWDD

I toss lost, so it was straight to the field for us on a bright Sunday, and Tunbridge Wells CC took first knock. They ground their way to 133 all out from 40.3 overs, Peter Lloyd the chief architect with 45, Charles Wicker chipping in 29 and Jack Hickmott 28. We had our moments too — James Scrace and Jim Willard both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, which always raises a cheer from the boundary. But the story of the innings was Gordon Fenner, who bowled beautifully to finish with 7-31, a five-for and thoroughly deserved. John Carter (1-6) and Bob Brigden (1-22, with two tidy maidens thrown in) kept things honest at the other end.

Chasing 134 to win, we never quite found the fluency to match our bowling. Ray Howard was the mainstay with a well-made 32, Stan Wratten added 23 and Richard Harris 20, but wickets fell steadily enough that 130/7 from our 40 overs was as far as we got. I contributed my own small share along the way, though it was very much a team effort in the runs column, or lack thereof.

So honours shared, a draw when neither side could quite land the killer blow. On the balance of the afternoon's play I'd say we shaded it with the ball and let them off the hook with the bat — but a draw against a decent Tunbridge Wells side is no disgrace, and there's plenty to build on before the next fixture.

Sat 17 Sep 1955 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformDWDWD

Won the toss on a fine Saturday afternoon and never seriously considered doing anything but batting first — Bearsted CC in town, and a good pitch under our feet is not an invitation to be turned down. We laboured our way to 145 for 8 off 42 overs, which felt like harder work than the scoreboard perhaps suggests. I found some form at last, top-scoring with 45, and Stan Wratten gave the innings real backbone with a fighting 39, Frank Brattle chipping in usefully with 22. Poor C. Haylor will not thank me for mentioning it, but a duck is a duck, and the club gazette is nothing if not honest.

In the field we set about defending our total with more conviction than the total probably deserved, and were rewarded handsomely. Michael Morton was quite simply magnificent, ripping through Bearsted for 4 for 32 and, in doing so, sailed past the milestone of 100 career wickets — a thoroughly deserved piece of history. Basil Sears backed him up with 2 for 29, nudging past R W Mitchell into 24th on the club's all-time list, while J Donovan bowled two tidy maidens on his way to 1 for 13. Bearsted battled to 105 for 7, Snook and Hawkins doing the bulk of the resisting, but honours finished shared.

A draw, then — but with two lads etching their names into the record books, I'll happily call it a good day's cricket.

🗓️ 10 Sep – 16 Sep 1955 · 1955 — 2 matches

Sun 11 Sep 1955 🧢 1st XIv MT Medway Towns · HomeformDDWDW

Sunday, 11 September, and losing the toss meant Medway Towns had us in the field to begin with — bowl first, chase second, the traditional recipe for a testing afternoon. They made 192 for 6 from their fifty-four overs, built around a well-made 74 from H. Doust, with useful support from K. Walker (39) and A. Lawson (37). Len Conway was the pick of the lads, tidy and miserly with three maidens on his way to 2 for 35, Bob Brigden chipped in with 2 for 45, and I managed 1 for 30 to do my bit.

Set to chase, we replied with 166 for 7 from our thirty-six overs — a battling effort, though we ran out of overs before the finish line came properly into view. I found some form with the bat, top-scoring with 59, and had good company from Stan Wratten (32) and Tony Levick (22) as we pushed the score along.

Two things worth toasting in the pavilion afterwards: Stan reached 3,000 career runs for the club, a splendid landmark years in the making, and my one wicket this week nudged me past Jack Fryer into 8th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — while, for what it's worth, I now sit atop this season's run charts with 735.

With neither side able to force a result, honours were shared and the match ended in a draw — a fair reflection, I'd say, of an afternoon where both teams gave as good as they got.

A fine win by three wickets, then — onwards we go.

Sun 04 Sep 1955 🧢 1st XIv BW Birchington Wanderers · HomeformLWDDW

Sunday the fourth of September, and Birchington Wanderers made the trip to Linton Park for what turned into rather a stately affair. I won the toss and had no hesitation in batting first — the pitch looked a belter, and so it proved. William Buck played the innings of the day, 67 not out of pure, unhurried craftsmanship, with Len Conway chipping in a tidy 37 and John --'s Taylor a brisk 20. Poor John Ames, normally so reliable, was undone for a golden duck — the sort of dismissal that earns a sympathetic pat and no end of ribbing in the bar afterwards. Even our extras got in on the act with 25, and we closed on a healthy 197 for 6 from our 51 overs.

In the field we set about defending it with real purpose. Len Conway continued his good afternoon with 2 for 31, John Ames made amends with the ball for his earlier duck by picking up 1 for 61, and John --'s Taylor bowled beautifully tight, three maidens and thoroughly miserly throughout — fitting, given it was his hundredth appearance in Linton colours, a landmark the whole side was delighted to see. But E Webb played a fine innings of his own, 67, well supported by A Goodenough's 43 and D Edwards' unbeaten 37, and Birchington steadied to 165 for 3 from their 45 to deny us the win.

Honours shared, then — and with my own tally for the season now standing at 645 runs, I can't complain too loudly. A draw that flattered neither side, but a hundredth cap is worth more than two points any day.

🗓️ 27 Aug – 02 Sep 1955 · 1955 — 1 match

Sun 28 Aug 1955 🧢 1st XIv DX D Hambrook XI · HomeformWWLWD

Sunday, 28th August 1955, and D Hambrook XI came calling. Winning the toss, I put us into the field, and for a good long while it looked a sound decision entirely wasted, as Aiston creamed a fine 52, with Lawrence (36) and Basten (32) filling their boots either side of him. They closed on 189/9 from their 46 overs — a total that flattered them, frankly, until I found some rhythm and helped myself to 5-76, well backed up by Tony Tipples (2-33) and the parsimonious Tony Snell, whose 1-30 included two maidens that the batsmen will remember longer than their scores.

Our reply was a study in contrasts. William Buck batted with real class for 42, Tom Osborn chipped in a handy 24 — and reached 1,000 career runs for the club in the process, which rather overshadowed everything else and quite rightly so — while Tony Levick saw us home unbeaten on 22. As for the rest of us, the Duck Club did a roaring trade: Tipples, Weedon and, I'm mortified to report, your correspondent, all walked back without troubling the scorers. I shall say no more about my own batting, except that the bowling figures were some consolation.

189/9 versus 140/7, and honours shared. A draw that suited nobody and satisfied everybody — which, on reflection, is rather what Sunday afternoon cricket is all about.

Sun 21 Aug 1955 🧢 1st XIv HH Halfway House · HomeformLWWLW

Sunday 21 August, and Halfway House rolled up looking rather pleased with themselves — which, on this pitch, is usually a mistake we're only too happy to correct. Winning the toss, we sent them in, and while 179 all out from 44.4 overs was a respectable knock, it had the distinct flavour of a side that never quite got going against us. Jack Whibley's 55 was the innings' backbone, with Gordon Kemsley (45) and Denis Fowle (24) lending a hand, but three of their number — Don Capeling, Cyril Dyson and G Bruce — never troubled the scorers at all. John Ames was the chief tormentor, spinning his way to 5-47 with a couple of maidens thrown in for good measure, ably backed by Tony Snell (3-21) and Bob Brigden (1-37). A fine team bowling display, the sort that makes a captain's job look easy.

Chasing 180, we made harder work of it than perhaps we ought, closing on 135/8 from our 58 overs. William Buck's 32 was the innings of note, with Stan Wratten (24) chipping in nicely, though John Ames's golden duck rather cancelled out his heroics with the ball. I contributed my own small share along the way, for whatever that's worth in a match that refused to be won. Poor William Blight had a torrid time behind the stumps, leaking 8 byes, though he can console himself with having overtaken Joe Fenner into 25th on the club's all-time run charts with his 17.

So: honours shared, a draw that satisfied nobody and offended nobody — which, in truth, is the most English result of all.

Sat 20 Aug 1955 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformLLWWL

Saturday 20 August 1955 saw Charles Arkcoll roll into Linton Park, and having lost the toss I found us asked to field first — never my favourite way to spend a warm afternoon, but there we are. The visitors ground their way to 190/9 from their 48 overs, Randolph Thompson the chief tormentor with a composed 85 not out, well supported by Gordon Fenner (27) and Stan Wratten (25). Basil Sears, though, had other ideas, ripping through their order with 5-50 to keep us firmly in the contest, while Richard Harris and J Donovan chipped in with a wicket apiece, Donovan's three maidens the mark of a man giving nothing away. Humphrey Morris departed for a duck, which I'm sure he'll want forgotten by teatime.

Chasing 190 is never a formality, and 192/7 from 37 overs tells you it wasn't. I'm rather pleased to say I led from the front with 65 not out, though the real backbone came from Bob Arnold's excellent 46 and Basil Sears following his bowling heroics with 30 more with the bat — not a bad afternoon's work from Basil all told. Peter Weedon's duck was the one blot on an otherwise handsome scorecard, and poor Bob Arnold had a torrid time behind the stumps, leaking 10 byes, though his runs quietly moved him past Ernest Cruttenden to 20th on the club's all-time list.

We scrambled home by three wickets, hearts in mouths but pride intact. A thoroughly good day for the club, and one Charles Arkcoll will, I hope, remember rather less fondly than we shall.

Two wickets and two points - not pretty, but thoroughly earned. A win's a win, and I'll take it with a smile and a pint in hand.

🗓️ 23 Jul – 29 Jul 1955 · 1955 — 1 match
