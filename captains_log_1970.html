🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1970 · 1970 — 1 match

Sat 26 Sep 1970 🧢 1st XIv SC St Lukes Maidstone CC · HomeformLLDWW

From the captain's chair — Saturday 26 September 1970. St Lukes Maidstone CC were the visitors, and having called correctly at the toss, I had no hesitation in choosing to bat, the pitch looking far too good to leave to the openers of the other side.

And how the lads repaid that confidence. We ground our way to a handsome 227 for 3 from 46 overs, the sort of total that has you enjoying your tea rather more than usual. Basil Sears led from the front with a fine 54, John Ames matched him almost run for run with 52, and David T Lowen anchored things beautifully with an unbeaten fifty of his own. Three half-centuries in one innings is not something this reporter gets to write every week, and I shan't pretend I wasn't rather pleased with myself for winning that toss.

With the ball, however, we rather ran away with ourselves. St Lukes were bowled out for a mere 95 in 39.2 overs, E- Aiston's 29 and B- Pearce's 22 the only real resistance, while poor B- Lamb and Ron Hickman departed without troubling the scorers. Ron himself made ample amends with the ball, a magnificent 4 for 7 including five maidens — miserly doesn't begin to cover it — with Graham Mountford (4-45) and Mike Stephens (2-18) mopping up the rest.

A thumping 132-run win, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work. Heads up, backs straight — and back to the nets we go, already looking forward to the next one.

🗓️ 19 Sep – 25 Sep 1970 · 1970 — 2 matches

Sun 20 Sep 1970 🧢 1st XIv HC Harrietsham CC · HomeformLLLDW

Under a sky that had clearly consulted the fixture list, we welcomed Harrietsham CC to Linton Park on Sunday, and the coin – for once – smiled kindly on me. I had no hesitation in putting the visitors into the field, a decision that looked rather better once Paul Bowles set about compiling a handsome half-century. I chipped in with 45 myself, and Dick Langridge steered us home unbeaten with a tidy 22, as we posted 163 for 6 from our fifty overs. Not every name troubles the scorebook favourably, mind – Brian Humphries and Jon Harris will be keener to bat again after their respective ducks.

Defending 163 always promises an afternoon of nerves, but the lads were magnificent with the ball. Paul Bowles, evidently not content with his runs, ran through Harrietsham with 4 for 12, while David T Lowen (3 for 10) and Dick Langridge (2 for 51) mopped up the rest. Tony Gauna bowled with the miserly discipline of a man who simply refuses to be hit, four maidens to his name. Michael Lidstone and Edward Smith departed without troubling the scorers, and the innings folded for 104 in 33 overs. Poor Nigel O'Toole had a torrid time behind the stumps, 9 byes his one blot on an otherwise cheerful day.

A 59-run victory, then, and a thoroughly satisfying one at that. Onwards we go, buoyed and rather pleased with ourselves.

Sat 19 Sep 1970 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWLLLD

I called correctly and, on a bright September morning against West Farleigh, there was never really a decision to make — up went the bat, and in we went.

It wasn't our finest hour with the willow. 127 all out from 51.3 overs tells its own story of graft rather than glory, with Graham Mountford's 25 the backbone of the innings and Basil Sears chipping in a handy 23 around him. Less said the better about the three ducks on the card — Paul Bowles, Tim Thirkell and Jim Hall all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorer, which is the sort of thing that makes a captain stare rather hard into his tea.

Happily, 127 proved just enough, because what followed was as fine an afternoon's bowling as Linton Park could wish for. West Farleigh were bundled out for 95 in a brisk 21.4 overs, undone above all by Graham Mountford, who turned the ball square for a magnificent 7-40 — no higher praise than to say he was simply unplayable. Mike Stephens backed him up admirably with 3-18, including two miserly maidens, and en route reached the landmark of 50 career wickets, a fine achievement quietly earned. I chipped in with the bat earlier and was glad enough just to have contributed. Don Fermor's gutsy 52 for the visitors, well supported by Simon Day's 23, was the only real resistance, but three more West Farleigh ducks showed just how relentless our attack was.

Two points, 32 runs to the good, and a thoroughly deserved win: we batted like village cricketers and bowled like champions, and on the day that's a trade well worth making.
