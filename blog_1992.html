🦆 Duck News — 11 October 1992 🦆

0 ducks
Week 25 · 11 Oct 1992
A quiet week on the poultry front, ducklings. One fixture played on the 11th, and for once the zero club had to sit this one out entirely — not a single batsman could be persuaded into the exclusive company of the nought. Bats connected, singles were nudged, and the art of not scoring went, agonisingly, unpracticed. The Duck Club regrettably has nothing to report from the middle this week. Do not mistake this for good news. Somewhere out there a duck-shaped opportunity was missed, and the leaderboard below remains admirably undisturbed as a result — no new blood, no shuffling of the pack, just the same familiar names holding their ground in dubious distinction. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — 1992 0️⃣ 🥇 Matt Large — 5 ducks in 22 innings (22.73%) 🥈 Rob Doick — 5 ducks in 34 innings (14.71%) 🥉 Richard Piper — 4 ducks in 6 innings (a startling 66.67% — consistency of a very particular kind) 4. Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks in 23 innings (17.39%) 5. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks in 33 innings (12.12%) Richard Piper's rate remains the stuff of legend this season — four ducks from just six trips to the crease is triumph through adversity if ever there was any, and the rest of the field will need a proper collapse to catch him on volume alone. 🏏 Onward to the next fixture, then, where normal service will surely resume. The ducks are out there somewhere, biding their time — and rest assured, the moment anyone obliges the club with a nought, they'll be welcomed into these pages with the ceremony they deserve. Until then — keep your bat straight, and good luck avoiding the golden variety. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Week of 19–20 September

0 ducks
Week 22 · 19 Sep 1992 – 20 Sep 1992
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, and for once the Duck Club has nothing whatsoever to show for it. Not a single quack. Not a golden duck, not a pair, not so much as a nervous nudge back to the pavilion for nought. Eleven-and-eleven, twice over, and every single bat found something to show for its efforts. The exclusive zero club, so reliably stocked most weeks, sat this one out entirely — the shelves are bare, the honours board gathers dust, and the correspondent is left filing a report about the absence of a story. Some will call it good batting. Around here we call it a wasted opportunity. Still, the season is young and the regulars will be back. Consistency, as we know, comes in two varieties at Linton Park — and this week the other kind won out. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 5 ducks in 22 innings (22.73%) - Rob Doick — 5 ducks in 33 innings (15.15%) - Richard Piper — 4 ducks in 6 innings (a startling 66.67% — truly living dangerously) - Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks in 23 innings (17.39%) - Piers McDonald — 4 ducks in 33 innings (12.12%) Richard Piper's strike rate for nought remains the standout of the season so far — four ducks from just six trips to the crease is the kind of percentage that gets a man talked about in the wrong sort of company. No movement at the top this week, but the chasing pack won't stay quiet for long. Get out there next weekend, gentlemen — the Duck Club awaits fresh recruits, and this drought can't last. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 12 to 16 September 1992

2 ducks
Week 21 · 12 Sep 1992 – 16 Sep 1992
A quieter week in the hide this time round — the ducks proved cannier than usual, with only two willing to poke their heads above the reeds across three fixtures. Quality over quantity, though, because what we lack in numbers this week we make up for in pedigree. 2nd XI — Southborough CC (Sat 12th) Tim Thirkell strode out at first wicket down and departed almost as promptly, bowled by Lee Thorpe without so much as troubling the scorers. It's his 2nd duck of the season and — take a bow — his 66th career nought, a tally that plants him a majestic 4th on the all-time leaderboard of 1,174 duckers. Tim is a Full House man too (a duck at every position, 1 through 11, the completist's badge of honour), sits proudly in the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, and once claimed Duck of the Year back in 1972 for good measure. He shares his "Quacking Together" pairing with club legend Jim Hall, and the noughts run in the family — son John already has 90 of his own, with brothers Nigel (47) and Simon (25) not far behind. Consistency, it seems, is hereditary. On this very day in 1959, S Lusher also fell for nought, against Bowaters CC — kindred spirits separated by 33 years and a scorebook. Midweek XI — Lord Cornwallis XI (Wed 16th) Batting at 9, Ian Brattle was undone by M Fleming for his 3rd duck of the season and 24th of his career, good for 33rd on the all-time list. Ian is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from way back, boasts a startling "Ducks in a Row" streak of seven consecutive noughts, and shares his "Only Two Quacks" pairing with Michael Beeney. Brother Paul is keeping pace on 26 career ducks of his own — clearly a family that prefers to leave the crease early and often. On this day in 1962, Richard Ashby also made a duck, against Staplehurst CC, a full three decades before Ian's own effort. Week in summary Just 2 ducks from 3 matches — the traps were set but the pond stayed largely empty. Both fell to seam bowling, both to players deep into distinguished duck careers, and both with a "this day in history" echo to keep the archivists happy. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 5 ducks in 21 innings (23.81%) 2. Rob Doick — 5 ducks in 31 innings (16.13%) 3. Richard Piper — 4 ducks in 6 innings (a startling 66.67%) 4. Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks in 22 innings (18.18%) 5. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks in 32 innings (12.5%) Richard Piper remains one to watch — two-thirds of his innings this season have ended in the exclusive zero club, a strike rate for failure that borders on the heroic. 🍩 That's your lot for this week, quackers. The traps are reset for the next round of fixtures — plenty of scope yet for milestones, family doubles and a few more names added to the honours board. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — Tour of Cornwall, 5–9 September 1992

1 duck
Week 20 · 5 Sep 1992 – 9 Sep 1992
Greetings, quackers. The 1992 touring party ventured deep into Cornish territory this week, and while the cider flowed freely, the ducks proved rather shy — just the one specimen bagged across three matches. Still, quality over quantity, as any self-respecting member of the exclusive zero club will tell you. 🏏 Tour XI At Beacon CC, Cornwall, on the 6th, Rob Doick strode out at first drop and departed rather promptly, bowled by I Watson without ever really getting his tour boots dirty. Away form, it seems, does not always agree with him. This was Rob's 5th duck of the season and his 8th for the club overall, from 52 innings — enough to slot him in at a very respectable 94th of 1174 in the all-time standings. A player clearly building a portfolio of noughts to be proud of, one careful edge at a time. Neatly enough, on this very day back in 1967, Roger Craddock — a fleeting presence on the books between 1966 and 1968 — also trudged back for a duck against Birchington CC. Different opposition, different decade, same eternal truth: the duck respects no calendar. 📅 Week in Summary Three matches, one duck — the Cornish tour was largely a vegan affair by Duck Club standards, with bat generally troubling ball rather more than usual. Whether that reflects good form or simply good weather for batting, we leave to the historians. Either way, Rob Doick carries the flag alone this week, and carries it well. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 5 ducks, 20 innings (25.0%) 2. Rob Doick — 5 ducks, 30 innings (16.67%) 3. Richard Piper — 4 ducks, 6 innings (66.67%) 4. Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks, 20 innings (20.0%) 5. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks, 31 innings (12.9%) Richard Piper's strike rate remains a thing of terrifying consistency — four ducks from six knocks is triumph through adversity of the highest order. 🍩 Onward to the next fixture, where the ducks will surely be waiting to reclaim their rightful numbers. Keep your bats straight, gentlemen — or don't, and join the club properly. 0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck Club Bulletin — 29 August to 4 September 1992

7 ducks
Week 19 · 29 Aug 1992 – 4 Sep 1992
Seven ducks fell across five matches this week, as tour season took hold and the Cheesman and Piper households both added fresh entries to the ledger of nought. 🏏 Saturday 29 August — 2nd XI v Sheldwich CC (away) A family affair to open proceedings. Stuart Cheesman departed for his 2nd duck of the season, caught M Holden bowled A Creed, and moves to 3 career ducks (rank 193 of 1174) — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club and a Full House honouree besides. On this very day in 1954, Stan Wratten was performing the identical trick against D Hambrook XI, en route to 35 career ducks of his own. Next in, Richard Piper, stumped by K Voller off J Sandys for his 3rd of the season and 5th career duck — a rare double member of both the Homebody Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens, proof he needs no home advantage to fail expensively — also matching Wratten's 1954 vintage to the day. And finally Fred Cheesman, Stuart's own, stumped off the same bowling for his 4th of the season and a weighty 27th career duck (rank 26 of 1174) — twice Duck of the Year (1990 and 1991) and a Full House man in his own right. Fred and Stuart falling in the same innings earns the rare Duck and Ducklings ceremony, elder leading junior back to the pavilion. Steve Cheesman, sibling to Stuart and son to Fred, would complete the family set before the week was out. 🌴 Tour Week — Cornwall Wednesday saw Tour XI at Falmouth CC. Steve Cheesman duly obliged, lbw David Toseland, for his 3rd of the season and 9th career duck (rank 82), keeping company with the on-this-day exploits of Benny Bell against Staplehurst back in 1972, 30 career ducks and all. Alongside him fell Simon Thirkell — scion of Duck Club royalty, father John the club's all-time No.1 on 90, brothers Tim (65) and Nigel (47) both deep in the book — stumped off the same bowler for his 1st duck of the season and 25th career duck (rank 31), a Tourist Duck, a Ducks on the Road regular and a card-carrying "50 Before a 50" member. Benny Bell's 1972 shadow falls over him too. Friday's trip to Mullion CC produced a brace. Rob Doick was bowled by Danny Hickey for his 4th of the season and 7th career duck (rank 100), echoing A Pay's lone 1988 duck on that very ground. Richard Piper then returned to the crease only to fall again, bowled by the same Hickey, his 4th of the season and 6th career duck — a grim pair for the week, a nought-and-nought double even A Pay never had to endure twice. 📋 Week in Summary Seven ducks, five matches, one Duck and Ducklings ceremony, and one unwanted pair. The Cheesmans occupy three lines in this week's book and the Pipers rue a matching set. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 20 inn, 5 ducks (25%) 2. Richard Piper — 6 inn, 4 ducks (67%) 3. Fred Cheesman — 19 inn, 4 ducks (21%) 4. Rob Doick — 28 inn, 4 ducks (14%) 5. Piers McDonald — 29 inn, 4 ducks (14%) 🍩 The tour rolls on through Cornwall — more wickets, more quacks. See you next weekend, ducks permitting.

🦆 Duck Report — Weekend of 22–23 August 1992

6 ducks
Week 18 · 22 Aug 1992 – 23 Aug 1992
Six ducks in two days — the exclusive zero club took hearty new memberships either side of the weekend, and one unfortunate soul got the full weekend package. 🏏 Saturday 22 August — 2nd XI v Hadlow CC (home) Steve Cheesman fell early at three, bowled by Matt Portlock, his 2nd of the season and 8th of a growing career in 43 innings, 96th on the all-time list of ignominy. On this very day in 1953, J Barclay was doing much the same, one of seven ducks in a two-year career that still nets him a Duck of the Year crown and a Retirement Duck entry. Steve has poultry in the blood, father Fred sits on 26 career ducks and is currently mid-flight on this season's leaderboard, while brother Stuart carries 2 of his own. Rob Doick departed for the first of a busy weekend double, bowled by Dominic Boulle at five, his 2nd of the season, 5th of his career from 47 innings, 113th overall. Keep that number in mind. Trevor Back, genuine Duck Club royalty, edged behind off Matt Portlock's second victim of the innings, his 2nd this season and 36th in a mighty 282-innings career, 14th on the all-time roll, with Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top ten honours and shared quacks with Allan Cooper and Jon Harris. Matt Portlock, take a bow, two scalps, both ducks. 🏏 Sunday 23 August — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (home) Clive Newman, caught behind off S Smith at three, opened his career account with his 1st ever duck from 17 innings, rank 342 of 1174. Back in 1981, on this same calendar day against these very Old Anchorians, Brian Piper was doing the same, en route to 20 career ducks and 39th all-time. Matt Large, caught at five off K Bethell, took his 5th duck of the season, top of the current charts, and 34th of a storied career in 145 innings, 17th all-time. His honours cabinet groans: Duck of the Year in 1987, 1988, 1989, and fittingly, 1992 too, plus Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. And so to Rob Doick, back for more, bowled by K Bethell, his second victim of the innings, at six. His 3rd duck of the season and 6th of his career completes a grim weekend pair: out Saturday for the 2nds, out Sunday for the 1sts. On this day in 1981, Brian Piper was again keeping the Old Anchorians company. 📋 The Week in Summary Two matches, six ducks, two bowlers doing serious hunting of their own, Matt Portlock and K Bethell claimed a brace apiece. Rob Doick's weekend pair was the standout feat of dubious distinction. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Matt Large — 20 innings, 5 ducks, 25.0% - Piers McDonald — 29 innings, 4 ducks, 13.79% - Stewart Morrison — 14 innings, 3 ducks, 21.43% - Fred Cheesman — 16 innings, 3 ducks, 18.75% - Paul Bowles — 20 innings, 3 ducks, 15.0% Matt Large sits atop the pile, Duck of the Year 1992 already on his mantelpiece. Next fixture looms, the zero club never closes its books. Who's next through the turnstile? 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 15 to 19 August 1992 🦆

9 ducks
Week 17 · 15 Aug 1992 – 19 Aug 1992
Nine ducks in three matches this week, headlined by a genuine club landmark and a proper top-order-to-tail collapse for the seconds. Let's get into it. Saturday 15 August 1st XI (h) v Harrietsham CC - Paul Bowles opened up and departed via a simple catch off Kevin Grebbell — his 2nd duck of the season and 49th of an extraordinary career, still parked at 5th all-time. On this very date in 1964, the legendary John Thirkell (1943–1977, 90 ducks, still #1 all-time) was doing exactly the same thing against Bowaters CC. History repeating, quackily. 2nd XI (a) v Higham & Mockbeggar CC A collapse for the ages — six ducks in one innings, four of them consecutive from number 8 down. - Stewart Morrison, out cheaply at the top, notches his 3rd of the season and 11th career duck — a resume already carrying Debutante Duck, Full House nearly-man and Lone Duck top-10 honours. Sibling Phillip Morrison keeps a single career duck of his own in the family cabinet. - Alan Boyns (2nd season, 11th career, rank 60) — a proud 1990 Diamond Duck alumnus, though credit where due, he actually troubled the scorers with a ball faced this time. - Richard Piper (2nd season, 4th career, rank 148) adds another decoration to a groaning trophy cabinet — Ducks on the Road, Full House, Homebody and Lone Duck top-10 all present. A parent-child duck-club bond with Brian Piper, who carries 20 of his own. - Stuart Cheesman, run out (1st season, 2nd career) — kin to Fred Cheesman (26 career ducks, currently 4th on this season's table, see below) and sibling Steve Cheesman (7). - And then, both bowled by Richard Hawkins without scoring, R Batchelor and Ben Harris — genuine baptisms, first innings, first ducks, first entries on the Debutante Duck board. Both already flirting with the Retirement Duck honour, though nobody's writing off a second innings just yet. All six found the same illustrious company: John Thirkell's 1964 vintage against Bowaters CC, on this very calendar day. Wednesday 19 August Midweek XI (a) v Gravesend CC - Kevin Mullins fell early, caught Eddie Stanford bowled L Morley-Clark — 1st of the season, 2nd career, a graduate of the Debutante Duck club from an earlier vintage. On this day in 1956, John --'s Taylor (1949–1960, 15 ducks) made the same walk against Mote A. - Then the big one. Paul Bowles, back at the crease just four days after Harrietsham, bowled by L Morley-Clark — his 3rd of the season, but his 50th career duck overall. 🥇 Full Membership of the Duck Club Hall of Fame, secured. A pair for the week into the bargain — Saturday and Wednesday both — and he holds firm at 5th all-time. Fittingly, this one too falls on Taylor's 1956 calendar date. Week in Summary 9 ducks, 3 matches, one Hall of Fame induction and a 2nd XI tail that simply gave up asking questions. The Cheesman and Morrison/Piper clans keep multiplying their presence on the honours boards without lifting a bat. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 19 inns, 4 ducks (21.05%) 2. Piers McDonald — 29 inns, 4 ducks (13.79%) 3. Stewart Morrison — 14 inns, 3 ducks (21.43%) 4. Fred Cheesman — 15 inns, 3 ducks (20.0%) 5. Paul Bowles — 20 inns, 3 ducks (15.0%) Morrison and Bowles both add to their season tallies this very week — the race for Duck of the Year 1992 is well and truly on. Next fixture awaits its own contribution to the honours board. Pads on, gloves on, expectations low. See you out there 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 8th to 12th August 1992

3 ducks
Week 16 · 8 Aug 1992 – 12 Aug 1992
A quieter week for the Duck Club harvesters — just three ducks from four fixtures — but quality over quantity, as ever, at Linton Park. Saturday 8th August — 1st XI at Headcorn CC A grim afternoon for the top order. Paul Bowles departed at six, caught Tony Hunt bowled Graham Mackman, his 1st duck of the season and 48th of an extraordinary career — good enough for 6th on the all-time table from 792 innings, a duck roughly every 16 trips to the crease. Bowles remains a two-time Duck of the Year (1966, 1968) and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, one half of Only Two Quacks with Adam Pooley. On this very day in 1979, Paul Brattle was suffering the same fate against Maidstone Police — the calendar has a long memory. Mackman wasn't finished. Down the order, Jon Harris edged behind to Simon Phipps for his own 1st of the season and 69th for his career, 3rd on the all-time list from a startling 494 innings. Harris's honours board reads like a testimonial dinner menu — Debutante Duck, three-time Duck of the Year (1976, 1980, 1981), Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), Homebody, Lone and Tourist Duck top 10s, plus shared quacking honours with Allan Cooper, Jim Hall and Trevor Back. He also carries the family gene — son Richard Harris already has 36 career ducks of his own. Harris, too, finds Paul Brattle for company on this day in 1979. Sunday 9th August — 1st XI v Benenden CC (home) Piers McDonald fell at five, caught off an unidentified fielder off Mike Chambers, for his 4th duck of the season — 15th of his career and 49th on the all-time list. A paid-up member of the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top 10s. On this day in 1964, none other than John Thirkell, the club's all-time duck king with 90, was doing exactly the same thing against Harrietsham CC. The other two fixtures of the period passed off duck-free — a rare mercy. Week in Summary Three ducks, two of them off the same bowler in the same innings — Graham Mackman can consider his week's work done. Piers McDonald's latest also nudges him level with Matt Large atop this season's hunting standings. 🦆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 4 ducks, 18 innings (22.2%) 2. Piers McDonald — 4 ducks, 29 innings (13.8%) 3. Fred Cheesman — 3 ducks, 15 innings (20.0%) 4. D Cooke — 2 ducks, 3 innings (66.7%) 5. Ian Brattle — 2 ducks, 5 innings (40.0%) Fixtures resume shortly — plenty of time yet for someone to close the gap on Large. Bats up, ducks watching. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 25 to 29 July 1992

6 ducks
Week 14 · 25 Jul 1992 – 29 Jul 1992
Six fresh entries for the ledger this week across four fixtures, with Ashford CC providing a genuine flock in one innings. Read on. 🏏 1st XI v The Mote CC (home, Sun 26 July) Piers McDonald departed lbw to D Thomas at number 7 — his 2nd duck of the season and 13th of a career now sitting proudly in both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, rank 53 of 1170 all time. On this very day back in 1940, J Short was performing the identical ceremony against Boughton Monchelsea CC. Some traditions never die. Three down the order, Fred Cheesman went caught Foster bowled Booth — his 3rd of the season (he currently tops the Duck Hunting charts for it) and a mighty 26th career nought, good enough for rank 26 of 1170. A double reigning Duck of the Year (1990 and 1991), Full House member and one half of Only Two Quacks with Adam Pooley — Fred's honours board is getting crowded. He too joins J Short's 1940 vintage on this date, and keeps it in the family, with son Steve (7 career ducks) and grandson Stuart (1) already enrolled. 🏏 2nd XI at Ashford CC, Kent (away, Sat 25 July) A genuinely torrid innings for the seconds, who managed to lose four wickets for nought without ever repeating themselves. Tim Thirkell fell caught Burbridge bowled Cole at first drop — only his 1st of the season, but his 65th career duck lodges him at a rarefied rank 4 of 1170, agonisingly shy of the club century. Duck of the Year 1972, a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Full House, Homebody and Lone Duck top tens, and one half of Quacking Together with Jim Hall — Tim barely needs introducing. It runs in the blood too: father John (90), brothers Nigel (47) and Simon (24), and cousin Stuart (1) all keep the family name on the honours board. Roly Edwards was bowled by Norman Lambert at 7, opening his career account in his 19th innings. All eleven at the crease got off the mark, denying the exclusive zero club its usual weekly intake. No golden ducks, no pairs, no king pairs, not so much as a nervous nudge to third man followed by the long walk back. The art of not scoring, it seems, was simply not practised on this occasion. Whether this is due to a sudden and alarming outbreak of competence, or simply that 1992's early fixtures haven't yet found their footing, the committee cannot say. Either way, the Duck Club watches on, patient as ever — the season is long, and consistency of this particular kind rarely lasts. 🍩 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard Nothing to report here either — the 1992 standings are yet to open their account. A blank leaderboard for a blank week: fitting, if faintly unsettling for those of us who prefer our cricket with a bit of ignominy in it. 🏏 Looking ahead Rest assured, dear members, this drought cannot last. Somewhere out there a nervous nick, a first-ball yorker, or a needless run-out is already being prepared with your name on it. The Duck Club committee remains on standby, quietly optimistic that normal service — nought, naught, and nothing at all — resumes at the very next fixture. Until then: bat carefully, or don't. We'll be watching either way. 0️⃣🦆
