Sat 22 Aug 1896 🧢 1st XIv SC 1st Innings Maidstone CC · AwayformDLLLW

Saturday last, I led the lads down the road to Maidstone CC, for a match that turned out to be a day of two very different halves.

Handed first use of the ball, we set about the home side with real purpose and did not concede an inch — or indeed many runs. H G Smith was in irresistible form, running through the order for six wickets without conceding a single one, well supported by J Edmonds with three and S Smith with one, while J H Hobbs and E Larder both trooped off for ducks. Maidstone were shot out, and for a happy hour or two it looked rather like our day.

Our own reply told a different tale entirely. Ducks for S Smith, W Edwards and D H Kennard left the card looking distinctly sorry for itself, and though I managed a top score of 15, it was nowhere near enough to drag us over the line. We too were dismissed in turn, and Maidstone closed the match out 43 runs to the good.

One bright note amid the gloom: H G Smith's six-wicket haul carried him past F Garrett to 25th on the club's all-time list of wicket-takers — no small feat. I find myself, for what it's worth, fourth in this season's run charts with 151 and third among the wicket-takers with 16, which is scant comfort for a batting effort that never got going. A match won with the ball and lost with the bat, then. Heads up, and back to the nets.

🗓️ 01 Aug – 07 Aug 1896 · 1896 — 1 match

Mon 03 Aug 1896 🧢 1st XIv RC Rochester CC · HomeformDDLLL

We lost the toss on a bright Monday and were invited to have first use of the ball — Rochester CC electing to bat, which in hindsight they may regret. L Cooper played the innings of the day, 80 well-struck runs, with R A Arnold ably supporting on 73 and L A Goldie chipping in a tidy 29. E L Baker, less fortunate, departed for a duck. J N Tonge did the real damage with 3-0, K McAlpine matched him, and I managed to find some rhythm myself, finishing with 3-0 of my own — the sort of figures a captain tries very hard not to look too pleased about in front of the players.

Chasing was never going to be straightforward, but Arthur Cornwallis had other ideas, striking a magnificent 116 to lead us home, with J N Tonge weighing in handsomely alongside him on 76. We got there by three wickets — comfortable enough on paper, rather more nail-biting from the boundary rope.

Milestones abounded: Cornwallis's century sees him leapfrog F S W Cornwallis to sit top of the club's all-time run-scorers, while those same wickets of mine edged me past W H Fryer into third on the all-time list — and I remain, for now, at the head of both the 1896 run and wicket charts. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a fine advertisement for village cricket at its best.

Wednesday 22nd July 1896
