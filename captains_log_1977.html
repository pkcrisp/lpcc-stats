🗓️ 01 Oct – 07 Oct 1977 · 1977 — 2 matches

Sun 02 Oct 1977 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDDDW

There are days when winning the toss feels like the easy bit, and Sunday was one of them. I called correctly, saw no earthly reason to do anything but bat first, and the lads set about proving me right in fits and starts. Paul Bowles was the man of the innings, ghosting past his fifty and on to a thoroughly composed 74, while Tim Thirkell stood at the other end lending quiet, sensible support with a well-made 52 of his own. Between them they did most of the heavy lifting.

The rest of the card, if I'm honest, made for less cheerful reading. Colin Dicker, Martin Mears and Keith Miller all trooped back for ducks, and I shall draw a merciful veil over the details rather than dwell on them here. Still, 161 for 9 from our 41 overs was a total with enough substance to work with, and work with it we duly did.

Elsewhere in the season's ledger there is quiet satisfaction to be had: I'm sitting fifth in the 1st XI run charts with 316 to my name, Paul leads the Married averages with his 74, and Brian Woodcock tops the Singles pile on 50. Plenty still to play for on all fronts.

As for the chase, we strolled home by 9 wickets, barely breaking stride. All in all, a thoroughly good day for the club, ducks and all.

Sat 01 Oct 1977 🧢 1st XIv Lordswood CC · HomeformWWDLD

The toss went against us — as it so often does — and Lordswood CC, our visitors, wasted no time electing to bat. Not that I minded overly; there's an honest pleasure in taking the new ball on a morning that still smelled of dew and quiet optimism.

They ground their way to 130 for 6 from their 53 overs, a couple of watchful contributions of 25 and 22 doing most of the damage down the order, with Keith Miller chipping in a brisk 20 alongside them. I was fortunate enough to find some rhythm with the ball, leading the attack with three for 42 — seven of those overs going for nothing at all, which felt rather like the one thing I did properly all afternoon — while Keith Miller picked up two for 18 and John Bloomfield one for 25 to keep things admirably tight.

In reply we mustered a modest 95 for 6 from our 40 overs. Paul Bowles played the innings of the day, a watchful, well-made 68 not out that single-handedly kept us respectable, while Ian Webb, Tim Thirkell and Brian Woodcock each contributed the same golden nothing to proceedings — there is safety in numbers, if not in scorebooks.

Paul continues to lead our run charts for the season with 1,581, and I'll modestly note I'm topping the wicket column with 83. In the end honours were shared — a draw that flattered nobody and disappointed nobody either, which, on reflection, is exactly the sort of result Saturday cricket was invented for.

🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 1977 · 1977 — 2 matches

Wed 28 Sep 1977 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformWLDWD

Here's the match report:

I'll confess I lost the toss at The Mote, which meant we were shown straight out into the field – hardly the afternoon I'd have chosen, but there's a certain honesty to bowling first that appeals to the traditionalist in me. Allan Cooper proved the difference, spinning his way to a magnificent 5-39 with eight maidens thrown in for good measure – miserly barely covers it. Trevor Back weighed in with 3-44, and I managed to prise out a couple myself for 25, with Eddie Page and David Sayer both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. J. Shepherd's 22 and David Twiddy's 21 were the best The Mote could muster, and for all their effort they were bowled out for 113 in the 43rd over – a total that never felt quite substantial enough.

Chasing 117 is never entirely comfortable, whatever the scoreboard suggests, but Nigel Thirkell's 34 and Paul Bowles's 33 saw us home in good order, and we closed on 117/4 with overs and wickets to spare. A word too for Allan, whose five-for today lifts him past Frank Taylor and into 15th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list – no small achievement, and thoroughly deserved.

With Nigel still leading our 1977 run charts and yours truly out in front with the ball, it's shaping into rather a fine season. A six-wicket win, then, earned with bat, ball, and a good deal of Kentish sunshine. Onwards indeed.

Sat 24 Sep 1977 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWWWDL

Saturday 24th September and West Farleigh rolled up for what turned out to be our last hurrah of the 1977 season. I lost the toss again — some things never change — and we were put into the field.

West Farleigh made a solid fist of it, 154 for 7 off their 42.3 overs, built around a fine fifty from Roger Crooks, with Reg Oldfield and Don Fermor chipping in handsomely either side of him. Credit to Terry Donovan, whose 2 for 53 kept things honest, and I managed to prise out a couple myself for 58. But the real story with the ball was Nigel Thirkell, three maidens of parsimonious, teeth-gritting accuracy that must have driven their middle order to distraction.

Chasing 155, we never quite got the rhythm going, subsiding to 114 for 9 off our 32 overs. I got my head down for 49 and did most of the scoring, though I'd have swapped every one of those runs for a touch more support from the other end. Jim Hall and Martin Mears both trudged back for ducks, and the Duck Club ledger grows fatter still — 🦆🦆 for the collection.

Not a bad way to add to the run tally for the year, mind — 1,513 now and counting. In the end, honours were shared, a draw when neither side could quite land the killer blow. On reflection, a fair result: we bowled with more discipline than we batted, and a draw against a side who made 154 probably flatters us more than it should. Still, cricket's a funny old game, and there's always next season.

Wednesday nights are usually when I do the arithmetic on the season, and this one gave me plenty to chew over. I chipped in with 2-42, Paul Bowles picked up 1-3, and between us we kept the visitors pinned to 119/9 off their 45, M. Brown's 22 the top score amid the rubble. A draw was the fair result when neither side could quite land the killer blow.

Small mercies for the club ledger, mind: Nigel's 41 nudges him past Frank Brattle into 7th on our all-time run-scorers list, he sits 2nd in this year's Midweek XI charts on 196, and I find myself leading the wicket-takers with 19. Honours shared on the field, then, but rather a good night for the record books.

Skipper's Log — Wednesday 14 September 1977. Tonbridge Trade at home, and a fine one to have on the card.

Winning the toss, I put Tonbridge Trade in, and it proved the right call. They battled to 131 all out from 45.3 overs, P. Styles top-scoring with 31 and J. Nigel Thirkell was simply imperious, striding to 91 not out and carrying the innings almost single-handedly, with Martin Mears adding a tidy 21 alongside him. I contributed my own small share along the way, though the day belonged to Nigel, who in passing his fifty also brought up 5,000 career runs for the club — a milestone worth raising a glass to, and one that had the boundary applauding for rather longer than usual. Eight byes did rather give the game away for poor Jim Hall behind the stumps, a lesson for another day.

Nigel now sits second in the Midweek XI run charts with 155, and John second in the wicket charts with 9 — form both will want to carry forward. Seven wickets to spare, two points banked, and a genuinely handsome afternoon's work all round.

Poor David Buck contributed his customary duck 🦆, and Allan Cooper will want a word with himself after conceding 12 byes among a generous 31 extras.

Seven wickets to the good, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon by the sea. Nigel Thirkell continues to lead our wicket charts and sit handsomely second among the run-scorers — the man is having quite a summer. A good day for the club.
