🗓️ 06 Oct – 12 Oct 1979 · 1979 — 1 match

Sat 06 Oct 1979 🧢 1st XIv BC Boughton Monchelsea CC · AwayformWWWDD

Boughton Monchelsea, Saturday last, and for once the coin fell their way — they had first knock and, more fool them, made rather a meal of it. 188 all out in 46.4 overs, which sounds respectable until you note that Nigel Thirkell was among the wickets again — 2-12, tidy as you like, part of a run that's seen him climb to second in our own wicket charts this summer. Ross Brice and Paul Bowles chipped in with two apiece, and Howard Usher went for a duck at our hands, which I mention only because he'll be mortified to see it in print. Well played anyway, Howard — could happen to anyone.

Chasing 189, we made an absolute mess of the opposition's morning by simply not getting out. Eighty-one on the board before the first wicket fell — a stand to make a captain's tea taste sweeter than usual — before Paul Bowles went on to 69, David Carden 43, and Tim Thirkell saw us home unbeaten on 28. We finished 189/4 in 35.2 overs, which is the sort of arithmetic I could get used to.

A word too for Bowles, whose two wickets that morning quietly nudged him past John Ames into third on the club's all-time list — the kind of milestone nobody notices until someone points it out, so: noticed.

Six wickets, then, and a fine one at that. Onwards indeed.

🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1979 · 1979 — 1 match

Sat 29 Sep 1979 🧢 1st XIv Lordswood CC · HomeformDWWWD

Lost the toss again — I'm beginning to suspect the coin has a grudge — so Lordswood CC took first use of a good-looking Saturday and elected to bat. They made us work for it, closing on 142/8 from 51.1 overs, though we had them 3-1 early before a stubborn 54-run second-wicket stand steadied things. R Brown top-scored with 27, J Hendon finished unbeaten on the same total, and Alan Pattenden chipped in 24 — but we did for Colin Gardener without troubling the scorers at all. I found some rhythm with the ball and finished with 4-27, with Paul Bowles (1-21) and John Bloomfield, three maidens and all, chiselling away at the other end.

Our reply never quite got out of second gear. An early wicket with nothing on the board set the tone, and having steadied to 50-3 we then contrived to lose three more for a dozen runs in the sort of collapse that has grown men reaching for the tea urn early. Nigel Thirkell's 39 was the innings' backbone, Martin Thompson's unbeaten 20 the only other real resistance, while Paul Bowles, Don Fermor and Mike Iliffe all took the long walk without troubling the umpire's finger for the right reasons. Graham Cove had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, eight byes testament to a pitch with ideas of its own.

Nigel keeps his nose in front for runs this season and I'll take the wickets column gladly. Honours even in the end — a draw, and on this evidence, a fair one.

🗓️ 22 Sep – 28 Sep 1979 · 1979 — 2 matches

Sun 23 Sep 1979 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDDWWW

I fielded first — no, elected to bat, and I'll stand by that call, right call as it turned out even if the top of the order didn't quite agree with me. A jittery start saw us slip to 6 for 1 before David Carden calmed the nerves with a thoroughly well-made fifty, 51 of the good, honest variety, and found a willing partner in Tim Thirkell, whose 21 kept the innings ticking along nicely. We limped to 154 all out from our 43 overs — not a total to frighten anyone, but enough, we hoped, to give the bowlers something to work with.

And work with it they did. Anchorians never quite got away from us, subsiding to 117 for 9 in 41 overs despite John Prodger's fine fifty of 56 and a stubborn third-wicket stand worth 50. Once that partnership broke the floodgates opened in a hurry — 108 for 5 became 109 for 8 before you could refill your tea, with Tappin, Fish and Wykes all sent back without so much as a single to their name. Allan Cooper was the chief architect, spinning through them for a marvellous 5 for 18, while I chipped in with 3 for 58 and four maidens for good measure — a happy day with the ball, if not with the bat, where I fell for a duck we shall discreetly draw a veil over.

A word for Mike Iliffe too, whose 16 runs eased him past Jim Hall into 15th on the club's all-time run-scorers list. In the end honours were shared, a draw when neither side could quite force the issue — and on the balance of the afternoon's fortunes, a fair result all round.

Sat 22 Sep 1979 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformDDDWW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 22 September 1979. West Farleigh CC away at Linton Park, and I lost the toss, which around here usually means a long afternoon leaning on a fence. Not today. West Farleigh got up to 177 for 4 off their 44, Barry Brigden compiling a fine unbeaten 80 with Roger Crooks chipping in 37, and we did have them 3 for 1 at one stage before a 70-run stand steadied things rather more than we'd have liked. Credit to Nigel Thirkell, who bowled beautifully for 2 for 29 with six maidens thrown in for good measure — miserly barely covers it — while John Bloomfield and I picked up a wicket apiece, mine costing 39, so at least I contributed something with the ball before doing rather better with the bat.

Because contribute I did. Chasing 178, David Carden played his part with a tidy 49, Nigel Thirkell rounded things off with an unbeaten 31, and I finished not out on 91, which I'll admit felt rather satisfying after a summer of more modest returns. We cantered home for the loss of a single wicket, with overs to spare.

A word, too, for Nigel, who brought up 7,000 career runs for the club today — a proper landmark, and typical of the man that he marked it with bat and ball both. He sits second in this year's wicket charts and top of the run charts, and long may it continue.

A fine win by nine wickets, then, and a thoroughly happy captain writing this up. Onwards we go.

Wed 19 Sep 1979 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformLWWDW

As it happened at The Mote, 19th September 1979:

We won the toss out at The Mote and, with the sun on our backs and a batch of optimism in my kit bag, I had no hesitation in electing to bat — the right call, as it turned out, and I'll take the credit for that much at least. Paul Bowles then proceeded to bat as though he owned the place, cruising to a majestic 110 not out, and found the perfect foil in Steve Gower, whose well-crafted 59 helped stitch together a third-wicket stand worth 129. We closed on a handsome 202 for 3 from our 39 overs, and there was a definite spring in the tea-interval step.

Defending it proved rather harder than posting it. Robert Holmes decided he too fancied three figures and duly obliged with an unbeaten 109, well supported by Malcolm Bristow's composed 29 not out and Roger Crookes' 26. They wobbled early, losing a wicket with just 3 on the board, but a 72-run third-wicket stand steadied them for good. John Hartridge did the damage with the ball, snaring 1 for 34, and I chipped in myself with 1 for 58, alongside Steve Petty's 1 for 46 — while poor Chris Milsted departed for a golden duck at our hands. Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 13 byes testament to a pitch with ideas of its own.

We fell four wickets short in the end. Chins up, though — Bowles remains ominous atop the run charts and I'm quietly pleased to be sitting second in the wickets column. We'll be better for it next week.

Sun 16 Sep 1979 🧢 1st XIv Mersham Le Hatch CC · AwayformWDDDW

Here's the match report:

Sunday saw us travel to Mersham Le Hatch, and when I lost the toss and they chose to bat, I confess my heart sank slightly — it's never the news you want on a warm afternoon. But the lads dug in, and the ball did the talking. Tony Gauna was simply magnificent, spinning his way to figures of 7-80 and leaving the Mersham order in tatters. I chipped in with 2-34 and seven maidens of my own — happier holding up an end than making headlines, on this occasion. J Copeland's 37 and Bruce Campbell's 23 gave the innings some respectability, and a seventh-wicket stand of 58 threatened to make a match of it, but M Epps' golden duck rather summed up their day, and 136 all out from 44.5 overs left us with a target to chase.

Chasing it, we didn't make it easy on ourselves — 5-1 inside the first few overs is not the calm start any captain dreams of, and poor Jim Hall's own duck won't be one for his scrapbook. But Graham Cove played the innings of the day with a fine 38, and I was glad to see us home with an unbeaten 36, guiding us to 139/6 from 42.3 overs.

A scratchy, nervy, thoroughly typical village win — three wickets to the good, two points banked, and a happy coach ride home. On days like this, cricket remains a wonderfully daft game, and I wouldn't have it any other way.

Sat 15 Sep 1979 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · HomeformDWDDD

Southborough lost a wicket before troubling the scoreboard, and further ducks for Ken White and Paul Rickwood kept the pressure on. Southborough's Gordon Baldwin battled hard for his 31, and they were eventually done for 103, though not before 14 byes had Jim Hall wincing behind the stumps.

One run. That was the final margin – enough to have the tea urn shaking almost as much as the fielders. A scrappy, nerve-shredding, thoroughly typical afternoon of club cricket, and a very good day indeed for the club.

🗓️ 08 Sep – 14 Sep 1979 · 1979 — 2 matches

Sun 09 Sep 1979 🧢 Tour XIv BC Beacon CC, Cornwall · AwayformWWWLW

Sunday the 9th of September found us on the road to Beacon CC in Cornwall, a fixture I approach each year with equal parts affection and dread, given what the pasties do to the fielding after tea. I won the toss — a rare enough event to merit its own entry in the log — and, sensing runs in the pitch, had no hesitation in batting. Mike Tritton set the tone with a fine 55, and Nigel Thirkell, ever the reliable sort, chipped in an unbeaten 47, with Paul Bowles's 28 knitting things together nicely. A commanding 100-run stand for the sixth wicket did most of the heavy lifting, and we closed on 186/6 from our forty overs feeling rather pleased with ourselves.

Defending it proved a nervier business than the scoreboard now suggests. Beacon rallied through R Tonkin's unbeaten 38 and useful contributions from D Piper and Jonathan Lean, both 28, but they never quite escaped the grip Ron Hickman had on proceedings — 3-26 with three maidens, miserly as ever. Paul Bowles and Allan Cooper chimed in with two apiece, and three Beacon batsmen departed without troubling the scorers at all. Allan did have a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 16 byes gifted like stray sixpences, but no matter — Beacon fell for 165.

We got home by 21 runs, and Nigel Thirkell quietly brought up 7,000 career runs for the club along the way — a landmark worth toasting properly at the bar. A good day for the club, and a thoroughly satisfying one for its captain.

Sat 08 Sep 1979 🧢 Tour XIv PC Perranarworthal CC · AwayformLWWWL

Saturday the 8th of September, and I called correctly at the toss for once — small mercies — and had no hesitation whatsoever in sticking us in to bat first at Perranarworthal.

The ball began doing extraordinary things early on — three ducks in the innings, including one poor soul walking for nought at 6 for 1 — and I found some rhythm I didn't entirely know I still possessed, finishing with 7 for 19. Paul Bowles bowled beautifully in support, three wickets and seven maidens of pure attrition, while John Hall's defiant 31 for the opposition was about the only thing standing between us and an even heavier margin. Fred Greenfield had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps — nineteen byes is not a figure one likes to see next to the wicketkeeper's name — but nobody's perfect, and he'll have forgotten it by Tuesday nets.

A fine win in the end, 37 runs to the good, and with 277 runs and 13 wickets on the season so far I shan't pretend I'm displeased with how the Tour XI is shaping up. Onwards to the next one.
