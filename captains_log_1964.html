🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1964 · 1964 — 2 matches

Sun 27 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWWDL

Sunday last, under skies that couldn't quite decide their mood, we welcomed Linton Park CC to the ground, and I won the toss and had no hesitation in taking first knock — the right call, I still maintain, whatever the scoreboard went on to suggest.

We were bowled out for 135 in 40 overs, which on reflection was a touch light for the surface on offer. Alan Tucker was the mainstay of the innings with a fine 28, and I was glad to lend a hand with 23 of my own, the pair of us doing what we could to shore things up while wickets fell around us. Spare a thought for poor Robert Murray, though, who marched out, faced his lot, and marched back again for a duck — these things happen to the best of us, and he'll have his day.

In the wider scheme of the season, it's worth noting Fred Greenfield continues to purr along nicely, sitting second in the 1st XI run charts with 399 to his name, while John Thirkell leads the 2nd XI table with 56 — proof, if any were needed, that there's talent enough in this club to keep us cheerful even on a flat Sunday.

As for the match itself, Linton Park knocked off the runs for the loss of just three wickets, a seven-wicket win that flattered them rather more than it wounded us. We'll dust ourselves down and come back stronger next week — that's cricket, and that's us.

Sat 26 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDDWWD

Saturday 26 September saw the Anchorians roll into Linton Park under skies that promised more than we delivered. Winning the toss, I fancied our chances first up and put us in — a decision that looked shakier by the over as we slid to 149 all out in 38.1. Gordon Fenner top-scored with a battling 35, while poor Len Conway and Dick Langridge both trudged back for ducks, joining the club's least-wanted list before the tea urn had even boiled.

With the ball, we scrapped hard. John Ames was superb, 3-21 and enough to see him leapfrog Alan Wilkes to the top of the club's all-time wicket-takers — a genuinely proud moment on an otherwise chastening afternoon. Dick Langridge chipped in with 2-41 and three tight maidens, and we did have Anchorians rocking, turning 122-3 into 125-6 in a flurry that had us dreaming, with A Hart and S Harris both back in the hutch without scoring. But P Woolsey's fine 60, well supported by J Cavey's 41 and a stand of 62 for the third wicket, proved too much, and they got home at 150-6 with four wickets in hand. Poor Roy Clarke had a torrid time behind the stumps, 12 byes leaking away — no doubt he'll want to forget that one. I did my bit with 17 with the bat, small beer next to leading the season's run charts with 492, while Dick Langridge continues to run away with the bowling with 43 wickets.

Not our day, then — beaten by four wickets, heads down, and back to the nets we go.

Sun 20 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv TS Tilling Steven · HomeformWDDWW

Sunday last saw Tilling Steven roll up for what turned into a proper old-fashioned tussle, and having called correctly I had few qualms about putting them in the field first. As is often the way, I lived to regret nothing at all, because the top order set about repairing my confidence rather nicely. Fred Greenfield led the charge with a fine 59, timing the ball well past his fifty, while Terry Whittaker chipped in a brisk 31 down the order. I managed to hang about for an unbeaten 42 myself, more through stubbornness than style, and by the close of our innings we'd posted a healthy 221 for 7 off 43.2 overs — enough, I felt, to make an afternoon of it.

Tilling Steven's reply had its moments of alarm for us. G. Winter looked in determined touch for his 37, with useful support from D. Pound (30) and Maskell (25), and for a spell it felt as though the game might slip from our grasp. But Whittaker, having already done his bit with the bat, turned his arm over beautifully — four maidens and figures of 2 for 21 kept the scoring honest, while Dick Langridge (2-44) and Basil Sears (1-12) tidied up the rest. Poor Prout departed without so much as a run to his name, which I'm sure will sting more than the result.

In the end they closed on 151 for 8, well short, but with too many overs gone for us to force it home. A draw it was, then — honours shared, pride intact, and the tea urn, as ever, the real winner of the day.

Sat 19 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv MC Maidstone YMCA CC · HomeformWWDDW

Saturday, 19 September 1964. Home to Maidstone Ymca CC, and the coin let me down again — they had first use of both toss and bat, though as it turned out that suited us rather nicely.

Terry Donovan (2-27) and Ron Hickman (1-15) tidied up the rest between them. I confess our 9 byes suggest Roy Clarke behind the stumps had rather a testing afternoon, but nobody was going to mention it after a display like that.

Chasing 108, Terry Whittaker played the innings of the day, an unbeaten 74 built on good sense and better timing, with Basil Sears keeping him company on 26 not out as we cantered home to 110 without loss in just 24 overs. Ten wickets, no fuss, home before the pub shut.

A thoroughly good day for the club — the bowlers did the damage, the openers did the tidying up, and I merely had to stand at slip and enjoy the show.

🗓️ 12 Sep – 18 Sep 1964 · 1964 — 2 matches

Sun 13 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformDWWDD

1964, home lost by us, straight to the report.

At Staplehurst on a fine September Sunday, I won the toss and had no hesitation in batting first — a decision I stand by, even if 126 all out from 35.2 overs wasn't quite the fortress I'd hoped for. We lost our first wicket for just a single run, which is never the ideal way to settle the nerves, and poor Basil Sears will want to forget his golden duck rather quickly. Thankfully Joe Fenner steadied things with a fine 32, and I chipped in with 30 of my own to give the total some respectability.

Still, 126 always felt defendable on a day when the ball was doing things, and so it proved. Staplehurst were bowled out for 77 in 35.1 overs, with only G. Murrell offering any real resistance for 24. Openers P. Hollands and H. Catt both departed without troubling the scorers, and from there it was really one man's afternoon: Len Conway span a web through their middle and lower order for magnificent figures of 6 for 29, eight of his overs maidens besides. Terry Whittaker and Alan Tucker mopped up either end with a wicket apiece.

A word too for Roy Clarke, whose 14 runs quietly nudged him past Len Conway and up to 16th on the club's all-time run charts — history made almost in passing.

A 49-run win, two points banked, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round.

Sat 12 Sep 1964 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformLDWWD

Saturday 12th September, and Bowaters CC came calling under skies that, for once, gave nothing away.

Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in batting first — and for once the decision looked sound rather than merely stubborn. Basil Sears played the innings of the day, 60 well past his fifty, with Terry Donovan a fine ally on 23. Losing our first wicket at 5 might have set nerves jangling, but the fifth-wicket pair steadied things with a stand of 66 that gave the innings its shape. Then, as is so often the way with us, the wheels came off in spectacular fashion — 109 for 5 became 120 for 8 almost before anyone had finished their tea, with poor Frank Brattle collecting a painful duck along the way. We closed on 148 for 8 from our 44 overs, a total that felt like both an achievement and a missed opportunity.

Bowaters replied with William Preston looking ominously fluent for his 57, ably supported by Roy Cole's unbeaten 21, and they too stumbled early at 5 for 1. But where we wobbled, they properly subsided, 84 for 4 crumbling to 101 for 7 as the pressure told. Terry Whittaker was superb with 2 for 23, backed handsomely by Dick Langridge's 2 for 31 — a landmark day for Dick, who climbed past Bert Buckoke into 25th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — and I chipped in with 1 for 8, which I shall mention only the once and modestly at that. Poor Roy Clarke had a torrid time behind the stumps, 8 byes testament to a tricky pitch rather than his gloves.

In the end neither side could force it home, and honours were shared. A draw, then — honest, hard-fought, and entirely in keeping with the game we love.

Cliff leads both the run and wicket charts for the Midweek XI this season, and no one begrudges him it after a display like that. We got over the line by 42 runs in the end — a proper team performance from start to finish, and a very happy day for the club.
