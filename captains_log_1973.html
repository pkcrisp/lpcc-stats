🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1973 · 1973 — 1 match

Sat 29 Sep 1973 🧢 1st XIv BC Bexhill CC · HomeformDLLLD

Here is the match report.

The toss went against us on Saturday, and once again we found ourselves trudging out to field rather than pad up — a state of affairs I am beginning to take rather personally. Bexhill made their way to 117 for 9 off 48.3 overs, built patiently around Shrubb's 24, with useful support from King (21) and Cowley (20). If it's any consolation to my aching shoulder, I managed 2 for 8 with the ball, which I like to think set the tone, while Moss (2-11) and Gauna (2-34) chipped in nicely. Special mention, though, must go to brother Nigel, who bowled five maidens without conceding so much as a sniff — the sort of miserly spell that makes the opposition question their life choices.

Chasing 118 should have been well within our compass, but cricket has a way of humbling the confident. We were bowled out for 105 in just 32 overs, Andrew Moss batting nicely for 30 and Ken Webb chipping in with 23, but too many of us came and went without troubling the scorers. Nigel, having bowled like a dream, rounded off his day with a golden duck — the game's cruel sense of balance at work.

There was, at least, cause for quiet pride: Tony Gauna's brace of wickets nudged him up to 20th on the club's all-time list, past Allan Cooper, while Paul Brattle and Nigel continue to sit second in this season's batting and bowling charts respectively.

We fell 12 runs short in the end. A game we ought to have won, and one we'll be better for having lost.

Sun 23 Sep 1973 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLDLLL

Sunday's fixture against Anchorians CC was billed as a battle, and it very nearly delivered one — except I lost the toss again, which by now is less bad luck than a personality trait. They chose to bat, and set about their 44 overs with quiet purpose, posting 136/6. E. Cherry led the way with a tidy 34, well supported by Derek Ufton (23) and Alan Dixon (22), while our attack chipped away throughout. Nigel Thirkell was the pick of the bowlers by some distance — 4-34 with five maidens thrown in for good measure, proof that not a run was given away lightly. Tony Gauna and Allan Cooper each took a wicket apiece to keep the visitors honest.

Chasing 137, we made light work of it in the end. Paul Brattle and Paul Bowles simply refused to be parted, carrying us to 78/1 from 19 overs with unbeaten knocks of 35 and 32 respectively — the sort of composed batting that makes a captain's job look easy, even when it wasn't him doing any of the composing.

A nod, too, to the record books: Tony Gauna's solitary wicket nudged him past Len Conway into 21st on the club's all-time list, while Paul Brattle continues to sit 2nd in this season's run charts on 555, and Nigel Thirkell holds 2nd in the wicket charts with 51.

In the end honours were shared, neither side quite able to force the issue. A draw, then — honest, hard-fought, and entirely fitting for a Sunday when both attack and bat had their say.

Sat 22 Sep 1973 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformLLDLL

Saturday, 22 September 1973, and West Farleigh came to Linton Park in fine spirits, which state I'm sorry to report we then rather punctured with the bat. Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in batting first — though quite why, on 131 all out from 46 overs, remains a question for the tea tent rather than these pages. Mike Iliffe held things together with a fine 45, well supported by Nigel Thirkell's 36, but the rest of us rather let the side down, and 131 always looked a touch light against a side of West Farleigh's calibre.

Still, cricket is a funny old game, and for a while it looked like we might defend the indefensible. Terry Donovan bowled like a man possessed, ripping through the top order for 6-57 with four miserly maidens thrown in for good measure, and even accounted for Norman Underdown without so much as troubling the scorer. I chipped in with 1-8, and Thirkell picked up another for 34, but Peter Cooper's 38, well supported by J Archer's 34 and Simon Day's 22, steadied West Farleigh's ship just enough, and they scraped home at 133/8 from 39.5 overs.

There was, at least, one moment of real pride amid the gloom: Mike Iliffe brought up 2,000 runs for the club, a landmark worth toasting whatever the scoreboard says. Thirkell continues to lead our run charts for the season with 599, and I'm pleased to say the wickets column still has my name on it with 61. As for the match itself — beaten by two wickets, fair and square. Not our day. Heads up, back to the nets.
