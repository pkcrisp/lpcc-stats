🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1961 · 1961 — 1 match

Sat 30 Sep 1961 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformLLWDW

Saturday 30 September 1961, and we rattled up the road to Staplehurst for what proved a fittingly diplomatic finish to a fine day's cricket. Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in taking first knock — and for once the skipper's hunch paid off handsomely. Ron Aiston set the tone with a stylish 53, Tony Snook matched him run for run with a half-century of his own, and J Mills saw us home unbeaten on 52 to leave us purring at 180 for 2 from our 34 overs. Not a bad afternoon's work with the bat, even if I say so myself.

Staplehurst's reply was a different shape of innings altogether. P Hodges dug in for a defiant 56, well supported by D Thirkell's 23 and J Lefevre's 20, while at the other end Jack Eastland and K Hyde trudged back without troubling the scorers. I managed to prise out a couple myself, finishing with 2 for 28, though the day's real damage was done by Michael Chambers, whose 3 for 34 led the attack, with Alan Tucker (2 for 22, and two miserly maidens besides) squeezing the life out of the middle overs. Staplehurst crawled to 156 for 7 from their 37 overs, leaving us short of victory but comfortably on top of proceedings.

A draw it was, then — honours shared, tea no doubt excellent, and my own season's tally now standing at 66 wickets, top of the 1st XI charts, with Robert Murray's 542 runs holding steady in fourth among the batsmen. All told, a thoroughly satisfactory way to spend a Saturday.

🗓️ 23 Sep – 29 Sep 1961 · 1961 — 2 matches

Sun 24 Sep 1961 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWDLWW

Here's the match report:

Sunday 24th September, and with the toss falling my way I had no hesitation in electing to bat — the ground was true, the sky was kind, and I fancied our top order would enjoy themselves. So it proved. Robert Murray played the innings of the day, a wonderfully composed 67 that anchored everything around him, while I chipped in with 38 alongside a similarly useful 38 from John Crellin — sorry, I mean myself — and John Crellin's own tidy 29 gave the total real substance. We closed on a handsome 197 for 7 from our 42 overs, the sort of score that makes a captain's declaration-less life rather pleasant.

There was more to celebrate than mere runs, mind you. Robert's innings brought up 2,000 career runs for the club — a landmark achieved with typical understatement — and his 67 also eased him past Frank Taylor into 11th on our all-time run-scorers list, which is the kind of thing that gets a nod in the bar rather than a fuss on the field. He now sits proudly atop this season's Married run charts, while Robert Wood leads the Singles with 58 — a friendly rivalry I suspect will run all summer.

As for the cricket itself, honours were shared: a draw, neither side able to force the issue. Frustrating, perhaps, but with milestones tumbling and the sun still out, I shan't complain too loudly. A satisfying afternoon's work, all told.

Sat 23 Sep 1961 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformDLLWD

It was against Bearsted CC that we lost the toss and were put into the field, which on a warm September afternoon is generally the point at which I start composing excuses in my head. As it turned out, none were needed. Ron Parsons anchored the visitors' innings with a fine fifty, well supported by Colin Fleetwood and George Lambell, but Alan Tucker bowled with real purpose to finish with three wickets, and Robert Wood and Terry Whittaker chipped away either side of him to keep the total to a very gettable 169 for 7 from their 43 overs.

Chasing it, we barely broke sweat. I found some rare and welcome form of my own, ably supported by Robert Murray at the other end, and between us we knocked the runs off with more than a few overs to spare, finishing on 170 for the loss of just two. Robert Wood, having bowled his heart out earlier, returned the favour with the bat by contributing precisely nothing — a golden duck, and I'm sure he'll dine out on that story rather less than the rest of us will dine out on it for him.

A personal contribution of 128 not out was, I confess, rather satisfying, and it keeps me handily placed at the top of the season's run charts, with Terry Whittaker still doing the business with the ball as our leading wicket-taker. Eight wickets and two points: a proper thumping, thoroughly earned, and exactly the sort of Saturday that makes the winter months bearable.

Sun 17 Sep 1961 🧢 1st XIv SC Sevenoaks Vine CC · HomeformWDLLW

Lost the toss on a fine Sunday, 17th September, and watched Sevenoaks Vine trot out to bat first — never my favourite outcome, but there are worse fates than a long afternoon in the field with tea to look forward to. R J Pavitt made an infuriating nuisance of himself with a chanceless century, 102 in all, and found a willing ally in R P King's 42, the pair carrying Vine to 199 for 9 off their forty. Ray Howard, bless him, refused to let the game drift entirely away from us, bowling with real discipline for 5 for 60 including three maidens, while John Ames chipped away for 3 for 74 at the other end.

Fortunately Ray Howard, having bowled us back into it, now proceeded to bat us back into it too, with a magnificent 113. I did what I could in support with 32 — a modest contribution, though it keeps me handily placed atop the 1st XI's run charts for the season with 711 so far. We closed on 193 for 8 off forty-five, agonisingly short.

A draw, then — honours shared, nobody quite satisfied, everybody rather pleased all the same. Ray Howard's day, by any fair reckoning, though the whole side can hold its head up.

Sat 16 Sep 1961 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformWWDLL

John Thirkell here, filing this one with a satisfied sigh and slightly cleaner boots than of late.

Bowaters came to Linton Park on a fine September Saturday and I won the toss — took one look at the strip and elected to bat, which I still maintain was the right call, whatever anyone mutters at the bar. We were bowled out for 128 in 37.2 overs, a total that flattered to deceive at times: Robert Wood's 34 was the innings' backbone, with John Francis (31) and Robert Murray (22) chipping in handsomely. Less gloriously, both Terry Whittaker and Brian Relf departed without troubling the scorers — the sort of pair of ducks that has men staring hard into their tea.

Defending 128 always felt like a stretch, but the lads had other ideas. Bowaters were shot out for 94 in 37 overs, William Preston's well-made fifty their only real resistance. At the other end it was carnage — John Woollett, John Evans and Bill Brown all bagged ducks against us — as Alan Tucker produced the spell of the season, 8 for 20 with five wickets and five miserly maidens to his name. I managed to get among the wickets myself, and John Francis and Terry Whittaker mopped up the rest between them.

A fine afternoon all round, with Robert Wood's 34 easing him past Arthur Cornwallis to 21st on the club's all-time run charts, and my own tally for the summer now standing at 679 with Whittaker's 63 wickets leading our attack. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a 34-run win to send us home content.
