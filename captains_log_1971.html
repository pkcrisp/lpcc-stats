🗓️ 25 Sep – 01 Oct 1971 · 1971 — 1 match

Sat 25 Sep 1971 🧢 1st XIv RX Roy Clarke XI · HomeformWWDDW

Sitting here on the veranda with a cup of tea, I can report that it was a proper cricketing afternoon at Linton Park, and rarely have seven wickets felt so thoroughly deserved. We put Roy Clarke XI in, and they laboured to 135 all out in 39.5 overs, M Waghorn the pick of them with 31 and A Pooley chipping in with 20. J Stevens, bless him, went back to the pavilion without so much as troubling the scorers. The real story, though, was Paul Bowles, who bowled like a man possessed to return 6-36 with four maidens thrown in for good measure — the sort of spell that has opposition batsmen checking the fixture list for next year. Nigel Thirkell and David T Lowen chipped in with a wicket apiece to tidy up the rest.

With that platform, chasing 136 should have been a formality, and so it proved, though not without the odd wobble, as we reached 138/3 in 42 overs for the win. Paul Bowles was again the story, following his bowling heroics with an unbeaten 71 to see us home, well supported by David T Lowen's 31. I chipped in with a small contribution of my own along the way, for what it was worth alongside such company. Fourteen byes did rather suggest poor Cliff Anscombe had a testing afternoon behind the stumps, and no doubt he'll hear about it in the bar.

A word for the history books: Bowles's 71 sees him leapfrog Robert Murray into 7th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and he now leads both the 1971 run and wicket charts for the 1st XI — 612 runs and 56 wickets and counting. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a thoroughly good day to be captain.

🗓️ 18 Sep – 24 Sep 1971 · 1971 — 2 matches

Sun 19 Sep 1971 🧢 1st XIv HC Harrietsham CC · HomeformWWWDD

I called correctly on Sunday and had no hesitation putting Harrietsham in the field first — a decision that, for once, didn't come back to haunt me. We posted a tidy 176/7 from our 51 overs, with Cliff Anscombe leading the charge on 39, myself chipping in with 37, and Paul Bowles adding a brisk 29. Not every innings was a thing of beauty, mind — poor Benny Bell endured the walk of shame for a golden duck, and I dare say he'll be hearing about it in the bar for weeks yet.

If our batting was solid, our bowling that afternoon was something closer to demolition. Harrietsham never remotely settled, subsiding to 49 all out in just 20.5 overs. Paul Bowles, fresh from his runs, turned his arm over to devastating effect, spinning through their order for 5-28, while Allan Cooper strangled the innings from the other end with a miserly 4-7 and five maidens to his name. Jon Harris weighed in with 1-11, and Dennis Stones, Micky Tong and Keith Chaney all trooped back without so much as a run to show for their troubles — three ducks in one innings is not something you see every Sunday.

A win by 127 runs, then, and a thoroughly good day for the club. With Andrew Moss now sitting proudly atop the 1971 run charts and Paul Bowles leading the wickets by some distance, the season has a pleasingly settled look to it. Harrietsham, I fear, will not remember this fixture fondly.

Sat 18 Sep 1971 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformLWWWD

Lost the toss on a fine September Saturday and duly found myself leading the troops into the field rather than out to bat — the story of my captaincy career in one sentence. West Farleigh made themselves at home, R Woolley top-scoring with a polished 48, well supported by Colin Clark's 36 and Peter Cooper's unbeaten 35, the openers putting on 66 for the first wicket before we finally prised one out. They closed on 201/6 from their 45 overs, a total that felt distinctly optimistic from the boundary rope. Paul Bowles was superb, 4-62 and now up to 10th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list, past Frank Taylor — a fine piece of company to keep. Nigel Thirkell chipped in with 2-42, and Ron Hickman, all miserly discipline, sent down three maidens.

Chasing 202, we made a fair fist of it without ever threatening to get there, closing on 136/6 from our 46. Tim Thirkell was the rock, 47 not out, while I managed 28 and Andrew Moss a tidy 21 of his own — enough, remarkably, to vault him past Frank Taylor into 17th on the run-scorers' list, and he now sits proudly atop this season's charts with 544. We wobbled from 19-1 to 38-4 in a hurry, but a stubborn sixth-wicket stand and a defiant tail wag of 48 saw us home to safety. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 12 byes escaping his grasp. Honours shared, then — a draw that flattered us more than we deserved, but I'll take it.

I toed the line at Mersham Hatch on Sunday 12th September with the toss going our way, and I opted to have first use of the ball — a decision that, in classic form, I'd spend the rest of the afternoon second-guessing.

Moss now leads our 1971 run charts with 523, and Harris sits fourth among the bowlers with 33 wickets.

A draw, then — honours shared, and perhaps fitting that neither side could quite land the decisive blow.

Sat 11 Sep 1971 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformWDLWW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 11 September 1971. Charles Arkcoll at home.

Lost the toss, asked to bowl first, which on a day like this always feels like being handed the washing-up before you've had your tea. Still, we made merry with the ball. Charles Arkcoll were skittled for 115 in 43.5 overs, and while Brian Aiston gave us all a fright with a fine 80, the rest of their order rather crumbled around him — three ducks in there, poor devils. Jon Harris chipped in with 2 for 3 and David T Lowen 2 for 28, and by the time we came off the boundary rope had earned its keep.

Chasing 116 was never going to trouble us unduly, and so it proved. David T Lowen, having already done his bit with the ball, then set about the bowling with a composed 55 not out, while Tim Thirkell played the perfect foil with an unbeaten 33. I contributed my own small mite along the way, more spectator than hero as we cruised home on 116 for 3 in 41.5 overs.

A red-letter day all round — Allan Cooper reached 50 career wickets, and continues to lead the season's bowling charts with 43, while Andrew Moss sits proudly atop the run charts with 505. Seven-wicket win, two points banked, and tea tasted all the sweeter for it.

Mon 30 Aug 1971 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · AwayformWDDWD

Skipper's Log — Monday 30 August 1971. The road took us to Bearsted, and with the coin landing kindly I did what any captain with a firm belief in fresh pitches and thin evidence does: stuck us in.

It did not, on this occasion, repay the faith. We were bowled out for 117 in 43.2 overs, a total that flattered to deceive largely because David T Lowen batted like the sensible adult in the room, making 39 while the rest of us treated the crease as something to be visited briefly and left without fuss. Richard Ashby and Alan Wilkes joined me in that peculiar fraternity of the golden duck — three ninepence, no tuppence, and I can only apologise for setting such a poor example from the top.

With the ball, though, we scrapped. Bearsted crept to 120 for 8 off 51.4 overs, losing an early wicket at 7 and wobbling agreeably from 87 for 3 to 96 for 6 — Michael Livingstone and R. Parsons both back in the pavilion without a run to show for it. Colin Fleetwood's 43 and Denis Cleggett's unbeaten 36 were the difference in the end. Alan Wilkes was superb, 4 for 49 with three tight maidens, backed up by Jon Harris (3 for 31) and Paul Bowles (1 for 28).

Beaten by two wickets, then — desperately close, and no complaints. A captain who blobs after asking the openers to do the hard work has few grounds for grievance. We'll regroup, remember how to hold a bat, and come back stronger next week.
