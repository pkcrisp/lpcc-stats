The Duck Club Bulletin — 28th September 1952

1 duck
Week 23 · 28 Sep 1952
A single entry only graces this week's ledger, but the exclusive zero club never turns away a new subscription, however modest the week's takings. Singles - Ray Tippen, batting at number two in a home fixture against Linton Park CC, was undone by a smart piece of work behind the stumps — caught by John Taylor off the bowling of Gordon Fenner — and made the familiar walk back to the pavilion. It is the first duck's egg of his season and the third of a career now standing at seven-and-twenty innings, placing him 55th of the 625 names inscribed upon the club's roll of dubious distinction. Mr Tippen is also, from a season long since concluded, a member of the Retirement Duck honour board — a gentleman who once bowed out of the game upon a nought — though this week's failure is no such farewell, merely another instalment in a long and honourable acquaintance with the art of not scoring. He now sits level with A Kent, who compiled his own three ducks in a brief season of 1893 and was that year's Duck of the Year, kindred spirits separated by the better part of sixty summers yet united upon the very same tally. The Week in Summary One match, one wicket falling for nought, and the credit goes to Gordon Fenner — a name that will be seen again below, for he numbers among this season's most industrious duck-hunters, having evidently dispensed more trouble with the ball than he has met with the bat. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 34 innings, 8 ducks (23.53%) - Bob Arnold — 13 innings, 3 ducks (23.08%) - Gordon Fenner — 13 innings, 3 ducks (23.08%) - Don Hartridge — 23 innings, 3 ducks (13.04%) - Gerald Wright — 5 innings, 2 ducks (40.00%) A modest week's harvest for the club's chroniclers, but the eleven take the field again before long, and the roll of dubious distinction is seldom short of volunteers. Until then, warm and solemn congratulations to Mr Tippen upon this latest instalment, and every confidence that the next fixture will bring fresh additions to the ledger.

The Duck Club Gazette — 20th to 21st September, 1952

1 duck
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1952 – 21 Sep 1952
🦆 A quiet week upon the Linton Park square, ladies and gentlemen — the eleven, by and large, played the straighter bat this weekend, and the Duck Club's tellers were left twiddling their thumbs through the greater part of two fixtures. But the club never closes its doors entirely, and one gentleman of the 1st XI has this week paid his dues to the exclusive zero club. 1st XI Away in the field against Hayle Party — beg pardon, Hayle Place CC — on the 21st, Richard Harris strode out at number seven and departed caught H Gosling, bowled G Ashby, without troubling the scorers. Not a golden duck, mind — he faced at least the one delivery before submitting to the inevitable, which in this club counts as a plucky show of resistance. This is his second duck of the summer of 1952 and his eleventh in a career now stretching to fifty-one innings, which plants him a very respectable 5th of 625 on the club's all-time roll of dishonour. He joins none other than the venerable Bert Buckoke — a two-time Duck of the Year, no less, in 1928 and 1950 — on exactly eleven career ducks apiece, and there can be no finer company in which to sit becalmed on a duck's egg. The week in summary Two matches played, and but a single duck laid between them — a lean week indeed for the collectors among us, proof if proof were needed that the art of not scoring is not to be rushed. No pairs, no golden ducks, no family affairs to report this time round. The ducks, it seems, took the weekend off — though Mr Harris was good enough to keep the tradition alive single-handed. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. John Thirkell — 33 innings, 8 ducks, 24.24% 2. Gordon Fenner — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.00% 3. Bob Arnold — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% 4. Don Hartridge — 23 innings, 3 ducks, 13.04% 5. Gerald Wright — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% A word of caution to Mr Wright, who at the head of the percentage table finds himself on a sticky wicket indeed — two failures from four attempts is a rate that would make even the most seasoned duck-hunter blush. Until the eleven next take the field, the Duck Club watches on with quiet optimism, for there is always another fixture, and always another batsman destined for the long walk back to the pavilion with nothing to show for his trouble. Play up, and mind the zero.

The Duck Club Gazette — 13th and 14th September 1952

0 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1952 – 14 Sep 1952
A quiet week at the crease, gentlemen. Two fixtures were played across the weekend, and for once the exclusive zero club found no new candidates for its rolls. Not a single duck's egg was laid, not one batsman was required to make that shortest and most melancholy of walks back to the pavilion. Whether this speaks to a sudden outbreak of straight bats and sound judgement, or merely to the bowlers having an off couple of days, the committee declines to speculate. The art of not scoring, it seems, took its rest this week — though the club has every confidence the ducks will return before the season is out. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard The season's honours remain keenly contested, and this week's inactivity has done nothing to trouble the standings: - John Thirkell — 32 innings, 8 ducks, 25.0% — the season's most reliable attender of the zero club, sitting a comfortable distance clear at the head of affairs - Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% — a fine strike rate of failure, rather better than his rivals manage in their sleep - Bob Arnold — 13 innings, 3 ducks, 23.08% — quietly keeping pace, a plucky knock away from respectability on any given Saturday - Don Hartridge — 22 innings, 3 ducks, 13.64% — the most measured of the leading pack, his ducks arriving with a certain dignified restraint - Gerald Wright — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.0% — fewest innings of the five yet half of them ending at nought, a sticky wicket indeed for so brief an acquaintance with the crease Members will note the table stands exactly where it did — a week's grace for the bowlers, no doubt, rather than any lasting reform among the batting order. The eleven return to the field next weekend, and the committee trusts that the ducks, having evaded capture this week, will not make a habit of it. Should any gentleman fancy his chances of a fresh entry to the rolls of dubious distinction, the wicket awaits him. Until then, we remain, as ever, watchful.

THE DUCK CLUB GAZETTE — Saturday, the 6th of September, 1952

0 ducks
Week 20 · 6 Sep 1952
A quiet week, gentlemen, and a rare one at that. Your correspondent has combed the scorebook from Saturday's fixture with the diligence of a man searching for his spectacles, and must report, with no small measure of disappointment, that not a single member of the eleven troubled the umpires for the zero. Every batsman who walked to the crease walked back again having troubled the scorers at least once, however modestly. The exclusive zero club, so often swelled on a Saturday afternoon, gains not one new subscription this week. Whether this reflects a sudden and alarming outbreak of application at the crease, a touch of luck on a summer wicket, or merely the bowlers having an off day, history does not record. The art of not scoring, so lovingly cultivated by this club's membership, was for once left unpractised. One suspects it was but a brief truce, and that ambition for the duck's egg will return with the first ball of the next fixture. A single match played, not a duck to show for it — a sticky wicket indeed for the chronicler with nothing to chronicle. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 30 innings, 8 ducks, 26.67% - Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% - Bob Arnold — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.00% - Don Hartridge — 22 innings, 3 ducks, 13.64% - Gerald Wright — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% Mr Wright's rate of one duck in every two visits to the crease remains the season's most remarkable statistic, a consistency of a very particular kind that lesser men could only aspire to. Thirkell continues to lead the field on sheer volume, a man of considerable, if unwanted, experience in the walk back to the pavilion. The tail, as ever, will wag again before long. Members are cordially invited to the next fixture, where the eleven's finest are confidently expected to resume their pursuit of the duck's egg with the enthusiasm for which this club is justly renowned. Until then — a plucky knock to all, and may it not last.

The LPCC Duck Club Gazette — for the week ending the 31st of August, 1952

3 ducks
Week 19 · 30 Aug 1952 – 31 Aug 1952
A modest week for the exclusive zero club, but a memorable one, for one member of the 1st XI contrived to visit us twice inside a single weekend. 1st XI John Thirkell opened proceedings on the 30th, away to Hunton Wanderers CC. Sent in at four, he was bowled by R Venner without troubling the scorers — his seventh duck of the season and the thirteenth of a career that stands second in the whole Club register of 625 hopefuls. He was not to be outdone by himself. The very next afternoon, at home to A Levick's XI and promoted to first wicket down, he was bowled again, this time by John Ames, for his eighth of the summer and fourteenth for his career — a grim pair taken across two days and two grounds, the sort of feat that asks a great deal of a man's patience and rather more of his sense of humour. In arriving at fourteen he draws level with the great Frank Sutton, who carried the same tally through a career from 1925 to 1949 and was twice crowned Duck of the Year for his trouble. Thirkell, for his part, already wears this year's Duck of the Year sash, sits proudly among the top ten Lone Ducks, the top ten Homebody Ducks and the top ten Ducks on the Road, and shares the honours board twice over in Quacking Together, with both Bob Arnold and William Buck. H Newport followed him back to the pavilion on the same afternoon, number ten, caught D Latham off the bowling of the ever-busy R Venner, for nought — his second duck of the season and sixth of a career now twenty-four innings long, good enough for seventeenth place in the Club's grim ledger. He sits on precisely the same career total as D Sells, who plied his trade between 1943 and 1944, gathered his own six ducks in fourteen innings, and was Duck of the Year in 1943 besides. Newport is, we might add, a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck club, an honour recording some distant and presumably premature farewell rather than any valediction of his own this week. The week in brief Two fixtures, three ducks, and one gentleman of the 1st XI shouldering two of them single-handed — proof, if proof were wanted, that the art of not scoring is best practised with commitment. R Venner of Hunton Wanderers CC may fairly claim the ball of the week, having accounted for both of Saturday's victims. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 29 innings, 8 ducks, 27.59% - Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks, 27.27% - Bob Arnold — 12 innings, 3 ducks, 25.00% - Don Hartridge — 22 innings, 3 ducks, 13.64% - Gerald Wright — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.00% The season stands at a lively pitch, and with fixtures yet to be played there is every confidence the honours board will find fresh names to add. Members are warmly encouraged to attend the next match, bat straight, and, failing that, to fail with style.

The Duck Club Gazette: 23rd–24th August, 1952

4 ducks
Week 18 · 23 Aug 1952 – 24 Aug 1952
Two fixtures took the field this week, and one of them proved uncommonly generous to the exclusive zero club. Four eggs were laid in a single innings against Charles Arkcoll on the Saturday, while Sunday's match passed the ducks by entirely, to the quiet relief of at least eleven gentlemen. The First Eleven - D Simmons, in at first wicket down, was caught by Humphrey Morris off the bowling of Stan Wratten — a plain enough dismissal, honestly earned. His third career duck in fifteen innings settles him at 48th of 624 on the club's roll of dishonour, drawing him level with the venerable A Kent, whose own trio of noughts were gathered in a single unhappy season back in 1893. - Three places along, Gordon Bugge fell to the very same Stan Wratten, whose afternoon was plainly a rewarding one for all the wrong reasons. This too was his third career duck, and he joins Simmons in keeping curious company with A Kent on that count — two members of the present eleven, fifty-nine years and one bowler apart, arriving at the identical figure by rather different roads. - At number six, Bob Arnold was bowled by Gordon Fenner for his third duck of the season and his seventh of a career that continues to write itself firmly into the honours boards. He remains a proud holder of Ducks in a Row for four consecutive eggs laid without troubling the scorer, and stands paired for perpetuity in Only Two Quacks with Alan Wilkes and in Quacking Together with John Thirkell — evidently a man who prefers not to fall alone. His seventh career duck draws him level with the formidable A Startup, whose own seven noughts between 1868 and 1882 earned three Duck of the Year honours and a place in the five-consecutive company of Ducks in a Row — a target Arnold may yet have designs upon. - Last man in, Gerald Wright was bowled by John Ames for his second duck of the season and his second in a mere four innings played, a strike rate for failure that would give a lesser man pause. He joins A S Day on that same tally of two. In sum, four wickets fell to nought in the one innings on Saturday — Messrs Simmons, Bugge, Arnold and Wright strung from number two clean through to number eleven, wanting only the middle order to make the collapse entire. Gordon Fenner, who appears further down this very report as a leading light of the season's duck-getting, spent his Saturday afternoon adding to the ledger from the bowling end instead, claiming Arnold's wicket while presumably hoping his own name might rest quiet a while longer. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 6 ducks in 27 innings (22.22%) - Gordon Fenner — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) - Bob Arnold — 3 ducks in 11 innings (27.27%) - Don Hartridge — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.0%) - Gerald Wright — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.0%) The eleven take the field again shortly, and the club secretary has every confidence the zero club's books will not stay quiet for long. Members are warmly invited to attend, watch, and — should fortune so incline them — apply for membership themselves.

The Duck Report — the weekend of the 3rd and 4th of August, 1952

2 ducks
Week 15 · 3 Aug 1952 – 4 Aug 1952
A quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club, but what it lacked in numbers it made up for in doggedness. Two matches, two ducks, and both laid by the same unfortunate hand. 1st XI Number ten Gordon Fenner opened proceedings on the Saturday, stumped by E Webb off the bowling of D Thomas — undone not by rash stroke but by a wandering foot outside his ground. His second duck's egg of the season, and the eighth of a career now stretching to sixty-three innings, which places him level with the venerable George Champion (74 innings, 1886–1912) on exactly eight career ducks — fine company for a fine failure. Curiously, on this very day in 1903, one H G Smith trudged back for nought against the same opposition, 1st Innings Rochester CC, a reminder that the club has been repeating itself for the better part of fifty years. A day was not enough to shake the habit. Sent in last man on the Sunday against Lord Cornwallis's XI, Fenner was bowled by J Larking for his third duck of the season and his ninth of a career that now stands ninth on the all-time list of some six hundred and twenty-three names — drawing level, by neat coincidence, with B Westover's wartime tally of nine. The scorebook records the weekend plainly as a pair, that grimmest of doubles, two ducks laid within twenty-four hours of one another, though happily neither was a golden one — Fenner played himself in each time before the axe fell, triumph through adversity of a very particular kind. It may be noted, for those who like their history layered, that on this same date back in 1869 one W H Fryer made his own duck against Horsmonden CC — the ritual, it seems, is older than the club itself. Members with long memories will also recall that Fenner's brother Joe carries five career ducks of his own — evidently a family with an inherited gift for the art of not scoring. The week in brief Two matches, two ducks, one player — a rare instance of a gentleman keeping the whole week's dismissals entirely to himself. Whether this counts as generosity or simple bad luck is left to the reader's judgement, though the sticky wicket of consistency clearly runs in the Fenner blood. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. John Thirkell — 25 innings, 6 ducks, 24.0% 2. Gordon Fenner — 8 innings, 3 ducks, 37.5% 3. Don Hartridge — 16 innings, 3 ducks, 18.75% 4. P Ruddy — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% 5. Bob Arnold — 10 innings, 2 ducks, 20.0% The tail of the season is far from wagged. Members are cordially reminded that the next fixture offers every batsman a fresh opportunity to add his name to the honours board, and the Duck Club, ever hospitable, stands ready to welcome him.

We look forward with quiet confidence to next week's fixture furnishing rather more material for these pages. The Duck Club Gazette — a report from the 12th and 13th of July, 1952

0 ducks
Week 12 · 12 Jul 1952 – 13 Jul 1952
