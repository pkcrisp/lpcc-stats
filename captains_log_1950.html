🗓️ 02 Sep – 08 Sep 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 02 Sep 1950 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformDDDWL

John Thirkell won the toss on a bright Saturday and chose to bat — the right call, as it turned out, though 148 all out from 44.2 overs felt like scraping the barrel rather than raiding the larder. D Simmons led the way with a fine 39, Arthur Cradduck batted sensibly for 30 not out, and I contributed 24 down the order. Brian Relf and Bert Buckoke both fell for ducks, proof that not every innings can be a classic, but between us we cobbled together a total that felt defendable rather than daunting.

And defend it we did, emphatically. Bowaters were bowled out for a paltry 48 in 20 overs, with Henry Martin, Stephen Jarrett and John Ibbotson all departing without troubling the scorers. William Hammond's unbeaten 24 was the only real resistance on offer. I had the day of my life with the ball — 7 for 19, four maidens included — while H Newport chipped in admirably with 3 for 29. Between us we barely gave Bowaters a run to call their own.

We ran out winners by 100 runs, which even by the generous standards of village cricket counts as a proper thumping. A modest total defended in style, a few ducks forgiven, and a captain rather pleased with his afternoon's work — all in all, a very good day for Linton Park.

🗓️ 08 Jul – 14 Jul 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 08 Jul 1950 🧢 1st XIv HC Hayle Place CC · HomeformLDDDW

Saturday the 8th of July found us at home to Hayle Place, and I won the toss and sent them in — a decision that, for once, didn't come back to haunt me. Their top order wobbled straight away at 5 for 1, before Gosling made a fine 39, well supported by Ewell's 23; but from 96 for 5 the innings fell off a cliff to 98 for 8 in a hurry, and poor Lockyer and Pilbeam trudged back without troubling the scorers. I finished with 5 for 45, three maidens included, Basil Sears chipped in a lovely 4 for 17, and John --'s Taylor picked up 1 for 37 as they were all out for 100 in 37.3 overs — a thoroughly satisfactory morning's work.

Batting, we stumbled early ourselves, 0 for 1 hardly the ideal start, but Baker played beautifully for 66, well backed by Arthur Cradduck's unbeaten 37 and John Thirkell's 22, the pair adding a sturdy 61 for the sixth wicket to see us safely home. John --'s Taylor's afternoon with the bat brought only a golden duck — swings and roundabouts, given his day with the ball. We were all out for 197 in 41.1 overs, comfortably the superior total.

Milestones were plentiful: Thirkell's 22 took him past Joe Fenner into 12th on the club's all-time run charts, while John --'s Taylor's solitary wicket eased him past E C Streatfield into 17th among the wicket-takers — both still leading this season's charts, Thirkell on 343 runs, Taylor on 34 wickets. All told, 197 to their 100 tells its own story: a thoroughly deserved win, and just the sort of afternoon that makes the teas taste sweeter.

🗓️ 01 Jul – 07 Jul 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 01 Jul 1950 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformWLDDD

Toss lost, as ever, and Bearsted chose to bat — I've stopped taking it personally. Vic Pound top-scored gamely for them with an unbeaten 67, but around him wickets tumbled: 7 for 1 became 16 for 4 in a hurry, with Gordon A Hawkins, Norman Tiley and John Colegate all trooping back without a run to their name. John --'s Taylor did the damage with 4 for 47 (five maidens in there too), C Horton chipped in with 3 for 43, and I sneaked among the wickets myself with 2 for 5 — figures I shan't pretend not to be pleased with. Bearsted were shot out for 99 in 35.5 overs.

Chasing it down should have been a formality, and for a while it was: John --'s Taylor and his opening partner put on 72 for the first wicket before John went on to a fine half-century of 54, well supported by Basil Sears's 30. Then, as is tradition here, we made it needlessly dramatic — 106 for 2 became 109 for 5 in a flurry, with poor Bert Buckoke collecting a golden duck. We scrambled home by 5 wickets regardless.

Two names for the honours board: Basil Sears's 30 took him past E- Humphreys to 14th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and John --'s Taylor's four wickets moved him past S Christopherson to 18th among the wicket-takers — this with 249 runs, second in the season's run charts, and a league-leading 33 wickets. A scrappy, well-earned win by 5 wickets. Onwards.

🗓️ 24 Jun – 30 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 2 matches

Sun 25 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv DR Desert Rats · HomeformLWLDD

Sunday 25 June, the Desert Rats came to Linton Park, and I won the toss and sent them in — a decision that for once looked rather clever. They mustered 121 all out in 35.1 overs, T Hendley's 31 the backbone of it, with X Dominey (26) and X Thorndycraft (20) chipping in respectably. But once we got among them it was carnage of the best sort — 47 for 1 became 48 for 4 before anyone had time to put the kettle on, with E Wright, X Baker and X Dove all trudging back without troubling the scorers. Robert Muir was the chief tormentor, spinning his way to 5 for 33, while I chipped in with 3 for 25, and H Newport, miserly as ever with two maidens, took 2 for 42.

Our reply was rather more of a rollercoaster. William Buck anchored things beautifully with an unbeaten 57, and a stand of 52 for the sixth wicket looked to have seen us home — until a collapse from 20 for 1 to 29 for 4 threatened to undo all the good work. Poor Don Hartridge fell for a duck, joining the club's less illustrious ledger, and we closed on 108 for 7 from our 33 overs.

Small mercies amid the wobble: my 13 runs nudged me past John Thirkell into 12th on the club's all-time run-scoring list, and I now sit fourth in this season's charts with 184 runs, with Muir fifth among the wicket-takers on 11. In the end neither side could force it home, and honours were shared — a fair reflection, I'd say, of an afternoon where both bat and ball had their say.

Sat 24 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv T Tovil · HomeformWLWLD

Right, then. Here's the report:

There is a particular satisfaction in a Saturday that ends all square, and against Tovil we earned ours the hard way. Having asked our visitors to bat first, I can't say the morning began with any great sense of destiny — but Basil Sears and I settled into a rhythm that Tovil never quite escaped, sharing the wickets between us as their innings folded from a promising 120 for 7 to all out for 128. My figures of 5 for 41, three of them maidens, were the pick of the day, with Sears not far behind on 4 for 29. S. Carrington's fifty for the visitors deserved better support than it got.

Our reply was the sort of innings that keeps a captain's hair greying prematurely. Sears again led from the front with a fine 37, and I chipped in with 26 as our second-wicket stand added a crucial 50 — before the middle order contrived to lose four wickets between 91 and 98 in a flurry that rather undid our good work. Poor Arthur Cradduck departed for a golden duck, and we limped to 104 for 7 off our 33 overs.

Honours shared, then — though there was quiet cheer in the pavilion for Sears, whose 37 nudged him past Frank Sutton and into 16th on the club's all-time run-scorers list. A draw is rarely a thing to celebrate, but with bat and ball both playing their part, I'll happily call this one an honourable stalemate.

🗓️ 17 Jun – 23 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sun 18 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv HB Hambros Bank · HomeformWWLWL

Sunday 18 June, Hambros Bank the visitors, and for once the toss went my way — no hesitation in taking first knock, and by close of play I was rather pleased with myself for it. Tony Levick played the innings of the day, an unbeaten 61 that gave the whole order something to build around, with Stan Wratten (33) and Richard Harris (30) chipping in handsomely. Poor Joe Fenner will want to forget his duck in a hurry, but the tail more than made up for it, wagging its way to a final flourish of 90 that left the Hambros fielders looking thoroughly fed up. We closed on a healthy 201 for 7 from our fifty overs.

With the ball in hand, we set about restricting the visitors, and restrict them we did — 81 for 7 from 38 overs, with only G Clarke (29) offering any real resistance. S Andrews and R Murrell both departed without troubling the scorers. Don Hartridge was pick of the attack with 2 for 19, John Thirkell chipped in with 1 for 2, and Stan Wratten took 1 for 7 — a wicket that pleasingly nudged him past Herbert Sunley into 12th on the club's all-time list. I added three tidy maidens of my own, form that keeps me atop the season's wicket charts with 29, while Thirkell now leads the run column with 321.

Sunday's honours, though, were shared — neither side able to force the issue. A draw, then: a fair reflection of a match evenly contested, and no bad way to spend a June afternoon.

🗓️ 10 Jun – 16 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 3 matches

Mon 12 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv RS Rochester Maths School · HomeformDWWLW

Beaten by 15 runs on Monday, and I'll confess the toss went our way before anything else did — I looked at that Rochester Maths School attack, decided we'd bat, and for a good while felt rather clever about it.

We made a fist of it too: 188 for 3 off our 44, built on an opening stand of 66 that gave the innings a proper foundation. John Thirkell was the class of the day, a fluent 56, with D Simmons unbeaten on 40 and yours truly chipping in with 35 as the third-wicket pair put on a crucial 78. All very tidy from the captain's chair, even if I say so myself.

Defending it was another matter. Rochester openers put on 54 and for a while looked entirely too comfortable, Jameson (35), Lambkin (32) and Finch (30) all getting starts without ever cutting loose. Then, mercifully, the wheels came off theirs rather than ours — 158 for 7 became 173 all out in the blink of an eye, William Newport chiselling away for 3-69 while I had one of those afternoons with the ball, 7-57 including nine maidens, that a captain dares not admit he enjoyed quite so much. It nudged me past A Eltham into 21st on the club's all-time wicket list, and Thirkell's half-century pushed him past Joe Fenner into 12th for runs — not a bad day for the record books, whatever the scoreboard said.

Fifteen runs short in the end. Chins up — we'll be better for it next week.

Gordon Fenner top-scored with a classy 69 not out, Stan Wratten weighed in with 47, and I was rather pleased to lead from the front with an unbeaten fifty of my own. We eventually got home by four wickets from 309 for 6 — comfortably more runs than were strictly necessary, but nobody at Linton Park has ever complained about batting practice. A fine team performance, and a thoroughly good day for the club.

Sat 10 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv LC Lloyds Social Club · AwayformDWDWW

Left arm over the wicket, straight through the covers — here's the report.

Saturday saw us pack the kit bag for Lloyds Social Club, and I'll confess I had a quiet fancy we might come home with something to show for it. We won the toss, put them in, and watched R- Thompson play the innings of the day, 69 well-made runs, with L- Luscombe (29 not out) and G- Williams (23) lending sturdy support. Their fourth-wicket pair put on a stubborn 73 that did most of the damage, and they closed on 173/7 from their forty overs — S- Jarrett at least departed cheaply, for whatever comfort that offered. Basil Sears was the pick of our attack with 3-28, John Thirkell and D Simmons chipped in with a wicket apiece, and I was pleased enough to keep things tight with three maidens of my own.

Chasing 173 was always going to ask questions of our batting, and we answered them badly. From 59-6 to 60-9 in what felt like the time it takes to pour a tea, we subsided to 63 all out in 22 overs — a proper collapse, the sort that makes you stare hard at your bootlaces. Joe Fenner, Bert Buckoke and E Lockyer all made twin ducks between them, and poor Norman Young had a torrid time behind the stumps, conceding eight byes for good measure. One bright note: Don Hartridge's 11 runs nudged him past Basil Sears into 19th on the club's all-time run-scorers list.

A chastening 110-run defeat, then. Nothing for it but heads up, and back to the nets.

Sat 03 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv OC Old Williamsonians CC · HomeformDLDWD

Saturday 3 June 1950 dawned fair at Linton Park, and with Old Williamsonians CC the visitors, I won the toss and had no hesitation in electing to bat — a decision I stand by entirely, and will happily remind anyone who'll listen.

Joe Fenner played the innings of the day, an unbeaten 51 that anchored everything around him, with William Buck's 24 and Don Hartridge's unbeaten 24 giving the innings a proper spine. Not every man could find his range, mind — E Lockyer's duck will be quietly filed in the ledger, as these things always are. We closed on 136 for 6 from our 33 overs, a total that felt, at tea, like it might just be enough.

Don Hartridge was magnificent with the ball, 4 for 16, backed superbly by John Thirkell's 3 for 8 and Joe Fenner's 1 for 11, while I did my bit tidily enough with a couple of maidens. Only B Weatherhogg, unbeaten on 26, offered any real resistance as they folded for 97.

A 39-run win, then, and a thoroughly good day for the club.

Richard Harris and Bob Arnold both fell without scoring, proof that ducks come in pairs as readily as magpies. Sears's 25 was quietly historic, nudging him past A W Fulcher to 20th on the club's all-time run-charts — a fine, unfussy sort of immortality.

With John --'s Taylor now second in the season's run charts and top of the wicket charts, the lad is fast becoming indispensable. A draw, then — honours shared, pride intact, tea presumably excellent.

Sun 28 May 1950 🧢 1st XIv FC Folkestone CC · HomeformDWDLD

Sunday 28 May 1950, and Folkestone rolled up to Linton Park with the sun out and, worse for us, the coin landing their way. Asked to bowl first, we watched John Lynch and his opening partner put on 90 before we so much as glimpsed the ball doing anything for us. Lynch went on to a fine 96, Cecil Blacklocks chipped in with 29 and J Court 28, and 184/7 from 41 overs looked a handsome total, if not quite the mountain it might have been — a wobble from 162/3 to 169/6 gave us hope, R Barrett departing without so much as a nibble at the strike. Robert Muir was magnificent, 4-23, and I managed a couple myself for 56, though the figures weren't the flattering kind.

Chasing 185, we were made to work for it, losing our first wicket for just the one run, but Maurice Fenner and John Thirkell put matters right in spectacular fashion. Fenner's unbeaten 106 was a joy to watch from the boundary — and, I'm told, edges him past Joe Fenner into 15th on the club's all-time run charts, which is the sort of thing that gets you a pint bought for you at the bar. Thirkell's 74 was the perfect foil, the pair sharing 146 for the third wicket. Ray Tippen made a nought, and as for the captain — well, a golden duck is not the example one hopes to set, but leadership sometimes means taking one for the side in more ways than one.

We got home by four wickets. A fine team performance carried the day, and I shall claim credit for the toss I lost and say nothing more about my batting.

🗓️ 13 May – 19 May 1950 · 1950 — 2 matches

I lost the toss, which these days feels less like misfortune and more like tradition, and so we trooped out into the field on a fine Sunday to see what Yalding could conjure. They set about their task with more application than elegance, W. Osborn did leak 15 byes behind the stumps, which he will no doubt hear about at training, and my four wickets nudged me past Dennis Smith into 7th on the club's all-time list — a nice bonus on a good day. We cruised home by seven wickets. Chase completed, pride intact, tea well earned.

That confidence took a battering. We stumbled to 94 all out in 32 overs, the innings unravelling from a modest 11 for 1 into a distinctly wobbly 27 for 4 before we knew what had hit us. Only my own knock of 30 kept the scorebook from looking truly indecent.
