🗓️ 02 Sep – 08 Sep 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 02 Sep 1950 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformDDDWW

I elected to bat when I won the toss on Saturday against Bowaters CC, and looking back on the day's play I have no cause to regret it. Our innings, it must be said, was not without its alarms, and 148 all out from 44.2 overs was a total built more on application than flourish. D Simmons led the way with a well-made 39, and Arthur Cradduck came through unbeaten on 30 to give the lower order something to build upon. I contributed 24 myself, glad enough to have played my part, though poor Brian Relf and Bert Buckoke were both undone without troubling the scorers, the sort of thing that happens to the best of men on any given afternoon.

If our batting had its sticky moments, our bowling more than made amends. Bowaters were dismissed for a modest 48 in twenty overs, William Hammond alone showing any real resistance with an unbeaten 24, while Henry Martin, Stephen Jarrett and John Ibbotson departed without scoring. I found my length that day and returned figures of 7 for 19, with four maidens besides, a spell I shall remember with no small pleasure, and H Newport chipped in handsomely with 3 for 29 to wrap matters up in short order.

A hundred runs to the good at the close, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's cricket all round. Bowaters played their part like true sportsmen throughout, and if the scoreboard read rather one-sidedly by tea, the game itself was played in the best traditions of the club, which is, after all, the thing that matters most.

🗓️ 08 Jul – 14 Jul 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 08 Jul 1950 🧢 1st XIv HC Hayle Place CC · HomeformWDDDW

Saturday last we were at home to Hayle Place, and we took the field first in good heart, hopeful of making early inroads. We were not disappointed, for the first wicket fell for a mere five, and though H Gosling settled in for a watchful 39, well supported by T Ewell's 23, the innings never quite found its feet. From 96 for five it fairly capitulated, three wickets falling for the addition of a mere two runs, X Lockyer and C Pilbeam both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. I found my length that morning and finished with 5 for 45, three of my overs going for nothing at all, while Basil Sears bowled tidily for 4 for 17 and John --'s Taylor chipped in with 1 for 37. Hayle Place were all out for 100 in 37.3 overs, a fair reward for a morning's honest toil.

Our reply began shakily, a wicket down before a run was scored, but the middle order set matters right. J Baker played the innings of the day, a well-made 66, and found sterling company in Arthur Cradduck, unbeaten on 37, and John Thirkell with a useful 22, the sixth wicket adding 61 in fine style. John --'s Taylor's contribution was a golden nothing, but it mattered little as we reached 197 all out in 41.1 overs and got comfortably over the line.

A word for the record books. Thirkell's runs carried him past Joe Fenner into twelfth on the club's all-time list of scorers, while John --'s Taylor, for all his duck, passed E C Streatfield into seventeenth among our wicket-takers, and leads this season's bowling with 34 to his name, just as Thirkell leads the batting with 343. A thoroughly good day for the club, and one played, I should say, very much in the best traditions of the game.

🗓️ 01 Jul – 07 Jul 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sat 01 Jul 1950 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformWWDDD

The toss went against us, and Bearsted chose to bat. An early stumble at 7 for 1 set the tone, and we ran through them smartly, 7 for 1 becoming 16 for 4, with Gordon A Hawkins, Norman Tiley and John Colegate all back without troubling the scorers. Only Vic Pound played a defiant hand, carrying his side to 99 all out in 35.5 overs with a fine unbeaten 67. John Taylor did the damage, four for 47 with five maidens, C Horton chipped in three for 43, and I was pleased to take two for 5.

A target of a hundred looked modest, yet cricket has a way of humbling such certainties. John Taylor and Basil Sears gave us a flying start, putting on 72 for the first wicket, and John went on to a fine half-century of 54 while Basil made a useful 30. Then came one of those familiar flurries, 106 for 2 becoming 109 for 5, poor Bert Buckoke departing for a golden duck among them, but the runs were safely banked and we came home by five wickets.

It was a fine day for the record books. Basil Sears's 30 runs carried him past E Humphreys into 14th on the club's all-time run-scorers, while John Taylor's four wickets took him past S Christopherson into 18th among the wicket-takers, fitting reward for a season in which he stands second in our run charts with 249 and leads the wickets with 33. A sporting contest, in the best traditions of the club, and a win to remember.

🗓️ 24 Jun – 30 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 2 matches

Sun 25 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv DR Desert Rats · HomeformLWWDD

Desert Rats visited us on Sunday the twenty-fifth of June, and I chose to have first use of the ball, a decision the wicket soon justified. The visitors were put out for 121 in 35.1 overs, T Hendley's 31 the mainstay of their innings, well supported by X Dominey's 26 and X Thorndycraft's 20, but once that trio departed we ran through the rest in no time, 47 for 1 becoming 48 for 4 with E Wright, X Baker and X Dove all falling without scoring. Robert Muir was quite unplayable, taking 5 for 33, well backed by Joe Fenner's 3 for 25 and H Newport's miserly 2 for 42, two of his overs going down as maidens.

Our reply brought 108 for 7 from 33 overs. William Buck batted soundly for an unbeaten 57, and a stand of 52 for the sixth wicket did much to steady an innings that had lately seen 20 for 1 crumble to 29 for 4. Don Hartridge, less fortunate, walked back for a duck.

There was a pleasing footnote to the day, for Joe Fenner's 13 runs took him past John Thirkell to twelfth on the club's all-time list of run-scorers, and he stands fourth in this season's first eleven run charts with 184, with Robert Muir fifth among the bowlers on 11 wickets. Neither side could force a result, and we parted with honours shared in a draw that did credit to both elevens, the Desert Rats proving generous and sporting opponents throughout.

Sat 24 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv T Tovil · HomeformWLWWD

The captain from Linton Park, Gordon Fenner, presents his account:

Saturday last, the twenty-fourth of June, saw Tovil come to us on a fair afternoon, and I had no hesitation in sending them first to the crease when the toss fell my way. They mustered 128 all out from their 38.5 overs, S. Carrington playing the pick of the innings with a fine 54, well supported by J. Branchett's 23, though the middle order rather lost its way, subsiding from 120 for 7 to all out in short order. I had the good fortune to find my length that day, finishing with 5 for 41 and three maidens into the bargain, while Basil Sears bowled with equal purpose for 4 for 29 — between us we accounted for the greater part of their innings.

In reply we made a steady if unspectacular 104 for 7 from our 33 overs. Basil Sears carried on his good work with the bat to top-score with 37, and I was glad to contribute 26 of my own, with G. Breden chipping in 22 and a useful stand of 50 for the second wicket giving us a platform. The innings rather stumbled thereafter, four wickets falling between 91 and 98, and poor Arthur Cradduck could not trouble the scorers, but the tail held firm enough to see us safely through to the close.

A word too for Basil Sears, whose 37 lifted him past Frank Sutton on the club's all-time run-scorers list, a fine achievement quietly earned. Honours were shared in the end, and a draw against so competitive a Tovil side, decided by neither bat nor ball but by the clock alone, was no bad afternoon's cricket for either eleven.

🗓️ 17 Jun – 23 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 1 match

Sun 18 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv HB Hambros Bank · HomeformWWLWW

We welcomed Hambros Bank to our ground on Sunday the eighteenth of June, and winning the toss I elected to bat, a decision I felt was the right one from the outset.

The lads did not let me down. Tony Levick played a fine innings of 61 not out, the backbone of our total, well supported by Stan Wratten with 33 and Richard Harris with a sound 30. Poor Joe Fenner was undone for a duck, but the tail wagged handsomely thereafter, a final ninety runs coming to vex the fielding side no end, and we closed on a most respectable 201 for 7 from our fifty overs.

In the field we held them well in check. G Clarke batted soundly for 29, the best of their effort, but S Andrews and R Murrell both went back without troubling the scorers. Don Hartridge was pick of the attack with 2 for 19, John Thirkell and Stan Wratten each took a wicket, and for my own part I was pleased to send down three maidens as Hambros Bank were held to 81 for 7 from their thirty-eight overs. Stan Wratten's wicket, I might add, carried him past Herbert Sunley and into twelfth place among the club's all-time wicket-takers, a milestone quietly and deservedly won.

Honours were shared in the end, a draw that left both sides, I think, well content, and Hambros Bank proved companionable opponents throughout.

🗓️ 10 Jun – 16 Jun 1950 · 1950 — 3 matches

Mon 12 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv RS Rochester Maths School · HomeformDWWLW

On Monday the twelfth of June we welcomed Rochester Maths School to our own ground, and winning the toss I had no hesitation in asking the eleven to bat, a decision that answered handsomely. John Thirkell played the innings of the day, a fine half-century of 56 that carried us along in style, the openers first putting on 66 before a wicket fell and the third-wicket pair adding a crucial 78 besides. D Simmons batted with great good sense for an unbeaten 40, and I was glad to chip in with 35 of my own before we closed on 188 for 3 from our 44 overs, a total that promised a sporting afternoon's work in the field.

Rochester made a fair fist of the chase, Jameson leading them with 35, well supported by Lambkin's 32 and Finch's 30, and an opening stand of 54 that gave their innings a solid foundation. But once the middle order was broken the tail could not wag for long, and they went from 158 for 7 to all out for 173 in a mere handful of overs. It fell to me to lead the attack, and I finished with 7 for 57 from nine tidy maidens, figures that carried me past A Eltham into twenty-first place on the club's all-time list of wicket-takers, with William Newport a fine ally at 3 for 69.

Thirkell's innings likewise lifted him past Joe Fenner to twelfth among our run-scorers, and with the season young he leads our batting for 1950 as I am pleased to lead the bowling. We won by 15 runs, a close and honest contest played in the best traditions of the club, and Rochester left us, I think, with no complaints about the manner of their defeat.

Sat 10 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv LC Lloyds Social Club · AwayformDWDWW

Sat 03 Jun 1950 🧢 1st XIv OC Old Williamsonians CC · HomeformDLDWD

Joe Fenner played the innings of the day, a handsome fifty not out that anchored all that followed, with William Buck's brisk 24 and Don Hartridge's unbeaten 24 lending it good company. The tail, never to be despised at Linton Park, wagged merrily for a further 47 before I called a halt at 136 for 6 from our thirty-three overs. Only poor E. Lockyer trudged back for a duck, and bore it, as our club expects, like a sportsman.

Weatherhogg battled gamely to 26 not out, there was no reprieve for the rest, P. Cox falling for nought among them. Don Hartridge bowled beautifully for his 4 for 16, well backed by John Thirkell's 3 for 8 and a wicket apiece for Joe Fenner, while I was content to keep matters tight with a pair of maidens of my own. The innings closed at 97, and the match with it, a fine margin of 39 runs to us.

It was, besides, a day of quiet milestones — Fenner's fifty carries him past Frank Sutton to fourteenth on our all-time run-scorers' list, Thirkell stands second in this season's charts with 146 runs, and I find myself, not without pride, at the head of the 1950 wicket-takers with 21.
