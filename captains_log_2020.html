🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 2020 · 2020 — 1 match

Sat 26 Sep 2020 🧢 1st XIv The Mote CC · HomeformWWWLD

Here's the match report:

Saturday brought us up against The Mote CC, and with a cool, damp, blustery afternoon on the cards, I won the toss and had no hesitation putting them in — the right call, I felt, and I'll stand by it even after what followed.

The Mote made themselves rather too comfortable, racking up 216/6 from their 35 overs. Lewis Fawcett and Matthew White both eased past fifty and finished unbeaten, with Jason White chipping in a watchful 49 alongside them. There was one small mercy — Oliver Hills back in the pavilion for a duck — courtesy of Alex Keeler, who did the damage with 2-17. Tobey Castle (2-26) and Anthony Karassellos (2-58) lent honest support, but 216 always looked a stiff ask on that surface.

And so it proved. Our reply limped to 112 all out in 27 overs, a chase that never found its rhythm. Luke Welsman's 30 was the innings' backbone, with Jordan Austin's 26 the only other real resistance. The rest of the card made grim reading — Richard Piper, Anthony Karassellos and I all fell for ducks apiece. I shan't dwell on my own contribution; we shall draw a veil over it and move swiftly on.

Down by 104 runs, then — not our day by any stretch. Small consolation that Tobey Castle continues his fine season, sitting second in the run charts and top of the wicket pile. For the rest of us, it's heads up, chins out, and back to the nets.

Sun 20 Sep 2020 🧢 Sunday 2nd XIv Meopham CC · HomeformWWLWL

Right by the fire, another season's page turned. Sunday, 20 September, Meopham CC the visitors, a warm and overcast afternoon that promised little but delivered plenty. I called correctly at the toss and, in a fit of uncharacteristic decisiveness, elected to bat.

The lads rallied to 161/7 from our 35 overs — nothing to have Meopham quaking in their whites, but a total with enough substance to work with. Henry Bourdillon led the way with a tidy 37, Fred Taylor chipped in handsomely with 35, and Benjamin Austin added a further 29. Not fireworks, but honest runs, gathered with the sort of quiet application that wins matches nobody remembers losing.

Defending it was another matter entirely. Meopham's Alex Bryant threatened to spoil the afternoon with a fine half-century (60), well supported by David Pollard's 36 and Andrew Bryant's 30 — for a while it looked ominously like their day. But Ben Pearce and James Innes departed without troubling the scorers, and Fred Taylor, following his runs with the ball, took 2-36 to lead the attack, with James West (1-11) and Anton Bourdillon (1-19) chipping away at the other end. Meopham fell agonisingly short on 160/8.

Won by a single run — the kind of margin that ages a captain by several years but tastes all the sweeter for it. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a reminder that in this game, one run is as good as a hundred.

Sun 20 Sep 2020 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · AwayformLLDWW

Another Sunday, another coin toss lost — I'm beginning to suspect it's rigged against me personally — and so off we trudged to Tenterden on a warm, overcast afternoon with the ball in hand rather than the bat. Not the worst hardship, as it turned out. Tenterden ground their way to 162 all out from 59.5 overs, with Mark Kokkinos looking rather too comfortable for our liking on his way to a well-made 51. Gary Woollett, mercifully for us, departed without troubling the scorers. Toby Hughes was the pick of our attack with 2-9, a spell of real discipline, well supported by Will Thew (2-27) and Anthony Karassellos (2-44), while Alex Keeler simply refused to give anything away, sending down six maidens for good measure.

Chasing 163, we made light work of it, cantering home to 164/2 from just 22.3 overs. Toby Hughes, evidently not content with his bowling heroics, carried his bat for a superb 78 not out, and found an able ally in Richard Piper (47) as the pair put on 78 for the second wicket to see us home in some style.

A word too for Anthony Karassellos, who brought up his 50th career wicket — a landmark worth raising a glass to — while Tobey Castle continues to lead our 2020 Sunday 1st XI run charts with 273 to his name. All told, an 8-wicket win, comfortably achieved, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work. Onwards indeed.

Sat 19 Sep 2020 🧢 2nd XIv SC Sheldwich CC · AwayformWWWLL

Skipper's Log — Sheldwich CC away, Saturday 19 September.

Warm and overcast at Sheldwich, the kind of afternoon that promises rain and never quite delivers it, and we were put into the field first. It looked, for a while, like a tidy day's work: Sheldwich were bowled out for 118 in 36.5 overs, Jon Taylor's 21 the only real resistance, while ducks for Alasdair Wilson Smith and Tim Thirkell suggested the home batting had rather left its boots in the changing room. Thirkell, not content with merely dismissing people cheaply, then took the ball and ran through them anyway — 4 for 10 is the sort of figure you scribble in the book and stare at for a moment. Ian Hollingworth chipped in with 2 for 23, and Anthony Karassellos bowled two tight maidens on his way to 1 for 6, giving away runs as if they were his own money.

Chasing 119 should have been a formality, and cricket duly reminded us it never is. We stumbled to 122 for 9 off 37.4 overs, John Karassellos having a torrid time behind the stumps (18 byes — sorry, John), and James West falling for a duck of his own. Will Thew's 20 held things together, and I managed 35 to top-score, mostly by refusing to get out rather than by any great artistry.

A win by one wicket, then — the narrowest of margins, entirely typical of us, and all the sweeter for the fright along the way.

Sat 19 Sep 2020 🧢 1st XIv Linden Park CC · HomeformLWWWL

Alex Keeler was the pick of our attack with 2-27, Tobey Castle chipped in with 2-42 to continue what's been a fine season with ball in hand, and I managed 1-5 of my own to do my bit from the other end.

In the end Linden Park closed on 198/8 from their 45, and honours were shared — a draw when neither side could quite force the door open. Not the win the scoreboard perhaps threatened at one stage, but a thoroughly good afternoon's cricket, and Jordan's 200th cap is the story that'll be told in the bar long after the numbers are forgotten.

Thu 17 Sep 2020 🧢 Midweek XIv KR King's School Rochester · HomeformWWWWW

A pleasant, grey Thursday brought King's School Rochester to us on the 17th, and it was we who had first use of the ball, which felt only right on a day with a touch of murk about it. The openers needed no encouragement from the conditions to get themselves out, mind: the first wicket went down for a solitary run, and from there the innings rather came apart at the seams, 2-1 collapsing to 16-4 before the visitors had time to catch breath. George Taylor played with real substance for his 47, dragging them up to a respectable-looking 72 all out from 23.2 overs, but everyone around him went cheaply, with G French, Max Butler and James Watkins all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. I found some rhythm with the new ball myself, finishing with 3-4 including three maidens, and had excellent support from Joshua Knowler (3-17) and Alex Keeler (2-6), who chipped away relentlessly.

Chasing 73 was never going to trouble us unduly, and so it proved. Luke Welsman played the anchor beautifully for 29, with Jordan Austin lending fine support on 25, and we cantered home to 73-2 in just 12.2 overs.

Up the season table, Jordan Austin now sits third in the Midweek XI run charts with 60, while Alex Keeler leads the wicket-takers with six — both thoroughly deserved.

Two points, and thoroughly earned: an eight-wicket win, bowled and batted with real discipline from start to finish, and a thoroughly pleasant way to spend a grey afternoon.

Sun 13 Sep 2020 🧢 Sunday 2nd XIv BC Brasted Invicta CC · HomeformLWWLW

Sunday 13 September, and Brasted Invicta arrived to a warm, cloudy afternoon at Linton Park with a scoreline I'm still wincing about. I lost the toss — no change there — and we were put into the field, which on a flat one and against Stuart Young in this sort of form was rather like being asked to hold back the tide with a tea towel. Young made a magnificent 125, ably supported by Michael Lea's 78 and A Pearce's unbeaten 27, and their 281 for 4 off their forty overs felt every bit as imposing as it sounds. Tim Thirkell was our pick of the attack with 2 for 48, toiling manfully, while I chipped in with 1 for 63 — more workmanlike than wonderful.

Chasing that lot always had the whiff of a tall order, but the lads gave it a proper go. Shehryar Gul's 68 was the innings of the day, well backed up by Richard Piper's fine 59 and Anthony Karassellos's 40, and a generous 34 extras suggested Brasted's bowlers were as generous with their line as we'd have liked with our shot selection. Sadly the Duck Club welcomed three new members — James West, George Rogers and Davy Matthews all walking the walk of shame — and behind the stumps poor John Karassellos had one of those afternoons, 16 byes going begging. My own 17 with the bat did little to plug the gap.

263 all out from 38.2 overs left us 18 runs short in the end. Richard Piper and Tim Thirkell continue to lead our season charts with bat and ball respectively, and there's real form in this side — we'll be better for it next week.

Sun 13 Sep 2020 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv LC Luddesdowne CC · HomeformWLLDW

Luddesdowne were bowled out for 145 in 33.1 overs, and we came home 60 runs to the good. A thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work — the kind of result that makes the drive home, and indeed the washing-up of the tea things, feel entirely worthwhile.
