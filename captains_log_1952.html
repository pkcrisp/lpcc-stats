🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 1952 · 1952 — 1 match

Sun 28 Sep 1952 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformW

On Sunday the 28th of September I led the eleven away from home, guests of a most hospitable side for what proved a fitting close to the season. The coin fell my way, and I asked my fellows to take first strike, trusting the wicket would favour good batting before the light drew in.

Trust well rewarded. Stan Wratten played an innings of real substance, seventy-nine runs of the soundest cricket, each stroke placed with the calm of a man who knows his ground. He found a willing ally in John Ames, whose own fifty-five was no less handsome, while Gordon Fenner chipped in a useful twenty further down the order. Between them our innings closed at 35 for 1 from six overs, every man playing a straight bat and asking nothing of fortune.

There was more to Wratten's innings than the runs alone, for his seventy-nine carried him past John Thirkell and into second place on the club's roll of honour for runs scored down the years, a distinction the whole eleven was glad to see recorded. It leaves him at the head of our married men's charts for the season, while young Thirkell continues to lead the bachelors with twenty-three.

In the end neither side could force home the advantage, and honours were shared, a draw that pleased both camps well enough. A fixture played throughout in the best traditions of the game, and I came away from the pavilion well satisfied with the afternoon's work.

🗓️ 20 Sep – 26 Sep 1952 · 1952 — 2 matches

Sun 21 Sep 1952 🧢 1st XIv HC Hayle Place CC · HomeformWDWWW

It was a fair, honest afternoon of cricket that we passed with Hayle Place CC, and one that rather typified the game at its best, for neither side could quite prise the other loose, and honours finished shared.

Put in to field first, we found Hayle Place in determined mood, and G Ashby set about compiling the runs that mattered, 43 well-made ones, with H Gosling weighing in with 27 and T Ewell 20 to carry the visitors to 155 all out from their 52.1 overs. Gordon Fenner bowled with real venom to return 4 for 34 and account for J Stinchcombe and C Pilbeam without troubling the scorers, and he had willing support from Frank Taylor, whose 2 for 46 came off a spell containing no fewer than 8 miserly maidens. I had the satisfaction of picking up a wicket myself, finishing with 1 for 21, a modest but useful contribution to the afternoon's work.

In reply we made steady if unspectacular progress to 131 for 7 from our 33 overs, William Buck top-scoring with a plucky 29, well supported by Don Hartridge's 28 and a brisk 20 from Taylor. Richard Harris was the one blot on an otherwise respectable card, undone for a duck, while poor Tony Levick had an uncomfortable time of it behind the stumps, conceding 9 byes on a pitch that clearly gave the bowlers some help.

There was cause for quiet celebration in the pavilion nonetheless, for Fenner's runs took him past Tom Osborn into 17th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and Taylor's wickets lifted him past Fenner himself to the very top of our all-time wicket-taking table, a fine landmark achieved in the best traditions of the club. A draw it was, then, but a thoroughly sporting one, and Hayle Place left us, I think, with a proper respect for the fight in this Linton Park eleven.

Sat 20 Sep 1952 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformDWDWW

So it proved. Hawkins, Rayner and B. Elswood were all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers, and it was Taylor who ran quite riot, taking the remarkable figures of six for 32, five of his overs maidens, not a run given away lightly. Wratten stands second in this season's run charts with 671, and Taylor leads the wicket-takers with 58, fine efforts both.
