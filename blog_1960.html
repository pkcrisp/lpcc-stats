🦆 DUCK REPORT — 24–25 September 1960 🦆

8 ducks
Week 22 · 24 Sep 1960 – 25 Sep 1960
A blustery end-of-season weekend produced eight ducks across two fixtures, and the exclusive zero club is looking rather plump for it. Two truths were reaffirmed: the art of not scoring is alive and well at Linton Park, and nobody should let Ray Howard near a cricket ball if they value their stumps. Saturday 24th — 1st XI v A. Trembeths Frank Brattle departed to H- Clancy without troubling the scorers, his 4th duck of the season and 12th of an increasingly decorated career from 104 innings — good enough for 15th on the all-time list. A family trait, it seems: kinsman Percy Brattle already carries 4 career noughts of his own. On this very date in 1955, John Carter also strolled back for nought, against Town Malling CC, in what proved his only season in Linton colours. Sunday 25th — a full house against Linton Park CC Three Linton sides took the field, and the zero club signed up six new members for the day. Ray Howard led the way, bowled by John Ames for his 2nd duck of the season (8th career, 32nd all-time from 80 innings) — and then went and did the same to others. With the ball he claimed a treble of duck-scalps in the same innings, sending back Sean Carter, John Abnett and Derek Pantony all bowled. Poacher and gamekeeper on the same afternoon, and arguably better with the ball than the bat. Cliff Anscombe edged behind for his 3rd season and 10th career duck (22nd all-time, 33 innings), a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club and a past (1957) Duck of the Year, still finding new ways to add to the collection. Sean Carter went one better — or worse — completing a pair: stumped off Len Conway's bowling for his 2nd duck of the season, then bowled by the rampaging Ray Howard for his 3rd, taking his career tally to 3 from just 6 innings (100th all-time). Two ducks, one date, no complaints. Len Conway, run out for his trouble, moved to 6 career ducks (44th all-time, 73 innings) and his 2nd of the season, himself a Debutante Duck alumnus of some vintage. John Abnett's was his 2nd of the season and 16th career duck from a mere 42 innings — a startling 7th on the all-time list — and sits comfortably alongside his own "Ducks in a Row" board entry. Derek Pantony's was his 5th of the season, a genuine title charge, and 6th career duck (39th all-time), the old 1947 Duck of the Year proving he's lost none of his touch for the art. History rhymed all afternoon: no fewer than six of Sunday's fallen discovered that on this exact date back in 1955, Frank Brattle — Saturday's own man of the moment — was himself out for nought against Linton Park CC. Some ghosts never leave the crease. 📊 Week in Summary 8 ducks, 2 matches, one treble of duck-taking, one pair, and Ray Howard doing more damage with ball than bat ever could. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Pat Hardwick — 6 ducks / 37 inns (16%) - Richard Harris — 5 ducks / 11 inns (45%) - Derek Pantony — 5 ducks / 12 inns (42%) - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks / 19 inns (26%) - Robert Wood — 5 ducks / 34 inns (15%) 🍩 The season presses on, and so does the hunt for glory — or its rather more common opposite. Next fixture, next queue at the pavilion gate. See you there. 🥇🏏

🦆 Duck News: 17th to 18th September 1960

4 ducks
Week 21 · 17 Sep 1960 – 18 Sep 1960
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the 1st XI shouldering the entire burden this weekend as the 1960 campaign heads toward its close. Saturday 17th September, v Bearsted CC (Home) Basil Sears opened the innings and promptly opened the Duck Club's account for the weekend, caught by E 'Ted' White off the bowling of Nick Wheeler Robinson. It is his 1st of the season and 10th of a career now spanning 200 innings, good enough for 21st on the all time list. Curiously, on this very date in 1955, C. Haylor also fell for a duck against these same opponents, Bearsted CC. Haylor finished his own career on 11, and rather fittingly bowed out on a duck himself. Down at number 10, Peter Hughes joined him in the book, bowled by the very same E 'Ted' White. It is Peter's 3rd duck of the season already and his 4th in a brief 11 innings career, placing him 69th on the roll of dishonour. He is a paid up member of the Debutante Duck club from his earliest days, having opened his account with a nought on debut, and he shares Haylor's this day in 1955 company against Bearsted. Sunday 18th September, v Tilling Steven (Away) Cliff Anscombe departed at number 6, bowled by R. Bailey, for his 2nd duck of the season and 9th of a career already decorated with a Duck of the Year title in 1957. Cliff too is a Debutante Duck club member of long standing. He climbs to 26th on the all time list. Elsewhere in the archives, Gordon Fenner marked this exact date in 1949 with a nought of his own, on his way to a career total of 15. Rounding things off, Terry Whittaker was run out for nought at number 9, his 4th duck of the season and 7th for his career, 35th on the all time list. Terry carries the Quacking Together honour alongside Brian Relf and is himself a Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell, though happily this was not that day. He joins Gordon Fenner in the very same this day in 1949 club as Cliff. Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every single one falling to the 1st XI while the rest of the club kept remarkably clean hands. Bearsted CC and Tilling Steven can each claim a share of the spoils, with two bowled, one caught and one run out for good measure. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Pat Hardwick, 6 ducks in 34 innings, 17.65% - Richard Harris, 5 ducks in 10 innings, 50.00% - Bob Arnold, 5 ducks in 16 innings, 31.25% - Robert Wood, 5 ducks in 34 innings, 14.71% - Derek Pantony, 4 ducks in 11 innings, 36.36% Richard Harris remains the club's most efficient collector, a nought for every other innings almost to the letter. The season is not done yet. Next fixture brings fresh opportunity for glory in reverse, who will be joining the honours board next time? 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 10th–11th September 1960

3 ducks
Week 20 · 10 Sep 1960 – 11 Sep 1960
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, and it was the 1st XI's trip to Staplehurst CC that did the damage for the exclusive zero club, with three names added to the collection in the space of one innings. 🏏 1st XI away to Staplehurst CC William Buck strode out at first wicket down and departed bowled by L Murrell without registering a run. It's the 2nd duck of Buck's season and the 16th of a career now standing at 8th in the all-time table of 738 — rarefied company indeed, and he remains one half of the Quacking Together honour alongside John Thirkell. On this very day in 1954, Len Conway also trudged off for nought, against Old Roffensians. Down at number nine, Roy Clarke fell to K Cherry — his 4th duck of the season and 20th for the club, good enough for 5th on the all-time list. Clarke arrives with previous form, having topped the Duck of the Year charts back in 1959, and shares the Quacking Together honour with the legendary John Ames. The sibling rivalry runs deep too — brother H Clarke carries three career ducks of his own, so this is very much a family trade. Fittingly, on this same date in 1954, Len Conway was also walking back for nought. And to round things off, last man Richard Harris was also despatched by K Cherry, handing the bowler a brace for the innings. It's Harris's 5th duck of the campaign and 34th of his career, a frankly staggering 3rd in Linton's all-time standings of 738 — a genuine title challenger in the sport of not scoring. He too finds himself keeping company with Len Conway, who made his own duck on this exact date in 1954. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks, all from the same innings at Staplehurst, with K Cherry claiming a brace of his own (Clarke and Harris) and L Murrell getting in on the act against Buck. A quiet weekend numerically, but a mighty one in quality — with three players inside the all-time top ten, this was as concentrated a dose of ignominy as the club has served up in some time. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Pat Hardwick — 6 ducks in 34 innings (17.65%) - Richard Harris — 5 ducks in 10 innings (50.00%) - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks in 15 innings (33.33%) - Robert Wood — 5 ducks in 32 innings (15.63%) - Derek Pantony — 4 ducks in 11 innings (36.36%) Harris's effort this week keeps him glued to top spot contention — half his innings this season have ended in a duck, a strike rate for failure that would make anyone proud. 0️⃣ 🍩 Roll on the next fixture — the ducks are surely circling, waiting for their next victims to step up to the crease.

🦆 Duck News — 27-28 August 1960 🦆

3 ducks
Week 18 · 27 Aug 1960 – 28 Aug 1960
A quiet weekend for fixtures but a productive one for the Zero Club, as St Lukes' visit to Linton Park on Saturday yielded a full house of naught for the 1st XI. 1st XI v St Lukes (home) St Lukes' R- Dillon clearly fancied his chances at Linton Park, accounting for three Linton batsmen without a run against any of their names — a bowling spell the Duck Club salutes even as it mourns the victims. First to fall was Robert Murray at four, bowled by Dillon for his third duck of the season and his 5th career nought from 72 innings — 53rd on the all-time list of 737. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1949 Richard Harris was suffering an identical fate against Hunton Wanderers, a piece of calendar symmetry the Duck Club treasures. At six, Robert Wood followed his captain back for his 5th duck of the season, moving to 12 career ducks from 73 innings and climbing to a rather exalted 16th of 737 all time — proper company for a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having ducked on debut long before this Saturday's effort. Same bowler, same story, same walk of shame — and, as with Murray, a Richard Harris duck on this exact date in 1949 for company. Dillon completed his trio at nine, removing Derek Pantony caught behind for a 4th duck this season and a 5th in the career column — remarkable going from only 11 innings, a strike rate that would embarrass most bowlers. Derek carries the honour of Duck of the Year 1947 on his mantelpiece, a reminder that even champions of the art have off days like this one. He too shares his calendar fate with Richard Harris's 1949 vintage. Week in Summary Two matches, three ducks, one rampant St Lukes bowler. All the damage came in a single innings on Saturday — Sunday's fixture, mercifully or otherwise, passed off duck-free. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Pat Hardwick — 6 ducks, 32 innings (18.75%) 2. Bob Arnold — 5 ducks, 15 innings (33.33%) 3. Robert Wood — 5 ducks, 31 innings (16.13%) 4. Richard Harris — 4 ducks, 9 innings (44.44%) 5. Derek Pantony — 4 ducks, 10 innings (40.0%) Richard Harris's name pops up twice this week without even playing — a 44% duck rate that keeps him firmly in the hunt. Next fixture awaits, and with it the Duck Club's eternal question: who fancies auditioning for R- Dillon's swing next? 🦆🍩0️⃣

Week 14 · 30 Jul 1960 – 1 Aug 1960
A quiet week across most of the club, but Saturday's trip to Harrietsham CC gave the Duck Club plenty to toast courtesy of one D Hampshire, who put three Linton batsmen back in the pavilion for nought inside a single innings. Not officially a hat-trick, but round these parts he'll take the credit anyway. 🏏 1st XI at Harrietsham CC In at three, Pat Hardwick was bowled by Hampshire without troubling the scorers, his 4th duck of the summer and 6th of an ever-growing career, 53 innings deep and good enough for 40th on the all-time list. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own first-ever knock years back, and a former Duck of the Year in 1957, Cliff shares this week's Cruttenden echo too. At number 11, Richard Harris completed Hampshire's set, held by P Powis, for his 3rd duck of the season. That's number 32 from a career of 166 innings, an astonishing 3rd on the club's all-time list, a place of dubious distinction few will ever trouble. Cruttenden's ghost strikes again. 📊 Week in summary: 3 matches, 3 ducks, all in the same innings, all off the same bowler. A flawless 100% strike rate Peter can be thankful he isn't chasing. Sunday 24 July — Folkestone CC (Away) John Spanswick took up the mantle, helping himself to three of Linton's four Folkestone wickets for nought. Frank Brattle fell first, bowled for his 3rd of the season, arriving at exactly the 11 career ducks Robert Wood reached a day earlier. Frank also carries family colours — relative Percy Brattle owns 4 career ducks of his own. Pat Hardwick edged Derek Waddup behind, his 3rd of the season and 5th career duck, joining Eddie Lawrence (1956–1960) on the same tally. Rather fittingly, Pat is also the club's reigning Duck of the Year for 1960 — the race, so far, is his to lose. William Buck fell to Spanswick next, a lone duck for the season but his 15th career nought, rank 11 of 736 — level with the great Gordon Fenner (1941–1960). William already shares "Quacking Together" honours with John Thirkell from campaigns past. Alan Wilkes rounded things off, Spanswick's third victim, his 3rd of the season and 14th career duck, joining Frank Sutton (1925–1949) on the same mark. Alan's own trophy cabinet includes "Only Two Quacks" pairings with both Bob Arnold and John Ames. Tuesday 26 July — Mote A (Away) A solitary entry at Mote A: Roy Clarke, bowled by Ashurst for his 3rd duck of the season and 19th career — rank 5 of 736, and a former Duck of the Year himself back in 1959. On this very day in 1959, none other than Tony Snell also departed against Folkestone CC — Linton's own "Runaway Duck" of 1958, a season memorable for yielding no runs whatsoever. Roy's sibling H Clarke also flies the family flag with 3 career ducks of his own. Week in Summary Eight ducks from three matches, with Bill Taylor and John Spanswick between them accounting for six of the eight — the bowlers rather outperforming Linton's top order this week. 🦆 Duck News — 16th & 17th July 1960

4 ducks
Week 12 · 16 Jul 1960 – 17 Jul 1960
A short, sharp weekend for the exclusive zero club — every sighting crammed into one unfortunate innings at home to Rumwood CC on the Saturday, while Sunday's return fixture passed off entirely fowl-free. Saturday — 1st XI v Rumwood CC (home) The top order held until Gordon Fenner departed at four, bowled by J Carter, for his 1st duck of the summer and 15th of a genuinely distinguished LPCC career — good enough for 8th on the all-time list of 736, level with John --'s Taylor (1949–1958, 142 innings) and John Abnett (1954–1960). A name already familiar to the honours board through his brothers Joe (5 career ducks) and Maurice (1) — and spookily, on this very day in 1910, F S W Cornwallis also strolled back for nought against Maidstone CC.
