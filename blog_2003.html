🦆 DUCK NEWS — Week Ending 27 September 2003 🥇🍩

0 ducks
Week 23 · 27 Sep 2003
A rare and troubling development from Linton Park this week, ducklings: one match played, and not a single soul offered themselves up to the exclusive zero club. Eleven — or twenty-two — batsmen went to the crease and, disgracefully, every one of them found the middle of the bat at least once. The art of not scoring, it seems, took the weekend off. We shall not pretend this doesn't sting. The Duck Club exists precisely for moments like these to be filled, and this week they were not. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or merely a temporary, unwelcome lapse in standards remains to be seen. Rest assured your correspondent will be watching next weekend's fixtures very closely indeed — someone, somewhere, is due. In the meantime, the season's serial offenders continue to circle ominously at the top of the table, and there is still everything to play for in this year's hunt for Duck of the Year. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Richard Piper — 5 ducks from 33 innings (15.15%) 2. Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks from 17 innings (23.53%) 3. Adam Pooley — 3 ducks from 6 innings — a startling 50%, the purest embodiment of triumph through adversity currently on the books 4. Michael Beeney — 3 ducks from 14 innings (21.43%) 5. Clive Newman — 3 ducks from 15 innings (20.0%) Adam Pooley in particular deserves a special mention: three ducks from just six trips to the crease is consistency of a very particular kind, and at this rate full membership of the club beckons rather sooner than most would like. 📅 Looking Ahead A quiet week for the collectors, but the season is far from over. Richard Piper leads the chase, Fred Cheesman lurks with intent, and Adam Pooley's remarkable strike rate suggests the next nought is never far away for at least one of our regulars. Get out there next weekend and do your duty for the club, ladies and gentlemen. The ducks won't collect themselves — well, actually, that's rather the point. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 20 to 24 September 2003

3 ducks
Week 22 · 20 Sep 2003 – 24 Sep 2003
The season creaks toward its close but the exclusive zero club still found room for three fresh inductees this week — a debut, a repeat offender, and a proper dynasty entry — spread across Saturday, Sunday and a Wednesday midweeker. Saturday 20 September 🏏 2nd XI away at Elliotts CC A brand new name joins the roll: M- Wood, caught and bowled by Alan Ricketts at number seven, becomes the newest arrival in the club's most dubious congregation. First-ever innings, first-ever duck — straight onto the Debutante Duck board, and with career figures reading a spotless 1 from 1, already filed under One and Done for the 2003 vintage. Every dynasty starts somewhere. On this day in 1997, Daniel Thirkell (1993–2003, 19 career ducks, all-time 52nd, Tourist Duck top ten) was doing precisely the same thing against Borden CC. Some traditions run deep. Sunday 21 September 🏏 Sunday 1st XI v Anchorians CC (home) Paul Gibson departed for nought at first drop, caught J- Bacon bowled D- Raggett — his 1st duck of the season and 25th of a long and distinguished career from 421 innings, good for 41st on the all-time list. Paul is no stranger to early ignominy, having once bagged a Platinum Duck back in 1991 for departing first ball of the entire game — this week's effort a rather more leisurely instalment by comparison. On this day in 1958, Stan Wratten (1943–1984, 35 ducks, all-time 24th, one half of Quacking Together with John Ames) also walked for nought, against Tilling Steven. The circle remains unbroken. Wednesday 24 September 🏏 Midweek XI away at Penshurst Park CC Brian Piper rounds off the week's charge sheet, run out for nought at number four — his 21st career duck from 414 innings, 50th all-time. The Piper name is thoroughly embedded in club folklore on the noughts front, Brian keeping company with parent Richard Piper (33 career ducks) and siblings David (7) and James (3) — a proper family firm of failure, even if nobody's quite level with anybody else yet. On this day in 1989, Piers McDonald (1986–2003, 48 ducks, all-time 10th, Duck of the Year 1997 and 1998) also fell for nothing, against Anchorians CC. History does love to repeat the boring bits. 🍩 Week in Summary Three ducks from four matches — a modest week by the club's own gluttonous standards, but each one earns its place: a debut, a repeat, and a dynasty. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this time — everyone failing quietly and alone. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Richard Piper — 5 ducks from 32 innings (15.63%) 🥈 Fred Cheesman — 4 ducks from 17 innings (23.53%) 🥉 Adam Pooley — 3 ducks from 6 innings (50.00%) - Michael Beeney — 3 ducks from 14 innings (21.43%) - Clive Newman — 3 ducks from 15 innings (20.00%) Adam Pooley's 50% strike rate remains the most alarming figure in the club — a genuine triumph through adversity, if adversity means never quite getting off the mark. The season's winding down but the ducks rarely read a calendar. Get the bat out one more time and see whose name joins the list next. 🦆🏏

Sunday 2nd XI v GNG CC GRFC Christopher Thirkell went first, caught off Jeff Allen at number two — his first duck of the new season and the 9th of a career now standing at 43 innings, good enough for 90th on the all-time list. He's a nearly-man of the Full House board, ten of eleven positions ducked at and still hunting the set. With a surname like Thirkell he was always destined for these pages too — John, Tim, Nigel, Simon, Daniel and Stuart between them prop up a small Thirkell dynasty of nought-getters on the honours board. Fittingly, on this very date in 1985 Paul Bowles was doing precisely the same thing against Penshurst Park CC — history repeating itself, 18 years on. Clive Newman followed at five, bowled by Nigel Dee for his first duck of the campaign and the 12th of a lengthy 288-innings career, good for 71st on the all-time chart. Same date, same fate as Christopher — 27 April 1985 was evidently a fine day for ducks, with Paul Bowles falling the identical way down at Penshurst. Michael Beeney made it three in the innings, caught off Neil Errington at nine for his 17th career duck, the season's first, moving him to 55th all-time. A paid-up member of the Diamond Duck and Retirement Duck boards from past honours, and holder of the rare Only Two Quacks distinction alongside Ian Brattle, Michael keeps it in the family too — son Thomas already has his own account open. And yes, 27 April 1985 claims another victim in Paul Bowles as well. Finally John Haggart, at ten, caught off Sukhpal Singh Rana — a debut innings ending in a debut duck, earning immediate and permanent membership of the Debutante Duck board, 477th on the all-time list from a standing start. Not the beginning he'd have scripted, but the Duck Club welcomes its newest member with open wings. Even the calendar joined in — 27 April 1985 saw Paul Bowles fall for nought too. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, every single one arriving in the same Sunday innings against GNG — call it a themed collapse.
