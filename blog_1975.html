🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 20–21 September 1975

4 ducks
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1975 – 21 Sep 1975
Two fixtures, four ducks, and the closing weeks of the season proving that nobody is safe from the quack until stumps are drawn for the year. 🏏 Saturday — West Farleigh CC Jim Hall departed for nought at number five, bowled by Eric Mitchell, bringing up his 6th duck of the season and 21st in an LPCC career now spanning 121 innings — good enough for 25th in the all-time standings. Jim carries a well-stocked honours cabinet too, including Debutante Duck, Duck of the Year 1970, and entries in the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens — consistency of a very particular kind. On this very day back in 1959, Eddie Lawrence (1956–1967, 8 ducks) was also walking back for nought, against Tilling Steven — the calendar, it seems, has a long memory. 🍩 Sunday — Anchorians CC A grim day at the crease as three Linton batsmen fell to the very same bowler, J Ward, inside a single Anchorians innings. First to go was John Thirkell, lbw for nought at four. It's his 4th duck of the season and, remarkably, his 86th for the club — comfortably the highest tally in Linton Park history, from a career of 770 innings and counting since 1943. Five Duck of the Year titles already sit in his cabinet. On this day in 1958, Stan Wratten (1943–1975, 31 ducks) was likewise dismissed against Tilling Steven — good company for a bad day. Jon Harris followed at number eight, bowled by Ward for his 4th duck of the season and 26th of his career, moving him to 16th on the all-time list. Jon has already completed the rare Full House — a duck at every position from 1 to 11 — and remains a fixture of the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens. Like his captain moments earlier, he shares this day in 1958 with Stan Wratten's duck against Tilling Steven. Last man Tony Gauna rounded out Ward's treble, caught for his 4th duck of the season and 14th of his career, sitting 38th all-time — and, fittingly, on the very same 1958 Stan Wratten parallel as his teammates. A vintage year for the club, it seems, in more ways than one. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from two matches, all falling in the 1st XI, with J Ward's spell against the Anchorians accounting for three batsmen in a single innings. The Duck Club welcomes them all warmly, if not to victory. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Colin Dicker — 7 ducks / 22 inns (31.82%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 7 ducks / 43 inns (16.28%) 3. Ian Brattle — 6 ducks / 9 inns (66.67%) 4. Phil Hickman — 6 ducks / 28 inns (21.43%) 5. Jim Hall — 6 ducks / 32 inns (18.75%) The season draws towards its close but the ducks show no sign of migrating early — see you at the next fixture, where the honours board waits patiently for its next volunteer. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 13 September 1975

2 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1975
A tight afternoon at home to Southborough CC produced two additions to the exclusive zero club, and this week's card carries a distinctly familial flavour. 1st XI v Southborough CC (home) First to fall was John Thirkell, undone at four by Richard Russell for his 3rd duck of the season — a modest tally by his own extraordinary standards, given John sits proudly at #1 in the all-time Linton Park duck rankings with 85 to his name from 769 innings. A five-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967, 1971), a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, and a paid-up member of the Quacking Together honours alongside Bob Arnold and William Buck, John remains the standard against which all Linton ducks are measured. On this very day in 1969, Derry Evans (1966–1971, 8 ducks, #59 all-time) also strolled back for nought against St Lukes Maidstone CC — the calendar has a long memory. Batting at six, son Tim Thirkell followed his father back to the pavilion, caught by Paul Willcock off the bowling of Terry Kingsmill. It's Tim's 7th duck of the season, vaulting him into a share of the season lead, and his 28th career duck, good for 14th on the all-time list from 181 innings. Tim carries the rare Full House board, a duck registered at every position 1 through 11, plus Duck of the Year honours from 1972 and a Quacking Together entry with Jim Hall. By curious coincidence, Tim shares his father's parallel exactly: Derry Evans made his nought on this same calendar date back in 1969 too. 🦆 A Family Affair Father and son, back to back, in the same innings against the same opposition — John and Tim Thirkell combine for a proper Duck and Ducklings, one of the rarest ceremonies the club has to offer. Between them the Thirkells carry 113 career ducks this afternoon, proof that whatever else runs in the family, so does the art of not scoring. Younger sibling Nigel Thirkell, a duck-club member in his own right with 23 to his name, will no doubt be taking notes. 📊 Week in Summary One match, two ducks, one thoroughly Thirkell weekend. Southborough's Russell and Kingsmill, with an assist from Willcock behind the stumps, can both chalk up scalps against Linton Park's 1st and 14th most prolific duck collectors of all time — not a bad afternoon's work for the visitors. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1975 1. Colin Dicker — 7 ducks, 22 innings (31.82%) 2. Tim Thirkell — 7 ducks, 41 innings (17.07%) 3. Ian Brattle — 6 ducks, 9 innings (66.67%, a truly committed effort) 4. Phil Hickman — 6 ducks, 28 innings (21.43%) 5. Nigel Thirkell — 6 ducks, 36 innings (16.67%) Tim Thirkell now shares the season lead with Colin Dicker on 7 apiece, though Dicker's fearsome strike rate keeps him marginally ahead on ignominy. With Nigel lurking in 5th too, the Thirkell name could yet dominate this year's Duck of the Year race three ways over. See you at the next fixture — the ducks are never far away. 🦆🏏

Week 20 · 6 Sep 1975 – 7 Sep 1975
A curiously restrained weekend on the duck front. Two matches, and the exclusive zero club admitted just a single new member. Quality over quantity, evidently. Saturday 6th September 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (home) Colin Dicker strode out at number 7 and departed almost as promptly, bowled by S. Lee for nought. It's his 7th duck of the season - extending his own lead at the summit of the current Duck Hunting Season table, well clear of the chasing pack. Career-wise this is nought number 20 in 56 innings, lifting him to 26th of 923 in the all-time standings. Colin is no stranger to this kind of form: a Duck of the Year laureate back in 1973, and by the look of that season tally, well on course to add 1975 to the cabinet too. Neatly, on this very day in 1967, Roger Craddock was also dismissed for a duck, against Birchington CC - proof that the calendar has long memories even when the fixture list has moved on. Sunday 7th September A vegan affair all round - not a feather ruffled. The ducks, it seems, took the day off. Week in Summary One duck from two matches - sparse pickings, but a significant one. Colin Dicker's 7th of the season keeps him firmly atop the pile as the campaign heads into its closing weeks, a testament to consistency of a very particular kind. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Tim Thirkell - 6 ducks / 40 innings (15.0%) Ian Brattle's 66.7% remains the strike rate to fear most - triumph through adversity indeed. Three men locked on 6 apiece means the race for runner-up spot is very much alive. Next weekend's fixtures await, and with the season entering its final furlong, the Duck Club watches, waits, and celebrates whatever nought comes next. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 30 & 31 August 1975

2 ducks
Week 19 · 30 Aug 1975 – 31 Aug 1975
A very Thirkell-adjacent weekend for the Duck Club, as Linton's top order visited Whitstable and left two names on this week's honours list. Saturday 30 August — 1st XI at Whitstable CC (away) The rot set in early. At first wicket down, Jim Hall departed for nought, caught R. Rush off the bowling of P. Keen — his 5th duck of the 1975 season and 20th of an increasingly distinguished career, now 26th on the all-time leaderboard of 923. Jim already carries the Debutante Duck honour from a duck-on-debut long past, and sits proudly among the club's Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens — a man equally hopeless home or away. Two wickets later, Nigel Thirkell followed him back, snared by Alan Nutten off — you guessed it — the very same P. Keen. A tidy Keen-inflicted brace for Whitstable. Nigel's duck is his 6th of the season and 23rd of his career, good enough for 23rd on the all-time list, one rung above Jim himself. Between them the pair complete the "Only Two Quacks" honour, and given they fell together this very afternoon, few club pairings have earned it so promptly. Nigel, incidentally, quacks from famous stock — his father John Thirkell sits top of the all-time charts on 84 career ducks, while brother Tim isn't far behind on 27. The art of not scoring, it seems, runs in the blood. Fittingly, both men might have felt a shiver of déjà vu — on this very day in 1965, Frank Brattle also fell for nought, against Bearsted CC, en route to 38 career ducks and 5th place on the all-time roll. Some anniversaries are best not celebrated. Sunday 31 August A quieter affair — the second fixture of the weekend passed off entirely duck-free. The zero club takes its rest days where it can. Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one bowler doing all the damage. P. Keen leaves Whitstable Road with a five-for of a very different kind — two Duck Club members to his name in a single innings. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Ian Brattle — 6 ducks in 8 innings (75%) - Colin Dicker — 6 ducks in 20 innings (30%) - Phil Hickman — 6 ducks in 28 innings (21.43%) - Nigel Thirkell — 6 ducks in 33 innings (18.18%) - Tim Thirkell — 6 ducks in 38 innings (15.79%) Five players locked on six apiece — the race for Duck of the Year 1975 is very much alive. Ian Brattle sets the pace on strike rate alone, but there's plenty of season left for the chasing pack to strike back. 🦆 Same time next week, same dubious distinction on offer. Get out there and get your nought.

🦆 Duck News — Week of 23 August 1975

3 ducks
Week 18 · 23 Aug 1975 – 27 Aug 1975
The season presses on, and while the harvest was light this week — just three noughts to log across the four fixtures — quality held firm over quantity. The exclusive zero club welcomes its members regardless. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — 1st XI at Bearsted CC Nigel Thirkell, ever a fixture in these pages, went lbw to Robin Cunningham at four, a departure that brings up his 5th duck of the season and his 22nd for the club — good enough for 24th on the all-time charts from 141 innings. Nigel is a paid-up member of the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens alike, proof that he can be relied upon to fail to score whether Linton is playing at home or away, a rare and admirable consistency. Fittingly, today's parallel needs no introduction: on this very day in 1974, Nigel's own honours-board duck partner Jim Hall made a nought against Mersham Hatch — the pair share the "Only Two Quacks" distinction, and the calendar has now conspired to have one commemorate the other. Ducking runs in the family too, quite literally — parent John Thirkell sits imperiously atop the all-time list with 84, sibling Tim Thirkell carries 27 of his own, and family member Stuart Thirkell has opened his account with a solitary one. The art of not scoring, evidently, is hereditary. 🌙 Wednesday 27th — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC Graham Hinks enjoyed a profitable evening with the ball, accounting for two Linton wickets without a run troubling the book. David Piper was first to go, bowled at seven for his 1st duck of the season and 5th of his career from just 16 innings — he now sits 83rd of 923 all time, level with company from eras long past. Piper has form in the family stakes too: sibling Brian Piper has three career ducks of his own. Then last man S- Oldfield fell caught and bowled by the same Hinks, and did so on his very first senior innings — a genuine Debutante Duck, the purest possible welcome to the club, nought on debut and into the honours board in the same stroke. Both Midweek victims share their parallel: on this same day in 1949, Richard Harris — who racked up 36 across three decades of service, 6th on the all-time list — also made a duck, against Hunton Wanderers CC. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from four matches, two of them shared neatly between one unfortunate over of Graham Hinks's bowling. The 2nd and 3rd XIs escaped entirely unblemished this week — enjoy it while it lasts, gentlemen. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ian Brattle — 6 ducks in 7 innings (85.71%) 2. Colin Dicker — 6 ducks in 20 innings (30.00%) 3. Phil Hickman — 6 ducks in 28 innings (21.43%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 6 ducks in 37 innings (16.22%) 5. Nigel Thirkell — 5 ducks in 31 innings (16.13%) Ian Brattle continues to lead on the fearsome strike-rate front, though the three chasing on six apiece will fancy their chances of a late overtake yet. 🍩 Until next time — the middle awaits, and so does the club's least wanted distinction. See you at the crease.

🦆 DUCK NEWS — Weekend of 16th–17th August 1975

8 ducks
Week 17 · 16 Aug 1975 – 17 Aug 1975
A full house at the crease this week: eight ducks laid across two 1st XI fixtures, and the exclusive zero club welcomes plenty of familiar faces back into its dubious embrace. 🏏 Saturday — v Bobbing Court CC (h) Don Capeling turned executioner at Bobbing Court, claiming three of the four scalps on offer — a proper poultry hat-trick. Martin Thompson (b Capeling) fell for his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of a young career (5 innings, 194th all-time) — a paid-up Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell. Colin Dicker (b Capeling) followed for his 6th of the season and 19th career (55 innings, 26th all-time). With Duck of the Year already on his mantelpiece from 1973 — and 1975 too — he's very much the reigning monarch of this particular throne. Phil Hickman (c Hudson b Capeling) made it three, his 6th of the season and 8th career (35 innings, 65th all-time). A chip off the old duck-block — father Ron Hickman carries 29 career noughts of his own, and as it happens quacked out too this very weekend, making it a two-duck family affair. Ian Brattle broke Capeling's spell (c Bourne b Mills) for his 6th of the season and 8th career, but from a mere 11 innings — 62nd all-time and a flawless 100% duck rate this year. He's a fixture on the "50 Before a 50" board (51 ducks, high score 33), plus Debutante Duck and Ducks in a Row (7 consecutive) honours, with an "Only Two Quacks" partnership alongside Michael Beeney. Curiously, all four share the same anniversary: on this day in 1953, John Ames — the club's #2 all-time duck-getter with 48 — also strolled back for nought, against Mote A. 🍩 Sunday — v Hawkhurst CC (h) X Williams did the damage twice at Hawkhurst. Tim Thirkell fell for his 6th of the season, 27th of a storied career (174 innings, 15th all-time). Proper duck stock — father John Thirkell tops the entire all-time list with 84, brother Nigel has 21. Tim already owns Duck of the Year 1972 and the rare "Full House" (a duck at every position 1-11). Alan Cradduck (c Samuel b Williams) added his 3rd of the season, 25th career (97 innings, 16th all-time) — and revived his old "Quacking Together" partnership with Ron Hickman in the very same innings. Paul Croucher was run out for his 3rd of the season, 8th career (22 innings, 64th all-time) — another Retirement Duck club member, though happily not retiring today. Ron Hickman (b Williams) completed the Cradduck reunion with his 3rd of the season, 29th career (67 innings, 13th all-time) — and closed out a Hickman family weekend, quacking a day after son Phil. All four Sunday victims share a 1969 echo: Percy Brattle, who finished his career on 16, also fell against Mote A on this very date. 📊 Week in Summary 8 ducks, 2 matches, one very busy weekend for the 1st XI. Capeling and Williams shared the wickets, the Hickmans and the Cradduck-Hickman duo kept it in the family, and Ian Brattle's 100% season rate is starting to look ominous. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ian Brattle — 6 ducks / 6 innings (100%) 2. Colin Dicker — 6 ducks / 20 innings (30%) 3. Phil Hickman — 6 ducks / 26 innings (23.08%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 6 ducks / 34 innings (17.65%) 5. Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 26 innings (15.38%) Four names tied at 6 apiece — the title race for 1975 is wide open. Next fixture, the ducks will surely be looking for new recruits. See you at the crease 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 9th to 13th August 1975 🦆

6 ducks
Week 16 · 9 Aug 1975 – 13 Aug 1975
A vintage week for the exclusive zero club: six new entries to the ledger, two matches, and the Thirkell name stamped across half of them. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🏏 Saturday 9th August — v Headcorn CC (h) Four ducks in one Saturday innings is triumph through adversity at scale. John Ames fell stumped off John Phipps for his 2nd duck of the season and 48th of a mighty career — good enough for 2nd on the all-time list, with only the immortal John Thirkell (84, and still top of the pile) ahead of him. Fittingly, on this very date in 1964 it was John Thirkell himself who ducked, against Harrietsham CC — history repeating to the day. In followed Nigel Thirkell, caught by Robert Westrop off the same bowler for his 4th of the season and 21st career duck, 24th on the all-time roll. A Homebody and Tourist Duck top-tenner, Nigel's "Only Two Quacks" partner-in-crime is one Jim Hall — who'd be adding to his own tally four days later. Martin Thompson chalked up his first-ever LPCC duck, caught off — Young, from just 4 career innings — a maiden entry to the naught column, and a name to watch on the register. Martin Mears rounded out the quartet, bowled by Richard Chapman for his 2nd of the season and 2nd career duck. His honours board already carries "Quacking Together (with Jim Hall)" — a pairing about to be renewed. 🍩 Wednesday 13th August — Midweek XI v Wanderers (h) Colin Fry did for two in a row. Jim Hall was caught off him for his 4th of the season and 19th career duck — level for the day with Pat Hardwick, who made a duck against Bowaters CC on this same date back in 1960. Jim sits one position shy of the Full House, ten of eleven batting berths now blighted, a former Duck of the Year (1970) still adding to the pile. Moments later Tim Thirkell was trapped lbw by the same bowler for his 5th of the season and 26th career duck, 15th all-time — a genuine Full House man, having now ducked in every slot from 1 to 11, and Duck of the Year in 1972. His and Jim's shared "Quacking Together" board honour was renewed in the very match that earned it. Tim is son to John Thirkell (84 career ducks, today's namesake above) and brother to Nigel, who fell just four days earlier — the family clearly has a nose for nought. 0️⃣ Week in Summary Six ducks, two matches, one bowler (Fry) claiming a brace, and the Thirkell name attached to three of the six dismissals in some form. The art of not scoring remains in rude health at Linton Park. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Ian Brattle — 5 ducks / 5 innings (100%) Colin Dicker — 5 ducks / 18 innings (27.8%) Phil Hickman — 5 ducks / 25 innings (20.0%) Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks / 32 innings (15.6%) Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 25 innings (16.0%) Tim Thirkell's Wednesday effort pulls him into a share of the season lead. Who fancies joining the ledger at the next fixture? The Duck Club watches, waits, and quietly hopes. 🦆

Rounding things off at ten, Michael Chambers was bowled by D Ledsham for his 9th career duck from 28 innings, #56 of 921 all time. A curious footnote unites all three Sennocke victims: on this same day in 1972, Andrew Moss was also dismissed for nought, one of 9 career ducks in a run from 1968 to 1974. The Week in Brief Four ducks, three matches, one clear theme — Sennocke CC proved happy hunting ground, claiming three of the week's four victims in a single innings. Staplehurst managed the other unassisted. Suthery for his 1st duck of the season, his career tally also standing at a solitary 1 from 3 innings (347th of 921 all-time). On this very day back in 1940, J. Short suffered an identical fate against Boughton Monchelsea CC — Les keeps venerable company. Ron Hickman was bowled by Ian Guy for his 2nd of the season, hauling his career figure to a formidable 28 from 66 innings — a rarefied 13th in the all-time list. Ron holds a Quacking Together honour (with Alan Cradduck) and once weathered a full Runaway Duck season in 1965. The nought gene runs deep too — son Phil Hickman owns 6 career ducks and currently sits mid-table in this very season's chase. 🏏 Midweek XI — away to Margate CC, Fri 1 August Brian Piper edged Kevin Parker to the keeper for his 1st of the season, career duck 3 from 23 innings (117th of 921). Brother David Piper carries 4 of his own — evidently a household skill. On this day in 1964, Basil Sears made a duck against Headcorn CC, good company across eleven years. David Buck fell to Desmond Gregory for his very first LPCC duck, from just 4 innings (271st of 921) — following, in his own small way, in father William Buck's rather larger 28-duck footsteps. Same day, 1964: Basil Sears again. Peter Elms departed to G. Wheeler and, with that, completed a grim weekend pair — Sibton Park to Margate, nought to nought, his 2nd duck of the season and career alike. Ian Brattle was bowled by Wheeler for his 5th duck of the season from just 5 innings — a perfect, unblemished 100% record that plants him top of the pile this year. Career duck 7 from 10 (65th of 921), a Ducks in a Row honouree for 7 consecutive failures, paired on the honours board with Michael Beeney, and a paid-up Debutante Duck alumnus from way back. Brother Paul Brattle's 15 career ducks suggest the trait runs in the blood. 📊 Week in Summary Seven ducks, three matches, and Suthery and Wheeler sharing the bowling honours as chief tormentors. Special mention to Peter Elms, who somehow found a way to nought out twice in five days — proof that lightning strikes the same crease more than once. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1.
