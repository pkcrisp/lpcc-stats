🗓️ 26 Sep – 02 Oct 1953 · 1953 — 1 match

Sun 27 Sep 1953 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformWD

Sunday last, the twenty-seventh of September, saw the Linton Park eleven travel as visitors, and winning the toss I did not hesitate to send us in — the pitch looked a true one, and I have rarely regretted taking first knock on such a morning.

It must be said the innings did not run quite to plan, for we found the whole side back in the pavilion before we should have liked. William Buck was the mainstay of the effort, carrying his bat for a most creditable 26 not out while wickets fell about him, and it was an innings of real substance amid an otherwise middling display. Tony Tipples and I were less fortunate, both walking back for nought — my own contribution, I freely confess, the sorriest sort of duck, and the less said of it the better.

Buck's knock, I might add, was no isolated flash, for it leaves him at the head of the club's Married run charts for the season on the strength of those same 26 runs, a fine effort well worth setting down for the record.

Neither side, in the end, could force home an advantage, and honours were shared in a draw that pleased no one greatly and offended no one either. A fair result, I thought, against opponents who played the game throughout in the right spirit, and there are certainly worse ways to pass a Sunday afternoon than in so even and honourable a contest.

🗓️ 19 Sep – 25 Sep 1953 · 1953 — 2 matches

Sun 20 Sep 1953 🧢 1st XIv HC Hayle Place CC · HomeformWDWWW

The Linton Park versus Hayle Place fixture on Sunday 20 September 1953:

I won the toss and had no hesitation in asking my side to bat, the pitch looking a fair one to build an innings upon. And build we did, to 185 for 6 in our 48 overs, the innings owing much to Tony Levick, who middled the ball beautifully for an unbeaten 63, and to Stan Wratten, whose well-made 51 gave the innings its backbone through the middle overs. Between them they set a total that promised our bowlers something substantial to defend.

And defend it they did, in the most emphatic fashion. Hayle Place never truly settled, losing E Watson, G Ashby and L Brunger without a run on the board between them, and from there the innings had a beleaguered look about it. Tony Snell was the chief tormentor, bowling with wonderful control for figures of 3 for 8 from nine maidens, hardly a bad ball among them, while Alan Wilkes chipped in with 3 for 27 and Michael Morton with 2 for 3. The visitors' innings closed for a modest 58 in 30 overs. I had my own small adventures behind the stumps, ten byes slipping past me on a day when the ball did not always behave, though it mattered little against such a score.

Two matters for the club's records deserve mention: Alan Wilkes brought up his fiftieth career wicket, and my own innings carried me past the Reverend F A Leveson-Gower on the all-time run-scorers' list, while Wilkes's exertions saw him move above H G Smith among the wicket-takers. A handsome win by 127 runs, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's cricket in the best traditions of the club.

Sat 19 Sep 1953 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformLWDWW

John Thirkell here at the crease of the pen once more, and glad to report a famous Saturday against Bearsted CC.

The toss went against us and Bearsted elected to bat, which on reflection they may have come to regret. Our attack made short work of them, dismissing the visitors for a modest 72 in 27.3 overs, A Snook's 25 being the only substantial resistance the innings could offer. Once the middle order gave way — 57 for 3 becoming 61 for 6 in the twinkling of an eye — there was no holding us, David Sayer, Gordon Hawkins and Mick Gibson all despatched back to the pavilion without so much as troubling the scorers. Alan Wilkes bowled quite beautifully to finish with 8 for 39, a spell the like of which is not seen every season, while Gerald Wright gave nothing away for the taking, his seven maidens speaking for themselves, and closed with 2 for 25.

Set so modest a target, we might have expected an easy passage, but cricket has its own sense of humour and duly obliged with a wobble of our own, subsiding from 72 for 4 to 76 for 7 before the finish. Tom Osborn played the innings of the day, 25 well-made runs, and I was glad to lend what support I could with 21 of my own. Richard Harris, Alan Wilkes and Gerald Wright each made the sorrowful walk without troubling the scorers, but the runs were already banked. Worth noting too that Osborn's knock carried him past Gordon Fenner into seventeenth place among the club's run-getters, and Wright's two wickets lifted him past H Freed into seventeenth on the bowling list.

A scrappy finish, perhaps, but a win all the same, and one earned honestly in the field. We shook hands with Bearsted well pleased with our afternoon's work.

🗓️ 12 Sep – 18 Sep 1953 · 1953 — 2 matches

Sun 13 Sep 1953 🧢 1st XIv MT Medway Towns · HomeformWLWDW

I captained the side against Medway Towns on Sunday the 13th of September 1953, and though the coin refused me its favour and we were sent into the field, the day repaid us handsomely in the end.

Medway Towns made steady progress to 173 for 7 from their 43.1 overs, with G. Lukehurst compiling a watchful 49 and K. Walker unbeaten on 48, R. Harris chipping in with 30. Stan Wratten was in fine fettle with the ball, taking 2 for 3 to trouble their top order sorely, and W. Cleaves departed without troubling the scorers at all. Gordon Fenner and John Ames lent capable support with two wickets apiece, while Alan Wilkes bowled with great economy, three maidens among his figures, and gave nothing away lightly.

Our reply was a thing to warm the heart. I was fortunate enough to find some form and carried my bat to 51 not out, and it would be churlish not to own a quiet satisfaction in leading from the front in that fashion. Tom Osborn played handsomely for 46 and William Buck for 35, and together we saw the side home to 176 for 4 in only 31.3 overs, a fine home for a Sunday afternoon. William Blight had a trying time behind the stumps, nine byes slipping past him, but such things happen to the best of wicketkeepers on an awkward pitch.

There was cause for quiet celebration beyond the result: Tom Osborn's innings carried him past the Reverend F. A. Leveson-Gower into eighteenth place among the club's run-scorers, and Alan Wilkes's wicket lifted him above W. H. Fryer into twenty-third on the bowling list. A six-wicket win, and a thoroughly good day for the club.

Sat 12 Sep 1953 🧢 1st XIv YC Yalding CC · HomeformLWLWD

The toss went against us, and the Yalding captain, winning it, chose to bat first — a decision he must have rued before the afternoon was out. Alan Wilkes bowled quite beautifully, spinning the ball through their order to finish with six wickets for thirty-three, and found a willing partner in Michael Morton, whose six maidens and four wickets for a mere eleven runs kept the batsmen honest at the other end. Between them they had Yalding all out for fifty-eight in twenty-six overs, F Hooker and H Davidson among those who departed without troubling the scorer, and it was as fine a display of combined bowling as we have seen at Linton Park this season.

Set so modest a target, we went about our business with a cheerful confidence. William Buck played the innings of the day, forty-four runs struck with real authority, and Basil Sears gave him excellent support with thirty of his own. I was happy to see us home, adding twenty-one not out as we reached one hundred and thirty-five for the loss of only three wickets from twenty-four overs. Bob Arnold, it must be said, had a trying time of it behind the stumps, twelve byes going begging, but such things happen to the best of wicketkeepers on an off day, and nobody thought the less of him for it.

A word, too, for Alan Wilkes, whose haul carried him past Robert Muir and into twenty-fourth place among the club's all-time wicket-takers — a fine landmark, warmly applauded in the pavilion. A seven-wicket victory, and two points thoroughly earned against a sporting Yalding side who took their reverse in the best traditions of the game.

The toss went against us, and Gillingham CC, up from Kent, made no bones about their intentions once put in. Poor Tony Levick had a trying time behind the stumps, seventeen byes slipping past him on what was evidently a lively surface.

So honours were shared, a draw when neither side could quite force the issue — no bad result against a well-drilled Gillingham eleven, and a fair reflection of an afternoon's cricket played very much in the right spirit.

Sat 05 Sep 1953 🧢 1st XIv YC Yalding CC · AwayformDDLWL

The road took us to Yalding on Saturday last, a pleasant enough journey for a fixture I have always been glad to keep in the card. The coin fell our way, and judging the wicket fit for batting, I had no hesitation in putting us in.

Our innings realised 157 all out in 36.4 overs, a total built soundly rather than spectacularly. Michael Morton led the way with a well-made 30, ably supported by D Simmons with 27 and Alan Wilkes, unbeaten on 22 at the close. I must own to a blot upon my own card, falling without troubling the scorers, a distinction shared with J Barclay. The tail did not entirely wag, but the total held its shape.

If our batting was workmanlike, our bowling that afternoon was a thing to savour. Yalding's reply crumbled to 56 all out in a mere 19.4 overs, with Lewis and Latter both back in the pavilion before either had opened his account. Gerald Wright bowled quite beautifully, his figures of 6 for 31, including three maidens, altogether too searching for the home batsmen, and Alan Wilkes lent fine support with 3 for 21. Wright's six wickets carry him past Tony Snell into eighteenth place upon the club's all-time list of wicket-takers, a distinction thoroughly deserved. In the season's own reckoning he now leads the 1st XI wicket charts with 31, while Basil Sears sits fifth among the run-makers with 264.

A victory by 101 runs, gained in the best traditions of the club, and a merry enough ride home it made.

🗓️ 29 Aug – 04 Sep 1953 · 1953 — 1 match

Sat 29 Aug 1953 🧢 1st XIv HC Hunton Wanderers CC · AwayformLDDLW

Saturday last took us to Hunton Wanderers CC, and the toss went against us from the very first, so that instead of setting the pace we found ourselves following it all afternoon. Wanderers, put in to bat, made themselves very much at home, with A Pilcher compiling a fine half-century of 65 and Reg Oldfield giving him staunch company for 47. Our attack was not without its answer, however, and R Murray departed for nought. Alan Wilkes bowled tirelessly to finish with 4 for 68, well backed by J Barclay's 3 for 48 and a single wicket to Gerald Wright.

Our reply, I am bound to record, was a sorry affair. The wicket seemed to turn traitor the moment we took guard, and we were all out for a mere 31 in 16.4 overs. N Simmons and Bob Arnold joined me in the sad fraternity of the duck that day, and I own my own blob on the card without excuse or embroidery — three ducks in one innings is a poor advertisement for the willow. Eleven byes besides told of a testing afternoon behind the stumps for William Blight, though no man alive keeps wicket cleanly on such an unfriendly pitch.

There was, at least, one bright thread in the ledger. Gerald Wright's solitary wicket carried him past W H Fryer and up to twenty-second on the club's all-time list of wicket-takers, a small and welcome landmark on an otherwise chastening day. We went down by 140 runs, thoroughly and fairly beaten, and can only doff our caps to Hunton Wanderers and trot back to the nets the wiser for it.

🗓️ 22 Aug – 28 Aug 1953 · 1953 — 1 match

Sat 22 Aug 1953 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformWLDDL

We lost the toss on Saturday and were sent in the field, which on a fine August afternoon is no great hardship to a side that fancies its bowling. Charles Arkcoll built steadily to 139 for 9 in their 41.5 overs, Stan Wratten playing the anchor's part with 38, and John Ames and Gordon Fenner lending sound support with 24 and 21 apiece. Alan Wilkes bowled quite beautifully, five maidens among his overs and figures of 7 for 60 that will stand long in the book, and I was glad enough to have taken a wicket myself for 28, with Gerald Wright chipping in for one more. Humphrey Morris and Ray Beale both departed without troubling the scorers, and it was as tidy a piece of bowling as we have managed all season.

Set 140 to win, we made rather harder work of it than the target deserved, being bowled out ourselves for 158 in 39 overs, though we got there and that is the main of it. William Blight batted with real substance for 42, and Richard Harris played a fine straight innings of 31 that carries him past Maurice Fenner into twelfth place on the club's list of run-getters, a handsome landmark handsomely earned. Poor J Barclay was undone for a duck, but the middle order held firm, and I was pleased to add 17 of my own along the way, my earlier wicket having lifted me past Ernest Cruttenden into eighth on the all-time list of wicket-takers.

A tight, honest contest throughout, and full credit to Charles Arkcoll for making us work for every run. We got over the line, as the saying goes, and the pavilion tea tasted all the better for it.

🗓️ 25 Jul – 31 Jul 1953 · 1953 — 2 matches

Sun 26 Jul 1953 🧢 1st XIv FC Folkestone CC · AwayformWDDDW

John Thirkell here, with the log of a sultry Sunday's cricket at Folkestone.

The toss went against us, and Folkestone elected to bat, which on a fine July wicket was no bad choice on their part. They set about their innings with real purpose, and Gordon Heyer played a handsome knock of 86, well supported by W Barrett with a well-made fifty of 70 and Ernie Thunder's useful 39, the home side closing on 240 for 5 from their 49.2 overs. Gordon Fenner bowled with admirable control to finish with 2 for 55 and three maidens into the bargain, while Bill Hall and John Ames chipped in with a wicket apiece, the latter earning his rewards the hard way at 1 for 85.

Set 240 to draw level, we made a fair fist of the chase without ever quite closing the gap. Stan Wratten was the mainstay of the innings with a fine half-century of 54, and William Buck's 43 and John Ames's 36 gave the innings real backbone.
