🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1951 · 1951 — 2 matches

Sun 30 Sep 1951 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · Homeform—

Sunday 30th September, and we welcomed Linton Park CC for what I'd hoped would be a season-defining afternoon. The coin obliged, and with a rare glint of tactical confidence I put us in — a decision I stand by, whatever the scorebook may later suggest.

We were bowled out for 183 in 58.5 overs, which sounds tidier on paper than it felt in the deckchairs. Basil Sears led the way with a handsome 66, and Frank Taylor chipped in a well-crafted 50, the two of them stitching together a 92-run second-wicket stand that was, frankly, the only thing holding the innings together with any dignity. Robert Muir's 34 added further respectability. Elsewhere, the less said the better — Don Hartridge, Dennis Smith and H Newport all departed for ducks, a trio of blobs that rather undid the good work of their colleagues.

There was at least a moment of quiet history amid the wreckage: Frank Taylor's fifty nudged him past Herbert Rayner and into 6th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — a nice line for the archives, even on a losing day. Basil Sears also tops our 1951 Married run charts, with Ray Tippen leading the Singles.

We went down by 128 runs in the end — no dressing it up. Heads up, chins high, and back to the nets we go.

Sat 29 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv SI 1st Innings Maidstone Christian Institute · HomeformLDDDD

The opposition won the toss and, having elected to bat, discovered rather quickly why village cricket keeps its heroes humble: 49 all out in 23.3 overs is not a total you build a season on. Gordon Fenner was in irresistible form, seven wickets for 25 runs, a spell to make the tea urn proud, while John Taylor bowled with the miserly discipline of a man who'd left his generosity at home — three maidens and 2-19. Merricks, Hooper and Adams departed for nought apiece, and by the time we'd gone from 28-1 to 38-4 the opposition's innings had the look of a house of cards in a gentle breeze.

Chasing was, frankly, a formality. Frank Taylor eased to 65 not out and William Buck to 58 not out, the pair strolling us to 134-1 in 25 overs without so much as a raised pulse. Nine byes did slip past William Buck behind the stumps — a reminder that even a good day has its blemishes — but nothing that troubled the outcome.

There was history in it too: Buck's runs took him past Jack Fryer into 10th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and Taylor's solitary wicket lifted him past Fenner into 2nd on the all-time wicket-takers' roll. Buck sits 3rd in this season's run charts, Taylor top of the bowling.

Victory by an innings and 32 runs. Now that, gentlemen, is how you spend a Saturday afternoon.

🗓️ 22 Sep – 28 Sep 1951 · 1951 — 1 match

Sat 22 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv HC Hunton Wanderers CC · AwayformWLDDD

Saturday last took us across to Hunton Wanderers, and having lost the toss — a coin that has taken quite against me this season — we were asked to field. Their openers settled in for a stand of 76 before we made any inroads, and the innings ran on to a healthy 160 for 4 off 46 overs, built chiefly on A Pilcher's watchful 90 not out, well supported by Reg Oldfield's 30. Gordon Fenner did the most damage with 2 for 27, Robert Muir chipped in with 1 for 12, and Frank Taylor toiled hard for his 1 for 62. I contented myself with containment rather than glory — six maidens, not a run given away lightly, if little else to show for it.

In reply we made a nervy start, an early wicket down with just 3 on the board, and I must report my own innings was a golden duck — we shall say no more about it. Frank Taylor, having already had a fine afternoon with the ball, then batted with real class for an unbeaten 66 to steady the ship and carry us to 90 for 3 off 31 overs. Nine byes, meanwhile, will have given poor Bob Arnold a testing day behind the stumps.

There was proper cause for celebration amid it all: Frank Taylor brought up 1,000 career runs for the club, a landmark long in the making. He now leads our 1951 wicket charts with 90, while William Buck sits third among the season's run-scorers with 621. A draw, honours shared — and on the day's evidence, a fair result for both sides.

🗓️ 15 Sep – 21 Sep 1951 · 1951 — 1 match

Sun 16 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv SH Shooter Hillians · HomeformLWLDD

Toss won, and with sunshine over Linton Park and no earthly reason to field first, I had no hesitation in choosing to bat. It was a knock built in fits and starts rather than one long procession — 245 for 6 from our 67 overs, anchored by a solid opening stand of 56 and rescued at the death by a defiant last-wicket effort worth another 56. William Buck's 40 and Bill Hall's unbeaten 40 gave the innings its spine, while I chipped in with 43 near the top — enough, I'm quietly chuffed to note, to nudge me past Arthur Cornwallis into fourth on the club's all-time run-scoring list.

Shooter Hillians began their reply in some disarray, a wicket down without a run scored, and though D- MacDonald (37) and C- MacDonald (22) threatened briefly to repair matters, the innings never recovered its composure. What followed was collapse of the most agreeable kind: 90 for 5 became 102 for 8 in a matter of minutes, with C- Trounce and P- Riley both back in the pavilion for ducks. Frank Taylor was the chief tormentor, bowling with real bite for 5 for 21 including two maidens, and in the process leapfrogged Herbert Rayner into second on the club's all-time wicket-taking list. Bill Hall weighed in with 2 for 36 as the visitors folded to 106 for 8 from their 39 overs.

Honours shared, then — neither side quite able to force the door open. With 854 runs and 89 wickets apiece this season, Frank and I seem to be carrying rather more than our fair share of the load; a draw on such a fine Sunday, though, is hardly cause for complaint.

🗓️ 08 Sep – 14 Sep 1951 · 1951 — 2 matches

Sun 09 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv GK Gillingham CC, Kent · HomeformLLWLD

I called correctly at the toss on a fine Sunday, and against Gillingham CC there was never any question in my mind — we would bat.

It was worth it. William Buck and I put on 94 for the first wicket before anything went our way for the bowlers, and by the time I was out for 59 the platform was well and truly laid. Buck fell just short of the same landmark on 51, Don Hartridge chipped in with a tidy 23, and we closed on a healthy 196 all out from 52.4 overs — though Tony Levick and Richard Harris will both want the book kept shut on their ducks for a while yet.

With the ball, Gillingham never quite got away from us despite losing a wicket for 8 early doors — R Beckenham played the innings of the day with a fine 57, well supported by A Townley's 31, and the pair added 84 for the second wicket to drag their side back into contention. Frank Taylor was the pick of the attack with 2-46, Richard Harris chimed in with 1-0, and I was happy enough with 1-40 and a couple of maidens to show for my afternoon's toil. They closed on 122-4, honours even, a draw when neither side could quite force the matter.

A word too for Don Hartridge, whose 23 today eased him past Ernest Cruttenden into 11th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — a proper achievement on a proper cricketing afternoon. All told, a draw was a fair result, and with the sun out and 196 on the board, nobody went home unhappy.

Sat 08 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv OR Old Roffensians · HomeformLLLWL

Won the toss on a fine Saturday and had no hesitation in taking first strike — the right call, I felt, and for once nobody in the pavilion argued with me. We batted with real purpose to reach 211 for 3 from our 36 overs, a total built on a lovely 66-run stand for the third wicket. I was fortunate enough to bat right through for 70 not out, well past my fifty, while John Taylor made an equally handsome 69 alongside me. Basil Sears chipped in with a tidy 25, and the extras column swelled by a generous 25 of its own — Old Roffensians' bowlers doing us more favours than they'd have liked.

In the field we made early inroads, the first wicket going for just 5, and Dennis Smith set the tone with a miserly 1 for 12. Ray Tippen and John Taylor both picked up a wicket apiece, Taylor bowling three maidens as tight as his grandmother's purse strings. But J. Wragg dug in for an unbeaten 86 and P. Bradley added 47 not out, and between them they saw their side safely to 169 for 3 — just enough to deny us victory.

Honours shared, then, and not a bad afternoon for the record books either: Taylor brought up 1,000 career runs for the club, Sears climbed to 12th on the all-time run-scorers' list, and Tippen moved into 18th among our wicket-takers. A draw, but one played in the right spirit — and with milestones like that, nobody left the ground too disappointed.

🗓️ 25 Aug – 31 Aug 1951 · 1951 — 2 matches

Sun 26 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv HC Hayle Place CC · HomeformDLLLW

Called it right at the toss on a fine Sunday, and with Hayle Place visiting Linton Park there was never much doubt I'd have first use of a decent-looking pitch. We wobbled early, four down for just the one, but the middle order set about repairing things in style. A stand of 57 for the third wicket steadied the ship, and from there Don Hartridge batted beautifully for an unbeaten 51, Basil Sears chipped in with 33, and I was fortunate enough to find some form myself, getting past fifty to finish on 58. We closed on a handsome 186 for 4 from our 36 overs - the sort of total that lets a captain sleep easy at tea.

A word for Don, too: that fifty nudges him past Basil and up to twelfth on the club's all-time run-scorers list, which is no mean feat and thoroughly deserved.

Then it was our turn to make merry with the ball, and merry we were. Hayle Place slipped from 7 for 1 to 15 for 4 in a hurry, with Waller, Hosking and Beckett all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. John Taylor was quite magnificent, tearing through them with 6 for 14 and six maidens to boot, while Frank Taylor (2 for 3) and Gordon Fenner (2 for 21) mopped up the rest. All out for 40 in 25.4 overs.

A win, then, by 146 runs - the sort of afternoon that makes the winter nets feel worthwhile. Onward to next week.

Sat 25 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · AwayformWDLLL

Skipper's Log — Saturday 25 August 1951. The road took us to Charles Arkcoll, and the coin, as it so often does when it matters least, went against us. They chose to bat, which on another day might have been the end of the story, but this was not another day.

An early wobble at 6-1 had us thinking this could be over by tea, and when Gordon Fenner, D Skinner and Frank Wright all went for ducks, it rather looked that way. But Stan Wratten dug in for an unbeaten 39, with Randolph Thompson chipping in 27, to drag them to 114/8 from their 47 overs. Frank Taylor was magnificent with the ball — 4 for 34, four maidens, not a run given away lightly — with Dennis Smith (2-33) doing his bit and I managed a wicket myself, 1-23, more by persistence than menace.

Chasing 115, we made hard work of easy work, as is our way. Frank Taylor, having already done the damage with the ball, then set about doing it with the bat — a fine 67, a proper made fifty that lifted him past F S W Cornwallis into 8th on the club's all-time run-scorers, to go with a haul that now has him 3rd among our wicket-takers. From 103-5 we somehow found our way to 117-8, B Lloyd's golden duck adding to the general nervousness, and poor Bob Arnold spilling nine byes behind the stumps for good measure.

Two wickets to spare, then, and a win that owed everything to Frank Taylor and rather too much to our nerves. We'll take it.

Sat 18 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv FX F R Sutton XI · HomeformLWWDL

Skipper's Log — Saturday 18 August 1951. F R Sutton XI came to Linton Park, and with the toss won and the ball in our hands, I fancied our chances of a tidy afternoon. So it largely proved: their innings folded for 130 off 38 overs, K Harris top-scoring with a stubborn 47 and I Machin chipping in 29, but everyone else found the going considerably harder — B Goodbody and Frank Sutton himself both trudging back for ducks. Robert Muir was in imperious form with the ball, 4 for 42 including four maidens, the sort of miserly spell that has opposition batsmen muttering into their tea. Bert Buckoke (2-19) and J Enfield (2-27) tidied up what was left.

Chasing 130 should have been the easy part. It wasn't. Gordon Bugge held things together with a fine 29, Robert Muir following his heroics with the ball with 24 more, and I contributed a modest 15 to proceedings. Peter Weedon, alas, bagged a golden duck, and between us we contrived to lose our last pair cheaply enough that what should have been a stroll became a nervy, self-inflicted slog — all out for 143 in 38.1 overs, scraping past their total by the barest of margins before finally getting there.

A curious footnote: my solitary wicket that day nudged me past S Christopherson into 20th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — small consolation for an afternoon that did nothing for the nerves. We got there in the end, but this was a match won by our bowlers and rescued, rather than commanded, by our bats. Heads up, lads — back to the nets.

🗓️ 11 Aug – 17 Aug 1951 · 1951 — 1 match

Sun 12 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv LC Lenham CC · AwayformDLWWD

Called correctly for once, I took one look at the Lenham track and fancied runs first. What we got instead was 108 all out in 40.2 overs, a total that flattered to deceive as it collapsed like a poorly pitched gazebo — 95 for six became 105 for nine before we could blink. Frank Taylor's watchful 26 and Tony Levick's brisk 21 were the mainstays; poor John Thirkell, so often our rock with the ball, contributed nought with the bat and wore the inevitable duck with good grace.

D- La Plain's unbeaten 59 held the innings together against everything we threw at him, and we did throw plenty: 59 for five became 62 for eight in a hurry as John Thirkell (4-37) ran through them, well backed by yours truly (3-43, ten maidens — I'll admit to enjoying that spell) and Arthur Cradduck (1-3). Tony Levick's gloves had a torrid afternoon, twelve byes the toll of trying.

They got there in the end, 140 all out, and no amount of milestones — my hundredth career wicket, Frank's climb past Ernest Cruttenden, John's past Tom Peach Snr — quite softens a defeat built on batting that let the side down. We'll be better for it next week.

🗓️ 21 Jul – 27 Jul 1951 · 1951 — 2 matches

Sun 22 Jul 1951 🧢 1st XIv RB R H Birketts · HomeformLWWLD

Sunday the 22nd of July found us at home, and as skipper I lost no time at the toss—correctly called, I might add, for once—and stuck us straight in to bat. A word, too, for William Buck, who continues his quietly excellent season and sits third in the 1st XI run charts with 342 to his name—proof, if any were needed, that not everything at this club is chaos and good fortune.

In the end honours were shared, a draw when neither side could quite force the issue further. Not the stuff of legend, perhaps, but an afternoon of sunshine, contest, and companionship on the square—which, when you strip away the scorebook, is really what we're all here for.

Sat 05 May 1951 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformDWLWW

Saturday the 5th of May found West Farleigh CC down at Linton Park, and precious little went right for us from the outset — I lost the toss, and they wasted no time electing to bat.

Not that it did them much good in the end. An early wicket with just 8 on the board set the tone, and though they steadied for a spell, the innings folded spectacularly from 48 for 7 to 53 all out, the last three wickets tumbling for next to nothing. R Dove, Trevor Fermor and S Pingery all trooped back without troubling the scorers, poor souls. I had the good fortune to finish with 4 for 11, with J Enfield (3 for 8, two miserly maidens) and H Buckoke (2 for 4) doing the damage either side of me.

Fifty-three seemed gettable — until it emphatically wasn't. We stumbled to 6 for 1 almost before anyone had settled into their deckchairs, subsided further to 15 for 4, and were bowled out for 47 in reply, J Enfield completing a wretched afternoon with a golden duck to set against his bowling heroics.

So we fell six runs short — the cruellest of margins, and a proper lesson in never chasing a small total casually. There was a crumb of comfort amid the gloom: those same four wickets nudged me past Don Hartridge into sixth on the club's all-time list, and left me second in this summer's wicket-charts. Small mercies. Heads up, back to the nets, and better luck — surely — next week.
