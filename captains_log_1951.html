Sun 30 Sep 1951 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · Homeform—

Sunday last brought Linton Park Cricket Club to our ground, and the toss falling kindly to us I had no hesitation in sending the eleven in to bat, the wicket looking a fair one for runs.

And runs we made, though not without the odd hiccup along the way. Basil Sears played the finer knock of the day, a well-made fifty carried through to 66, while Frank Taylor gave him stout company with 50 of his own, the pair putting on 92 for the second wicket in a stand that was the true backbone of our innings. Robert Muir chipped in with a useful 34, and between them these three did the bulk of the work, for elsewhere the card showed rather thinner fare, Don Hartridge, Dennis Smith and H Newport all departing without troubling the scorers. All out for 183 from 58.5 overs was nonetheless a total to be pleased with, and I was glad to add my own small contribution to the total before giving way to better men. It was a pleasant footnote to the afternoon that Frank Taylor's fifty carried him past Herbert Rayner and into sixth place among the club's all-time run-scorers, a milestone he wears lightly but which the pavilion noted with proper satisfaction.

With Linton Park set 184 to chase, our bowlers and fielders went about their business in the best traditions of the club, and the visitors were dismissed well short, the match falling to us by a handsome 128 runs. A thoroughly sporting contest throughout, and two points well and truly earned.

Sat 29 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv SI 1st Innings Maidstone Christian Institute · HomeformWDDDD

Saturday last, the 29th of September, saw Maidstone Christian Institute visit us in fine late-season sunshine, and I had no hesitation in sending them in to bat first. It proved a happy decision. Our bowlers found their length at once, and the visitors were bundled out for a modest 49 in but 23.3 overs, Smith alone offering real resistance with a top score of 22. Merricks, Hooper and Adams departed without troubling the scorers, and the innings slid from 28 for 1 to 38 for 4 almost before the pavilion clock had turned.

The spell of the day, and richly deserved, belonged to Gordon Fenner, whose 7 for 25 was bowling of the highest class. He was well supported by John Taylor, whose 2 for 19, built upon three tidy maidens, gave the batsmen not an inch, while Frank Taylor chipped in with a wicket of his own for good measure.

Our reply was a thing of quiet authority. Frank Taylor and William Buck put together unbeaten fifties of 65 and 58 respectively, seeing us home to 134 for the loss of a single wicket in 25 overs, though nine byes conceded told of a testing afternoon behind the stumps for Buck. There was cause for celebration besides the runs themselves: Buck's innings carried him past Jack Fryer into tenth place among the club's all-time run-scorers, while Taylor's wicket lifted him past Fenner into second on the all-time bowling list, a fine addition to a season in which he already leads our 1951 wicket charts with 91.

A handsome win, then, by an innings and 32 runs, and two points banked in the best traditions of the club against a sporting Maidstone side.

🗓️ 22 Sep – 28 Sep 1951 · 1951 — 1 match

Sat 22 Sep 1951 🧢 1st XIv HC Hunton Wanderers CC · AwayformWWDDD

In September I broke my run in the field, watching Hunton Wanderers post their innings from a captain's proper vantage, and a fine one it was for them. Their openers gave us little joy for the first hour, running up seventy-six before we prised the first breach in the order, and thereafter it was A Pilcher who took charge, ninety not out and never in any real trouble, with Reg Oldfield's thirty a handsome second string to the bow. Gordon Fenner bowled with real craft for his two for twenty-seven, Robert Muir and Frank Taylor chipped in behind him, and I like to think my own six maidens kept the scoring honest even if the wickets went to others. They closed on 160 for 4 from their forty-six overs, a total that asked serious questions of us.

We began our reply none too steadily, losing an early wicket with the board reading only three, and I must confess I contributed nothing to the recovery, caught out for a duck that the less said about the better. It fell to Frank Taylor to play the innings of the day, sixty-six not out of real substance and patience, and it was a particular pleasure that in compiling it he brought up his thousandth run for the club, a landmark thoroughly deserved. Nine byes told of a testing afternoon behind the stumps for Bob Arnold, but we closed on 90 for 3 from thirty-one overs with the match unresolved.

So honours were shared, and rightly so, for neither side could quite force the issue. A hard, honest game against a sporting side, and Frank Taylor's landmark gave us all something warm to carry back to the pavilion.

The visitors closed on 122 for 4 from 44.4 overs, unable quite to force the pace, and so honours were shared, a draw that seemed a fair reflection of two sides well matched and in the best traditions of the game.

Sat 25 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · AwayformWDLWL

Skipper's Log — Saturday 25 August 1951

The road took us to Charles Arkcoll on a fine Saturday, and the toss going against us I little minded, for our fellows have never wanted for enthusiasm with the ball in hand. Charles Arkcoll made their way to 114 for 8 from their 47 overs, though not without alarm, for they lost a wicket at 6 and never quite settled thereafter. Stan Wratten stood firm to finish 39 not out and was well supported by Randolph Thompson's 27, but Frank Taylor bowled beautifully to take 4 for 34, four of his overs going quite unscored, and Dennis Smith chipped in with 2 for 33. I was pleased to have a hand in it myself, taking 1 for 23, and three of their number departed without troubling the scorers.

A target of 115 looked a modest enough task, yet cricket has a way of humbling the confident. Frank Taylor, fresh from his exertions with the ball, played the innings of the day with a well-made 67, and for a time all seemed serene. Then, as so often on a sporting wicket, the innings wobbled alarmingly, tumbling from 103 for 5 to 117 for 8 in the space of a few overs, with B Lloyd unlucky enough to depart without scoring. Bob Arnold had a busy afternoon behind the stumps, though nine byes were the price of an otherwise willing effort.

It fell to the lower order to see us home, and in the best traditions of the club they did not fail us, bringing off a fine win by two wickets. A capital match, and a proud day too for Frank Taylor, whose runs and wickets both carried him up the club's all-time lists — a fellow in irresistible form this season, and not to be envied by the bowlers who must face him next.

Sun 12 Aug 1951 🧢 1st XIv LC Lenham CC · AwayformDLWWD

Here is the report.

At Lenham on the twelfth of August I won the toss and, with fair weather overhead, saw no reason to do anything but bat. It was not, in truth, our finest hour with the willow — 108 all out in forty-two overs and two balls tells its own tale — though Frank Taylor batted with real application for 26 and found a willing partner in Tony Levick, whose 21 kept the innings from total collapse. The middle order came apart in a rush, six down for ninety-five becoming nine down for a hundred and five before we could draw breath, and poor John Thirkell will not wish to be reminded of his golden duck. A modest total to defend, and we knew it.

Yet cricket has a way of restoring a side's spirits, and so it proved with the ball. Lenham were given a fine start by a solitary run for their first wicket, and might have run away with the match but for a spirited last-wicket stand of seventy-three that owed everything to D- La Plain's admirable unbeaten fifty-nine. We hauled them back regardless, from fifty-nine for five to sixty-two for eight in the twinkling of an eye, John Thirkell atoning handsomely with four for thirty-seven, Arthur Cradduck chipping in with one, and I found some rhythm of my own, finishing with three for forty-three from ten miserly maidens. Tony Levick had a trying afternoon behind the stumps, twelve byes going begging, and Lenham reached 140 all out to take the honours.

A word for the record books: I passed my hundredth career wicket, Frank Taylor climbed the run-scorers' list, and Thirkell moved up among our leading wicket-takers. Beaten, then, but not disheartened — we shall go again.
