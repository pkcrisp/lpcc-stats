🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 1960 · 1960 — 2 matches

Sun 25 Sep 1960 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformWLDWW

Sunday last, the 25th of September, saw us take the field as visitors, and when the coin fell my way I had no hesitation in asking us to bat first — the right call, I still maintain, whatever the scorebook might say about it afterwards.

It must be recorded, in the interests of accuracy rather than pride, that our innings amounted to precisely nothing — all out for none, a collapse so complete that poor Len Conway didn't even trouble the umpire's finger, marching off for a golden duck before he'd had time to take guard. Not our finest hour with the willow, and no amount of village-green charm will dress that scoreline up as anything other than what it was.

Yet the day had its consolations. Pat Hardwick's 24 runs, whatever the final total made of them, were enough to nudge him past Len Conway and into 19th place on the club's all-time run-scorers list — a small but permanent piece of Linton Park history secured on an otherwise chastening afternoon. For the season, I'm pleased to report I continue to lead the 1st XI run charts with 658 to my name, while Gordon Fenner tops the 2nd XI pile with 11.

However the arithmetic hangs together, the result on the board reads a fine win by six wickets. I shan't ask too many questions of a good thing. Onwards.

Sat 24 Sep 1960 🧢 1st XIv A A.Trembeths · HomeformWWLDW

Lost the toss on a fine late-September Saturday and were duly sent out to bowl against A.Trembeths — never my favourite start to proceedings, but the lads set about it with good cheer. Trembeths ground their way to 133 for 8 off their 44 overs, Fell top-scoring with a watchful 46 and Richardson chipping in with 45, but nothing that ever felt beyond us. I found some rhythm through the middle and finished with 4 for 22, with Terry Whittaker miserly as ever — four maidens and 2 for 24 — and Frank Brattle chipping away for 1 for 17.

A chase of 134 is never quite the formality the scoreboard suggests, and so it proved, though poor Frank had the misfortune of a duck to balance his good work with the ball. Bob Arnold anchored things nicely with an unbeaten 27, and I was glad to add 23 of my own, with John Waters weighing in with a tidy 22, to see us home by three wickets with more than a few overs to spare.

A quietly satisfying afternoon all round — and I'm told those four wickets nudged me past Michael Morton into 8th on the club's all-time list, which is more flattering than I probably deserve. A fine win, honestly earned. Onwards to the next one.

🗓️ 17 Sep – 23 Sep 1960 · 1960 — 2 matches

Sun 18 Sep 1960 🧢 1st XIv TS Tilling Steven · AwayformWWWLD

Sunday last took us out to Tilling Steven, a fixture that always promises more scenery than seam movement, and having called correctly I elected to bat, a decision I stood by with rather more confidence than the scoreboard later warranted. Seventy-six for eight from our sixteen overs was hardly a declaration of intent, rescued chiefly by Ray Howard's 36, made while others around him treated the crease as somewhere to be visited briefly and left. Cliff Anscombe and Terry Whittaker both departed for ducks, proof that cricket remains a game capable of humbling anyone who has ever middled one before. I added 17 of my own, more workmanlike than watchable, and was glad enough to reach double figures.

Seventy-six felt like a total to be defended rather than trusted, but Ray Howard had other ideas, tearing through the Tilling Steven order with figures of 6 for 28 that sent J. Mills, E. Priest and J. Bristow back to the pavilion without troubling the scorers. Alan Wilkes chipped in with 3 for 32 to finish matters off, and the innings folded for 66 from 15.5 overs.

It proved a landmark afternoon besides. Ray Howard brought up 2,000 career runs for the club, Len Conway's 2 runs eased him past Pat Hardwick into 19th on our all-time run-scorers list, and Howard's six-for lifted him past Terry Whittaker into 13th among the club's wicket-takers. I still lead this season's run charts with 621, and Alan Wilkes the wickets with 52. A scrappy 76 was never going to look pretty on the card, but ten runs is ten runs, and we'll take a win of any complexion going. A good day for the club.

Sat 17 Sep 1960 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformLWWWL

John Thirkell took guard 64 and was last man out, top score by a distance in an innings that had rather more twitches than a village hall whist drive. Winning the toss and choosing to bat looked shrewd early doors — until we lost the first wicket for the price of a pint at 1-1 and I began wondering whether I'd made a dreadful error of judgement. Roy Clarke's 20 steadied things, and the fifth-wicket pair's stand of 55 was the innings' spine, but the tail folded in the manner of a deckchair in a stiff breeze: 122 for 5 became 128 for 8 before we scraped to 129 for 9 off our 43.4 overs. Basil Sears and Peter Hughes both contributed ducks, for which I shall forgive them, being a captain of famously forgiving temperament (or so I keep telling the committee).

Defending 129 always felt like asking a deckchair to stop a train, but the bowlers made a proper fist of it. Ricky Lusher's 2 for 20 was the pick, with Robert Wood (2-30) and Tony Snell (1-30) chipping away and Terry Whittaker tying an end down with two maidens. Bearsted's Ron Parsons (32) and the unbeaten Colin Fleetwood (23) threatened to see it home, but they stalled at 103 for 5 off 33 overs, run out of overs rather than intent.

Honours shared, then — a draw that satisfied nobody and offended nobody, which is, I suppose, cricket's way of being diplomatic. Warm applause, too, for Basil Sears on his 200th appearance in Linton colours and Frank Brattle on his 100th — the sort of milestones that remind you what this club is really for.

Sun 11 Sep 1960 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformWLWWW

Beaten but not bowed once more, this being the sorry summary of our efforts against Staplehurst CC on a Sunday that started so promisingly with a correct call at the toss. Batting first seemed the obvious course, though 120 all out from our 37 overs suggests the pitch had other ideas. Frank Brattle's 22 and Robert Murray's 22 were the only real substance in the innings, while William Buck, Roy Clarke and Richard Harris each contrived to depart for a golden duck apiece — a hat-trick of nought that did nothing for the tea-interval mood.

In the field we fought harder than the scorecard suggests. Gordon Fenner was magnificent, 3 for 44 with five miserly maidens thrown in for good measure, and John Ames chipped in handsomely with 3 for 60. Between them they accounted for D. Burton, J. Klemen and H. Catt without so much as a run to their names. But L. Murrell had other plans, compiling a chanceless 74 not out to steer Staplehurst home at 121 for 6, a four-wicket win that flattered nobody more than it did him.

Amid the gloom, one shaft of light: Stan Wratten's 19 runs today were enough to nudge him past William Buck into second on the club's all-time run-scorers list, a small consolation on an otherwise humbling afternoon. Pat Hardwick continues to lead our 1960 charts with 599 runs, and John Ames now sits fourth among the season's wicket-takers. As for the result — well, beaten by four wickets, chastened, and already looking forward to putting it right next week.

A good day for the club, and no arguments from this captain.
