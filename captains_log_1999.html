Wed 22 Sep 1999 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDDWDL

I called it right and, for once, took the harder road — bat first on a Wednesday, September the 22nd, out at Penshurst Park. Whether that was captaincy or bloody-mindedness, the lads didn't argue.

We ground our way to 166 for 6 off 43 overs, which felt like a proper score without ever feeling like a big one. Mike Elworthy was immense — 89 of the finest, carrying the innings almost single-handedly and doing his level best to make it three figures before falling agonisingly short. Stuart Cheesman chipped in nicely with 39, the sort of unfussy support act every long innings needs. Elsewhere the Duck Club took its usual toll: Peter Whyte and Trevor Back both trudged off for nought, joining the season's growing and increasingly infamous roll of honour.

With the ball, we set about defending it — and Penshurst simply couldn't be shifted, closing us out unbeaten. A stalemate at the death, honours even, nobody quite able to land the killer blow.

Up on the season's run charts, Elworthy continues to make the rest of us look ordinary, his 89 pushing him to 310 for the summer and keeping him well clear at the top of the Midweek XI.

The verdict, then: a draw that flattered no one and disappointed no one — a fair result on a day when 166 always looked defendable, and very nearly wasn't enough to matter either way.

Sun 19 Sep 1999 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLDWLL

Sunday 19 September saw the Anchorians roll into Linton Park, and having called correctly at the toss I didn't dither for a second — a good pitch under a decent sky meant only one sensible decision, and we set off to bat. Piers McDonald led the charge with a fine 64, Daniel Thirkell followed suit with a classy 58, and Clive Newman weighed in with a well-crafted fifty of his own, the three of them hauling us to a handsome 261 for 7 from our thirty overs. Spare a sympathetic thought for poor Richard Piper, who contributed nothing but a golden duck to proceedings — these things happen to the best of us, and no doubt he'll dine out on the story rather more than he'd like.

Two hundred and sixty-two to win is no stroll, and the Anchorians gave it a proper go. Gary Myers top-scored with 45, well supported by Eddie Stanford's 37 and a useful 31 from T Appleby, but our attack never let the required rate settle. K. Holmes was the pick of the bowlers with a superb 3 for 36, and I was glad to chip in with 2 for 25 as we squeezed the innings shut at 201 for 9, Stuart Cheesman adding a tidy 1 for 4 for good measure.

Sixty runs the right way, two points banked, and — for the record — I now find myself atop the 1999 run charts with 943, while Jon Harris sits second among the wicket-takers on 29. A thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work, and one the lads can be rightly proud of.

Sat 18 Sep 1999 🧢 2nd XIv EC Elliotts CC · HomeformDLLLL

Saturday 18th September saw Elliotts CC visit, and losing the toss meant we were asked to chase — never the ideal start to a Saturday, but there we are. Not our day, then. Back to the nets, and back next week determined to put it right.

Sun 12 Sep 1999 🧢 1st XIv NS Northend Sunday · HomeformDLDWL

Sunday 12th September saw Northend Sunday roll into Linton Park with the sun out and expectations, frankly, modest. Darren Styles, meanwhile, made his contribution with the bat in the traditional style of a village pond — round, wet, and entirely without value. I'll spare him further commentary; cricket has a way of evening things out, as we'll see.

With ball in hand it was a different story on paper but not, alas, on the scoreboard. N Taylor and T Leach put together a stand of real substance — 76 not out and 58 respectively — and simply refused to be parted. Our bowlers toiled honestly: Darren Styles top of the pile with 1 for 24 (redemption, of sorts, for the earlier duck), Stuart Cheesman and Michael Beeney chipping in a wicket apiece, and Matt Large tying an end down with two tidy maidens. I contributed what I could with bat and ball, though not enough to turn the tide.

In the end Northend cantered home by six wickets. Not our day. Heads up, and back to the nets.
