🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 1986 · 1986 — 1 match

Sat 27 Sep 1986 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformLLLLD

Toss lost again — story of my season — and Hastings & St Leonards Priory duly asked us to field on a fine Saturday afternoon. We did strike early, removing the first wicket for just six, but from there Terry Wright settled in for a fine half-century, 60 well-made runs, ably supported by Tim Booth-Jones with a tidy 30, and between them they hauled the visitors up to a competitive 147 for 7 off their 51.2 overs.

I can't claim it was all one-way traffic for the bowlers, mind — I found some rhythm with the ball, finishing with 3 for 23 including seven maidens, hardly a run given away lightly, while Paul Gibson chipped in gamely with 3 for 37 and Paul Bowles took 1 for 17 for good measure.

Chasing 148, we never quite got out of second gear, subsiding to 98 for 8 off 39 overs — poor Dave Todd contributing nothing but a duck to the cause, bless him. Still, on a personal note it's pleasing to report I lead the 1st XI's run charts this season with 965 and sit second in the wickets with 48 — scant consolation for a Saturday when the runs simply wouldn't flow for the rest of us.

A draw, then — honours shared when neither side could force the matter. Not the win we wanted, but with the bowling doing its job and the batting still finding its feet, I'll happily take that and live to fight another Saturday.

🗓️ 20 Sep – 26 Sep 1986 · 1986 — 3 matches

Wed 24 Sep 1986 🧢 Midweek XIv P Penshurst · AwayformLDWWD

We travelled down to Penshurst last Wednesday with the sun doing its best and, having won the toss, I had no hesitation in sending us in to bat — a decision I shall claim full credit for regardless of what followed. What followed, happily, was rather good: 188 for 5 off our 35 overs, built on a marvellous 133-run stand for the third wicket between Nigel Thirkell and Paul Bowles. Nigel cruised to 66, Paul pressed on past his own fifty to 61, and David Castle chipped in with a tidy 32 to round the innings off nicely.

Penshurst, to their credit, did not fold. C. Fry made a fine 66 of his own, and D. Williams saw them home to a respectable 185 for 6 from 40 overs with an unbeaten 58, though Littlechild and D. Cooper will both want quiet words with themselves after departing without troubling the scorers. It was Matt Large who caused the real bother — 3 for 29 with two miserly maidens — while Michael Chambers took 1 for 37 and I managed 1 for 47 myself, more through persistence than penetration.

A word too for Michael Chambers, whose solitary wicket nudged him past Ian Brattle into 25th on the club's all-time list — a small footnote in a long and honourable ledger. With Nigel now top of our 1986 run charts and my own 18 wickets leading the bowling, the season has plenty still to say. As for Penshurst: honours even, a draw that flattered neither side but disappointed nobody either — the very essence of midweek cricket.

Sun 21 Sep 1986 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWLLLL

Called it right at the toss and, with visions of a flat pitch and a fast outfield dancing before me, had no hesitation in sticking us in to bat first — some decisions you live to regret, but this wasn't quite one of them. In the end Anchorians closed on 121 for 7 from their 37, and honours were shared — a draw when neither side had quite enough left to force it. Not a win, but not a defeat either: a fair result, honestly earned, and a pleasant enough Sunday for all that.

Wed 10 Sep 1986 🧢 Midweek XIv The Mote CC · AwayformDLDWW

Wednesday 10 September brought us to The Mote CC, and for once the toss went my way — I had no hesitation putting us in, a decision that for the first hour or so of Dalrimple's fifty looked positively inspired. We'd stumbled early, losing a wicket with just 3 on the board, the sort of nervy start that has openers muttering into their tea, but Dalrimple and Ian Hammond restored calm with a fine second-wicket stand of 105. Dalrimple went on to 86, Hammond made a tidy 44, and I chipped in with 25 lower down, as we closed on a healthy 199/6 from our 46 overs.

Defending it was always going to be the sterner test, and The Mote made us work for every run. Hatcher's 33 and Mullarkey's 31 gave their innings some backbone, with Bristow's 23 and a stubborn 55-run third-wicket stand threatening to bat us out of it altogether. But we kept chipping away — Alan Boyns led the attack beautifully with 2-23, I managed 2-24 with three maidens to keep things tight, and Michael Chambers picked up 2-50, a landmark effort that nudges him past Ian Brattle into 25th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list. Poor Jordan departed without troubling the scorers, but The Mote dug in to close on 161/8 from their 42 overs.

In the end neither side could quite land the killer blow, and honours were shared. A draw it may have been, but with Dalrimple third in this season's run charts and my own 9 wickets keeping me second in the bowling table, I left The Mote reasonably satisfied with an afternoon's honest toil.

Sun 07 Sep 1986 🧢 1st XIv MH Mad Hatters · HomeformWLWLL

In the end we fell agonisingly short, beaten by four wickets — a result that stings rather more given how well we'd bowled. Still, with 949 runs to my name and Harris purring away with 73 wickets this season, the engine room is in fine order. We'll be better for it next week.

And gettable it nearly was. We fell agonisingly short, all out for 182/6 in 44 overs, beaten by 4 wickets.

A defeat, then, but not a disgrace — and with the runs and wickets still stacking up nicely on the season charts, we'll dust ourselves down and come back stronger next week.
