🦆 Duck News — 28 September 1985

0 ducks
Week 23 · 28 Sep 1985
A single fixture out at Linton Park this week, and for once the Duck Club's talent-spotters returned home empty-handed. All eleven took guard, all eleven got off the mark, and not so much as a nervous nudge for a duck-hunter to pounce on. 0️⃣ Zero ducks. Genuinely zero — the rarest scoreline the Club can produce, rarer even than a king pair. It happens. Even the most committed order occasionally forgets how to get out cheaply, and this was one of those days where the art of not scoring simply wasn't practised by anyone in whites. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to induct into the honours reel — the exclusive zero club adds no new members this week, and the existing membership can rest easy for a few more days. Spare a thought, too, for the season's front-runners below, who will have watched this result with quiet frustration — no fixture, no chance to add to the tally, no shot at closing the gap. The hunt resumes properly at the next opportunity. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏆🍩 1. Jim Hall — 5 ducks in 24 innings (20.83%) 2. Andrew Kember — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) 3. David Castle — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 4. Dennis Salisbury — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.86%) — a fearsome strike rate for all the wrong reasons 5. Matt Large — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) Andrew Kember and Dennis Salisbury lead the percentage race by some distance, though Jim Hall's volume keeps him top of the pile for now. Plenty of season still to play for. A quiet week for the Duck Club, then — but forewarned is forearmed. Next time out, someone's luck runs out, and the ledger starts filling again. Bats up, quacks ready. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 21–25 September 1985

3 ducks
Week 22 · 21 Sep 1985 – 25 Sep 1985
Only three visits to the exclusive zero club this week, but the standards of the association remain as rigorously upheld as ever. Quality over quantity, as always. Saturday 21 September 1st XI away to West Farleigh CC At number seven, Malcolm Pay departed to Micky Back without scoring — his 3rd duck of the season and 26th of a long and storied Linton career, good enough for 23rd on the all-time table of 1069. He's not carrying the family torch alone either: sibling Colin Pay has a single career nought of his own, presumably still waiting for his invitation to the top table. On this very day back in 1958, club great Stan Wratten was also trudging back for nought, against Tilling Steven — he went on to finish with 35 (12th all-time) and a seat on the "Quacking Together" honours board alongside John Ames. Malcolm keeps distinguished historical company. Wednesday 25 September Midweek XI away to Penshurst Park CC A costly outing at Penshurst, who shipped two ducks in the same innings. Opening up, David Castle fell to N Fowler for nought — his 4th duck of the season and, remarkably, his 4th career duck from just 23 innings. A rapid introduction to the art of not scoring that already has him keeping company on the Tourist Duck top 10. At four, Nigel Thirkell edged F Manville for his only duck of the season but his 33rd overall, enough for 16th on the all-time list from a monumental 531 innings. A permanent fixture on the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and one half of the "Only Two Quacks" pairing with Jim Hall. The surname is practically its own duck dynasty — father John sits on 90 career ducks, brother Tim on 55, brother Simon on 14, and relative Stuart on 1. The apple, the tree, and several other apples. Both Castle and Thirkell share good on-this-day company — back in 1960, Len Conway also made a duck against Linton Park CC en route to 12 career ducks and a place on the Debutante Duck board. Week in summary A lean week — 3 ducks from 3 matches, with two arriving in the same Midweek innings at Penshurst. Not the club's most productive week for non-productivity, but Malcolm Pay continues his relentless climb up the all-time ladder regardless. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jim Hall — 5 ducks, 23 innings (21.74%) 2. Andrew Kember — 4 ducks, 12 innings (33.33%) 3. David Castle — 4 ducks, 14 innings (28.57%) 4. Dennis Salisbury — 3 ducks, 7 innings (42.86%) 5. Matt Large — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25.00%) Jim Hall holds firm at the top, but with Castle and Kember snapping at his heels on percentage alone, this year's Duck of the Year race is far from settled. 🏏 One more round of fixtures before the season's out — plenty of time yet for the club to welcome its newest members. Bats up, ducks incoming. 0️⃣🦆

Sean is already a paid-up member of the club's Retirement Duck honours board, a note reserved for whenever that day eventually comes, not for today. Castle is also a fixture in the Tourist Duck top 10, gathering noughts on his travels with the same enthusiasm he shows at home. Exactly a quarter of a century earlier, on this same date in 1960, Cliff Anscombe (1957–1975) was also making the long walk back against Tilling Steven, on his way to a career haul of 25 — the Duck Club's calendar strikes again. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches is a modest return by Linton standards — the 3rd XI and Sunday sides evidently kept their heads down and their bats straight. 🦆 Duck News — 7–11 September 1985

3 ducks
Week 20 · 7 Sep 1985 – 11 Sep 1985
A short midweek flurry closed out the week, with three ducks across three matches and one repeat offender picking up a rare "pair" for his troubles. Saturday — 1st XI v Madhatters Dennis Salisbury departed for nought at four, caught by a bemused Edmonton off a delivery nobody could quite pin a bowler's name to ("c Unsure b - Edmonton" — the scorebook's honesty is appreciated). His second duck of the season and second of a fledgling Linton career, 2 in just 6 innings, sits him 149th of 1069 in the all-time list. On this very day in 1981, Richard Ashby was doing much the same against Paul CC, en route to 26 career ducks across 1962–1985 and 23rd on the all-time table. Dennis has some way to go, but the calendar, at least, is on his side. Wednesday — Midweek XI v The Mote CC Jim Hall, bowled by Richard Chapman at six, brought up his 5th duck of the season and — take a breath — his 62nd of an extraordinary Linton career spanning 399 innings. That's second on the all-time list, club royalty of the exclusive zero club, and with "Duck of the Year 1985" already sitting on his honours board he's very much defending that crown live rather than reminiscing about it. His cabinet also carries two separate Only Two Quacks partnerships and three Quacking Together citations — Jim, it seems, rarely goes down alone. On this same day in 1954, Len Conway was walking back for nought against Old Roffensians, en route to 12 career ducks and 50th all time. One place lower in the order, Dennis Salisbury struck again — bowled by the same Richard Chapman, for his third duck of the season and third of his career. That completes a grim little pair for the week: out for nought on Saturday, out for nought on Wednesday, four days and two different bowlers apart. Fittingly, Len Conway keeps him company on the this-day-in-history front too. Week in Summary Three matches, three ducks, and Dennis Salisbury responsible for two-thirds of the harvest — a proper double for the collection, triumph through adversity in its purest form. Elsewhere Jim Hall's 62nd continues to look positively unassailable at the top of the all-time table. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Jim Hall — 5 ducks, 21 innings, 23.8% - Andrew Kember — 4 ducks, 12 innings, 33.3% - Dennis Salisbury — 3 ducks, 6 innings, 50.0% - Matt Large — 3 ducks, 11 innings, 27.3% - David Castle — 2 ducks, 8 innings, 25.0% Dennis's 50% strike rate is one to keep an eye on — at that pace he'll be knocking on Jim's door before Christmas. 🍩 Next fixture, next chance. The zero club never closes its doors — see you at the crease.

🦆 Duck Report — 25 to 29 August 1985 🦆

5 ducks
Week 18 · 25 Aug 1985 – 29 Aug 1985
Five fresh entries to the exclusive zero club this week, spread across four fixtures as the 1985 season rounds into its final furlong. The art of not scoring remains alive and well at Linton Park. 🏏 Saturday 25th — 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC Jim Hall opened the week's account, bowled by R Cross for his 4th duck of the campaign and a chastening 61st of an already legendary career, good enough for 2nd on the all-time list of 1069 names. Jim arrives at this milestone as reigning Duck of the Year for 1985 itself, and with entries on the Debutante Duck, Full House nearly-man, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, he is less a batsman than a walking honours board. On this very day in 1976, Brian Piper made a duck of his own against Gravesend CC, kindred spirits separated by nine years and a scorebook. Fittingly, Jim's old sparring partner in the "Only Two Quacks" pairing, Allan Cooper, followed him back to the pavilion in the very same innings, bowled by D Goodger at number 11 for his 2nd of the season and 33rd of his career, 14th on the all-time roll. Allan shares Jim's on-this-day parallel too, both matched by Brian Piper's 1976 effort. 🏏 Saturday 26th — 1st XI v Bearsted CC The middle order wobbled at home to Bearsted. Paul Gibson edged behind for his 1st duck of the season, a 6th career quack from 47 innings and 102nd on the all-time list. He joins the on-this-day roll alongside Tim Thirkell, who made his own on this very date in 1979. Two down the order, David Piper was bowled by Robin Cunningham, also his 1st of the season and 7th career duck in just 20 innings, 86th all-time, a brisk introduction to senior Duck Club life for the younger of the Piper siblings, whose brother Brian already carries 14 of his own. David shares the same 1979 Tim Thirkell parallel as Paul, a matching set from the same collapse. 🌴 Thursday 29th — Tour XI v Falmouth CC On tour and far from home, David Castle became the tourists' latest offering, bowled by Keith Meneer for the very first duck of his senior career, in just his 15th innings, 321st on the all-time list. A paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10 even before this one, he now has genuine away-day pedigree to go with it. On this day in 1954, Stan Wratten was doing exactly the same thing against a D Hambrook XI, en route to 35 career ducks of his own. 📊 Week in summary Five ducks from four matches, with the 1st XI doing the heavy lifting on four of the five. Old Anchorians and Bearsted between them made for a genuinely grim weekend at the crease, while the Tour XI kept its away form suitably eventful with one lonely entry out at Falmouth. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Andrew Kember — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.3%) - Jim Hall — 4 ducks in 20 innings (20.0%) - Matt Large — 3 ducks in 9 innings (33.3%) - Allan Cooper — 2 ducks in 9 innings (22.2%) - Jon Harris — 2 ducks in 13 innings (15.4%) Jim Hall closes to within touching distance of top spot, one more anywhere and he shares the summit. Roll on the next fixture. The zero club never sleeps, and there's always room for one more member. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 17–21 August 1985

3 ducks
Week 17 · 17 Aug 1985 – 21 Aug 1985
Three matches on the card this week, and Gravesend CC did all the concentrating for us: every duck of the period fell in the one fixture, at home to Gravesend on the 21st. A trio for the price of one — Gravesend's attack clearly fancied its chances at Linton Park. Midweek XI v Gravesend CC (home, 21 August) Dennis Salisbury opened the account at first drop, caught K Prytherch off Dave Elliott without troubling the scorers. It's the first duck of a young Linton career — just his 3rd innings — and moves him to 1 for the season and 1 overall, 321st in the all-time standings. Good historical company for the date, mind: on this very day in 1955, John Ames also made a duck, against Halfway House, on his way to 49 in a career that ran 1952 to 1978. Next in was Andrew Kember, run out by an attribution the scorebook simply records as "Unsure" — not a dismissal that reflects terribly well on the calling, but the Duck Club doesn't discriminate by method. It's his 4th of the season and 4th of a 12-innings career, good for 116th all-time, and it keeps him a fully paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having ducked on his very first outing, plus the rare and slightly ominous Quack-to-Quack and Retirement Duck honours awaiting whenever that eventual day comes. Same date, same 1955 John Ames echo for Andrew too. Then Jim Hall, caught Colin Myers off Ben Williams, chalked up his 3rd of the season and a remarkable 60th for the club — second on the all-time list. Jim already wears the crown for 1985's Duck of the Year, and this latest zero only tightens his grip on it. A card groaning with honours besides: Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top ten, Full House nearly-man on 10 of 11 positions, and no fewer than five Only Two Quacks and Quacking Together partnerships to his name. And yes — same 21 August, same 1955 John Ames company as his teammates. A tidy little cluster, then: three ducks, one match, one shared date in the history books. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Andrew Kember — 12 inns, 4 ducks (33.33%) 2. Matt Large — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.86%) 3. Jim Hall — 18 inns, 3 ducks (16.67%) 4. Jon Harris — 12 inns, 2 ducks (16.67%) 5. Malcolm Pay — 15 inns, 2 ducks (13.33%) Jim Hall leads the chasing pack for the club's most decorated duck-hunter, but with Andrew Kember and Matt Large both boasting duck rates north of 30%, the 1985 race is far from settled. Next fixture, the exclusive zero club stands ready to welcome its newest and returning members alike. 🦆🍩

The 1st XI's other two fixtures this week passed off duck-free, a rare and faintly suspicious outbreak of competence up and down the order. 📅 Week in Summary Three matches, one duck — a distinctly lean harvest by Duck Club standards, but the club maintains that quality trumps quantity, and Malcolm's solitary contribution was a fine specimen of the art of not scoring. Rest assured the Duck Club's scouts remain vigilant. 🦆 Duck News — 29–30 June 1985 🦆

2 ducks
