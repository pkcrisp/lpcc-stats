Sun 19 Sep 1976 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWWWDW

Wednesday afternoon at the ground, and I'll admit tossing that coin still gives me a flutter after all these years — heads it was, and bat first I said, feeling rather bullish. The lads rewarded that confidence in style: 202 for 5 off our forty overs, built around a knock I'm rather proud of, 101, my first century in more years than I care to count. Nigel Thirkell gave the innings real backbone with a crisp 56, and Paul Brattle chipped in a handy 21 to round things off nicely. Tea never tasted better.

Anchorians came out chasing with intent, but J Coppins' 40 and support from Alan Dixon (38) and S Tapping (28) never quite had the legs against a Linton Park attack that smelled blood. E Cherry went for a duck, which is always a special kind of misery to inflict on a batsman, and from there the innings rather unravelled — all out for 155 in 40.4 overs. I found some rhythm with the ball myself, finishing with 4 for 11, while Trevor Back bowled beautifully for 3 for 9 and Tony Gauna was miserliness personified, 1 for 9 with two maidens to his name.

A word of pride away from the scoreboard too — Nigel's 56 today nudges him past Cliff Anscombe into 8th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, a fine achievement quietly earned.

A 47-run win, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round. Onwards we go.

Sat 18 Sep 1976 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformDWWWD

Saturday 18 September 1976, and West Farleigh CC came calling. I won the toss and, with the sun out and the outfield running true, there was only ever going to be one decision — we batted.

What a decision it proved. Paul Bowles played the innings of the season, a magnificent hundred, 104 no less, and with Paul Brattle chipping in a fine 42 we posted 178 for 3 off our 45 overs — a total with a bit of menace to it. As a nice postscript, that century nudges Paul past Stan Wratten and into 4th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, which is the sort of thing that gets mentioned in the bar for weeks.

Defending it was another matter, and West Farleigh made us work. Don Fermor top-scored with 36, Brian Piper made 31 and Peter Cooper 20, and they battled to 160 all out off their 50 — though Trevor Fermor's duck was some small consolation for us. I managed to find a bit of rhythm with the ball and finished with 4 for 68, while Nigel Thirkell bowled a spell of real discipline, 2 for 15 with five maidens, and Martin Mears chipped in with 2 for 18. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, ten byes going begging, but no matter.

Eighteen runs the right side of the ledger at the close. A thoroughly good day for the club, and one Paul Bowles in particular will remember for a long while yet.

Sunday 12 September, and Mersham Hatch rolled up for a fine late-summer fixture.
