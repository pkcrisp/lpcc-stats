🦆🍩 Duck Report — 27-28 September 1997 🍩🦆

3 ducks
Week 23 · 27 Sep 1997 – 28 Sep 1997
The final weekend of September delivered a modest but perfectly formed haul: three ducks from four fixtures, each one added with due ceremony to the club's ledger of dubious distinction. 1st XI Sunday at M.Large saw Alan Boyns depart at number 2, bowled by "Unsure", for his 1st duck of the season. It's his 17th career nought from 174 innings, good enough for 51st on the all-time list, and keeps him a paid-up member of the Diamond Duck board from way back in 1990. Curiously, on this very day in 1952, one Ray Tippen was doing exactly the same thing against Linton Park CC — four ducks across a career that ran 1949 to 1955, and a Retirement Duck alumnus to boot. History, as ever, repeats. 2nd XI Away at Hunton Wanderers, the top order took the brunt of it. Opener Mike Elworthy fell early, bowled by Gavin Day, for his 4th duck of the season and 26th of a long and storied career — 257 innings deep and sitting 32nd all-time, with a place on the Tourist Duck top 10 for his trouble. At number 5, Richard Ellis followed him back, bowled by Dave Leppard, his 3rd of the season and 11th overall from just 45 innings — a healthy strike rate in this particular discipline. Richard remains one of the select few to carry the Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack boards, having opened his account with a nought all those years ago — standing honours, not fresh news, but worth a nod all the same. Both fell on the same calendar date that, back in 1964, saw Robert Murray of the 1958-1965 vintage do precisely likewise against Linton Park CC — 17 career ducks and 53rd on the all-time list. Some things never change. Week in Summary Four matches, three ducks, and not a single pair or family affair to report — a quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, more a light drizzle than a downpour. The zero club welcomes its latest inductees regardless. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🏆 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks / 42 inns (14.3%) 2. Mike Elworthy — 4 ducks / 26 inns (15.4%) 3. David Castle — 4 ducks / 39 inns (10.3%) 4. Adrian Caddick — 3 ducks / 12 inns (25.0%) 5. Adam Pooley — 3 ducks / 14 inns (21.4%) Elworthy's latest addition keeps him snapping at Piers McDonald's heels for the season crown — one more nought and he's level at the top. As the season winds towards its close, who'll sneak onto the honours board before stumps are drawn for winter? We'll be watching. We'll be counting. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 20 to 24 September 1997

6 ducks
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1997 – 24 Sep 1997
Six ducks fell across four fixtures this week as the season edges toward its close, with familiar names and famous surnames both adding to the pile. Saturday 20th — Borden CC (away) The 2ns did brisk business at Borden. Daniel Thirkell, latest scion of the club's most prolific duck-laying dynasty (kin John, Tim, Nigel, Simon and Stuart Thirkell own 241 career noughts between them), was caught behind off Stuart Blackbourn for his 2nd of the season and 7th career duck from 64 innings — 107th of all 1225 names ever recorded, and level in the calendar with Eddie Lawrence, who made his own on this very date back in 1959. David Lines followed at nine, caught off Richard Truelove for his maiden season duck and 6th career one from a brisk 14 innings. He's a paid-up Retirement Duck club member from some past farewell, though nobody's suggesting he's done yet. He too joins Eddie Lawrence's 20 September 1959 club. Adrian Caddick completed the set, bowled by Truelove for his 3rd of the season and 12th career duck, a tally shared with the legendary Bert Buckoke (1928–1953). Adrian's season strike-rate of 27.27% is the sharpest in this week's top five 🍩 — and with sibling Mike Caddick already sat on 7, futility clearly runs in the blood. Sunday 21st — Anchorians CC (home) The 1st XI offered two for the honours board. Matt Large fell at number two for his 3rd of the season, his 56th career duck moving him to 5th of all 1225 — a card already stacked with Duck of the Year honours from 1987, '88, '89, '92, '93 and '95, plus top-ten places on Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck. On this very day in 1958, Stan Wratten was doing much the same. Stuart Cheesman went two overs later, bowled for his 2nd of the season and 24th career duck — level with Dick Langridge (1943–1978). Stuart's Full House and Debutante Duck honours are long since banked, and with Fred Cheesman on 32 career ducks and sibling Steve on 13, the family name keeps this ledger well fed. He shares Stan Wratten's 1958 date too. Wednesday 24th — Penshurst Park CC (away) The Midweek XI's lone contribution came from Mike Elworthy, bowled for his 3rd of the season and 25th career duck — level with Cliff Anscombe (1957–1975) and 35th on the all-time list. On this day in 1989, Piers McDonald, who already holds this season's Duck of the Year crown, was doing exactly the same. Week in summary Six ducks, four matches, one fixture that stayed conspicuously fowl-free. Borden took the lion's share with three in a single innings, and the Thirkell and Cheesman clans both reminded everyone that a taste for nought tends to run in families. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 42 inns, 6 ducks (14.29%) 2. David Castle — 37 inns, 4 ducks (10.81%) 3. Adrian Caddick — 11 inns, 3 ducks (27.27%) 4. Adam Pooley — 14 inns, 3 ducks (21.43%) 5. Fred Cheesman — 15 inns, 3 ducks (20.0%) The season's nearly out, but there's still time for one more name to sneak onto the board. See you at the next fixture — bat, and luck, permitting. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck News — 13-14 September 1997

1 duck
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1997 – 14 Sep 1997
A curiously quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club — three fixtures on the go, and only one man brave enough to accept membership. Triumph through adversity was thin on the ground, but where it landed, it landed with pedigree. 🏏 1st XI — Northend Sunday Coming in at six, Nigel Thirkell departed c R Swaisland b N Taylor for the weekend's lone duck. It's his 2nd of the season and a hefty 51st of an illustrious career spanning 945 innings — good enough for 7th place in the all-time table of 1225 duck-collectors, rarefied air by any measure. Nigel is a former Duck of the Year (1986), a fixture of the Homebody Duck top 10, a card-carrying member of the Tourist Duck elite, and shares an Only Two Quacks entry with Jim Hall — a résumé of failure most club stalwarts would frame. And it runs in the blood: John, Tim, Simon, Daniel and Stuart Thirkell all hold career ducks of their own, making this very much a family trade practised down the generations. Splendidly, on this very date in 1958, Stan Wratten also strolled back for nought — against Tunbridge Wells CC, no less — on his way to a career total of 35, good for 18th all-time. The calendar, it seems, has a long memory for these things. 📋 Week in Summary Elsewhere the rest of the club kept remarkably clean sheets — the 2nd, 3rd and other sides let the ducks slip through the net entirely. One duck from three matches is a lean return for the hunting season, but the tally ticks forward all the same, and there's still time yet for the collection plate to fill up. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🥇 1. Piers McDonald — 40 inn, 6 ducks (15.0%) 2. David Castle — 36 inn, 4 ducks (11.11%) 3. Adam Pooley — 13 inn, 3 ducks (23.08%) 3. Fred Cheesman — 13 inn, 3 ducks (23.08%) 5. Ian Brattle — 7 inn, 2 ducks (28.57%) With the season entering its final stretch, the race for Duck of the Year is far from settled. Pads on, gentlemen — the honours board has room for fresh nominations next weekend. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 6-10 September 1997

0 ducks
Week 20 · 6 Sep 1997 – 10 Sep 1997
A quiet week down at Linton Park, and not in the way the Duck Club likes to celebrate quiet. Three fixtures came and went between the 6th and the 10th, and — brace yourselves — not a single nought was troubled into existence. Eleven batsmen per side, three times over, and every last one of them found the wherewithal to get off the mark. Whether through genuine improvement, blind luck, or simply not being seen by whoever keeps the book, the exclusive zero club gained not one new entry this week. It happens. Even the art of not scoring has its off weeks. The bowlers, presumably, are furious. Spare a thought for the regulars on the leaderboard below, several of whom will have spent the weekend nervously eyeing the fixture list, wondering when their next opportunity for glorious failure will present itself. Fear not, gentlemen — the season isn't over yet, and Linton Park has never gone two weeks without paying its respects to the duck. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1997 🏏 🥇 Piers McDonald — 6 ducks in 38 innings (15.79%) 🥈 David Castle — 4 ducks in 35 innings (11.43%) 🥉 Adam Pooley — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.00%) 🍩 Fred Cheesman — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.00%) 0️⃣ Ian Brattle — 2 ducks in 7 innings (28.57%) Piers McDonald sits proudly atop the pile, though Adam Pooley and Ian Brattle's frankly alarming duck rates suggest either of them could close the gap in a hurry given the chance. 🦆 DUCK NEWS — 5 September 1997 🦆

0 ducks
Week 19 · 5 Sep 1997
A rare and troubling silence descends upon Linton Park this week. One solitary fixture took to the field on the 5th of September, and — brace yourselves — not a single duck was laid. Eleven-a-side, bat after bat, and the entire Duck Club roll of honour left entirely untroubled. The zero club, for once, recorded a big fat nothing of its own, which is either deeply ironic or exactly the kind of self-referential joke the ducks would engineer if they had a sense of humour. Somewhere out there, an entire batting line-up got off the mark, ran quick singles, middled a few, and generally behaved like the game was meant to be played. Frankly, unacceptable levels of competence. The correspondent has checked twice. There is no golden duck to salute, no pair to mourn, no family bloodline to induct into the honours board this week, and certainly no King Pair to canonise. The ducks, it seems, took the weekend off. One suspects they'll be back with interest. Let this serve as a reminder to the current chasing pack — Piers, David, Adam, Fred and Ian below — that quiet weeks like this one are precisely how leads get built without anyone noticing. Sneak a nought in while the world isn't watching and you could leapfrog the lot of them by next Saturday. 🍩 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 🍩 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks from 36 innings (16.67%) 2. Plenty of season left for a late surge. 🏏 Onwards to the next fixture, then — where normal service, i.e. the dreaded nought, will surely resume. 🦆 Duck News — 23 to 29 August 1997

4 ducks
Week 18 · 23 Aug 1997 – 29 Aug 1997
A quieter week for the exclusive zero club, but four names still queued up for membership renewal across the club's fixtures, home and abroad. The tourists in Cornwall did their bit for the cause too. Saturday 23 August — 2nd XI v Marden (home) - Paul Bowles fell at first drop, bowled J- Kent, for his 2nd duck of the season and 54th of an epic Linton career — he sits at 6th of 1225 in the all-time table, a rarefied neighbourhood indeed. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966, 1968) and permanent fixture of the Ducks on the Road top 10, Paul finds history repeating itself: on this very day in 1981, Brian Piper (1973–1997, 20 ducks) also trudged off against Old Anchorians CC. - Adam Pooley followed him back not long after, lbw to S- Andrews at six, his 3rd of the season and 9th career duck from just 31 innings — a startling strike rate that already has him at 84th of 1225. Fittingly, the honours board has long since bound the pair together as "Only Two Quacks (with Adam Pooley / Paul Bowles)" — a partnership gloriously reaffirmed this very innings, both back in the shed inside the same session. Adam, too, echoes 1981's Brian Piper on this day. Thursday 28 August — Tour XI v Truro CC (away) - Fred Cheesman, touring in style, was bowled by Martin Phillips at five for his 3rd duck of the season and 32nd of his career, good enough for 22nd of 1225 all time. A Full House member (a duck at every position, no less), two-time Duck of the Year (1990, 1991) and Tourist Duck top 10 regular, Fred proves the noughts travel just as well as the runs don't. On this day in 1996, Daniel Thirkell also made a duck, against 1st Innings Mullion. A chip off a well-quacked block too — sons Stuart (23) and Steve (13) are handsomely represented in the Duck Club ledgers. - Last man Darren Styles rounded off the innings, bowled Stephen Heayn, for his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from 50 innings — 235th of 1225 and climbing. He shares Fred's echo of Daniel Thirkell's 1996 duck against Mullion. Week in summary Four ducks from four fixtures — the other two matches went entirely fowl-free, which the correspondent finds almost suspicious. Both casualties came in pairs, with the 2nd XI doubling up at home and the touring party matching them stroke for stroke down in Cornwall. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 35 innings (17.14%) - David Castle — 4 ducks, 33 innings (12.12%) - Adam Pooley — 3 ducks, 11 innings (27.27%) - Fred Cheesman — 3 ducks, 12 innings (25.0%) - Ian Brattle — 2 ducks, 7 innings (28.57%) Piers McDonald leads the chase, but with Adam Pooley and Ian Brattle both boasting duck rates north of a quarter, the race for Duck of the Year is far from settled. Next fixture, same invitation: middle stump, first ball, and a place in history awaits. 🦆🍩

Darren Styles, in at first drop, was caught and bowled by Jim Paulett — the bowler doing his own fielding, sparing nobody the walk of shame. After 47 innings of quietly avoiding the dreaded nought, Darren finally opens his account: 1 for the season, 1 for the career. Welcome to the club, officially. Fittingly, on this very day in 1953, John Ames — one of Linton's most decorated quackers, 49 ducks to his name and 8th in the all-time charts — was also sent back for nought, against Mote A. It's Clive's second duck of the campaign, nudging him to 9 for the club from 164 innings, a solid 85th of 1225 in the all-time standings — the art of not scoring is clearly a discipline he's committed to studying. On this day in 1969, Percy Brattle — a Linton stalwart from 1934 to 1973, 16 ducks to his name, ranked 54th all-time, and Duck of the Year that very season — also departed for nought, against Mote A. History, as ever, repeats itself in the most unscoring of ways. 📋 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches is about as restrained as the exclusive zero club gets — no pairs, no king pairs, and certainly no family affairs to report this week. 🦆 Duck Report — 9 to 15 August 1997

5 ducks
Week 16 · 9 Aug 1997 – 15 Aug 1997
Five matches across the week, five ducks laid — and the 1st XI's trip to Mersham Le Hatch alone supplied a hat-trick of noughts. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. 🏏 1st XI — Mersham Le Hatch (away) A torrid middle order for Linton. David Castle fell to A Childs, a 4th duck of the season and a startling 28th of his career from 465 innings — 28th in the all-time table and a paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10. On this very day back in 1964, club great John Thirkell (1943–1977, 90 ducks, still #1 of 1225 all time) also made a duck — Castle keeps fine historical company. Same bowler, same fate for Jim Hall — only his 1st duck of the season, but a monumental 73rd of his career, 3rd on the all-time list from 466 innings. Hall's honours board reads like a roll of honour in itself: three Duck of the Year titles (1970, 1977, 1985), Full House nearly-man, Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top 10, and five separate pairings across Quacking Together and Only Two Quacks. A genuine club institution of not scoring. Completing the set at 11, Ian Brattle was bowled by Peter Carman for his 2nd of the season and 40th career duck — 12th all time from just 119 innings. A paid-up member of the "50 Before a 50" club (51 ducks banked against a highest score of just 33), plus Ducks in a Row (7 consecutive) and Only Two Quacks with Michael Beeney. His sibling Paul Brattle carries 26 career ducks of his own — clearly a family trait. Thirkell's 1964 shadow falls over Ian too, 33 years to the day. 🏏 2nd XI — Higham & Mockbeggar (away) Adam Pooley was run out for his 2nd duck of the season and 8th of his career from 29 innings — 90th all time. A double Only Two Quacks member (with Fred Cheesman and with Paul Bowles) and a Retirement Duck club veteran from a past farewell. Thirkell, naturally, was there in 1964 too. 🌙 Midweek XI — Selsdon (home) Phil Randall departed to Ken Shivmangal for his 2nd duck of the season and, notably, only his 2nd career duck too — still finding his feet in the zero club from just 17 innings, 284th all time. On this very day in 1964, John Thirkell fell against Bowaters CC — the great man simply everywhere this week. 📋 The Week in Nought Five ducks from five matches, three of them arriving in a single spell at Mersham Le Hatch. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this week — just honest, workmanlike failure spread across three sides. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks / 32 inns (18.75%) 2. Phil Randall — 2 ducks / 8 inns (25.0%) Next fixtures await, and the bowlers will no doubt keep the honours board busy. Bring your bat, gentlemen — or don't. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 2 to 5 August 1997 🦆

4 ducks
Week 15 · 2 Aug 1997 – 5 Aug 1997
Four fixtures took to the field this week and the Duck Club banked four fresh entries in the book — a tidy, if modest, harvest. 🏏 1st XI — home to Maidstone YMCA CC Peter Whyte departed at four, stumped off the bowling of P- Ecclestone — a duck by the less common route. His first of the season and 17th of a career now 204 innings deep, good for 51st on the all-time list. On this very day in 1952, Gordon Fenner (1941–1973, 34 ducks, 19th all-time) was also trudging off for nought — Peter keeps fine historical company. And it clearly runs in the family: sibling Tom Whyte already carries 2 career ducks of his own. Midweek XI — home to Bexley CC A three-for-the-price-of-one afternoon as Andel Carty and company ran through the middle order. Piers McDonald fell first, caught behind off Carty without troubling the scorers. His 6th duck of the summer takes him to 35 career, 17th all-time, and keeps him well clear at the top of the Duck of the Year 1997 race — a crown he's making very much his own. Fittingly, on this exact date in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden was doing precisely the same thing en route to his own Duck of the Year 1895. Richard Piper followed, caught off the same bowler, for his 12th career duck in just 45 innings — 68th on the all-time list, and a proud member of the Full House club (a duck at every position 1 to 11) plus the Homebody, Lone Duck and Ducks on the Road top tens. Quite the collection for one man. It plainly runs in the family too — parent Brian Piper sits on 20 career ducks of his own. Matt Large rounded off the trio, caught off Jack Leek, for his 2nd of the season and 55th of a genuinely glittering career — 5th on the all-time roll, a six-time Duck of the Year (1987, 88, 89, 92, 93, 95) and agonisingly one position shy of a Full House. Rare company indeed. And yes — 2 August 1895 struck a third time, Ernest Cruttenden completing an entirely unwitting hat-trick of tributes across the afternoon. A quiet week in the counting house overall, just the two fixtures troubled the scorers, but Bexley's attack made the very most of its chance — Andel Carty alone accounting for two of Wednesday's three. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Piers McDonald — 28 inns, 6 ducks, 21.4% - David Castle — 27 inns, 3 ducks, 11.1% - Adrian Caddick — 7 inns, 2 ducks, 28.6% - Roly Edwards — 7 inns, 2 ducks, 28.6% - Richard Ellis — 8 inns, 2 ducks, 25.0% Piers leads the chase, but Adrian and Roly are lurking on a fearsome percentage. Next fixture is never far away, and the exclusive zero club always has room for one more. See you out there. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 26 to 30 July 1997 🦆

6 ducks
Week 14 · 26 Jul 1997 – 30 Jul 1997
Four matches, one increasingly familiar sound: leather on stump, bat under arm, name in the book. The zero club welcomes its latest inductees. Saturday 26 July — 1st XI v Hollingbourne CC Nigel Thirkell departed at first wicket down, bowled Dan Pritchard, and in doing so brought up a genuinely historic 50th career duck — Full Membership territory, rank 7 of 1224 all time. A Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top-tenner in the same breath, which takes some doing. Ducking clearly runs in the blood too — brother Tim sits on 66, sibling Simon on 33, and the next generation (Daniel, Stuart) are already contributing. On this very date in 1940, J Short made an identical trudge back against Boughton Monchelsea CC. Piers McDonald joined him in the pavilion soon after, also undone by Pritchard — his 4th duck of the season, 33rd of a career now well underway toward Duck of the Year 1997 honours. Sunday 27 July — 1st XI v Knockers CC Clive Newman went for nought at 2, caught off C Spencer Allen — his 1st of the season and 8th career duck (92nd all time). Basil Sears marked the same calendar date back in 1952 with a duck of his own versus Folkestone CC. Piers McDonald was back at it barely a day later, bowled Spencer Allen for his 5th duck of the season, 34th career — a grim pair completed inside 24 hours and an early statement of intent in the Duck of the Year race. Fred Cheesman also fell to Spencer Allen without scoring — 1st of the season, 30th career, keeping company with his own Duck of the Year gongs from 1990 and 1991, a Full House, and an Only Two Quacks pairing with Adam Pooley. Basil Sears, again, was his man for this day in history. Wednesday 30 July — Midweek XI v Ipswich Greyhounds CC (Kent Tour XI) Fred Cheesman completed a swift pair, bowled Malcolm Newson at number 10 — his 2nd duck of the week, 31st of his career. Fittingly, on this exact date in 1989, none other than Nigel Thirkell — this week's other headline nought — was doing precisely the same thing against The Mote CC. Small club, long memory.
