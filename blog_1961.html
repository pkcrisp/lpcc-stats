🦆 Duck News — Sunday 30 September 1961

0 ducks
Week 23 · 30 Sep 1961
A single fixture on the books this week, and — remarkably — not a duck to be found anywhere in it. Every batsman who walked out walked back having troubled the scorers, however modestly. The Duck Club's most dependable source of income has, for one afternoon only, run bone dry. It happens. Even the most committed purveyors of the nought need a rest now and then, and as the 1961 season draws towards its close it seems the closing weeks have decided to go out on a note of uncharacteristic competence. Somewhere, quietly, the regulars will be relieved. Somewhere else, they'll be wondering what on earth they did right. 📅 Week in Summary - 1 match played - 0 ducks recorded - A rare weekend of almost total batting competence — enjoy it while it lasts Let the record show: the ducks evaded capture this week, but the Hunting Season presses on regardless, and there's still time before 1961 closes its books for someone to make a late charge on the summit — or, more fittingly, an early one back to the pavilion. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. John Crellin — 6 ducks in 21 innings (28.57%) 2. Robert Murray — 6 ducks in 33 innings (18.18%) 3. Brian Relf — 5 ducks in 9 innings (55.56%) 🍩 — a rate bordering on the vocational 4. Robert Wood — 5 ducks in 41 innings (12.2%) 5. John Ames — 4 ducks in 22 innings (18.18%) Brian Relf remains the season's most efficient collector by some margin — better than one duck in every two visits to the crease is dedication the rest of the club can only admire from a respectful distance. 🦆 Until the next fixture, when normal service — noughts, quacks and all 0️⃣ — will no doubt resume in full. Keep the bat down and the faith up.

🦆 Duck News — Week of 23 September 1961

3 ducks
Week 22 · 23 Sep 1961 – 24 Sep 1961
Two fixtures, three ducks, and the Zero Club welcomed its usual suspects back into the fold this week. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — v Bearsted CC Robert Wood, promoted to first wicket down, departed without troubling the scorers, caught by Colin Fleetwood off Nick Wheeler-Robinson. It's Wood's 5th duck of the season and his 17th for the club, moving him to 10th in the all-time standings — level with John Abnett, who reached the very same 17 in a considerably more economical 44 innings between 1954 and 1961. Wood remains, of course, a long-serving member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his LPCC account the traditional way some seasons back. 🍩 Sunday 24th — v Linton Park CC (Singles) A quietly ruthless spell for the home attack, with Gordon Fenner accounting for both of the day's ducks in the same innings. Opener John Ames went early, caught by Stan Wratten off Fenner — faintly amusing given Ames already sits on the "Quacking Together" honours board alongside the very man who has just pouched him. It's his 4th duck of the season and 25th of a storied career, keeping him at a lofty 4th on the all-time list. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1960, Frank Brattle was performing the identical trick against A.Trembeths — some anniversaries mark themselves. At number 10, Brian Relf joined him in the shed, bowled by Fenner for his 5th duck of the season and 13th of his career, good enough for 16th on the all-time roll. Relf carries the honours of both the Quack-to-Quack and Retirement Duck boards from milestones past, and — like Ames — shares his date with Frank Brattle's own nought of 24th September 1960. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, and Gordon Fenner can rightly take a bow, having personally dispatched both of the Singles' batsmen for nought. No pairs, no family affairs to report — just honest, old-fashioned failure to trouble the scorers. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - John Crellin — 6 ducks / 21 innings (28.57%) - Robert Murray — 6 ducks / 32 innings (18.75%) - Brian Relf — 5 ducks / 9 innings (55.56%) - Robert Wood — 5 ducks / 41 innings (12.2%) - John Ames — 4 ducks / 22 innings (18.18%) Relf's rate for the season now sits above 50% — a truly committed pursuit of nought. The next fixture is never far away, and neither is the next duck. Bring your bats, gentlemen — and your sense of humour. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 16 & 17 September 1961

5 ducks
Week 21 · 16 Sep 1961 – 17 Sep 1961
Saturday 16 September — 1st XI v Bowaters CC (h) The rarest ceremony on the Duck Club calendar turned up at Bowaters: two Members back in the hutch inside the same tail-end collapse. Terry Whittaker (9) was stumped, Bob Bourne to Bill Brown, for his 2nd duck of the season and 9th of a career now 59 innings deep — 30th of 756 all time. Ray Howard, who played 1954–1963, made his own nought against Tunbridge Wells CC on this very calendar day back in 1956. Happy anniversary, Terry. In walked Brian Relf at 10, bowled John Evans, for his 4th of the season and 12th career duck from a mere 27 innings — 17th on the all-time list and a strike-rate for ignominy few can rival. The honours board already lists Terry and Brian "Quacking Together" — and this week it was not paperwork, it was live theatre, both dismissed in the same tightening finish. Ray Howard's 1956 duck keeps them company on the date too. Brian, incidentally, is a paid-up member of the rare Quack-to-Quack club from an earlier chapter of his career — duck on debut, duck on farewell — though no danger of a repeat sign-off applies here. Sunday 17 September — 1st XI v Sevenoaks Vine CC (h) Three more fell to keep the harvest generous. John Crellin (5), run out in some confusion, notched his 6th duck of the season — and his 6th career duck from a brutal 20 innings, a 30% duck rate that plants him top of the current Duck of the Year 1961 standings. Basil Sears, who played 1947–1961, made his own nought against Bearsted CC on this same date in 1960. William Buck (6) was bowled by Alan Hurd for his 2nd of the season but 18th of a marathon 243-innings career — 8th of 756 all time, a genuine elder statesman of the art of not scoring. Alan Tucker (10) departed caught Peter Williams bowled Cliff Judd for his maiden duck, opening both season and career accounts from just 11 innings — welcome to the club, 178th of 756 and counting. Both share Basil Sears's 1960 vintage for company on the date. Week in Summary Five ducks, two matches, and the tail did the heavy lifting — three of the five fell at number 9 or below. A fine demonstration that failing to score is very much a team pursuit at Linton Park. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. John Crellin — 6 ducks / 20 inns (30%) 2. Robert Murray — 6 ducks / 30 inns (20%) 3. Brian Relf — 4 ducks / 8 inns (50%) 4. Robert Wood — 4 ducks / 39 inns (10.26%) 5. Gordon Fenner — 3 ducks / 13 inns (23.08%) Next fixture awaits its own crop of noughts. Bats up, ducks watching. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 9th & 10th September 1961

3 ducks
Week 20 · 9 Sep 1961 – 10 Sep 1961
Two fixtures, three ducks, and the 1st XI doing all the honours this week at home to St Lukes Maidstone CC. Saturday 9th September — 1st XI v St Lukes Maidstone CC The home side found the zero club in fine voice. John Crellin, in at four, fell lbw to J. Francis for his 5th duck of the season — which, handily, is also his 5th of 1961, keeping him well out in front for the Duck of the Year 1961 honours he already carries on the board. That's 5 from 19 career innings, 54th on the all-time list, and — on this very day back in 1951 — Tony Levick was doing exactly the same against Gillingham CC, Kent, on his way to 7 career ducks across a decade in the middle (1950–1960). Michael Chambers followed not long after, bowled by B. Pearce for his 2nd duck of the season and of his career alike, from just 8 innings — 138th in the club's long roll of dishonour. Like Crellin, Chambers shares this exact 9th of September with Tony Levick's 1951 nought at Gillingham. Last man down was Ricky Lusher, caught B. Lamb off J. Francis for his 1st duck of the season but 2nd of an increasingly eventful career, now on 2 from 20 innings and 149th on the all-time chart. Ricky too finds his September date shared with the ghost of Tony Levick's 1951 vintage — three Linton men, one long-departed kindred spirit, same calendar day a decade apart. Sunday 10th September A quieter day by comparison — no further additions to the honours board. Even the ducks need a rest. 📊 Week in summary Three ducks from two matches, all concentrated into one unfortunate Saturday afternoon for the 1st XI. J. Francis alone accounted for two of the three dismissals — a fine day's work for St Lukes Maidstone CC, a less fine one for Linton Park. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Robert Murray — 6 ducks, 29 innings, 20.69% - John Crellin — 5 ducks, 19 innings, 26.32% - Robert Wood — 4 ducks, 37 innings, 10.81% - Brian Relf — 3 ducks, 7 innings, 42.86% 🍩 - Gordon Fenner — 3 ducks, 13 innings, 23.08% Crellin's afternoon closes the gap on Robert Murray at the summit — with the season still to run, the race for 1961's Duck of the Year is far from decided. Next fixture, next opportunity. The stumps await, and so, inevitably, do the ducks. 🦆🏏

Week 18 · 26 Aug 1961 – 27 Aug 1961
A tidy little weekend for the exclusive zero club — just two visits recorded, but both from 1st XI stalwarts keeping the honours board ticking over nicely. 1st XI Saturday, at home to Aylesford Paper Mills CC — Richard Harris, in at five, fell to the bowling of J Simmonite for his 1st duck of the season. It's the 35th of a long and distinguished career spanning 172 innings, which keeps him a commanding 3rd on the all-time list of 756. Fittingly, on this very day in 1945 Herbert Rayner (1939–1948) also strolled back for nought, against 1st Innings Fifth Leicester — one of six such visits in a career good enough for 45th all-time. Consistency of a very particular kind, across the decades. Sunday, hosting A L Newberry XI — Stan Wratten, opening at two, was bowled by M Heroys without troubling the scorers. It's his 1st of the season and the 17th of a career now 200 innings deep, enough for 9th on the all-time roll — rarefied company indeed. A paid-up member of the Quacking Together club alongside John Ames, Stan can add a neat footnote to this week's entry: on this same date back in 1949, none other than fellow ducker Richard Harris — freshly returned to the fold above — made his own duck, against Hunton Wanderers CC. Two names, one calendar date, twelve years apart. Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one apiece — Saturday and Sunday both doing their bit for the cause. Nothing spectacular, but the art of not scoring rarely needs an audience to be appreciated. 🦆 Duck Report – 19th & 20th August 1961

3 ducks
Week 17 · 19 Aug 1961 – 20 Aug 1961
Two away fixtures, three visits to the exclusive zero club, and the 1st XI doing all the travelling and all the not-scoring. A quiet weekend for the honours board, but never let it be said the art of not scoring took a rest. 🏏 1st XI – On Tour, and Found Wanting Saturday saw the lads at Bowaters CC, and openers do love to set the tone. Robert Wood strode out first wicket down and departed lbw to Roy Ecclestone without so much as a bother to the scorers - his 4th duck of the season and 16th of a career now spanning 107 innings, a tally that plants him a lofty 11th in the all-time standings. A long-paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own first-ever innings, Robert's consistency of a very particular kind continues. Curiously, on this very date back in 1956, one John --'s Taylor was doing exactly the same thing against Mote A. Not to be left out, Michael Chambers came in at five and was bowled by Edward Chick for nought - his maiden career duck, arriving in just his 6th innings and installing him at 275th of 755 on the all-time roll. Welcome to the club, Michael - triumph through adversity starts somewhere. Fittingly, that very same 19th of August in 1956 also claimed John Taylor for a duck. Sunday's trip to The Mote CC brought a third scalp. Robert Murray, batting three, was snapped up in the field off J Harrison for his 5th duck of the season and 10th of his career from 98 innings - 26th on the all-time list and now the club's early pacesetter for Duck of the Year. Seventeen years to the day earlier, in 1944, W Bunting was performing the identical vanishing act against KCC Maidstone, also his 5th. 📅 Week in Summary Three ducks, two matches, both away, both entirely one-sided in the wrong direction for the batsmen concerned. The 1st XI seem to travel well for everything except runs. No pairs, no family affairs this week - just three solo performances in the fine tradition of returning with nothing to show for the journey. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Robert Murray - 5 ducks, 23 innings, 21.74% - John Crellin - 4 ducks, 18 innings, 22.22% - Robert Wood - 4 ducks, 31 innings, 12.90% - Brian Relf - 3 ducks, 6 innings, 50.00% - Gordon Fenner - 3 ducks, 12 innings, 25.00% Robert Murray leads the chase with a game in hand on the field, but Brian Relf's 50% strike rate is a masterclass in efficiency should anyone doubt the numbers. 🍩 Next fixture beckons, and the club stands ready to welcome its next inductees. 🦆 DUCK NEWS — 5th to 7th August 1961

7 ducks
Week 15 · 5 Aug 1961 – 7 Aug 1961
Two fixtures, seven ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomed its usual faithful plus a proper avalanche at Bearsted. Onwards. Saturday 5th August — Headcorn CC (home) Gordon Fenner departed for nought at six, caught L- Murrell off v- Ward — his 2nd duck of the season and 17th for the club, good for 7th of 754 all-time. Little did he know a carbon copy awaited him 48 hours later. He's not alone in the family either — brothers Joe (5 career ducks) and Maurice Fenner (1) are also paid-up club members. On this very day in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden was doing much the same against Rochester CC, en route to 10 career ducks and the 1895 Duck of the Year title. At nine, Brian Relf was bowled by C- Smith without scoring — his 3rd of the season, 11th career, 18th of 754 all-time. A long-standing member of the Debutante Duck society from his very first innings, Brian shares Saturday's misfortune with the same Ernest Cruttenden of 1895. Monday 7th August — Bearsted CC (away) A two-innings friendly at Bearsted turned into a feeding frenzy — five noughts in one fixture. Opening up, John Ames was bowled by Mike Morton for his 2nd duck of the season (23rd career, 4th of 754 all-time) — then walked out again in the second innings and was caught George Lambell off Peter Longley for his 3rd of the season and 24th career duck. A genuine pair, both halves in the same match. Fittingly, his old honours-board accomplices Roy Clarke and Alan Wilkes — both immortalised alongside him for shared quacking down the years — were there to make it a proper reunion. Gordon Fenner obliged again at eleven, caught George Lambell off Mike Morton, his 3rd duck of the season and 18th career — and with Saturday still fresh in the memory, a pair spread neatly across the weekend. Roy Clarke (b Bryan Elswood) fell without scoring for his 2nd of the season, 22nd career, 5th of 754 all-time — solid work for a man who once carried the 1959 Duck of the Year title. His brother H Clarke sits on 3 career ducks of his own. Alan Wilkes (b Mike Morton) rounded things off with his 2nd of the season and 16th career, 10th of 754 all-time. All four of Monday's fallers share the same on-this-day parallel — back in 1909, C Stevens was doing exactly the same against the Royal West Kent Regiment CC, en route to the 1909 Duck of the Year gong. 📋 Week in Summary Seven ducks from two matches, with Monday's Bearsted friendly doing the heavy lifting — five for nought in one afternoon is precisely the team effort this club exists to celebrate. Two full pairs recorded too: Fenner's spread across the weekend, Ames' compressed into one glorious afternoon. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Robert Murray — 20 innings, 4 ducks (20.0%) - Brian Relf — 5 innings, 3 ducks (60.0%) - Gordon Fenner — 11 innings, 3 ducks (27.27%) - John Ames — 16 innings, 3 ducks (18.75%) - John Crellin — 17 innings, 3 ducks (17.65%) Murray leads for now, but Relf, Fenner, Ames and Crellin are queuing up right behind him with plenty of season left to close the gap. 0️⃣ Next fixture, next chance for immortality — see you all next week for the inevitable additions to the book. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 22 to 25 July 1961

5 ducks
Week 13 · 22 Jul 1961 – 25 Jul 1961
Good evening, quackers. Three fixtures on the go this week and the exclusive zero club welcomed five new entries — Rumwood CC did the early damage, John Larkin's XI mopped up the rest. Saturday 22 July — v Rumwood CC Things went wrong for the 1st XI at number 8, where Brian Relf was run out for nought — his 2nd duck of the season and 10th of an increasingly decorated career, now 23rd of 754 all time from just 23 innings. A paid-up Debutante Duck, and one of the rare few on the club's Quack-to-Quack board (duck on debut and farewell) — triumph through adversity, bookended. Two wickets later William Agate fell to John Carter without scoring, his 2nd of the season and 3rd career nought (104th of 754) — a startling rate from only 3 innings in the book. And to complete the top-order collapse, Derek Pantony was run out at number 11 for nought, his 2nd of the season and 8th career duck (31st of 754), a name that once topped the Duck of the Year honours back in 1947 and shows no sign of forgetting the old ways. All three, as it happens, share a curious echo — on this very day in 1956, John Abnett also strolled back for a duck, one of 17 that eventually carried him to 7th of 754 all time and a place on the Ducks in a Row board. History does love to repeat itself at Linton. Sunday 23 July — v John Larkin's XI Season pace-setter Robert Murray was bowled by A Allom for nought at number 4, his 4th duck of the season — nobody else in the club is close — and 9th career (29th of 754) from 90 innings. Fittingly, on this same day back in 1960, none other than this week's own Brian Relf was also collecting a duck, against Tovil, the art of not scoring clearly running in the squad's blood. Three down the order, William Buck was run out for nought, only his 1st duck of the season but a hefty 17th for his career — 8th of 754 all time, a genuine elder statesman of the format, and a long-standing member of the Quacking Together board alongside John Thirkell. He too keeps company with the 1960 vintage — that same Tovil duck from Brian Relf, on this very date. The week in brief Five ducks from three matches, all falling to the 1st XI, and Rumwood CC can be proud of taking three scalps for nought in a single innings.
