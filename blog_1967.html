🦆 DUCK NEWS — 30 September 1967

0 ducks
Week 23 · 30 Sep 1967
A single fixture took place in this period and, remarkably, the ducks went entirely uncaptured. Eleven names in the book, not one troubled the exclusive zero club. Whether this was superb defensive technique or simply generous bowling, the record does not say — but the Duck Club's hunters return home empty-handed. A quiet weekend for connoisseurs of the art of not scoring. Even Jim Hall, sitting on a fearsome 37.5% strike rate this season, kept his powder dry. Let it be known: the ducks evaded capture, not that they don't exist. 🏏 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard — 1967 1. John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 34 innings (11.76%) 2. Jim Hall — 3 ducks in 8 innings (37.5%) 🍩 3. Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks in 20 innings (15.0%) 4. Frank Brattle — 3 ducks in 27 innings (11.11%) 5. Paul Bowles — 3 ducks in 30 innings (10.0%) Jim Hall remains the season's most efficient collector by some distance — better than one duck in every three trips to the crease is a rate the rest of the club can only admire from a respectful distance. John Thirkell holds the outright lead on raw numbers, four for the campaign and counting, while Bowles, Brattle and Greenfield lurk in close company on three apiece, each no doubt eyeing the others nervously. 📅 Looking ahead A blank week is no cause for complacency — the zero club never sleeps for long, and normal service will surely resume at the next opportunity. Get your whites on, gentlemen, and may fortune desert at least one of you. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 23 September 1967

2 ducks
Week 22 · 23 Sep 1967
Late-season action from Bearsted way, and a modest but perfectly formed harvest of two from the one fixture on the books. The exclusive zero club never sleeps, even as the year draws in. Saturday — 1st XI v West Farleigh CC (home) - Basil Sears opened proceedings and departed just as promptly, lbw b D Kemp for a duck. His 2nd of the season and a hefty 22nd for the career from 336 innings — a genuinely industrious relationship with the nought, good enough for 10th on the all-time list of 831. Curiously, on this very day back in 1962 Terry Whittaker (1958–1967) also fell for a duck against Linton Park CC — Terry sits one behind on 22 himself, a paid-up member of both Quacking Together (with Brian Relf) and the Retirement Duck club. History, it seems, likes a September fixture. - Malcolm Pay, sent in at number 8, was bowled by Ron Kemp without troubling the scorers. His maiden duck of the season and only his 2nd in a career of 12 innings so far — rank 167 of 831, plenty of room to climb for the ambitious young quacker. Fittingly, that same 1962 apparition, Terry Whittaker, echoes down the years once more — proof that West Farleigh has form for extracting noughts on the 23rd of September. Week in Summary One match, two ducks, and both fell to the Kemp family bowling — D Kemp and Ron Kemp sharing the spoils. Basil's continued fluency in the art of not scoring keeps him firmly inside the all-time top ten, while Malcolm's early education in the discipline is coming along nicely. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - John Thirkell — 33 inns, 4 ducks (12.12%) - Jim Hall — 8 inns, 3 ducks (37.5%) 🍩 - Fred Greenfield — 20 inns, 3 ducks (15.0%) - Frank Brattle — 27 inns, 3 ducks (11.11%) - Paul Bowles — 29 inns, 3 ducks (10.34%) Jim Hall's eye-watering 37.5% keeps him the most efficient collector in the field, though John Thirkell's raw tally of four leads the chase as the season winds down. The next fixture awaits, and with it, no doubt, further additions to this most dubious of honours boards. Bring your bat, and bring your sense of humour. 🏏0️⃣

0 ducks
Week 21 · 17 Sep 1967
A single fixture out on the park today, and — remarkably — not a single member of the exclusive zero club was inducted. No golden ducks, no pairs, no family affairs to report — just runs, glorious runs, cluttering up the scorebook where a nought or two would have been so much tidier. 🦆 Duck News — 9–10 September 1967

2 ducks
Week 20 · 9 Sep 1967 – 10 Sep 1967
Two fixtures on the board this weekend, but only one troubled the scorers where it mattered — the ducks, mercifully or not, all came from a single innings at home to A. Trembeths. 1st XI v A. Trembeths (home) In at four, Keith Pride departed for nought, bowled by B. Stalley — his first duck of the 1967 season and, with only three career innings to his name, his very first for the club too. That puts him at 1 career duck, 301st of 831 in the all-time standings, and quietly signs him up to the club's Retirement Duck honours board — a standing distinction for those whose careers eventually close on a nought, not a comment on today's business. On this very day back in 1961, John Crellin fell the same way against St Lukes Maidstone CC — Crellin went on to be crowned Duck of the Year that season with 9 to his name, so Keith walks back in fine historical company. Six down the order at ten, Jon Harris followed him for nought, caught behind off the same relentless Stalley. His second duck of the season lifts him to 4 career ducks from just 6 innings — 87th of 831 all-time — and adds another line to an already crowded honours cabinet. Jon carries Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), places in both the Lone Duck and Tourist/Homebody Duck top tens, and shared pairing honours with Allan Cooper, Jim Hall and Trevor Back. He's also a card-carrying member of the Debutante Duck club from his actual first-ever innings, long before this one. A chip off the old block, too — his kinsman Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own. And, fittingly, Jon shares today's on-this-day parallel with Keith: Crellin's 1961 duck against St Lukes, on the very same date. Week in summary Two ducks from two matches, both from the same fixture and the same bowler — B. Stalley can consider his afternoon's work well spent. The other game on the weekend's card passed off entirely fowl-free. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 John Thirkell — 4 ducks, 31 innings (12.9%) 🥈 Jim Hall — 3 ducks, 8 innings (37.5%) 🥉 Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks, 18 innings (16.67%) 4. Frank Brattle — 3 ducks, 27 innings (11.11%) 5. Paul Bowles — 3 ducks, 27 innings (11.11%) Jim Hall's blistering 37.5% strike rate keeps him firmly in touch at the top, and with Jon Harris's own "Quacking Together" honours tied to him, the club's zero-scoring bloodlines run deep this season. The exclusive zero club never sleeps 🍩 — next fixture, more opportunity, more ignominy. See you at the crease. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 2 to 6 September 1967

8 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1967 – 6 Sep 1967
Eight ducks in three matches this week, the exclusive zero club welcoming a fresh intake with real ceremony — two debut victims, one long-serving colossus, and a Birchington collapse that reads like a shopping list. Saturday 2 September — 1st XI v Beverley CC, Kent (home) A rich harvest at home, three careers dented in a single innings. - R Venner (4), c B Short b M Bryant — first-ever senior innings, first-ever duck. Instant membership of the Debutante Duck and (so far) One and Done 1967 rolls. On this very date in 1945, D Clark made exactly the same duck against 1st Innings MCPC — company across the decades. - Jon Harris (8), c B Short b K Collard — his 3rd career duck from just 5 innings, a rate that already has him deep in the club's exotic leaderboards: Lone Duck, Tourist Duck and Homebody Duck top tens, plus Full House for ducking at every position 1–11. Quacking Together honours with Jim Hall and Trevor Back besides. Relative Richard Harris already carries 36 career ducks — clearly a gift runs in the family. Joins Venner in the 1945 echo of D Clark. - P- Williams (10), c B Short b K Collard — another debut, another instant duck, another name for the Debutante and One and Done boards. D Clark's 1945 effort keeps him company too. Sunday 3 September — 1st XI v Frindsbury CC (home) - John Ames, c G Fox b B Hill — opened and departed without scoring, his 37th career duck from 284 innings and 4th on the all-time list. Chasing his own Ducks in a Row landmark, with Quacking Together links to Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. On this day in 1944, Jimmy Jameson found similarly little joy against the Home Guard's Royal West Kent 11th Battalion. Wednesday 6 September — Midweek XI v Birchington CC (away) Four down for four ducks, as if the middle order travelled as a package. - Roger Craddock (2), c R Abraham b P Smith — his first duck of the season, and his only one in 18 career innings. John Ames, coincidentally, ducked against Tovil on this exact date back in 1958. - John Thirkell (4), c R Abraham b P Smith — his 4th duck of the season, 61st of his career, and still comfortably #1 on the all-time list. Already installed on the Duck of the Year board for 1952, 1954, 1955 — and now 1967, a season he leads by daylight. - William Buck (5), c R Abraham b P Smith — P Smith's third victim of the innings. His 28th career duck, 9th all-time, and a Quacking Together partnership with his fallen colleague Thirkell, now doubled up on the board and in the scorebook. - Percy Brattle (7), b R Yates — his 6th career duck from 44 innings. Relative Frank Brattle already sits on 31 career ducks for the club — the bloodline for scorelessness runs deep. The week in brief Eight ducks, three matches, two collapses of three-plus in a single innings. Beverley and Birchington will not be missed fondly. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 John Thirkell — 29 inn, 4 ducks (13.79%) 🥈 Jim Hall — 8 inn, 3 ducks (37.5%) 🥉 Fred Greenfield — 18 inn, 3 ducks (16.67%) Paul Bowles — 25 inn, 3 ducks (12.0%) Frank Brattle — 26 inn, 3 ducks (11.54%) Next fixture looms, and with it another chance for someone to join this week's roll of honour. Pads on, bats ready, nought expected. 🦆🏏0️⃣

1 duck
Week 18 · 26 Aug 1967 – 28 Aug 1967
Three fixtures on the go this weekend and, remarkably, the zero club kept its numbers modest. Just the one nought to report — proof that even the most dedicated poultry farmers need a Sunday off now and then. 🏏 1st XI v Aylesford Paper Mills CC — Saturday 26th Derry Evans, walking in at number 8, departed bowled David Keynes — no first-baller ignominy this time, but the outcome was the same: back to the pavilion with nothing to show for it. This is Derry's 2nd duck of the season and his 4th in an LPCC career now spanning 20 innings, a return that plants him at a solid 81st of 828 in the all-time roll of dishonour. No honours-board silverware to his name yet, but the club always has room for one more subscription. Rather splendidly, on this very day back in 1962, none other than John Ames also strolled back for nought — against Sevenoaks Vine CC, as it happens, rather than Aylesford, but a duck's a duck regardless of opposition. Ames played from 1952 to 1967 and finished his career with a magnificent 36 ducks, good enough for 4th of 828 all-time, plus a mantelpiece groaning with honours: Ducks in a Row for four on the bounce, a Homebody Duck top ten placing, and shared quacking honours with both Roy Clarke and Stan Wratten. Derry has some way to go to match that legacy, but every 81st-placed finisher started somewhere. 🌾 A Quiet Weekend Elsewhere The 2nd and 3rd fixtures passed off without incident — no additions to the ledger, which either speaks to some uncharacteristically sound batting or simply means the scorers were asleep at the wheel. Either way, the Duck Club will take the rest while it can, because it never lasts. 📊 Week in Summary Three matches, one duck, and a distinctly peaceful few days for the honours board. Derry Evans stands alone this week, carrying the flag for the entire club. Quality over quantity, as the saying almost goes. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1967 1. John Thirkell — 3 ducks in 27 innings (11.11%) Jim Hall sits pretty at the top on percentage alone, but with three names locked on three ducks apiece behind him, this season's crown is very much up for grabs. 🍩 Next weekend's fixtures await, and the zero club never stays quiet for long. 🦆 Duck Report — 20 August 1967

0 ducks
Week 17 · 20 Aug 1967
Well, ducklings, brace yourselves for a rare and faintly troubling headline: the hunters returned from the one fixture played this period with their nets entirely empty. Not a single nought to report. Every batsman across the one match on the books this week contrived, infuriatingly, to actually score. 🏏 The Great Escape We can only assume the ducks got wind of the Club's reputation and scattered for cover. Somewhere out there a bowler is nursing a golden opportunity spurned, and somewhere a scorer's "0" column sits gathering dust, unblemished and unloved. The exclusive zero club held no induction ceremony this week — a place of dubious distinction briefly, and only briefly, closed for business. Do not mistake this for good news. The art of not scoring is a proud Linton tradition and this is merely a pause, not a retirement. The ducks will return — they always do. 📊 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard — 1967 Even in a quiet week the season standings make for sobering reading: - Jim Hall — 3 ducks from 6 innings, a startling 50% strike rate. At that pace he's less a batsman than a season-long tribute act to the number nought. - Fred Greenfield — 3 ducks from 18 innings (16.67%), keeping respectable company near the top. - Paul Bowles — 3 ducks from 22 innings (13.64%), quietly consistent in the worst possible way. - Frank Brattle — 3 ducks from 23 innings (13.04%), proof that persistence and poultry can coexist. - John Thirkell — 3 ducks from 26 innings (11.54%), rounding out a top five that between them have contributed precisely nothing, fifteen times over, to the Linton Park run column. Jim Hall sits handsomely clear on percentage and will want watching — at one duck every two innings he's setting a pace that would make the club's greats sit up and take note. 🔭 Looking Ahead A blank week is a dangerous thing — it only ever builds pressure for the next one. With the season's contenders circling and Jim Hall setting an ominous rate, it surely can't be long before someone obliges. Pads on, cameras ready, standards low. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆 0️⃣
