🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 2003 · 2003 — 1 match

Sat 27 Sep 2003 🧢 1st XIv Sherwood CC, Kent · AwayformWWDLD

Sunny at the toss, and I'll admit "we'll have a bowl" felt more like hope than tactics — but sometimes the village magic works. Ian Brattle was relentless, tearing through Sherwood for 4-18 while Dennis Salisbury bowled as though he had personal business with the scoreboard, four maidens and barely a run to his name. Between them, Adam Jackson chipped in with a couple, I managed one for not very much at all, and poor Michael Hooker never troubled the umpires, back in the pavilion without scoring. All out for 66 in 27.3 overs — only Tim Newlyn, with a stubborn 20, seemed to have read a different script to the rest of his side.

Chasing such a modest total ought to be straightforward, and for once it was: Richard Piper and Rob Jackson simply strolled to the target, unbeaten on 27 and 23 respectively, and had the job wrapped up inside sixteen overs without so much as a hint of alarm. No wickets lost, no fuss, no nervous tail-enders required — just two lads batting like the pitch owed them money.

A word too for Dennis Salisbury, who continues his quiet march up the season's charts and now sits second in the 1st XI wicket-takers for 2003 with 23 to his name — remarkable consistency from a man who seems constitutionally incapable of bowling a bad over.

All told, a thoroughly professional afternoon's work: bowled with discipline, batted with ease, and the long trip to Kent repaid with two points and a comfortable nine-wicket win. Sherwood, I fear, never really got going.

🗓️ 20 Sep – 26 Sep 2003 · 2003 — 4 matches

Wed 24 Sep 2003 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformDWDWD

Right, I'm the captain, and Wednesday brought us a run out to Penshurst Park CC — the sort of afternoon that makes you glad you bothered getting out of bed. We lost the toss, were sent out to field, and to be honest I feared the worst as Eames and Smith both cashed in with half-centuries of 68 apiece, with Hunter chipping in 45 to help them post a hefty 218/6 from their 38 overs. Credit where it's due, though — Darren Styles was magnificent with the ball, tearing through with 3-36, and I managed a tidy 2-21 myself, with Trevor Back weighing in with one more to keep the total from getting truly ugly.

Two hundred and nineteen looked a stiff ask on paper, but nobody told Richard Piper and Craig Sands. The pair simply took the game away from Penshurst, Piper compiling a superb unbeaten hundred of 107 and Sands matching him stroke for stroke with 100 not out of his own — the sort of murderous, chanceless batting that leaves the rest of us on the boundary shaking our heads in quiet admiration. Brian Piper's contribution began and ended with a duck, bless him, but with performances like that alongside it, nobody was counting against us.

We cruised home on 222/2 with overs and wickets to spare — an eight-wicket win, no less. Sands now sits proudly atop the season's run charts with 408, and I'm rather chuffed to lead the wicket-takers with 15 for the year. A thumping win, a fine team effort, and the tea urn has rarely tasted sweeter. Onwards.
