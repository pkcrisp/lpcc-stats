Sat 30 Sep 2000 🧢 1st XIv BC Bidborough CC · HomeformDWLWD

I'm the captain writing the match report — here it is.

Saturday last saw Bidborough CC roll into Linton Park, and true to form I lost the toss, so it was pads off and boots on for the field first. Bidborough compiled a workmanlike 133 all out from their 46 overs — R. Warner top-scoring with 27, Craig Denness and Ian Moore chipping in either side of 20 — but John Taylor was in irresistible touch, ripping through with 4-22, while I managed a tidy 3-10 and Michael Beeney bowled with the miserly discipline of a man counting every penny, 1-15 off six maidens.

With 134 to chase, we set about it steadily rather than spectacularly. I top-scored with 32 before handing the baton to Mike Elworthy (30 not out) and Daniel Thirkell (28 not out), who eased us home in fine style. Poor Stuart Cheesman will not thank me for mentioning his golden duck, but a match report without a little gallows humour isn't a proper one. Spare a thought too for Piers McDonald behind the stumps, who had his hands full to the tune of nine byes — the sort of afternoon that ages a wicketkeeper prematurely.

Four wickets the margin, two points the reward, and thoroughly deserved. A tidy, unspectacular win of the kind that quietly wins seasons — and with the run charts and wicket charts both looking rosy, long may it continue.

Sun 24 Sep 2000 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWDWLW

Skipper's Log, Sunday 24th September 2000: we welcomed Anchorians CC to Linton Park, and for once I actually got a coin toss right — up it went, down it came in our favour, and I had no hesitation in sending us in to bat. There's a certain pleasure in making that call and then not immediately regretting it, which, for those who've watched me captain before, you'll know is rarer than it ought to be.

What followed was about as untroubled an innings as this diary has had the pleasure of recording. We cruised to 65 without loss in just 15.1 overs, Russell Baldwin batting with the sort of unflappable authority that's become his trademark this summer, finishing unbeaten on 37. I chipped in with 25 not out myself, happy enough to play the supporting role while Russell did the heavy lifting — not that I'll ever turn down a knock like that, mind. It's no shock, either, given the lad currently sits second in the whole 1st XI run charts for the season with 575 to his name; bowlers up and down the league must be heartily sick of the sight of him.

Sadly the cricketing gods, having smiled on the toss and the batting, then declined to produce a result — honours were shared, neither side able to force the win. A draw, then, but a thoroughly pleasant one: unbeaten opening stands, healthy scoreboards, and an afternoon in the Kent sunshine. I'll take that.
