🦆 Duck Report — 30 September to 1 October 1989

3 ducks
Week 23 · 30 Sep 1989 – 1 Oct 1989
Two fixtures, three ducks, and Linton Park CC on the receiving end of both wickets taken against opposition wearing our own colours — the annual Married v Unknown affair rolled round again, and the exclusive zero club welcomed its usual trickle of new business. Unknown XI v Linton Park CC Tim Thirkell continued his long and distinguished residency at the top table, caught Mike Bains bowled Steve Cheesman for his 2nd duck of the season and 61st of a career that now sits 4th on the all-time list of 1,119 unfortunates. A genuine club institution — Duck of the Year 1972, a place in the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and the small matter of a Full House, having now been dismissed for nought from every position 1 through 11, a feat of comprehensive uselessness few will ever match. The Thirkell name runs deep in these records too: brother Nigel (42 career ducks) is currently propping up this very season's top five, siblings Simon (23) and Stuart (1) also paid-up members, and father John leads the clan on 90. On this very day in 1988, Paul Sendles was going through an identical ordeal down at Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC — misery, it seems, keeps a calendar. Nick Froom fared little better, run out without troubling the scorers for his 1st senior duck from just 5 innings — no fault of his own bat, more a mix-up in the middle, but the ledger doesn't do nuance. Curiously, he too finds a kindred spirit in Paul Sendles' Hastings misadventure of this same date twelve months prior. Married v Linton Park CC Allan Cooper was bowled by Piers McDonald for his 1st duck of the season, his 38th in senior colours, keeping him handsomely placed at 11th all-time. Allan's honours board reads like a lonely hearts column of Only Two Quacks entries — paired historically with Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back, though none of that trio troubled the scorers this week to make it a reunion. And, fittingly, Allan joins the growing 1 October chorus line behind Paul Sendles' 1988 special at Hastings. Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, and Linton Park CC did the damage in both — some will call it a coincidence, others a conspiracy. No pairs, no king pairs, no family affairs this time round, just three honest men walking back for nought in reasonably good company. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 4 ducks in 19 innings (21.05%) - Dave Charlton — 3 ducks in 11 innings (27.27%) - Peter Whyte — 3 ducks in 30 innings (10.0%) - Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks in 36 innings (8.33%) - Ron Kemp — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.0%) 🍩 The season race remains wide open, Ron Kemp quietly posting the most alarming strike rate of the lot. Next fixture awaits — pads on, bats ready, and the zero club stands by to welcome its next member. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 24–27 September 1989

1 duck
Week 22 · 24 Sep 1989 – 27 Sep 1989
As the season winds toward its close, the ducks proved a shy quarry this week — just the one bagged from two fixtures. Quality over quantity, as they say in the exclusive zero club. Saturday – 1st XI v Anchorians CC (Home) Piers McDonald, in at five, departed lbw to Barry Diplock without troubling the scorers — the second duck of his season and the fourth of his LPCC career from 46 innings, installing him at 141 of 1119 in the all-time standings. A fully paid-up member of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top 10s, Piers continues his season-long audition for the honours board with the quiet consistency of a man who has made not scoring something of a speciality. Neatly enough, on this very day back in 1977, none other than Jim Hall was doing exactly the same thing — falling for nought, en route to a career haul of 68 ducks that still sits him at a mighty 2nd of 1119 all time. A three-time Duck of the Year (1970, 1977 and 1985) and a man who found the art of not scoring on debut and never quite let go, Hall remains the standard by which all Linton failure is measured. Piers has some way to go to catch him, but every journey to immortality begins with a single lbw. The 1s' Sunday outing, and the club's other fixture across the period, passed off entirely fowl-free — eleven batters apiece, not a quack to be heard. Week in Summary A tidy little haul of one duck from two matches — the ducks, it seems, took the last weekend of September off. Still, one is all it takes to keep the honours boards ticking over, and Piers McDonald has done his bit for the cause. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 4 ducks / 18 innings (22.22%) - Dave Charlton — 3 ducks / 11 innings (27.27%) - Peter Whyte — 3 ducks / 28 innings (10.71%) - Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks / 33 innings (9.09%) - Ron Kemp — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.00%) Ron Kemp's 50% strike rate remains one to admire from a safe distance, while Matt Large sits pretty atop the pile heading into the season's final stretch. That's your lot for this period, but the ducks never sleep for long — roll on the next fixture, and with it, no doubt, fresh additions to the roll of dubious honour. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 17 September 1989

0 ducks
Week 21 · 17 Sep 1989
A single fixture on the books this week, and — brace yourselves — not a single one of our own troubled the umpire's raised finger for nought. The 1989 season's harvest sits untouched for once, the exclusive zero club left to twiddle its webbed thumbs on the sidelines. It happens. Even the most dedicated congregation of the scoreless needs the odd week off, and Linton Park's batting order apparently decided, en masse, to actually trouble the scorers. Whether this represents a genuine outbreak of competence or merely a temporary ceasefire in hostilities against the number zero remains to be seen. The Duck Club committee views it with polite suspicion. Rest assured, the ducks have not retired — they are merely regrouping, sharpening their beaks, and waiting for a rash shot or an unplayable yorker to bring them out of hibernation. Normal service, we are confident, will resume shortly. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Matt Large — 4 ducks from 18 innings (22.22%) - Dave Charlton — 3 ducks from 11 innings (27.27%) - Peter Whyte — 3 ducks from 27 innings (11.11%) - Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks from 31 innings (9.68%) - Ron Kemp — 2 ducks from just 4 innings, a truly alarming 50% strike rate Ron Kemp's figures deserve a special mention here — two ducks from four trips to the crease is the kind of consistency of a very particular kind that the club exists to celebrate. Keep it up, Ron, membership beckons. 📅 Looking ahead With the ducks lying dormant this week, all eyes turn to the next fixture. Matt Large leads the chase for Duck of the Year honours, but Dave Charlton's blistering conversion rate suggests this contest is far from settled. Somewhere out there, a Linton Park batsman is shouldering arms to a straight one, or shuffling across their stumps for a fatal lbw, just waiting for their moment of dubious glory. We watch. We wait. We celebrate — next week, no doubt, will not disappoint. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 10 to 13 September 1989

3 ducks
Week 20 · 10 Sep 1989 – 13 Sep 1989
Two fixtures on the books this stretch, and the exclusive zero club opened its doors exactly once — but when it did, it did so with real conviction. 🏏 Saturday 9th — 1st XI away at Sibton Park CC The top order set the tone early. Trevor Back, promoted to number 2, departed caught Andy Allon bowled S Stickles for his first duck of the season and the 29th of an increasingly distinguished career — 22nd on the all-time list of 1,119. Trevor arrives here a paid-up committee man of ducking, a fixture of the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and a proud half of two standing partnerships on the honours board, Only Two Quacks with Allan Cooper and Quacking Together with Jon Harris. Fittingly, on this very day in 1955 — thirty-four years to the week — John "-'s" Taylor was walking back for nought of his own against Old Roffensians, en route to 15 career ducks across his 1949–1963 span. History, it seems, keeps a diary. Down at number 8, Alan Boyns followed suit, caught S Stickles bowled S Butler, his second duck of the season and eighth of his career from just 59 innings — 77th on the all-time roll. A tidier statistical footprint than his captain's, but the same instinct for arriving and departing without incident, and he too shares this day's spectral company with the 1955 vintage. And to round out a collapse that reached all the way to the tail, Matt Large — caught David Stewart bowled S Butler at number 10 — logged his fourth duck of the season, his 22nd overall, and moves to 35th of 1,119 in the all-time table. Matt is no stranger to this fixture list: a three-time reigning Duck of the Year for 1987, 1988 and now 1989 — a hat-trick very much alive this very season — plus a Debutante Duck (he ducked on debut, long before this week's effort) and a nearly-man for the Full House, having now visited 10 of the 11 batting positions in pursuit of the complete set. Like his top-order colleagues, the ghost of 1955's Old Roffensians duck kept him company on the walk back. Three wickets, three ducks, positions 2 through 10 — a proper top-to-tail collapse, and a rare instance of an entire batting card haunted by the same 34-year-old echo. Sunday's other fixture, mercifully for the scorers, stayed vegan — not a duck in sight. 📊 Week in summary: 3 ducks from 2 matches, all in the same innings at Sibton Park CC. Consistency of a very particular kind. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Matt Large — 4 ducks / 18 innings (22.2%) 2. Dave Charlton — 3 ducks / 11 innings (27.3%) 3. Peter Whyte — 3 ducks / 26 innings (11.5%) 4. Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks / 30 innings (10.0%) 5. Ron Kemp — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.0%) Ron Kemp's strike rate remains the one to fear. Roll on the next fixture — the club always finds room for a few more members.

🦆 DUCK CLUB TOUR BULLETIN — Beacon CC, Cornwall, 3rd September 1989 🦆

4 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1989 – 3 Sep 1989
Greetings from the far south-west, where the Tour XI ventured deep into Cornish territory and discovered that Beacon CC's bowling — and, on two occasions, Tour XI's own running between the wickets — travels rather better than the batting did. Of the two fixtures played on this leg of the tour, it was the trip to Beacon CC that yielded the quacking, and yielded it in some style: four ducks in a single innings, a proper collection for the away kitbag. 🏏 Tour XI at Beacon CC Peter Whyte, in early at first drop, departed c Bryn Martin b S Harris for his 3rd duck of the season and 7th of an increasingly distinguished career, from 57 innings — 95th in the all-time reckoning. Curiously, on this very day back in 1972, Benny Bell (1966–1975, 30 ducks, 18th all-time) was also nought not out against Staplehurst CC. The 3rd of September, it seems, has form. Mark Stevenson followed him back not long after, bowled S Harris for a second-ball-of-the-season nought, his 2nd career duck from a tidy 22 innings, 242nd of 1119 on the club's grim all-time roll. And — remarkably — on this same date in 1972, Benny Bell was, once again, doing precisely the same thing against Staplehurst. The calendar has clearly got a taste for it. Down at seven, Mike Bains was run out — the scorer noting only "Unsure," which tells its own story — for his 2nd duck of the season and career alike, from 16 innings, 243rd all-time. And yes, 3rd September 1972 strikes a third time: Benny Bell, same duck, same date, seventeen years apart in spirit if nothing else. David Castle then joined him in the run-out queue, also given "Unsure," for his 2nd duck of this season and 13th of a long and storied career across 190 innings — good enough for 48th all-time and a paid-up place on the Tourist Duck top 10, appropriately enough for a man doing his damage on tour. Benny Bell, 1972, Staplehurst CC, once more — by now the date itself deserves an honorary membership. Whatever the mix-up was that claimed Bains and Castle in quick succession, it clearly ran in the family of misfortune rather than the family of relatives — no shared surnames here, just shared bad luck. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks, one unfortunate Cornish afternoon, and a running theme — quite literally — of mix-ups in the middle. Two clean bowled, two run out in confusion, and not a golden duck among them, so at least everyone had the decency to trouble the scorers for a ball or two before the inevitable. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Dave Charlton — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) 2. Matt Large — 3 ducks / 17 innings (17.65%) 3. Peter Whyte — 3 ducks / 25 innings (12.0%) 4. Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks / 29 innings (10.34%) 5. Ron Kemp — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.0%) Peter Whyte's afternoon at Beacon CC keeps him lodged in the season's top three — a podium finish nobody actually wants. 🦆 The tour rolls on, and so, evidently, does the Duck Club. Next fixture, same risk, same reward — see you in the exclusive zero club.

🦆 Duck Club Dispatch — 27 August to 1 September 1989

5 ducks
Week 18 · 27 Aug 1989 – 1 Sep 1989
Five ducks laid across five matches this week as the tourists made hay abroad and Bearsted did the honours at home. The exclusive zero club welcomes a fresh intake. 🏏 1st XI v Bearsted CC (28 August) Nigel Thirkell fell at three, caught Bateman off Chandrasekera, for his 3rd duck of the season and 42nd of an extraordinary career — 9th in the all-time table from 669 innings. Duck of the Year back in 1986, and one half of "Only Two Quacks" with club great Jim Hall. It rather runs in the family: father John Thirkell tops the all-time list outright on 90, brother Tim sits 4th on 60, and brother Simon isn't far behind on 23 — a Thirkell dynasty built on the art of not scoring. On this very day in 1974, Roland Parr also strolled back for nought against Gravesend CC. Batting at nine, Sean Charlton joined him in the column, bowled by Chandrasekera for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd career, 184th in the all-time reckoning. Sean is a paid-up Debutante Duck club member from his first-ever knock — and keeps it in the family, with brother Dave Charlton already prowling the top of this season's leaderboard. Sean shares Nigel's on-this-day company in Roland Parr, 1974 vintage. ✈️ Tour XI — Falmouth and Troon Touring duty proved no protection. Against Falmouth CC on the 29th, Stewart Morrison was caught behind off Steven Young at five, his 1st duck of the season and 6th career, 109th on the all-time table, and a Lone Duck top-tenner besides. Stan Wratten made a duck on this very date back in 1954, against D Hambrook XI. Two batters later, Tim Lush followed him back at nine, caught Gimpson bowled Young, and did so in some style — this was his very first senior innings for the club. A Debutante Duck the moment he walked out, now ranked 501 of 1119, with the whole career still ahead of him. He shares Stewart's on-this-day company in Stan Wratten, 1954. Then on to Troon CC on the 31st, where Piers McDonald was caught Dunstan bowled Williams at four for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd career, 177th all-time, and another name on the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. Allan Cooper made his own duck on this date back in 1980, against Staplehurst CC. 📋 The Week in Brief Five ducks, five matches, honours shared fairly between home soil and away fixtures — Bearsted, Falmouth and Troon each claimed a scalp. No pairs, no king pairs, and the family honours boards stayed untroubled this week, though the Thirkells and Charltons both proved the gene runs strong. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Dave Charlton — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) 2. Matt Large — 3 ducks in 17 innings (17.65%) 3. Nigel Thirkell — 3 ducks in 28 innings (10.71%) 4. Ron Kemp — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50.0%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks in 9 innings (22.22%) Two Thirkells in the season's top five — a family affair at the top of the table — while Dave Charlton leads the chase with brother Sean now also on the board. The next fixtures await, and with them, no doubt, further contributions to the cause. See you at the crease. 🦆🍩

2 ducks
Week 16 · 12 Aug 1989 – 13 Aug 1989
A restrained couple of days for the wider club, but the exclusive zero club still processed two fresh entries from the 1st XI over the weekend, one against Headcorn and one against Benenden. 🏏 1st XI Saturday at Headcorn CC saw Simon Thirkell depart for his 2nd duck of the season, caught Mick Everitt off the bowling of Rob Mansell batting at number 8. It's his 23rd career duck from 93 innings, good enough to hold 32nd spot in the all-time list of 1,117 names, and he arrives with pedigree attached — Duck of the Year 1983, a place among the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck elites, and 59 career noughts logged on the 50 Before a 50 board against a high score of just 48. Triumph through adversity, truly. Consistency of a very particular kind runs in the family too: father John sits on 90 career ducks, brothers Tim and Nigel on 60 and 41 respectively, and family member Stuart Thirkell has just the one to his name so far — plenty of scope to catch up. On this very date back in 1970, Mike Iliffe was doing exactly the same thing against Gravesend CC, part of a career haul of 43 that still holds him 8th on the all-time roll. Some traditions simply refuse to die. Sunday's trip to Benenden CC claimed Dave Charlton, run out for nought at number 2, the identity of the fielder lost to history along with the run. It's his 3rd duck of the campaign and 4th of a still-young career, 15 innings deep, which lands him 130th on the club's long and dishonourable list. Brother Sean is already on the board with 2 career ducks of his own, so the Charltons look to be quietly building a dynasty of their own. And on this same date in 1960, Pat Hardwick was doing his own bit against Bowaters CC, a performance that helped earn him the Duck of the Year title that season — the calendar has a long memory. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one apiece — a modest haul by Duck Club standards, but both arriving with proper credentials. No pairs and no family affairs this time, just two lone campaigners doing their duty for the art of not scoring. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Dave Charlton — 3 ducks / 9 innings (33.3%) - Matt Large — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.1%) - Ron Kemp — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50.0%) - Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks / 7 innings (28.6%) - Peter Whyte — 2 ducks / 16 innings (12.5%) Dave Charlton takes the honours twice over this week — top of both the weekend's card and the season's table. With Ron Kemp lurking on a frankly alarming 50% strike rate, the race for Duck of the Year is very much alive. 🦆 The season rolls on. Same invitation as ever stands for next weekend: middle stump, first ball, and a reserved seat in the honours book. See you out there.

🦆 Duck News — 5 to 10 August 1989

A quieter week for the exclusive zero club, but quality over quantity as ever — three ducks across four fixtures, including a fresh face making the most inauspicious of first impressions. ☀️ Sunday 6th August — 1st XI v Chadwell Thors Oak CC A curious symmetry at the crease this weekend, as two Linton batsmen fell to the identical combination of c Manning b Hewson within the same innings — proof, if any were needed, that misery does love company. First to go was Dave Charlton, holing out at number five for his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd of an increasingly eventful career, moving to 156th of 1117 in the all-time standings from just 13 innings. Dave shares a surname with the noughts if not (yet) the numbers — sibling Sean Charlton already carries 2 career ducks of his own, so the family ledger is filling in nicely. On this very day in 1975, Paul Brattle was doing much the same against Sennocke CC, en route to 25 career ducks (28th all-time) and a place on the Debutante Duck board. Moments later, Peter Whyte followed him back for his own 2nd duck of the season, his 6th overall, sliding to 108th of 1117 from 48 innings. Peter, too, finds himself keeping company with Paul Brattle's ghost of 1975 — the calendar clearly has a soft spot for Sennocke CC ducks. 🌙 Thursday 10th August — Midweek XI v Mundford CC The Midweek fixture at Mundford produced a full initiation ceremony, as Keith Francis marked his very first senior innings — three balls survived, then bowled by T Sizer — with a duck on debut. A genuine arrival into the club's most dubious of traditions, Keith goes straight onto the Debutante Duck board, his opening account reading 1 duck from 1 innings, 420th of 1117 all-time. Not the start he'd have scripted, but the Duck Club welcomes him warmly all the same. On this day in 1958, Roy Clarke suffered similarly against Harrietsham CC on his way to a mighty 40 career ducks, the Duck of the Year crown in 1959, and a shared Quacking Together entry alongside the legendary John Ames — exalted company for a first outing. 📊 Week in Summary Three ducks from four matches, with the 1st XI supplying a matching pair against Chadwell Thors Oak and the Midweek boys inducting a brand new member into the fold. A tidy, unspectacular week by Duck Club standards — but the season race remains very much alive. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Matt Large — 3 ducks, 11 innings (27.27%) - Ron Kemp — 2 ducks, 4 innings (50.00%) - Dave Charlton — 2 ducks, 7 innings (28.57%) - Peter Whyte — 2 ducks, 16 innings (12.50%) - Nigel Thirkell — 2 ducks, 22 innings (9.09%) Ron Kemp's 50% strike rate remains the most alarming figure on the board — a coin toss with a bat in hand. Meanwhile the chasing pack is stacked and ready to pounce on Matt Large's lead. 🦆 Until the next fixture, when the middle order will once again be tested and, in all likelihood, found gloriously wanting. Get your ducks in a row, Linton.

🦆 Duck News — Week of 29 July to 3 August 1989 🦆

3 ducks
Week 14 · 29 Jul 1989 – 3 Aug 1989
A quieter week in the hunting grounds, but the exclusive zero club still found three new members to welcome through Bearsted, The Mote and Moreton. Quality over quantity, as ever, in the art of not scoring. 🏏 1st XI Away at Hollingbourne CC on the 29th, Matt Large strolled to the crease at number 10 and departed as briskly as he arrived, c Geoff Griffiths b Dave Kay. It's his 3rd duck of the season and 21st for his career, good enough for 36th on the all-time list of 1,116. A two-time reigning Duck of the Year (1987 and 1988), and on the current season standings below he's setting the early pace to make it three titles on the spin. He remains a nearly-man on the Full House board too, still hunting that elusive duck at every position from 1 to 11, and is a fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top 10s besides. On this very day back in 1963, Gordon Fenner (34 career ducks, 1941–1973, 15th all-time) was also trudging off against Birkenhead Grasshoppers CC — some things never change. The very next day at The Mote, Nigel Thirkell fell for nought at number 3, c Neil Tillott b Bruce Nissen. His 2nd of the season and 41st of his career lifts him to a mighty 9th on the all-time table. A former Duck of the Year (1986), a paid-up member of the Homebody and Tourist top 10s, and one half of Only Two Quacks with Jim Hall. Nigel also carries the family name proudly — his father John Thirkell sits top of the entire club's all-time list with 90 ducks, while siblings Tim (60, 4th all-time) and Simon (22) and cousin Stuart (1) round out cricket's most committed duck dynasty. On this day in 1972, Andrew Moss made his own nought against Glynde & Beddingham CC, one of 10 in a career that ran 1968 to 1987. ⏰ Midweek XI At home to Moreton CC on the 3rd, Ron Kemp was trapped lbw by T Murley batting at number 8.
