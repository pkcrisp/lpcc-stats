🦆 Duck News — 29 September 1984

0 ducks
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1984
A rare and troubling development from Linton Park this week: the ducks went to ground. One match played, not a single nought recorded. Eleven-plus batsmen apparently strolled to the crease, had a look around, and simply declined to contribute to the cause. The exclusive zero club sat idle, its gates unopened, its honours board untroubled. Somewhere out there a bowler will be feeling very hard done by. Let the record show this is not retirement, not decline, merely a temporary and no doubt shameful lapse in standards. The art of not scoring is a discipline, and discipline occasionally has an off week. Normal service — by which we mean abnormal, ignominious, thoroughly celebrated service — is expected to resume shortly. 🍩 Week in Summary - Matches played: 1 - Ducks laid: 0 - Vibes: suspiciously fowl-free Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Sean Cook — 5 ducks in 9 innings (56%) 🥈 David Iliffe — 5 ducks in 18 innings (28%) 🥉 Paul Gibson — 5 ducks in 30 innings (17%) 4️⃣ Sean Riley — 4 ducks in 8 innings (50%) 5️⃣ Ian Brattle — 4 ducks in 13 innings (31%) Sean Cook leads on strike rate as well as tally, which is either a badge of honour or a cry for help — the committee has not yet decided which. Paul Gibson meanwhile keeps grinding out his fifth the hard way, across thirty innings of quiet endurance. Take note, gentlemen: consistency of this particular kind does not happen by accident. 0️⃣ A quiet week, then — but the season is far from over, and Linton's batsmen have shown before that a barren afternoon is rarely more than a warm-up. Next fixture, someone will oblige. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🏏🦆

🦆 Duck News — 23-26 September 1984

4 ducks
Week 22 · 23 Sep 1984 – 26 Sep 1984
Only two fixtures on the card this week, but the Anchorians match found room for a hat-trick of noughts, plus one further victim to keep the Midweek lads honest. Four ducks in all — a quiet week by Duck Club standards, but every one earns its place on the record. Sunday 23rd — 1st XI vs Anchorians CC (h) Three down at Anchorians, and none of them cheaply forgotten. David Iliffe fell at four, caught Jim Fish bowled G Cooper, his 5th duck of the season and 27th of a career now 123 innings deep — 22nd on the all-time list. David remains a paid-up member of the "Quacked by Kin" honour, sent packing back in '82 by his own relative Mike Iliffe (himself sitting on 43 career noughts), and was Duck of the Year that same summer. Neatly enough, this very date in 1972 saw the legendary Tim Thirkell, 54 ducks and 4th of all time, also trudge off for nought. Jim Hall went next at seven, bowled Barry Diplock, his 3rd of the summer and a mighty 57th for his career — 3rd on the all-time roll of dishonour from 378 innings. Jim's honours board could fill a pamphlet: back-to-back Duck of the Year in 1970 and 1977, top ten in both the Homebody and Lone Duck charts, and three separate "Quacking Together" partnerships. He shares his duck-date with Thirkell's 1972 effort too — some anniversaries just keep giving. Sean Cook completed the set at ten, bowled S Grubb, his 5th duck of the season from a mere 9 innings and 11th of his career. Sean currently wears the Duck of the Year 1984 crown, very much up for grabs as the season runs down. And yes, Thirkell was at it again on this same date in 1972 — starting to feel less like history and more like a curse. Wednesday 26th — Midweek XI vs Penshurst Park CC (a) A lone entry: Tim Theobald, caught by a fielder even the scorebook couldn't name, bowled E Fowler, his first duck of both the season and his young career from just three senior innings — 468th on the all-time list. Tim already holds his place in the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, so this is merely business as usual for now. His parallel is a fine one — on this same date in 1965, Roy Clarke, Duck of the Year 1959 and 9th all-time with 40 ducks, also departed for nought. Week in summary Four ducks, two matches, three of them arriving in the same Anchorians innings — the Duck Club does enjoy a cluster. No pairs and no family affairs to report this time, just four honest individual failures. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Sean Cook — 5 ducks / 9 innings (55.6%) - David Iliffe — 5 ducks / 18 innings (27.8%) - Paul Gibson — 5 ducks / 30 innings (16.7%) - Sean Riley — 4 ducks / 8 innings (50.0%) - Ian Brattle — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.8%) Cook and Iliffe both feature in this week's ducks and the season's top five — form, as ever, clusters where you least want it. Until the next fixture, when no doubt fresh names will queue for the honour. Pad up if you dare. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Week of 16–19 September 1984

2 ducks
Week 21 · 16 Sep 1984 – 19 Sep 1984
A quiet week in the fields, but the Midweek XI made sure the season's tally didn't stagnate. Two fixtures played, one of them entirely fowl-free, but the Wednesday trip yielded a couple of fresh entries for the ledger. 🏏 Midweek XI v Hawkenbury CC (home, Wed 19th) Openers Andy Woollett and Mike Elworthy shared the top of the order and, in a rare show of solidarity, shared a rendezvous with the pavilion gate soon after. Andy Woollett fell first, bowled by X Robertson to register his 2nd duck of the campaign and 11th of an increasingly distinguished career, nudging him to 52nd of 1057 in the all-time table from 120 innings — a rate that keeps him comfortably inside cricket's most exclusive zero club. Neatly enough, on this very calendar day back in 1982, David Iliffe was going through precisely the same ritual against Anchorians CC — and Iliffe, appropriately, is currently making a habit of it again, sitting pretty in this season's top five below. Not to be outdone, Mike Elworthy departed lbw to X King for his 4th duck of the season and 9th of his career, moving to 73rd of 1057 on the all-time roll of dishonour. A paid-up member of the Tourist Duck top 10, Mike continues to prove that not scoring is a feat he can manage anywhere in the country — home soil included, evidently. He too shares that same 1982 echo with David Iliffe, meaning Hawkenbury's bowlers can claim a small piece of history: two batsmen undone on the anniversary of a duck struck against an entirely different club altogether. The other midweek fixture, mercifully or regrettably depending on your appetite for comedy, produced not a single duck — full marks to all eleven for keeping the scorebook honest. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both from the top of the Midweek XI order against Hawkenbury CC. A modest haul, but consistency of a very particular kind remains the club's watchword, and Woollett and Elworthy have both done their bit to keep the honours board ticking over. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Paul Gibson — 5 ducks in 29 innings (17.24%) 2. Sean Cook — 4 ducks in 8 innings (50.00%) 2. Sean Riley — 4 ducks in 8 innings (50.00%) 4. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) 5. David Iliffe — 4 ducks in 17 innings (23.53%) Sean Cook and Sean Riley's 50% strike rates remain a genuine marvel of triumph through adversity — coin-flip cricket at its finest. Gibson holds the outright lead for now, but with Iliffe's habit of finding trouble on this exact date in history, nobody should get too comfortable at the top. 🦆 Next fixture beckons, and with it the near-certainty of fresh additions to the ranks. The Duck Club never sleeps — see you at the crease, or more likely, on the walk back.

🦆 Duck News — Week of 8–12 September 1984

0 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1984 – 12 Sep 1984
Three matches. Three completed innings' worth of opportunity. And not a single soul willing to oblige. For the first time this reporting period, the Duck Club must report the unthinkable: a barren weekend. The exclusive zero club held its doors open, the honours board dusted itself down in anticipation, and Linton Park's batsmen — every last one of them — simply declined the invitation. Somewhere out there, a run was scored off nearly every first ball faced, and the Duck Club can only look on in quiet professional disappointment. It happens. Even the art of not scoring has its off weeks. No ducks means no fresh chapters in the honours board this time round — no Full Members welcomed, no Debutante Ducks minted, no father-and-son double acts denied by a single run. The season's front-runners simply sit tight, tallies untouched, watching the rest of the field fail to close the gap. Which brings us to where it matters most — the race is far from over. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 Paul Gibson — 5 ducks from 28 innings (18%) 🥈 Sean Cook — 4 ducks from 7 innings (57%) — the division's most efficient collector, statistically speaking 🥉 Sean Riley — 4 ducks from 8 innings (50%) 4️⃣ Ian Brattle — 4 ducks from 10 innings (40%) 5️⃣ David Iliffe — 4 ducks from 16 innings (25%) Sean Cook's 57% strike rate remains the number to fear this season — more than one innings in two ending in a walk back for nought. Paul Gibson leads on volume, but with Cook, Riley and Brattle all lurking on 4, this is very much a photo finish waiting to happen. One careless heave next weekend and the top of the table could be rewritten entirely. So rest easy, gentlemen — the reprieve is temporary. Ducks are patient creatures, and Linton Park's bowlers will be back next week with renewed intent. The Duck Club will be watching every dismissal with its usual solemn hope. 🍩0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — The Cornwall Tour, 2 September 1984

2 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1984
The Tour XI ventured deep into enemy territory for a fixture against Beacon CC, Cornwall — and returned with sunburn, seaside memories, and two fresh entries for the club's exclusive zero club. 🏏 Tour XI at Beacon CC, Cornwall First to fall was Allan Cooper, run out for nought at number three in a mix-up nobody was keen to claim ownership of. It's the 3rd duck of Allan's season and the 31st of a career now 208 innings deep, holding him steady at 16th in the all-time standings — rarefied company built one dismissal at a time. Allan is also a serial name in the club's "Only Two Quacks" ledger, having shared that dubious double-act with no fewer than three separate teammates down the years. On this very day in 1968, LPCC great Paul Bowles was doing much the same against Bearsted CC — Allan simply chose Cornwall for his own rendezvous with history. Not to be left out, Tim Thirkell followed at number ten, caught by R Hocking off the bowling of John Hutchins for his 2nd duck of the season. It's number 54 in a career now 438 innings long, and it keeps Tim parked at a lofty 4th on the all-time list — a paid-up member of the Full House club, having ducked at every position from 1 to 11, and a permanent fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens alike. Fittingly, on a tour to Cornwall, the road-duck pedigree shows. Tim last topped the Duck of the Year table back in 1972, and with a name like his running through the club — son John, brothers Nigel and Simon, and further Thirkells besides all carrying career duck tallies of their own — the nought clearly runs in the blood. As with Allan, this too fell on the very date Paul Bowles made his 1968 duck against Bearsted CC — the club calendar apparently has a taste for symmetry. 📅 Week in Summary One match, two ducks, both from the Tour XI's middle-to-lower order on a long day away in Cornwall. No pairs, no families, no golden ducks this time round — just honest, workmanlike failure from two of the club's most decorated campaigners. Triumph through adversity, as ever. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Paul Gibson — 5 ducks in 25 innings (20%) 2. Sean Cook — 4 ducks in 7 innings (57.14%) 3. Sean Riley — 4 ducks in 8 innings (50%) 4. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks in 10 innings (40%) 5. David Iliffe — 4 ducks in 16 innings (25%) Sean Cook remains the season's most efficient collector, a barely believable 57% strike rate at the art of not scoring. The tour rolls on, and the season isn't finished with us yet. Who'll be adding to the tally at the next fixture? We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck Report — 26 to 31 August 1984

6 ducks
Week 18 · 26 Aug 1984 – 31 Aug 1984
Six new inductees joined the exclusive zero club this week, courtesy of two fixtures — a grim afternoon at home to Old Anchorians CC and a tour double-header in Cornwall that agreed with nobody's batting. 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC (home, 26 Aug) David Iliffe fell lbw to J- Lycett for his 4th duck of the summer and 26th of a 121-innings career — 22nd on the all-time list and a Duck of the Year 1982 honours board member, not to mention once being "Quacked by Kin" by his own father Mike, who carries 43 career ducks himself. On this very day in 1979, Tim Thirkell (53 ducks, 4th all-time, Full House man) also made a duck against these same Old Anchorians — some fixtures clearly have it in for Linton batsmen. Steve Cheale was stumped off B Goodger for his 1st of the season and 4th overall, level with Charles Wycherley, Duck of the Year back in 1906, on the same tally. Steve's sibling David Cheale is no stranger to the club either, with 3 career ducks of his own — a fine show of family solidarity. Ian Brattle departed for his 4th of the season and 17th of his career, level with Robert Murray, a name from the 1958-65 era who took 179 innings to get there. Ian remains a paid-up "50 Before a 50" club member and shares Only Two Quacks honours with Michael Beeney. Ivan Diprose closed the innings bowled by C Marriott for his 1st senior duck in only his 4th innings, joining the one-and-done company of - Copeland, whose entire career amounted to a single innings and a single duck, back in 1979. Tour XI (away) Down in Cornwall, Paul Gibson enjoyed himself rather less than the tour committee intended. Bowled by R Fisher at St Austell for his 4th of the season, he returned a day later at Falmouth to nick Sean Williams to Jeremy Gimpson for his 5th — a grim pair to bookend the trip. On this same date in 1954 Stan Wratten made a duck against D Hambrook's XI, and in '65 Frank Brattle did likewise against Bearsted, so Paul keeps good historical company. Week in summary Five matches, six ducks, and two fixtures did all the damage — Old Anchorians clearly bring something out in us, and Cornwall was no kinder. Paul Gibson's pair was the standout feat, proof a change of scenery changes nothing. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Paul Gibson — 5 ducks / 24 innings (20.8%) - Sean Cook — 4 ducks / 7 innings (57.1%) - Sean Riley — 4 ducks / 8 innings (50.0%) - Ian Brattle — 4 ducks / 10 innings (40.0%) - David Iliffe — 4 ducks / 16 innings (25.0%) Four names locked on four apiece as the season enters its final furlong — plenty still to play for. Next fixture awaits, and the zero club never sleeps. See you out there 🦆🏏0️⃣

Three fixtures on the go this week and the 1st XI alone answered the call, surrendering four wickets for nought between them. A dubious honour, but honours all the same. Spookily, on this very day back in 1963 the club's all-time record holder John Thirkell (90 ducks, 1943–1977, five-time Duck of the Year) was also sent packing for nought, against The Mote CC. Allan Cooper, b Tom Robertson at number 6, brings up his 2nd of the season and 30th career duck — 16th on the all-time list. A three-time inductee of the "Only Two Quacks" honours reel, having previously shared the indignity with Jim Hall, Jon Harris and Trevor Back. On this day in 1956, John --'s Taylor was likewise dismissed for nought against Mote A. Mike Elworthy, b R Mills at 7, notches his 3rd duck of the campaign and 8th of his career, good enough for 79th all-time and a place on the Tourist Duck top 10 — evidently the away strips suit him no better than the home ones. He shares Cooper's ghostly company from 1956. Sean Cook, b R Mills at 8, made it a hat-trick of blanks for the innings — his 4th of the season and remarkably his 10th career duck already, from just 22 innings. That's a serious strike rate for failure, landing him 64th all-time. A fellow Debutante Duck alumnus, Sean also holds the Duck of the Year 1984 crown at present — and on this week's evidence, he's not relinquishing it lightly. The third fixture, refreshingly, went duck-free — someone has to keep the average down. Week 16 · 11 Aug 1984 – 15 Aug 1984
A quiet few days by Duck Club standards — three matches came and went, and only one of them coughed up any noughts. But when the Midweek XI travelled to Bobbing Court CC on the 15th, the zero club got its evening's entertainment in a hurry. 🌙 Midweek XI – Bobbing Court CC (away) Ian Brattle, in at six, was bowled by C Drury for his 3rd duck of the season and 16th of his LPCC career — good enough for 39th on the all-time list of 1057. Ian remains a proud member of the "50 Before a 50" club (51 ducks against a top score of a mere 33 — the art of not scoring, refined), first joined the honours board with a Debutante Duck all those innings ago, and once strung together 7 consecutive ducks in a row, a feat of quite magnificent consistency. He also keeps it in the family — brother Paul Brattle sits on 25 career ducks of his own, so the gene pool clearly runs shallow when it comes to troubling the scorers. Spookily, on this very date back in 1964, club legend and all-time #1 duck-getter John Thirkell (1943–1977, 90 ducks) also walked back for nought — this time against Bowaters CC. Three batters later, Sean Cook followed him back, stumped by Unsure off the bowling of Daniel Ball for his 3rd duck of the season and 9th of his career, 72nd on the all-time roll. Sean, like Ian, first announced himself with a Debutante Duck years ago, but the one that matters right now is fresher — he currently holds the Duck of the Year 1984 title, and this latest addition only tightens his grip on it. And by pleasing coincidence, Sean shares that same 1964-echo with Ian — the very date John Thirkell was doing exactly the same thing against Bowaters CC two decades earlier. Two Middlewick men, one shared footnote in history. 📋 Elsewhere The other two fixtures in the period passed off entirely duck-free — eleven-a-side vegan affairs where everyone got off the mark. Bobbing Court, evidently, brought out the worst in Linton Park's lower order. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks from three matches, both in the same innings, both at Bobbing Court. The Duck Hunting Season leaderboard ticks along regardless, a monument to consistency of a very particular kind.
