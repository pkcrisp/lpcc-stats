🗓️ 22 Aug – 28 Aug 1891 · 1891 — 1 match

Thu 27 Aug 1891 🧢 1st XIv ? · HomeformLWTLW

Thursday last, the 27th of August, we had the pleasure of receiving our visitors upon our ground, and the toss falling to me, I did not hesitate to elect for the bat, judging the wicket likely to play kindly before the day was done.

Our innings, taken as a whole, owed its character chiefly to two of our number. Captain F J Fox gave a capital display, standing firm at the wicket for five and twenty runs not out, watchful yet ready to punish anything loose. He found a most able partner in Major L T Spens, whose two and twenty not out at the other end did much to steady our affairs and carry the innings through to its close. Between them they saw off the best that our visitors' trundlers could offer, and it was a pleasure to watch them at their work.

In the end time proved the only victor, for neither side could contrive to force home an advantage, and the match was left drawn, honours shared fairly between the two elevens. I own I had hoped, upon winning the toss, that we might press to a more decided issue, yet a draw honestly come by against opponents who tested us keenly throughout is no mean result, and I thank our visitors for a hard fought and thoroughly sporting afternoon's cricket.

🗓️ 15 Aug – 21 Aug 1891 · 1891 — 1 match

Sat 15 Aug 1891 🧢 1st XIv EC Eton Ramblers CC · HomeformDLWTL

Saturday last, the fifteenth of August, saw Eton Ramblers CC as our guests upon the ground, and a fairer afternoon for the game one could scarcely have wished for. Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in electing to take first use of the wicket, a decision that answered handsomely as events proved. Captain H Hamilton gave a captain's lead with a well-made fifty, his 61 the innings' backbone, and he found a willing partner in R H Pemberton, whose 27 added substance to our total. All out we duly were, but not before a solid foundation had been laid for the day's work.

Called upon to defend our total with the ball, our men set about the visitors with a will. J A Gibbs offered the sternest resistance, his 34 the innings' chief contribution, but he found little support, for A C Rock bowled quite splendidly to claim five wickets, ably backed by W H Patterson with three and by Captain Hinde, who took one. The Ramblers were dismissed in their turn, and the match was ours by the margin of 21 runs.

It should not pass unremarked that Captain Hamilton's innings carried him past A M Streatfield into fifth place among the club's run-getters of all time, while Rock's five wickets lifted him above E M Owens into twelfth upon the bowling roll of honour, each fellow now sitting atop the season's charts besides, with 164 runs and 12 wickets respectively to his name this year. A pleasant and profitable afternoon's cricket, and two points thoroughly and deservedly earned.

🗓️ 08 Aug – 14 Aug 1891 · 1891 — 2 matches

Wed 12 Aug 1891 🧢 1st XIv FC Free Foresters CC · HomeformWDLWT

Wednesday last, the 12th of August, brought Free Foresters CC to our ground, and having lost the toss we were asked to field. Mr J A Turner gave a truly memorable display, going on to a magnificent century of 106, well supported by Captain Rawlinson with 37 and C L Metcalfe with 34, before their innings at length closed.

Our trundlers acquitted themselves with no little credit in reply. Captain Hinde took four wickets, a performance that carries him past F Garrett into seventeenth place upon the club's all-time list of wicket-takers, while L Friend claimed three and A C Rock one.

Set the task of making the runs, we did not prosper as we had hoped. Captain H Hamilton played a well-made fifty of 55, and F J Richardson gave him sound support with 31, but L Friend and R H Pemberton were each dismissed without troubling the scorers, and our innings closed all out. There was some small consolation for Friend, whose season's tally of 12 has carried him past Captain H Hamilton to ninth upon the club's all-time run-scorers' list.

We were beaten in the end by ten wickets, a margin that leaves no doubt as to our visitors' superiority on the day. I have myself enjoyed some fortune with the bat this season, my 123 runs still heading the eleven's charts, while Captain Hinde's nine wickets keep him at the head of our bowling. We accept the reverse in the spirit the game demands, and shall look to give a better account of ourselves next week.

Mon 10 Aug 1891 🧢 1st XIv RC Royal Engineers CC · HomeformWWDLW

Monday last, the tenth of August, saw us play host to the Royal Engineers upon our own ground, and though the toss went against us, our visitors chose to bat, leaving us first use of the ball. The Engineers batted with credit, C G Burnaby proving the mainstay of their innings with a capital 42, well supported by Captain Rice with 27 and S L Owen with 24, before the innings closed. Captain Rawson and Captain L Friend were less fortunate, both returning to the pavilion without troubling the scorer. Captain Hinde did the greater part of the damage among our trundlers, taking four wickets without cost, with Captain H Hamilton and A C Rock lending able support.

Taking up the willow in reply, we found in A C Rock a fine standard-bearer, his 38 the backbone of our innings, ably seconded by S Christopherson with 29, though L M Richards was sent back for a duck. For my own part I was glad to contribute 25 runs, which I am told carries me past W H Fryer into second place upon the club's roll of all-time run-scorers, a distinction received with more surprise than pride. I may add that I stand at the head of this season's run-getters with 97, while Rock occupies second place among the season's wicket-takers with six to his name.

When the last wicket had fallen the sides stood level, honours even after a full day's cricket. A tie is a result that flatters both camps and wounds neither, and we parted from our friends of the Royal Engineers in the best of spirits.

🗓️ 04 Jul – 10 Jul 1891 · 1891 — 1 match

Sat 04 Jul 1891 🧢 1st XIv SS Sutton Valence School · HomeformLWWDL

On Saturday the fourth of July, in the year 1891, we had the pleasure of receiving Sutton Valence School upon our own ground, and winning the toss I had no hesitation in asking our men to take first strike, a decision events were kind enough to vindicate.

Our innings, when the last timbers fell, owed its substance chiefly to my own effort of 72, carried past my fifty and supported in fine style by G Knight, whose 61 likewise passed the half-century mark, and by George Champion, who added a useful 33. I will not dwell overlong upon poor Jack Fryer's duck, save to note that even the soundest sides have their off moments.

In the field our visitors gave a spirited account of themselves, F De W Lushington playing a superb innings of 104 and G R Hunt following with a well-made 84, ably assisted by H W Hunting's 22. Yet the day belonged in no small measure to C Chambers, whose trundling returned the admirable figures of five wickets for 25 runs, five of his overs going entirely unscored, while the Reverend Thomas Burra claimed three wickets without conceding a run and George Champion a further one. A Perks, G L Bennett and E F Hemingway were each sent back without troubling the scorers.

We prevailed by the narrow but sufficient margin of 13 runs, and I count it a thoroughly good day for the club, our opponents having played throughout in the finest sporting spirit.

🗓️ 27 Jun – 03 Jul 1891 · 1891 — 1 match

Sat 27 Jun 1891 🧢 1st XIv HC Hunton CC · AwayformLLWWD

On Saturday last, the 27th of June, we journeyed to Hunton, where we had first use of the ball. Our bowlers found a good length from the outset, and though T Hards played a watchful innings of 24, well supported by C Hobbs with 23, the rest of the home batting could make little headway against us. Dr Smart and J Foord were both dismissed without troubling the scorers, and G Hurn bowled quite beautifully, finishing with six wickets without conceding a run, a performance I do not recall the equal of. F Bowles and H Cole lent capital assistance with two wickets and one apiece, and Hunton were all out.

Our reply did not do justice to the bowling that preceded it. E Fryer batted with great correctness for 21 and carried much of the innings, but too many of our men came and went without scoring, F Danells, S Ruck and I among that unhappy company, and I confess my own contribution amounted to a golden duck, out first ball, which sits ill with a captain's duty to lead by example. We were bowled out in turn and beaten by 26 runs at the close.

There was cause for quiet satisfaction nonetheless, for Hurn's splendid haul carried him past J C Crawford to twenty-fifth place among the club's all-time wicket-takers, and both he and E Fryer now stand at the head of the season's returns, he with the ball and E Fryer with the bat. We return home beaten but by no means downhearted, and shall look to give a fuller account of ourselves against Hunton another day.
