🦆 Duck News — 22–23 September 2007

3 ducks
Week 23 · 22 Sep 2007 – 23 Sep 2007
A grim procession at Sidcup Green this weekend as the Duck Club chalked up three quick additions to the ledger — all in the very same innings, numbers seven through nine marching out and back with barely time to adjust their gloves. The exclusive zero club welcomed its latest instalments in style. 🏏 Saturday — 1st XI vs Sidcup CC Jack Jenner (7) was stumped by Neil Bastin off Seetharam Krishnamurthy — his 3rd duck of the season and 8th for the club from 45 innings, 113th in the all-time list. A card-carrying member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, Jack this week draws level on career ducks with Adam Jackson, who reached the same tally of 8 in 87 innings across 2001–2007. Tim Thirkell (8) followed, caught Thomas Beament bowled David Bastin, for a modest 1st of the season but a chastening 79th of an extraordinary career — 4th on the all-time list from 820 innings, and a proud holder of the Full House (a duck at every position, 1 to 11), plus places on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. Ducking runs deep in that family — father John tops the all-time charts on 90, siblings Nigel and Simon aren't far behind on 57 and 56. This week Tim draws level with Jim Hall on 79 career ducks, Hall having taken 500 innings to get there across 1967–2007. Carl Davis (9) completed the set, caught David Bastin bowled Seetharam Krishnamurthy, for his 5th duck of the season — which is also, handily, his 5th career duck from just 27 innings, 150th on the all-time list. Carl too holds the Retirement Duck honour from a past occasion. On this very day back in 1977, club great John Thirkell was doing much the same thing against West Kent Wanderers CC — some traditions never die 🍩. 🌱 Sunday A quieter affair. The ducks took the day off, or simply couldn't get a touch — no additions to report. 📋 Week in Summary Three ducks from two matches, all condensed into one unfortunate lower-order collapse at Sidcup. Positions 7, 8 and 9 falling in sequence is exactly the kind of consistency the Duck Club exists to celebrate — triumph through adversity, and no shortage of it. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ray Dixon — 5 ducks / 16 innings (31.25%) — reigning Duck of the Year 2007, the crown to beat 2. Chris Newman — 5 ducks / 20 innings (25.0%) 3. Carl Davis — 5 ducks / 23 innings (21.74%) 4. Mark Christmas — 4 ducks / 29 innings (13.79%) 5. Ian Hollingworth — 3 ducks / 5 innings (60.0%) — the rate to fear 0️⃣ 🦆 Same time next week, same green squares — the Duck Club never sleeps. Get out there and give the bowlers something to celebrate 🏏

🦆 Duck News — 15 to 19 September 2007 🦆

4 ducks
Week 22 · 15 Sep 2007 – 19 Sep 2007
A quieter week by Linton's standards but the exclusive zero club still welcomed four new inductees across three fixtures. The art of not scoring continues apace. Saturday 15th — 2nd XI v Marden CC, Kent (h) - Christopher Thirkell, in at four, departed for nought, bowled Gareth Ward. His third duck of the season and 25th of an already storied career, tucking him in at 41st of 1337 in the all-time standings — a 2005 Duck of the Year and a proud member of the Full House nearly-man club, having managed ducks at 10 of the 11 positions. He now sits level on 25 with Rob Doick, who racked up the same tally across 213 innings from 1991 to 2007. With the Thirkell name good for well over 300 combined career ducks between John, Tim, Nigel, Simon and kin, quacking is clearly hereditary in this family. Sunday 16th — Sunday 1st XI v Anchorians CC (h) - Richard Piper, in at five, was bowled by D. Pask for his first duck of the season and 45th overall — 15th on the all-time list, a 2003 Duck of the Year, and a fixture of the Full House, Homebody and Lone Duck top tens. - Not to be outdone from the other end of the card, number eleven Jon Harris fell to Kennett for his second of the season and a frankly ridiculous 89th career duck, keeping him at number 2 of 1337 all time — behind only club royalty. Four separate Duck of the Year titles (1976, 1980, 1981, 1999), a Full House, and shared quacking honours with both Jim Hall and Trevor Back adorn his board. Both men share a parallel: on this very day back in 1962, Richard Ashby also made a duck against Staplehurst CC, en route to 28 career noughts of his own. Wednesday 19th — Midweek XI v Penshurst Park CC (a) - Chris Newman, at seven, was bowled by Ed Hill for his fifth duck of the season and 13th of his career, joining Rob Jackson, who reached the same tally between 2001 and 2006, on the honours board. Newman's season alone is shaping up nicely for all the wrong reasons — see below. Week in Summary Four ducks from three matches, and mercifully no king pairs to report this week. Special mention to the Anchorians fixture, which claimed both ends of the batting order in one afternoon — top five and bottom one, a proper pincer movement of failure. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - Ray Dixon — 5 ducks in 15 innings (33.33%) - Chris Newman — 5 ducks in 19 innings (26.32%) - Carl Davis — 4 ducks in 22 innings (18.18%) - Mark Christmas — 4 ducks in 27 innings (14.81%) - Ian Hollingworth — 3 ducks in 5 innings (60.0%, the club's most efficient failure) Plenty of season left for the standings to reshuffle. See you at the next fixture, where the exclusive zero club never closes its books. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 8th-9th September 2007

1 duck
Week 21 · 8 Sep 2007 – 9 Sep 2007
Three fixtures out on the park this weekend as the season heads into its final furlong, and the ducks were, for once, remarkably shy. Just the one nought recorded all weekend — proof that even the exclusive zero club takes the odd Saturday off. What it lacked in numbers, though, it made up for in class. Saturday 8th September 2nd XI away at Staplehurst CC In at number two, Mike Elworthy fell to Brett McElligott, bowled for a duck. It's the 3rd of his season and the 46th of a genuinely epic Linton career now standing at 474 innings — good enough for 14th on the all-time list and a season-ticket holder's spot on the Tourist Duck top 10, the honours board reserved for those who specialise in blobbing away from home. Fittingly, on this very date in 1981, Richard Ashby — Linton stalwart from 1962 to 1995, and another card-carrying Tourist Duck member alongside a place in the Ducks in a Row club — was doing precisely the same thing against Paul CC. Some traditions really do span the decades. The weekend's other two matches were, disappointingly, entirely fowl-free — all batters got off the mark, which frankly feels like a wasted opportunity this late in the season. Week in Summary Just one duck from three matches — a quiet weekend by the club's usual standards of dubious distinction. Mike Elworthy carries the flag alone this time, but with the season drawing to a close there's still time for the pack to catch up. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ray Dixon — 5 ducks / 14 innings (35.71%) - Chris Newman — 4 ducks / 17 innings (23.53%) - Carl Davis — 4 ducks / 21 innings (19.05%) - Mark Christmas — 4 ducks / 27 innings (14.81%) - Ian Hollingworth — 3 ducks / 5 innings (60.0%) 🏏 — that percentage takes some beating Ian Hollingworth remains the season's most efficient duck harvester by some distance, but with matches still to come, nobody's crown is safe. Get out there next weekend, LPCC — the art of not scoring waits for no one. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 1-2 September 2007

5 ducks
Week 20 · 1 Sep 2007 – 2 Sep 2007
Five ducks in three matches this weekend as the 2007 season heads towards its final furlong — the exclusive zero club welcoming old faces back for fresh dishonours. Saturday 1st XI — away at Shipbourne CC Mark Christmas got the weekend's proceedings under way, lbw to Barry Hart for his 3rd duck of the season and 11th of a career now standing at 114 innings — good enough for 79th on the all-time list, and level pegging with Darren Styles, who reached the same tally in 182 innings across 1989-2007. Mark remains agonisingly one position shy of Full House membership, having now ducked in 10 of the 11 batting slots. Keep an eye on this one — there's more to come from him before the weekend's out. At number 11, Ian Hollingworth edged behind to Mark Agate off Edward Gelling's bowling, his 3rd of the season and 7th career duck from just 25 innings, ranking 124th overall. He joins Jack Jenner, who reached 7 in 44 innings between 2003 and 2007 before bowing out — quite literally — as a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club. Saturday 2nd XI — home to Cobham CC, Kent Gareth Davies fell for a golden-adjacent nought, caught off Michael Boddy for his 2nd duck of the season and 9th of his career in 47 innings, sitting 100th all-time and drawing level with Craig Sands, who took a more leisurely 210 innings to reach the same landmark between 2002 and 2007. Sunday 1st XI — home to Cowdrey CC Ray Dixon set the pace early, bowled by Robert Hood for a 5th duck of the season — comfortably enough to be sitting on the Duck of the Year 2007 board as things stand. It's his 8th career duck in just 23 innings, 115th all-time, level with Adam Jackson's 8 in 87 innings. At a duck every three innings or so, Ray is making the art of not scoring look almost effortless. And there it was — Mark Christmas again, caught Lewis bowled Needham, completing a grim weekend pair: out cheaply on Saturday against Shipbourne and again on Sunday against Cowdrey. It's his 4th duck of the season and 12th of his career, and on this very day back in 1968 Paul Bowles was doing much the same against Bearsted CC on his way to a career haul of 54. Week in summary Five ducks, three matches, one weekend pair — Mark Christmas doing the double honours almost single-handedly. Elsewhere the club's stalwarts kept the tallies ticking over in the traditional fashion: quietly, repeatedly, and without much troubling the scoreboard. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ray Dixon — 14 innings, 5 ducks, 35.71% - Chris Newman — 17 innings, 4 ducks, 23.53% - Carl Davis — 20 innings, 4 ducks, 20.00% - Mark Christmas — 25 innings, 4 ducks, 16.00% - Ian Hollingworth — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60.00% Ray Dixon holds the whip hand for Duck of the Year, but Ian Hollingworth's fearsome 60% strike rate suggests nobody should get comfortable. Next fixture, same opportunity — the ducks await. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 25–26 August 2007

4 ducks
Week 19 · 25 Aug 2007 – 26 Aug 2007
Three fixtures, four ducks, and the Yalding trip turning into something of a group outing for the zero club. Let's get into it. Saturday – 1st XI v Sheldwich CC (h) Matthew Carter went first ball at number 8, bowled by Jon Taylor — a proper golden duck 🍩 to open this week's account. It's his 3rd of the season and 16th of an increasingly distinguished career (93 innings, rank 64 of 1335), a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club and a former Duck of the Year back in 2004. He now sits level on 16 with Clive Newman, who played from 1992 to 2007 across 355 innings — a tally, as it happens, that a certain Newman junior would be chasing just 24 hours later... Sunday – 2nd XI v Yalding CC (h) Yalding proved unusually hospitable to the Duck Club, with three names added to the sheet in the same innings. Christopher Thirkell fell lbw to John-Jacob Britz at first drop, his 2nd duck of the season and 24th of a career now standing at rank 44 of 1335 — a Duck of the Year winner in 2005 and forever propping up the Full House nearly-man board on 10 of 11 positions. Fittingly, on this very day in 1979 against Old Anchorians CC, his own relative Tim Thirkell — part of an extensive Thirkell duck dynasty and no stranger to this list on 78 career ducks — also trudged back for nought. Chris Newman followed at 7, lbw b Fergus Carty, his 4th duck of the season and 12th of his career (rank 76). His parent Clive Newman is a Duck Club stalwart in their own right on exactly 16 career ducks — the very number Matthew Carter drew level with earlier in the weekend. Chris shares that same 1979-vintage echo too, Tim Thirkell keeping company across the decades once again. And at number 11, Jon Harris duly obliged, bowled by John-Jacob Britz, for his 1st duck of the season but a mighty 88th of his career, anchoring him at number 2 on the all-time list, one place off top spot. Harris's honours board reads like a museum wing — Duck of the Year in 1976, 1980, 1981 and 1999, a Full House across every position, top-10 finishes in Homebody, Lone and Tourist Duck, and shared quacking honours with Allan Cooper, Jim Hall and Trevor Back. His parent Richard Harris isn't far behind on 36. And yes — on this day in 1979, Tim Thirkell was there too. Week in summary Four ducks from three matches, three of them landing in the very same Yalding innings — the visitors clearly fancied their chances against anything in a Linton Park shirt. One fixture kept its sheet spotless. Elsewhere, 1979 proved a popular vintage, with Tim Thirkell's Old Anchorians duck earning three separate mentions in one week alone. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏆 - Ray Dixon – 4 ducks / 13 inn (30.77%) - Chris Newman – 4 ducks / 16 inn (25.0%) - Carl Davis – 4 ducks / 19 inn (21.05%) - Matthew Carter – 3 ducks / 9 inn (33.33%) - Kevin Presneill – 3 ducks / 13 inn (23.08%) Chris Newman's latest at Yalding keeps him locked in 2nd place — watch this space for the season's crown. That's the lot from 25–26 August. Next fixture, fresh victims — the zero club never sleeps. See you at the crease 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Saturday 18 August 2007

1 duck
Week 18 · 18 Aug 2007
Two fixtures out on the park this weekend and, remarkably, only one member of the Linton faithful cared to make the trudge back to the pavilion without troubling the scorer. Quality over quantity, evidently. 🏏 2nd XI v Headcorn CC Battling away at number six, Simon Thirkell fell caught Jordan Vine bowled Peter West to register the solitary duck of the round. Nothing dramatic in the manner of it, but plenty in the maths behind it — this is Simon's 2nd duck of the 2007 campaign and his 56th for the club in 242 innings, a tally that keeps him lodged at a lofty 8th of 1,335 in the all-time standings. Simon arrives already garlanded. He's a three-time Duck of the Year champion (1983, 2001 and 2006), sits proudly among the "50 Before a 50" set having reached his half-century of noughts on a high score of just 48, and appears on both the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens — the honours board equivalent of a well-stamped passport, mostly stamped in disappointment. His 2006 season also earns him a Runaway Duck citation — a whole summer without so much as troubling the scoreboard. Reputation, it turns out, is not always a good thing. It should also be noted that ducking is something of a Thirkell family trade. Kin John (90 career ducks), Tim (78) and Nigel (57) are all fully paid-up members of the zero club in their own right, with Christopher, Daniel, Tom and Stuart rounding out a dynasty that could field its own team of noughts. Simon didn't need the company today, but he'd certainly have it. Fittingly, on this very date back in 2004, Rob Jackson also strolled back for nought, against Tunbridge Wells Wednesday CC — a small act of calendar solidarity across the years. 📋 Week in Summary One duck from two matches is a quiet return by Linton's usual standards — the other fixture got through entirely unscathed, batsmen apparently on their best behaviour. Make the most of the peace, because the Duck Club rarely stays quiet for long. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Ray Dixon — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.3%) - Carl Davis — 4 ducks in 19 innings (21.1%) - Kevin Presneill — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) - Thomas Beeney — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) - Chris Newman — 3 ducks in 15 innings (20.0%) Ray Dixon holds top spot for now, but with a chasing pack this tightly packed, next weekend's fixtures are sure to shuffle the order. Get out there, get your padding on, and give the selectors — and the Duck Club scorers — something to talk about. 🍩0️⃣🏏

This one marks a shared gift for getting out for nothing, six years and a change of opposition apart. 📅 Week in Summary Two matches, one duck — a rare week of restraint from the LPCC top order, and Mike Elworthy stood alone in carrying the torch of ignominy. He now draws level with Carl Davis, who reached the same tally in 23 innings between 1994 and 2007 before bowing out, fittingly, on a duck of his own. The Week in Summary Five ducks from seven matches — a modest harvest, but a spread one, taking in three different grounds and both ends of the experience spectrum, from Craig Sands' relatively tender 208 innings to Trevor Back's monumental 578. No pairs, no king pairs and no family affairs this week — the ducks kept themselves strictly to themselves. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Ray Dixon — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) - Carl Davis — 4 ducks in 19 innings (21.05%) - Kevin Presneill — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) - Thomas Beeney — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) - Trevor Back — 3 ducks in 12 innings (25.0%) Ray Dixon holds a slender lead, but Kevin Presneill's fresh nought this week keeps him within striking distance.
