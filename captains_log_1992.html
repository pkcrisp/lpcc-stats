🗓️ 10 Oct – 16 Oct 1992 · 1992 — 1 match

Sun 11 Oct 1992 🧢 1st XIv TC Tenterden CC · AwayformWDWWW

Called it right at the toss on a bright Sunday down at Tenterden, and with a pitch that looked made for batting I didn't think twice — we'd bat first and worry about the consequences later, in the traditional captain's fashion.

And for once the plan actually worked. Clive Newman played the innings of the day, unbeaten on a well-crafted 54, while Paul Bowles chipped in handsomely with 45. I managed 40 myself, which I mention here once and shall resist the urge to bring up again over tea. The foundation was a flying start — 70 on the board before Tenterden so much as troubled us, built on a first-wicket stand worth 70 — and we finished a healthy 169 for 3 from our 39 overs, the sort of total that lets a captain sleep easy at the interval.

Sleep, as it turned out, was misplaced. Tenterden's P Knight decided 87 not out sounded like a nice round number and set about making it happen, well supported by John Oliver's 21 and Spencer Gray's unbeaten 20, with a fifth-wicket stand of 60 doing the real damage. Rob Doick was our pick with 2 for 36, Ian Brattle chipped in with 2 for 45, and Alan Boyns bowled tight as ever — 1 for 29 with two maidens to show for his parsimony — but it wasn't quite enough, Tenterden getting home for the loss of four wickets.

Not our day, then. Heads up, chins high, and back to the nets.

Sun 20 Sep 1992 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDWDWW

Sunday 20 September saw Anchorians CC visit Linton Park, and with the coin obliging I had no hesitation in taking first use of what looked a decent strip. Dave Charlton set about proving me right, cruising past his fifty to finish on a splendid 78, while David Castle's 37 gave the innings real backbone. I chipped in with 25 as part of a 91-run stand for the fourth wicket that took the sting out of any early jitters, and we closed on a healthy 215/9 from our 37 overs — the sort of total that lets a captain enjoy his tea rather than gnaw at the biscuits.

Anchorians' reply never quite found its rhythm. An early wobble to 1-1, with D Roberts back in the pavilion for a golden duck, set the tone, and though J. Bacon battled to 34 with support from Keith Wooding (27) and D Stubbs (25), the innings had the feel of a side chasing shadows. Trevor Back was the chief tormentor with a fine 4-40, Rob Doick bowled with real discipline for 2-23 including three maidens, and Jon Harris weighed in with 2-27 as Anchorians folded for 151 in 40.2 overs.

A comfortable 64-run win, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round — bat, ball and the tea urn all pulling their weight. Onwards we go.

I'll be right back with the report.

A draw, then — honest reward for honest toil, and with Mike Elworthy still setting the pace atop the season's run charts and me happily lodged second among the wicket-takers, there's no complaint from this captain's chair.

Sat 12 Sep 1992 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · AwayformDDWDW

Nigel Thirkell's Match Report — Saturday 12th September 1992 Linton Park CC v Southborough CC (away)

Well, the toss went against us again — I really must have a word with whoever's in charge of coin karma at this club — and Southborough elected to bat, no doubt fancying their chances on what looked a decent surface. As it turned out, they'd have been better off chasing. Matt Large was in a spinning mood, twisting through their order for a magnificent 5-26, ably backed up by Paul Gibson, whose 4-18 (three of them maidens — not a run given away lightly) squeezed the life out of any partnership before it could breathe. I chipped in myself with 1-12, which I shall modestly describe as "tidy" and leave it at that. From 52-4 they slid to 52-7, and by the time the dust settled they were all out for a distinctly gettable 87 in 31.4 overs, Ray Grayland's 20 the only real resistance. Danny Coombs and Robert "Bob" Steadman departed without troubling the scorers, which is one way to keep an innings brisk.

Chasing 88, we made light work of it, getting home by 8 wickets in 21.4 overs. I found some form with the bat, seeing us home unbeaten on 50 — a personal contribution I was rather satisfied with, and one I shall enjoy remembering longer than is strictly dignified. Elsewhere, Brian Piper's useful 15 nudged him past Tim Thirkell into 5th on the club's all-time run charts, while Matt Large's five-for lifted him above Alan Wilkes into 4th on the all-time wicket list — a fine day for the record books as well as the result.

All told: a professional bowling display, a comfortable chase, and two names etched a little higher into club history. Verdict: routine on the scorecard, thoroughly satisfying in the clubhouse afterwards.

Chasing that lot, we lost an opener for just 6 and I confess a few of us reached for the biscuit tin in resignation. But Nigel Thirkell, having the game of his season, played an innings of real class — 97, agonisingly shy of the century he thoroughly deserved — and found a willing ally in Mark Stevenson, unbeaten on 36, the pair adding 95 for the third wicket to drag us back into contention. Poor Rob Doick contributed his customary golden duck, bless him, but by then the platform was well and truly laid, and we finished a creditable 168/3 from 30.4 overs.

Honours shared, then, and on this evidence entirely fair — Beacon had the runs, we had the momentum, and neither side quite had the minutes to finish the job. Nigel Thirkell continues to lead our run and wicket charts this tour, and long may it continue.
