🦆 Duck Report — 23-24 September 2006 🦆

5 ducks
Week 23 · 23 Sep 2006 – 24 Sep 2006
A weekend of grim entertainment as Sidcup CC arrived at Linton Park and promptly dismantled the top and middle order for nought, nought, and nought again. Five ducks in one innings — the zero club had a proper feast. Saturday 23rd — 1st XI v Sidcup CC (h) Tom Murphy fell for his 2nd of the season, caught Phil Robinson bowled Alex McCawley at first drop, and moves to 2 career ducks in 12 innings, 331st in the all-time list. He draws level with Glen Rawson, who reached the same tally in 11 innings across 2005-2006 — kindred spirits in the art of not scoring. Daniel McCarthy followed at four, caught Stephen Shanks bowled Rob Solway, his 3rd of the season and 3rd of his career from 18 innings, 198th all time. On this very day in 1972, Tim Thirkell also strolled back for nought against West Farleigh CC — good company, even if Tim went on to rack up 78 career ducks and a hatful of Duck of the Year titles. Ron McCormick was run out unsure at six for his 1st duck of the season, taking his career tally to 3 from a marathon 125 innings, 235th on the all-time list. Ron is already a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a previous sign-off — evidently the retirement didn't stick, and neither did his bat today. He shares today's on-this-day company with Tim Thirkell's 1972 effort too. Jim Hall, ever the club statesman, holed out caught and bowled Phil Robinson at number eight for his 3rd duck of the season, extending his imperious career tally to 79 from 494 innings — 3rd in the entire 1331-strong all-time list. His honours board is a small library: three Duck of the Year titles (1970, 1977, 1985), Full House nearly-man, Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top ten memberships, and fittingly a Quacking Together entry with none other than Tim Thirkell — who obliges again as today's on-this-day parallel. Tim Wright closed out the collapse at nine, caught Barry Patey bowled Phil Robinson, his 1st duck of the season and career from 12 innings, 609th all time. He joins - Howard on the same tally, a 2006 debutant who managed the rare feat of One and Done and a Retirement Duck in a single innings. The numbers Two matches, five ducks, all of them Sidcup's doing — Phil Robinson alone had a hand in three of the five dismissals. One innings, one bowling attack, one thoroughly bad afternoon for the Linton Park top and middle order. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks from 3 innings (100%) 2. Paul Webber — 3 from 5 (60%) 3. Ray Dixon — 3 from 8 (37.5%) 4. Jim Hall — 3 from 9 (33.33%) 5. Thomas Beeney — 3 from 9 (33.33%) Simon Thirkell sits on a perfect record so far — every innings a duck. Can anyone catch him before the season's out? 🍩 See you next fixture, when the zero club will no doubt be recruiting again. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 16 to 20 September 2006

3 ducks
Week 22 · 16 Sep 2006 – 20 Sep 2006
A quiet week by Linton standards — just two matches and three ducks — but quality over quantity, and this week's crop included a proper double. Saturday 16th — 1st XI v Holborough Anchorians CC Jim Hall departed at number 8, caught Gary Hawkins bowled Joe Bowles, for his 1st duck of the season. It's his 77th in senior colours, keeping him at a lofty 3rd of 1331 in the all-time standings — the exclusive zero club's senior common room, propped up by a mantelpiece of Duck of the Year honours (1970, 1977, 1985) and Only Two Quacks partnerships with Allan Cooper and Nigel Thirkell. On this very day in 1962, Richard Ashby (1962–1995, 28 career ducks) was doing exactly the same thing against Staplehurst CC. Wednesday 20th — Midweek XI v Penshurst Park CC Jim wasn't done. Bowled by David Smith at number 8, he collected his 2nd duck of the week — and the season — completing a grim pair either side of the weekend, moving to 78 career ducks and holding station at 3rd all-time. Consistency of a very particular kind. On this day in 1997, Daniel Thirkell (1993–2006) was similarly dismissed against Borden CC. Also falling in the same Penshurst fixture was Stuart Cheesman, caught Mike Smith bowled Geoff Ray at number 5 — his 1st duck of the season and 39th of a career that already boasts the rare Full House honour, a duck at every position 1 to 11. A paid-up Debutante Duck club member and two-time Duck of the Year (1994, 1996), Stuart joins Stewart Morrison (1987–2006, 354 innings) on exactly 39 — kindred spirits in the art of not scoring. A chip off the old block too, with father Fred Cheesman (42 career ducks) and brother Steve (14) both fellow members of the family firm. Week in summary Three ducks from two matches, and Jim Hall doing the work of two batsmen entirely on his own — a duck at both ends of the week is exactly the sort of triumph through adversity this club was built to celebrate. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks in 3 innings (100%) - Paul Webber — 3 in 5 (60%) - Ray Dixon — 3 in 8 (37.5%) - Thomas Beeney — 3 in 9 (33.33%) - Craig Sands — 3 in 43 (6.98%) Simon Thirkell sits on a flawless record so far this season — nobody else comes close to that particular consistency. Whether anyone can dislodge him before the campaign closes remains to be seen. Next fixture, next opportunity. The zero club never rests. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 LPCC Duck Report — 9th to 10th September 2006 🦆

6 ducks
Week 21 · 9 Sep 2006 – 10 Sep 2006
Six ducks across three matches to close out the campaign, and the club's most exclusive society welcomed a fresh face while an old firm carried on regardless. 🏏 2nd XI — Horton Kirby CC (away) Richard Piper opened the innings and, true to Full House form, opened his account for nought instead — caught behind off Dane Bull without troubling the scorers. His 3rd of the season nudges him to 44 career ducks, holding steady at 14th on the all-time table, a name that already adorns the Duck of the Year 2003 board plus the Full House, Homebody and Lone Duck top tens — a proper collector's set. On this very day back in 2000, Daniel Thirkell (1993–2006) was performing the exact same ceremony against Bearsted CC. Some traditions simply run in the Linton soil. 🌱 Sunday 1st XI — Anchorians CC (home) A bumper afternoon of scoreless artistry at home to the Anchorians, five ducks in one innings — nearly a full hand of nought. Mike Elworthy went second ball, caught behind off Dandridge, his 2nd of the season and 43rd of a long career — 15th on the all-time list and level with Mike Iliffe (1966–1987), who reached the same 43 in a mere 314 innings. On this day in 1955, John --'s Taylor was doing the same against Old Roffensians. At three, - Howard made an instant and emphatic arrival: bowled by Dandridge for his very first senior duck. A genuine debut, and a rare treble of honours in one visit to the crease — Debutante Duck, One and Done 2006, and Retirement Duck all struck off with a single ball. Whether he returns to trouble the bowlers again remains, delightfully, unwritten. He joins Graham Walker (1998–2006) on exactly 1 career duck. Peter Davies followed at six, caught off James Hockley, his 2nd duck of the season and of his career alike from just 14 innings — level with Daniel McCarthy, another 2006 arrival sitting on the same modest tally. Then came the afternoon's headline act. Michael Beeney fell for nought at seven, caught off Andy Tutt, his 2nd of the season and 23rd career duck, drawing level with Brian Piper (1973–2006) on the all-time list. Two batters later his son Thomas Beeney followed him back, bowled by J- Bacon, his 3rd of the season and 4th career duck. A parent and child both back in the pavilion without scoring in the very same innings — a proper Duck and Ducklings occasion, the rarest ceremony the club offers, and one for the family scrapbook alongside the ordinary scorebook. Thomas joins Ian Hollingworth (2004–2006) on 4 career ducks, and carries his own Debutante Duck and Ducks on the Road honours besides. 📊 Week in Summary Six ducks, three matches, one glorious family double act, and a brand new name etched onto the Debutante board. The Beeneys did more for the club's father-and-child statistics in one Sunday than most manage in a season. No golden ducks recorded — everyone at least troubled the bowling before the inevitable arrived. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Simon Thirkell — 3 innings, 3 ducks, 100% - Paul Webber — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60% - Ray Dixon — 8 innings, 3 ducks, 37.5% - Thomas Beeney — 9 innings, 3 ducks, 33.33% - Craig Sands — 41 innings, 3 ducks, 7.32% Simon Thirkell sits ominously on a perfect record atop the table, but with Thomas Beeney's family exploits fresh in the memory, don't rule out a late surge from the Beeney household. 🏏 Same time next fixture, ladies and gentlemen — the square awaits, and so does the zero. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — 2–3 September 2006 🦆

3 ducks
Week 20 · 2 Sep 2006 – 3 Sep 2006
A quiet couple of days on the fixture list — just two matches — but the zero club still found three fresh members to welcome into the fold. Saturday 2nd XI — away at Bearsted CC Simon Thirkell fell c & b Doug Reid at number six, his 3rd duck of the season and a staggering 54th of a career that now sits 9th on the all-time list of 1329. Simon is already a two-time Duck of the Year (1983, 2001) and — rather alarmingly — a fully paid-up member of the Duck of the Year 2006 club, having also claimed the dubious Runaway Duck honour for an entire season spent without troubling the scorers. A place on the 50 Before a 50 board (59 ducks, high score 48) rounds out a CV built almost entirely on triumph through adversity. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1968, Paul Bowles — later to retire on 54 ducks of his own (rank 8) — also strolled back for nought against these very same Bearsted CC, proof that this fixture has ducks written into its soil. Sunday 2nd XI — home to Cowdrey CC Sebastian Lewis enjoyed himself, claiming a brace for the zero club in the same innings. Paul Webber, batting seven, was caught (umpire understandably Unsure who by) for his 3rd duck of the season — and, from a mere 5 career innings, his 3rd career duck too. An eye-watering 60% strike rate for ignominy, and a paid-up Debutante Duck club member from his very first knock. On this day in 1972, Benny Bell (1966–1975) was making precisely the same journey back to the pavilion against Staplehurst CC. Thomas Beeney followed him at nine, caught Robert Hood off the same Sebastian Lewis, his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd of a 14-innings career. Thomas already holds a spot on the Ducks on the Road top 10 and, like Webber above, joins Benny Bell's 1972 vintage in spirit. A chip off the old block too — his father Michael Beeney carries 22 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring may well be hereditary. Week in summary Three ducks, two matches, one very productive spell from Sebastian Lewis. No pairs, no golden ducks, no family affairs this week — just steady, dependable service from the regulars. Simon Thirkell's season-long drought, however, is fast becoming the story of 2006. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 3 innings, 3 ducks, 100% 2. Paul Webber — 5 innings, 3 ducks, 60% 3. Ray Dixon — 8 innings, 3 ducks, 37.5% 4. Craig Sands — 39 innings, 3 ducks, 7.69% 5. Iain Rawson — 7 innings, 2 ducks, 28.57% Simon Thirkell sits imperious atop a perfect record, with Paul Webber snapping at his heels after this weekend's exploits. The rest of the club will need to pull their fingers out — or, ideally, pull their bats in — if they hope to catch them before season's end. 🏏 Next fixture beckons. Pads on, guard taken, and no doubt a fresh crop of noughts waiting to be harvested. See you all out there. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck Report — Sunday 27 August 2006

1 duck
Week 19 · 27 Aug 2006
A quiet weekend for the collectors, but the zero club never sleeps entirely, and one name was added to this week's roll of dubious honour. 🏏 Sunday 2nd XI — away to Edenbridge CC Tom Murphy, going in at first wicket down, was undone by Howard Johnson and trudged off having troubled the scorers not one jot — bowled, and back in the hutch. A tidy, uneventful sort of duck, no golden ignominy attached, just the plain honest failure to score that the club holds so dear. It's Tom's 1st duck of the 2026 season and the 1st of his LPCC career, taking him to a single nought from 10 innings and slotting him in at 607th of 1329 in the all-time standings — a debut entry on the list of dubious distinction, and welcome to it. He arrives sitting on exactly the same tally as Graham Walker, who plied his trade for the club 1998–2006 and also carries one career duck, albeit spread more thinly across 59 innings — Tom finding his nought rather more efficiently, for whatever that's worth. Walker sits 458th overall; Tom will hope to keep him company rather than overtake him any time soon. 📊 Week in summary One match, one duck, one new face on the honours ledger. Ten other Sunday 2nd XI batters presumably got on with the actual business of scoring runs, spoiling what might otherwise have been a fuller harvest. Still, quality over quantity — the art of not scoring only needs one committed practitioner per week to keep the tradition alive. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Ray Dixon — 3 ducks / 7 innings (43%) 2. Craig Sands — 3 ducks / 38 innings (8%) 3. Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks / 2 innings (100%) 🍩 4. Paul Webber — 2 ducks / 4 innings (50%) 5. 🦆 Duck News — 19–23 August 2006

4 ducks
Week 18 · 19 Aug 2006 – 23 Aug 2006
Only five matches on the go this week, but the Duck Club still found time to induct new members and give one man a rather unwanted double. Four ducks recorded across the period — read on. 🏏 Saturday 19th — 2nd XI v Matfield Gareth Davies departed for nought, bowled by Knott from number seven. It's Gareth's 1st duck of the season and 7th of his career, sliding him to 122nd of 1,329 in the all-time roll call — level pegging with Craig Sands, who compiled his seven across a rather more leisurely 176 innings between 2002 and 2006. Efficiency, as ever, is relative. Lower down the order, Iain Rawson edged Danby off G Petty for his maiden LPCC duck — 1st of the season, 1st of the career, from just five innings so far. He joins Graham Walker (1998–2006, 59 innings) on that tally, and can console himself that his sibling Glen Rawson already has two of his own — clearly a family with an eye for the art of not scoring. 🌱 Sunday 20th — Sunday 1st XI v Chelsfield Park CC Iain Rawson wasn't finished. A day later, and just as ominously, he was bowled by Miles Walker without scoring again — his 2nd duck of the weekend and of the season, doubling his career tally to two in five innings flat. A proper pair, and Iain vaults straight into this season's top five as a result (see below). On this very day back in 1944, W Bunting was doing much the same thing against KCC Maidstone — some traditions never die. Joining him in ignominy was Michael Beeney, caught Hilary Breeze bowled Miles Walker for his 1st duck of the season and 22nd of an increasingly decorated career, holding at a lofty 50th of 1,329 all time. He draws level with Christopher Thirkell (1998–2006, 121 innings), a fellow Duck of the Year 2005 honouree. Michael arrives with pedigree too — a paid-up member of the Diamond Duck club since 2002, one half of Only Two Quacks with Ian Brattle, and a name that will one day, presumptively, grace the Retirement Duck board. His relative Thomas Beeney already carries two career ducks of his own, so the talent, such as it is, clearly runs in the blood. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from five matches, but really only three victims — Iain Rawson generously did the work of two all by himself. A quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, but a memorable one for the Rawson household. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Ray Dixon — 3 ducks in 7 innings (42.9%) 2. Craig Sands — 3 ducks in 38 innings (7.9%) 3. Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks in 2 innings (100%) 4. Paul Webber — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50%) 5. Iain Rawson — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40%) Iain's weekend heroics fire him straight into 5th spot — one more quiet failure and he'll be knocking on the door of top spot. The season race remains wide open, and with more fixtures to come, the exclusive zero club awaits its next intake. See you all next weekend — someone has to keep the tradition alive. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 5th to 8th August 2006 🦆

6 ducks
Week 16 · 5 Aug 2006 – 8 Aug 2006
A tidy little haul this week: six ducks scattered across five matches, spanning three sides and a fair spread of misfortune. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees below. Saturday 5th August 1st XI, away at Eynsford CC: Richard Piper fell at number seven, caught Jamie Wise bowled Ian Playfoot, for his 2nd duck of the season and 43rd of an already stately career — good enough for 15th on the all-time chart. Richard's honours shelf groans under Duck of the Year 2003, places in both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens (ducking away from home is one thing, doing it at home too is dedication), and a completed Full House — a duck at every position, 1 through 11. On this very day in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden was performing the same ceremony against Rochester CC, en route to 10 career ducks and a Duck of the Year of his own. 2nd XI, at home to West Farleigh CC: a soft-boiled batting card. Russell Baldwin, caught Mick Goodrich bowled Wyn Barnardo, moved to 18 career ducks (58th all-time), drawing level with Dave Todd, who took 225 innings across 1978-2006 to reach the same total. Peter Davies followed shortly after, caught Jamie Webb bowled John Scott, for a maiden LPCC duck — his 1st in 11 innings — and finds instant kinship with Graham Walker, similarly stuck on one from a rather longer 59-innings apprenticeship, 1998-2006. Midweek Monday's fixture against The Dutch Uncles Team claimed Brian Piper, caught B- Winters bowled R Weyl, for his 23rd career duck — 44th all-time, and, pleasingly for the family scrapbook, just one place and one duck adrift of his own son Richard, busy at Eynsford two days prior. The Piper name runs deep in this society, with siblings and offspring David (7) and James (4) keeping the tradition alive. Brian's effort echoes Colin Dicker, who made an identical 23 look effortless against Headcorn CC on this same date in 1976, banking two Duck of the Year titles along the way. Tuesday's visit from Bexley CC was unkind to the Midweek middle order. Daniel McCarthy, lbw John Taylor at first drop, notched his 2nd duck of the season and of his career alike, joining Glen Rawson on that same modest tally. Matthew Carter followed, run out for reasons the scorebook simply shrugs at — "Unsure" — his 13th career duck, 71st all-time, level with Rob Jackson's 65-innings effort from 2001. Both are proud, paid-up members of the Debutante Duck club, each having opened their LPCC account with a blob. Week in summary Six ducks, five matches, no repeat offenders this time — nobody doubled up, no pairs, no kings. A quietly efficient week for the collectors. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Ray Dixon — 3 ducks in 6 innings (50%) 2. Craig Sands — 3 ducks in 34 innings (8.82%) 3. Simon Thirkell — 2 ducks in 2 innings (100%) 4. Paul Webber — 2 ducks in 4 innings (50%) 5. Ollie Howarth — 2 ducks in 5 innings (40%) Simon Thirkell's spotless 100% remains the season's most terrifying statistic — not a single run troubling the scorers yet. The willow stays stubbornly unblooded for plenty more. Next fixture, same invitation stands: middle one, or join the club. 🦆🏏0️⃣

5 ducks
Week 15 · 29 Jul 2006 – 2 Aug 2006
A tidy little haul this week: five matches, five ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees with the usual mixture of sympathy and ill-disguised glee. Saturday — 1st XI v Mersham Sports CC Glen Rawson got the weekend's proceedings under way at number 7, caught Darren Pugh bowled Kevin Brown. His 1st duck of the season and 1st of his career from 6 innings, ranking him 274th of 1329 in the all-time records — he joins Daniel McCarthy, whose own lone 2006 duck arrived in 11 innings. Little did Glen know, this was merely the opening act. Sunday — 1st XI v Downham & Bellingham CC Glen Rawson was back for more, top-edging his way to a second duck in as many days at number 2, caught J Pretorius bowled David Powell. Season tally 2, career tally 2 — and with that, Glen completes a grim weekend pair, out but not golden in either innings, a fine early example of consistency of a very particular kind. Ray Dixon fell next, also caught by J Pretorius, this time off Keith McCann, at first drop. His 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career from just 7 innings puts him 227th on the all-time list. On this very day in 1997, Fred Cheesman was making a duck of his own against the touring Ipswich Greyhounds — Fred went on to bank 42 career ducks, two Duck of the Year titles and a Full House along the way, so Ray keeps distinguished company. Sunday — 2nd XI v Wilmington CC Ollie Howarth was stumped by E Greenhalgh off R Rampersad at number 6, his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd career nought from 12 innings, 223rd of 1329 all-time — and, coincidentally, another member of the 1997 on-this-day club alongside Fred Cheesman. And at number 11, a genuine heavyweight of the art of not scoring: Jon Harris, caught off T Cotton by a scorer who clearly gave up trying to identify the fielder. This was only his 1st duck of the season, but his 87th career duck overall, from a staggering 635 innings — good enough for 2nd place on the entire 1329-strong all-time list. Jon's honours board reads like a museum wing: Duck of the Year in 1976, 1980, 1981 and 1999, a Full House, top-ten residency in the Lone, Homebody and Tourist Duck charts, Only Two Quacks with Allan Cooper, and Quacking Together with both Jim Hall and Trevor Back. He's also a Debutante Duck from way back. Fittingly, he keeps it in the family — son Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own, a proper chip off the old block. Week in summary Five ducks from five matches, with Sunday doing the heavy lifting — two apiece for the 1st and 2nd XIs after Saturday's opening salvo. Special mention to Glen Rawson for turning up twice to collect the same prize, and to Jon Harris for reminding everyone that true greatness in this club is measured in noughts, not runs. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Ray Dixon — 6 innings, 3 ducks, 50.0% 2. Craig Sands — 31 innings, 3 ducks, 9.68% 3. Simon Thirkell — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100.0% 4. Paul Webber — 4 innings, 2 ducks, 50.0% 5. Glen Rawson — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40.0% Ray Dixon holds a narrow lead, but with Glen Rawson's pair fresh in the memory and the season far from over, this table has plenty of scope for reshuffling yet. 🏏 Next fixture looms, and with it, fresh opportunities for glory-in-reverse. Bats at the ready, ducks at the crease — same time, same channel, next week. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 22–26 July 2006

4 ducks
Week 14 · 22 Jul 2006 – 26 Jul 2006
