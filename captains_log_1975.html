Sun 21 Sep 1975 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWLWDW

John Thirkell's Match Report — 21 September 1975 v Anchorians CC

Won the toss, elected to bat — the right call, I felt, and I'll stand by it in print even after what followed. We limped to 93 all out in 37.5 overs, a total that owed rather more to grit than grace. The scorebook makes for uncomfortable reading in places: three of us — Jon Harris, Tony Gauna and, I confess, yours truly — walked back for ducks, and I shall draw the aforementioned veil over my own contribution beyond noting it was rather shorter than I'd have liked.

But cricket, mercifully, has two innings, and ours came with ball in hand. Nigel Thirkell was simply magnificent, tearing through the Anchorians order for 5-25 with six miserly maidens thrown in for good measure — the sort of spell that makes a captain's job absurdly easy. Ron Hickman (4-20) and Tony Gauna (1-17) mopped up admirably, L. Neate departed without troubling the scorers, and the visitors were bowled out for 89 in 38.2 overs.

Two milestones to toast in the clubhouse: Alan Cradduck brought up his 100th appearance in Linton colours, a thoroughly deserved landmark, while Paul Brattle's 18 runs eased him past Maurice Fenner into 13th on the all-time run-scorers' list. And Nigel Thirkell, still leading both the 1975 run and wicket charts, continues to make the rest of us look faintly ordinary.

Four runs. That's all it was. A nervy, scrappy, thoroughly earned two points — the sort of win that reminds you why this daft game is worth turning up for.

🗓️ 13 Sep – 19 Sep 1975 · 1975 — 1 match

Sat 13 Sep 1975 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · HomeformWLWLW

Saturday the thirteenth of September found Southborough CC visiting Linton Park, and with the coin landing kindly I had no hesitation in sending us in first — a decision that, with the benefit of hindsight, looked rather braver in the morning than it did by teatime.

Paul Bowles played the innings of the day, an excellent 81 that formed the spine of our 168 for 8 off 44 overs, with John Ames chipping in a tidy 20 to keep things ticking. Tim Thirkell wasn't so fortunate, departing for a golden duck, and I can offer no better account of myself — I too walked back without troubling the scorers, out first ball. A captain, they say, must lead by example; on this occasion I led rather too literally back to the pavilion, bat still gleaming and pads barely scuffed.

With the total posted, we turned to defending it, and Southborough made steady if unhurried progress to 11 without loss from their seven overs before the elements or the clock — or perhaps simple club cricket good sense — brought proceedings to a close. Honours shared, then, a draw when neither side could quite force the issue.

Elsewhere in the season's ledger, Nigel Thirkell continues to lead our 1975 run charts with a splendid 619 to his name — proof, if any were needed, that not every Thirkell in this fixture book collects ducks. All told, a fair and gentle afternoon's cricket: nobody bowled out, nobody's pride entirely intact, and everybody home in time for tea.

Sat 06 Sep 1975 🧢 1st XIv OC Old Anchorians CC · HomeformDDWLW

Home defeat to Old Anchorians by seven wickets, September the 6th, 1975, and if there's a moral in it I haven't found it yet.

Winning the toss and choosing to bat felt like the right call at the time — I still think it was, for what that's worth. We were bowled out for 119 in 38.4 overs, which on a good pitch is the sort of total that makes you want to check your watch and wonder where the afternoon went. Young Nigel top-scored gamely with 33, and honourable mention must go to Colin Dicker, who contrived a duck of such quiet dignity that the whole clubhouse observed a respectful silence before getting on with the tea.

In the field we did our best to make a match of it. Nigel, having batted so nicely, then turned his arm over to remarkable effect — 2 for 48 and five maidens, not a run given away that he didn't have to. Brian Fuller chipped in with 1 for 9, tidy as you like, and I managed my own small contribution with bat and ball along the way, for whatever comfort that provided. But P. Francis was in no mood to be denied, cruising to an unbeaten 58, well supported by K. Hewitt's 30, and Old Anchorians eased home at 120 for 3.

Seven wickets is seven wickets — no dressing it up. Heads up, though, and back to the nets; Nigel's form, with 578 runs and 83 wickets to his name this season, remains the one thing keeping our spirits from the boundary rope entirely.

Sun 31 Aug 1975 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformWDDWL

Skipper's Log — Sunday 31 August 1975. We travelled down to Staplehurst, lost the toss, and found ourselves out in the field before the tea urn had even had a chance to warm up. Not the start I'd have chosen, but there are worse ways to spend a Sunday than fielding under a decent sky.

Staplehurst posted 149 all out in 43.2 overs, B Thurlow (31) and C Rogers (29) doing most of the damage, with Nigel Bardsley chipping in 23. But three of theirs went for ducks at our hands, and I found some real turn to finish with 5-18 off my three maidens — the sort of afternoon a spinner dreams about on the bus home. Nigel Thirkell (2-43) and Brian Piper (2-47) kept the pressure on from the other end, and 149 always felt a chaseable total on that pitch.

So it proved. We knocked off the runs for the loss of three wickets in 38.1 overs, with Robert Chance (30) and Paul Brattle (20) playing their part, while I was fortunate enough to bat right through for 97 not out — agonisingly close to the ton, but I shan't complain about a personal double of note. Spare a thought for Jim Hall behind the stumps, who had a torrid time of it with 20 byes going begging.

A nice footnote: Chance's 30 nudges him past Mike Iliffe into 16th on the club's all-time run list. All told, a thoroughly good day for Linton Park — seven-wicket win, bowling and batting both firing, and the perfect tonic for the drive home.

From the captain's chair, Wednesday 27 August 1975: we welcomed Gravesend CC, and when the coin fell my way there was only ever one decision to make. Batting looked the sensible course on the morning; whether it remained so by teatime is, as ever, a matter for the reader to judge.

Eight runs the margin, two points banked, and Nigel's nine wickets nudge him past Tony Snell into 11th on the club's all-time list. A thoroughly deserved win, snatched from what looked, at tea, like a moderate total.
