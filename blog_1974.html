🦆 Duck News — 22 September 1974

0 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1974
A quiet one, quackers. One solitary fixture took to the field this weekend and, remarkably, every single Linton bat found something more useful to do than nick off for nought. Eleven players in, eleven players surviving the indignity — the exclusive zero club held no induction ceremony this week. 🏏 The Match No ducks recorded. Not a sniff, not a golden nought, not so much as a nervous single turned into an LBW shout. Whatever demons usually stalk the Linton top order clearly had the weekend off — possibly attending a rival club's fixture instead, the ungrateful things. It happens. Even a club with this proud a tradition of not troubling the scorer has the occasional off-week for its off-form. Consider it a well-earned rest for the honours board, which can get back to accumulating names again next time out. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — 1974 The chase for Duck of the Year rages on regardless, and this lot are doing sterling work to keep the tradition alive even while the rest of the club behaved itself: - Alan Cradduck — 7 ducks in 17 innings (41.18%) — comfortably out in front, and setting a pace of ignominy that will take some catching - Tony Gauna — 5 ducks in 14 innings (35.71%) — the closest challenger, one bad afternoon from levelling matters - Benny Bell — 5 ducks in 15 innings (33.33%) — quietly keeping pace in the shadows - Allan Cooper — 5 ducks in 16 innings (31.25%) — level on ducks, trailing on rate, still very much in the hunt - Colin Dicker — 5 ducks in 17 innings (29.41%) — rounding out a top five that between them have turned "the art of not scoring" into something approaching a science With five men level on five-or-more ducks apiece, this season's title race is shaping up to be a proper scrap for the wooden spoon. Cradduck holds the whip hand for now, but a club that can produce a blank weekend like this one is more than capable of a sudden late flurry from anyone on that list. 📅 Next Time Out Normal service — or rather, abnormal service — will surely resume before long. The ducks evaded capture this week, but they're out there somewhere, and Linton's top order has never been one to disappoint for long. See you at the crease. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 14–15 September 1974

0 ducks
Week 21 · 14 Sep 1974 – 15 Sep 1974
A rare and troubling dispatch from HQ this week: two matches played across the weekend, and not a single duck to show for it. The exclusive zero club held its breath, its doors, and its ceremonial gavel — all in vain. Somewhere out there, elevens' worth of batsmen contrived to actually trouble the scorers. 🏏 The Weekend That Wasn't Both fixtures came and went without so much as a nervous single-ball nick. Eleven-a-side, twice over, and the art of not scoring went entirely unpractised. Whether this is a triumph of technique or simply a conspiracy of good fortune, the committee cannot say — but the honours board gathers no new names this week, and the Duck of the Year race stands exactly where it stood on Friday. Frankly, it's the kind of weekend that makes a duck-hunter nervous. When the ducks go quiet, they're usually plotting something for next week. 📊 Week in Summary - Matches played: 2 - Ducks recorded: 0 - New Duck Club members: none - Pairs, king pairs, or family ducks: mercifully, or perhaps disappointingly, none A blank weekend it may be, but the season's serial offenders remain firmly in credit, so let's turn to them. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Alan Cradduck — 7 ducks from 17 innings (41.18%) 2. Tony Gauna — 5 ducks from 14 innings (35.71%) 3. Benny Bell — 5 ducks from 15 innings (33.33%) 4. Allan Cooper — 5 ducks from 16 innings (31.25%) 5. Colin Dicker — 5 ducks from 17 innings (29.41%) Alan Cradduck sits imperiously atop the pile with a duck rate the rest of the top five can only admire from a respectful distance — over four ducks in every ten trips to the crease is a consistency of a very particular kind, and 1974's crown is very much his to lose. 📅 Looking Ahead Rest assured, the ducks are merely regrouping. No club can maintain a clean sheet forever, and with the season's front-runners still very much active, next weekend offers every opportunity for the honours board to fill up again. Get your bats out, gentlemen — or rather, don't, and we'll see you in the report. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — Saturday 31 August 1974

1 duck
Week 19 · 31 Aug 1974
A quiet one by Duck Club standards, but never let it be said Linton let a Saturday pass without at least one soul making the long walk back for nought. 🏏 1st XI at Whitstable CC Mike Iliffe, in at five, fell to a smart take by Tom Robertson off the bowling of S- Tritton. It's duck number 2 of the season for Mike, and more weightily his 30th in senior colours from 194 innings — good enough for 10th in the all-time standings, a lofty perch for a man whose speciality is emphatically not troubling the scorer. Fittingly, on this very day in 1952, club great John Thirkell also strolled back for nought, against A Levick XI — just one entry in an eventual haul of 82, still the club record and the reason his name fills half the honours board, including five Duck of the Year titles. Mike keeps distinguished historical company. 📅 The Week in Brief One match, one duck — slim pickings for the hunting season this week. Quality over quantity, as they don't say in this club. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - Alan Cradduck — 7 ducks in 16 innings (44%) - Tony Gauna — 5 in 14 (36%) - Benny Bell — 5 in 15 (33%) - Allan Cooper — 5 in 16 (31%) - Colin Dicker — 5 in 17 (29%) Cradduck sits well clear at the top of the table — the chasing pack have work to do before season's end. The ducks will return, they always do. See you at the next fixture 🦆🏏0️⃣

Beckwith for his 5th duck of the season and 29th of a career now 122 innings deep — good enough for 11th on the all-time list. On this very day in 1968, Gordon Fenner (1941–1973, 34 ducks, 7th all-time) was doing precisely the same thing against Aylesford Paper Mills CC. Some traditions never die. - Alan Cradduck went one worse at 10, bowled by the same M. Beckwith, for his 7th duck of the season and 22nd of his career — 20th all-time, and that's five in a row now on the honours board. With Duck of the Year 1974 already to his name, the pace is unmistakably his to lose. History, with a wink, pairs him today with clubmate Benny Bell, who made his own duck on this same 24 August back in 1968. 1st XI — Sunday 25 August, v Mersham Hatch (a) - Nigel Thirkell fell for nought at 2, bowled by B. Campbell — his 5th season duck, 17th career, 28th all-time. A Homebody and Tourist Duck top-tenner, he ducks in distinguished family company: father John sits alone atop the entire club's all-time list on 82, brother Tim carries 21 of his own. - Jim Hall departed at 8, bowled by F.H. Ames, for his 4th of the season and 14th career, sharing the innings with long-time 'Only Two Quacks' partner Nigel Thirkell — the old pairing freshly renewed. Both men also share today's parallel: on this day in 1904, T. Woollett made a duck too, in his one and only season. But Wednesday, unbeknown to Jim, had more in store. Midweek XI — Wednesday 28 August, v Gravesend CC (h) - Roland Parr, at 8, was bowled by Mukul Lall for a maiden season and career duck, 382nd on the all-time roll — welcome to the club. On this day in 1893, W.W. Tarrance was serving his own apprenticeship in the art, against The Mote CC. - And Jim Hall was back at it, run out at 10 for a second duck of the week — a proper pair, Saturday's Mersham Hatch nought followed by this Gravesend effort, taking him to 5 season ducks and 15 career, still 32nd all-time. A Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top-tenner, and Duck of the Year back in 1970, Jim proves twice in five days is exactly the sort of consistency this club was founded to celebrate. Week in summary Six ducks from four matches, and Jim Hall doing the work of two men to make sure of it. 🦆 Duck News — 17 to 21 August 1974

5 ducks
Week 17 · 17 Aug 1974 – 21 Aug 1974
Saturday 17th, Bobbing Court CC The 1st XI contributed three names to the ledger in one innings — a proper cluster of quacking, two of whom share the honour of doing it together. Alan Cradduck fell lbw to Roger Mills at nine, his 6th duck of the summer and 21st for the club, 22nd on the all-time list from 76 innings. A five-in-a-row veteran and paid-up Debutante Duck, he's the man to beat for Duck of the Year 1974 — and sits top of this week's leaderboard too. On this very day in 1969, Percy Brattle (1934–1973, 16 ducks) also made a duck, against Mote A — kindred spirits across the decades. Ian Brattle followed at ten, bowled by Mills, his first duck of the season and 2nd of his career from just 5 innings, 178th on the all-time list. He's a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his own long-ago first duck, and seven deep into a personal Ducks in a Row run. A chip off the old block too — sibling Paul Brattle carries 15 career noughts of his own. Percy Brattle's 1969 duck against Mote A keeps him kindred company on this date. Closing the innings at eleven, Ron Hickman was caught behind by Richard Witts off Mills, his 3rd duck of the season and 25th career, 14th all-time from 56 innings — sharing Quacking Together honours with Cradduck above, freshly reaffirmed in this very match. Parent-child fellow collector Phil Hickman (2 career ducks) watches on with pride. Percy Brattle, 1969, obliges again as this day's echo. Wednesday 21st, away at Gravesend CC (Midweek XI) Openers Jim Hall and Ron Hickman went in first and third respectively, both bowled by D Azall. Jim Hall's 3rd duck of the season took him to 13 career, 38th all-time from 85 innings — agonisingly one position shy of the Full House board, and a fixture of the Ducks on the Road and Lone Duck top tens. On this day in 1955, John Ames also fell for nought, against Halfway House. Ron Hickman, though, completed the story of his week: a second duck either side of the weekend, a grim pair, moving him to 4 for the season and 26 career, still 14th all-time. John Ames' 1955 duck keeps him company on the calendar once more. 📊 Week in Summary Five ducks across two matches — Bobbing Court did the early damage with three in a single innings, and Ron Hickman had the unwanted distinction of doing it twice, bookending the week with a pair. 🦆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Alan Cradduck — 14 inns, 6 ducks (43%) 2. Tony Gauna — 12 inns, 5 ducks (42%) 3. Colin Dicker — 14 inns, 5 ducks (36%) 4. Allan Cooper — 16 inns, 5 ducks (31%) 5. Ron Hickman — 6 inns, 4 ducks (67%) Ron Hickman now boasts the sharpest strike rate for nought in the top five — a dubious crown indeed. See you at the next fixture, when the ledger surely grows again. 🏏0️⃣

1 duck
Week 16 · 11 Aug 1974 – 14 Aug 1974
Two matches on the fixture list this week, but the ducks kept a low profile — only one found its way into the book. Quality over quantity, as they say in the exclusive zero club. 1st XI v Sibton Park CC (11 August, home) - Jon Harris walked out at five and was bowled by C. Wood, departing scoreless — his 4th duck of the season and 22nd of an illustrious LPCC career (119 innings), holding steady at 21st in the all-time standings of 908. Jon is a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having ducked on his very first LPCC innings many moons ago, and his honours cabinet has swelled considerably since — a Full House (a duck at every position from 1 to 11, a completionist's curse if ever there was one), plus top-10 residencies in both the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck charts, and a bit of a globetrotter too courtesy of his Tourist Duck and Ducks on the Road top-10 places. He's Only Two Quacks with Allan Cooper, and keeps Quacking Together with both Jim Hall and Trevor Back — clearly a man who prefers company in his misery. And speaking of company: on this very day back in 1973, Nigel Thirkell (1969–1974) also trudged back for nought against Headcorn CC, one of 16 career ducks that still see him perched at 30th all-time. Some anniversaries are best not celebrated. Jon's family file shows a chip off the old block too — relative Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own, so a taste for the number clearly runs in the blood. Elsewhere The period's other fixture passed off entirely duck-free — all eleven got off the mark, spoiling the sport for the rest of us. The Week in Numbers Just the one duck 0️⃣ across two matches — a rare quiet spell for the club's least-wanted honour. The season race, however, rumbles on regardless. A five-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967 and 1971), a fixture of both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, and a paid-up member of the Lone Duck elite, Thirkell remains the undisputed monarch of the art of not scoring, at home or away, it seems to make no difference. It is only his second senior innings for the club, and his first duck, so the honours board must wait — for now he sits a modest 350th of 902, a career very much in its infancy. Neat symmetry with history, though: on this very day in 1960, Brian Relf — later a 25-duck man and Debutante Duck club member in his own right — also fell for nought, against Tovil. 📋 Week in Summary Three matches, two ducks — a rare quiet spell by Linton Park standards. Fitting that the week's sole representatives spanned the entire spectrum of the Duck Club: number 1 of 902 shaking hands, metaphorically, with number 350. 🦆 Duck Report — 6 to 10 July 1974

Week 11 · 6 Jul 1974 – 10 Jul 1974
Four ducks fell across three matches this week, and Westerham CC did rather more than their fair share of the damage. Saturday 6th — 1st XI v Medway Valley Nigel Thirkell opened the week's account, caught D. Crosby bowled M. Barker for his 2nd duck of the season and 14th of his career — rank 32 of 902 in the all-time table. Nigel is a curious case, sitting in both the Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and a paid-up member of the "Only Two Quacks" club alongside Jim Hall — proof he can be relied upon to fail with a nought whether he's home or away.
