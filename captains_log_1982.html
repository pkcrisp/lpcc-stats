🗓️ 25 Sep – 01 Oct 1982 · 1982 — 2 matches

Wed 29 Sep 1982 🧢 Midweek XIv P Penshurst · AwayformWDDDL

Wednesday last took us down to Penshurst, and winning the toss I had no hesitation in taking first knock — a decision I stand by, whatever the lads mutter about my track record with a coin. We batted our way to 176 for 7 from the 45 overs, Paul Bowles the architect with a fine 57, well past his fifty, and useful support from Ian Hammond with 32 and Dave Latter with 21. Nothing flashy, but enough runs on a good pitch to make an afternoon of it.

Defending it was another matter entirely. Penshurst rallied around a battling 59 from Darby, with Ray Grayland chipping in 25 and Jenner 20, and for a spell it looked as though our total might not be enough. But Mike Elworthy simply refused to be collared, tearing through with 5 for 31 including five miserly maidens, and sent Walters and Bailey back for ducks along the way. I did my bit with the ball too, taking 2 for 54, and Steve Gower chipped in with 1 for 15. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid time behind the stumps, 14 byes going begging, which rather took the shine off things.

In the end Penshurst fell four runs short, 172 for 9 to our 176 for 7 — as tight a finish as you'll find on a Wednesday. Paul Bowles now leads our 1982 run charts with 520, and Mike Elworthy tops the wickets with 17. A nervy one, but a win's a win, and we'll take it gladly.

Sun 26 Sep 1982 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformLWDWD

Sunday last saw us welcome the visitors to Linton Park, and having won the toss I had no hesitation in electing to bat first — a decision I'm still rather pleased with, whatever the final scoreline might suggest.

Batting proved the day's chief pleasure. We racked up a thoroughly respectable 200 all out from our 25 overs, built on a fine platform from Andy Woollett, unbeaten on 21, with Richard Ashby also finishing not out on 20. I contributed 20 of my own down the order, which I shall take without complaint. The extras column weighed in with a generous 28 — whether that reflects generosity from the opposition's bowling or a certain wildness I couldn't possibly say, though I have my suspicions. Two hundred from 25 overs is the sort of total that ought to give any side a fighting chance, and for a good while it felt exactly that.

The real story of the afternoon, though, belonged to Mike Iliffe, who brought up 3,000 career runs for the club — a landmark achieved with typically little fuss, and one thoroughly deserving of the round of applause it received. Elsewhere in the season's charts, Nigel Thirkell continues his relentless progress, sitting second in the 1st XI run standings with 1,141 to his name.

As for the result, it wasn't to be — we came up short by seven wickets, our bowlers unable to defend what had looked a competitive score. Heads up all the same; back to the nets, and on to the next one.

Thu 23 Sep 1982 🧢 Midweek XIv WC Withnell Fold CC · HomeformWWDDD

Beating the drum first, I'll admit it: winning the toss and having a bat looked a fine idea for about three balls, at which point your captain's leg stump was rearranged and he trudged back for a duck. Withnell Fold, needing 141, made a proper fist of the chase. Michael Chambers was superb with the ball — 4 for 52, with three tidy maidens thrown in for good measure — and Mike Elworthy chipped in with 2 for 29, but it wasn't quite enough, the visitors getting home by four wickets with three balls to spare.

Bright spots amid the gloom: Paul Bowles now sits top of the Midweek XI run charts with 463 for the season, and Mike Elworthy is up to second in the wickets列 with a dozen scalps. As for my own contribution — a golden duck — the less said the better.

A defeat, then, but not a disgrace. We batted a shade under par, bowled with real heart, and were beaten by the better side on the day. We'll be better for it next week.

Wed 22 Sep 1982 🧢 Midweek XIv HC Hawkenbury CC · HomeformDWWDD

Wednesday 22 September 1982, and Linton Park hosted the visitors on what turned out to be a proper old-fashioned battle of attrition. I won the toss and had no hesitation in taking first strike — the pitch looked a belter, and I still reckon it was the right call.

We made hay to the tune of 175/3 from our 47 overs, which on this ground is a total with real teeth. Andy Woollett was in irresistible form, cracking a superb 83, and he found the perfect foil in Ian Hammond, whose polished 69 kept the scoreboard ticking along nicely. Between the two of them they did most of the heavy lifting, and I could hardly have asked for a better platform.

With the ball, we set about defending it in some style. T Theobald played the innings of the day for the opposition with a well-crafted 55, ably assisted by R Jones's 30, but it was our bowlers who had the last laugh. Paul Bowles was quite magnificent, ripping through with 3-18, S Gower departed without troubling the scorers, and Mike Elworthy bowled with real discipline — 2-31 including three maidens — while Trevor Back chipped in with 1-13. They finished on 156/7 from their 40, agonisingly short.

In the end neither side could quite land the killer blow, and honours were shared. A draw, then — but one that felt, in the best traditions of this fine game, rather like a win for everybody.

A comfortable 111-run victory, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work all round for the lads.
