🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 1969 · 1969 — 1 match

Sat 27 Sep 1969 🧢 1st XIv MA Mote A · HomeformLDLDL

Called it right at the toss on a bright September Saturday and, with the sort of misplaced confidence that has undone better captains than me, elected to bat. It did not go entirely to plan. We were bundled out for 100 in 43.4 overs, rescued from real embarrassment by Terry Donovan's watchful 30 and Mike Iliffe's tidy 21. Beyond that the order rather fell apart, and I'm afraid I must own my share of it — a first-baller myself, keeping close company with Brian Relf and Jon Harris in what the scorebook cheerfully calls the Duck Club. Three noughts in one innings takes a certain communal effort, and we managed it admirably.

Defending a total that modest was always going to be uphill work, and so it proved. Mote A eased to 101 for 1 in just 22 overs, with a Kendall innings of real substance — 60 not out — doing most of the damage while we toiled. Jon Harris, at least, had the decency to earn his corn with the ball, finishing with 1-19 and denying them a completely serene afternoon.

There was proper cause for cheer amid the wreckage, though: Richard Ashby brought up 1,000 career runs for the club, a landmark worth far more than the result. Paul Bowles, meanwhile, continues to lead the 1969 run charts with 921.

Beaten by nine wickets, no arguments. A captain ought to lead from the front — next week I shall try leading with the bat instead of a straight walk back to the pavilion.

🗓️ 20 Sep – 26 Sep 1969 · 1969 — 1 match

Sat 20 Sep 1969 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWLDLD

Won the toss on a bright September Saturday and elected to bat — the right call, I felt at the time, and nothing that followed made me regret it. We crawled and nudged our way to 185 for 8 from sixty overs, Basil Sears the rock at the end with an unbeaten 39, Paul Bowles chipping in a handy 32 and Andrew Moss adding 21. Not a total to frighten anyone, but respectable enough to bowl at.

And bowl at it we did, with West Farleigh replying in only forty overs — which tells its own story about how briskly they went about their business. Peter Cooper played the innings of the day, 66 not out of pure quality, with Ron Kemp keeping him company for a fine 51. We did have our moments: John P Day departed for a golden duck, and Brian Relf was the pick of the attack, tight as you like with three maidens on his way to 4 for 33. I managed to do my bit with 2 for 17, and Terry Donovan chipped in with 2 for 26 alongside me.

A word for the mantelpiece: Stan Wratten brought up his 300th appearance in Linton colours, a landmark that puts our narrow defeat rather in perspective. Bowles continues to lead the season's run charts with 911, and I find myself sitting second in the wickets column with 54.

As for the match itself — beaten by a single wicket, which is about as painful as losing gets. Heads up, lads. Back to the nets.

🗓️ 13 Sep – 19 Sep 1969 · 1969 — 2 matches

Sun 14 Sep 1969 🧢 1st XIv MH Mersham Hatch · HomeformWWLDL

Sunday 14th September, and honours ended up shared — but not before we gave the members plenty to talk about over their teas.

Winning the toss, I had no hesitation putting us in, and it proved a shrewd call as we rattled along to 205 for 3 off our 45 overs. The undoubted star was Paul Bowles, who compiled a magnificent unbeaten hundred — the kind of innings that makes a captain's decision look a good deal cleverer than it was. Andrew Moss chipped in nicely with 43, and Cliff Anscombe's 32 kept things ticking along handsomely in support.

With the ball, we set about defending our total, and the visitors battled hard to 164 for 9 off their 52, but never quite threatened to chase us down. J. Copeland led their resistance with 26, well supported by A. Gosling's 25 and R. Lewis's unbeaten 23. Paul Bowles, not content with his century, then took the ball and ran through them with 4 for 55 — a proper captain's nightmare to bowl against. Ron Hickman (3-29) and Dick Langridge (1-21) chipped in gamely, while Mike Stephens bowled with typical miserliness, sending down five maidens to strangle the scoring.

Nice to note, too, that Cliff's 32 nudges him past Bob Arnold to 17th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and Ron's three wickets lift him past Ernest Cruttenden to 22nd among the wicket-takers — the history books quietly rewriting themselves while the tea urn boiled.

A draw, then, honours shared — but with Bowles in this sort of form, leading both our run and wicket charts for the season, I'd wager we'll turn a few of these into wins before the summer's out.

In the end honours were shared, 119 for 8 leaving them agonisingly short. Not a classic for the purists, but a proper scrap — and with Andrew Moss and Ron Hickman both riding high in the season's charts, there's plenty still to play for.

One wicket in it at the close, and not a run to spare — the sort of nerve-shredding finish that makes club cricket worth turning up for. Two points banked, thoroughly earned, and Whittaker in irresistible form with bat and ball alike. A fine day's work all round.
