🦆 Duck Report — 28 September 2002

1 duck
Week 24 · 28 Sep 2002
Bidborough CC came calling at Linton Park and left with just the one scalp for the collection — quiet by our usual gluttonous standards, but a scalp that mattered. 1st XI v Bidborough CC (home) Rob Jackson, walking out at seven, was undone by Robert Cook and trudged back without registering — his sixth duck of the summer and eighth of an already storied career from just 20 innings, good enough for 106th of 1280 in the all-time reckoning. Six for the season also settles the matter that the numbers had already been whispering: Rob is confirmed as Linton's Duck of the Year 2002. He remains, too, a card-carrying member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his Linton account with a blob many years back — clearly the nought runs in the blood, given brother Adam Jackson carries 2 career ducks of his own. A neat footnote from the archive: on this very day in 1952, Ray Tippen — of the 1949–1963 vintage, and himself a Retirement Duck club member who signed off his Linton career on a nought — also walked back for nothing. Seventy-four years apart almost to the calendar day, the art of not scoring proves gloriously timeless. Week in Summary One match, one duck. A restrained weekend for the Club, but a title-deciding one — Rob Jackson's zero effectively seals this year's crown. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Rob Jackson — 6 ducks / 17 innings (35.29%) - Russell Baldwin — 5 ducks / 36 innings (13.89%) - Matthew Carter — 4 ducks / 9 innings (44.44%) - Jon Harris — 3 ducks / 10 innings (30.0%) - Adam Pooley — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) Honourable mention to Matthew Carter, whose 44% strike rate for nought remains the most alarming number in the division — a man seemingly allergic to the crease roughly every other visit. 🏏 Fixtures resume soon, and the exclusive zero club is always recruiting. Bring your bat, bring your luck, and may the noughts find you kindly. 🍩0️⃣

🦆 DUCK REPORT — 21st to 25th September 2002 🍩

4 ducks
Week 23 · 21 Sep 2002 – 25 Sep 2002
Three fixtures, four ducks, and the season creeping towards its curtain call. Modest numbers this week, but the quality of ignominy remains as polished as ever. Saturday 21st — 1st XI vs Elliotts CC Mike Elworthy departed at number four, bowled by Robin Tyler without scoring — his 2nd duck of the season and 37th of a long and distinguished Linton career, good enough for 18th in the all-time standings and a place among the Tourist Duck top 10. On this very day in 1958, Stan Wratten (1943–1984, 35 career ducks, a Quacking Together honouree alongside John Ames) was performing the identical trick against Tilling Steven. The zero club, it seems, keeps its anniversaries. Moments later Russell Baldwin followed him back, caught off the same relentless Robin Tyler — his 5th duck of the season and 9th career nought, ranking 95th of 1280 in the club's dishonours list. He too shares the 21st September 1958 with Stan Wratten, proving some dates are simply cursed for batting. Midweek XI vs Penshurst Mike Elworthy could not resist a second helping, bowled by K- Exton for his 3rd duck of the season and 38th career effort — a grim pair for the week, Saturday to Wednesday, and enough to draw him level with Allan Cooper (1971–1989, 38 ducks), himself no stranger to the Only Two Quacks club on three separate counts. On this day in 1960, Len Conway made his own contribution against Linton Park CC, on his way to the Debutante Duck club. And then Mark Christmas, arriving at seven and departing first ball — a proper golden duck, caught Rob Hunter off K- Exton without troubling the scorers at all. His 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from just 18 innings, ranked 287th of 1280, and a card-carrying member of the Full House nearly-man club, having ducked at 10 of the 11 batting positions. He shares his golden moment's date with Len Conway's 1960 vintage. The Week in Summary Four ducks, three matches, one grim pair to Mike Elworthy and one gleaming golden duck for Mark Christmas. Not a vintage haul by Linton standards, but a fitting late-season reminder that the art of not scoring never quite goes out of season. 🦆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Rob Jackson — 5 ducks in 16 innings (31.25%) - Russell Baldwin — 5 ducks in 35 innings (14.29%) - Matthew Carter — 4 ducks in 9 innings (44.44%) — a fearsome strike rate - Jon Harris — 3 ducks in 10 innings (30.0%) - Adam Pooley — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) With the season drawing in, the leaderboard is far from settled. Roll on the next fixture — the zero club always has room for new members. 🦆🏏0️⃣

A quiet week for the Duck Club statisticians — just three noughts recorded across seven matches — but quality over quantity, as ever, and this week's trio arrived with pedigree. 2nd XI — Meopham CC (Saturday 24th) Opening the batting and opening the Club's account for the week, Tim Thirkell was bowled by Liam Hogg without scoring. It's only his 1st duck of the season but his 73rd career nought, keeping him lodged at 4th on the all-time leaderboard — a place he's occupied so long the honours boards have practically named a wing after him: Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck, Lone Duck and the exalted Full House (a duck at every position 1-11) all bear his name, alongside a Duck of the Year crown from 1972. Fittingly, on this very day in 1968, Benny Bell (1966–1975, 30 ducks, 28th all-time) was also trudging off against Aylesford Paper Mills CC — the calendar has a long memory. Keep an eye out for brother Simon further down this report — the Thirkell name features twice this week. Tour XI — Falmouth CC (Thursday 29th) Mark Christmas became acquainted with the Duck Club for the very first time, bowled by George Popperwell for his maiden career duck — number 1 of 15 innings, and a Full House nearly-man (10 of 11 positions ticked off) in the making. On this day in 1954, Stan Wratten (1943–1984, 35 ducks, 22nd all-time) suffered the same fate against D Hambrook XI. Simon Thirkell wasn't far behind him in the same innings, caught Angove bowled Toseland for his 3rd duck of the season alone — the Thirkell sibling double now complete, with brother Tim's Saturday effort making it a big week for the family tree. Simon's tally climbs to 46 career ducks, 13th all-time, and he's chasing his own half-century of noughts — 59 needed for 50 Before a 50, with a highest score of just 48 to show for it. Two Duck of the Year titles (1983, 2001) and places in the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens confirm he travels well, in every sense. He shares his on-this-day parallel with Mark Christmas — Stan Wratten, 1954, still watching from the pavilion. A vintage start. 1st XI v Clarion CC - Adam Pooley, in at four, departed c & b P Elsden without scoring — a first duck of the season that nudges his career tally to a very tidy 21, good (or bad) enough for 48th of 1272 in the all-time table from 79 innings. That places him almost shoulder to shoulder with Robert Wood, who played 1958–1972 and needed 152 innings to reach the same 21 — sits one spot behind Pooley at 49th. Pooley remains a paid-up member of the Only Two Quacks fraternity, sharing the honour with both Fred Cheesman and Paul Bowles, and carries Retirement Duck status from whenever his own innings eventually closes. - J- Ferguson followed at eight, undone c J Elks b Peter Curran, and opens his season account with his very first LPCC duck — 1 from just 3 career innings, 465th of 1272 on the all-time list. He joins the exclusive and faintly tragic company of - Copeland, whose entire Linton career in 1979 amounted to a single innings and a single duck — the purest One and Done there is. Ferguson, too, is already logged for Retirement Duck honours, whenever that day comes. - Michael Beeney, at nine, was bowled by Peter Curran without facing so much as a genuine look at the ball — his 15th career duck, season number one, 63rd of 1272 from 113 innings, drawing level with John --'s Taylor, a name from 1949–1960 who took 143 innings to reach the same number. With the year lining up neatly against the season, Beeney's dismissal without troubling the scorer confirms his Diamond Duck status for 2002 — out without facing a ball, a rare and dubious art. He remains one half of Only Two Quacks with Ian Brattle, and a fully paid-up Retirement Duck member in waiting. Family connections run deep in the Beeney household too — kin Thomas Beeney already has a career duck of his own on the books. Week in Summary Three ducks, both matches accounted for, and the 1st XI shouldering the entire weekend's dubious distinction on their own — the middle and lower order doing the damage at four, eight and nine. A brisk, businesslike opening to the season's harvest.
