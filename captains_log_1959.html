Sun 27 Sep 1959 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformDWDLW

The coin behaved itself for once, and with it my mind was made up in an instant: we'd bat. Whether that owed more to instinct than genius is a debate for the clubhouse, but the lads repaid the confidence handsomely, posting 196 for 8 from our 37 overs. Tony Levick was the class of the innings, striding past his fifty to finish on a commanding 69, while Stan Wratten's watchful 43 and Pat Hardwick's brisk 28 gave the total real substance.

There was a quieter story unfolding too. Richard Harris's unassuming 7 runs carried rather more weight than the scorebook suggests, easing him past Don Hartridge and into 13th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, a milestone he will no doubt play down with the usual modesty.

The season's ledgers, meanwhile, continue to tell their own tale. Ray Howard sits third in the 1959 first-team charts with a formidable 558 runs to his name, Tony Levick now leads the Married run charts on 69, and John Abnett, with a modest but table-topping 8, leads the Singles.

With the bowlers and fielders doing their bit to defend the total, we came home 45 runs to the good. A thoroughly satisfying afternoon, and proof, if any were needed, that a captain's hunch at the toss can occasionally look like wisdom. Onwards indeed.

Sat 26 Sep 1959 🧢 1st XIv A A.Trembeths · HomeformDDLLW

Losing the toss on a fine late-September afternoon and being asked to bowl first is not usually my idea of a treat, but the lads set about A. Trembeths with real purpose. They ground their way to 186/9 from their 37 overs, Norris top-scoring with a stubborn 44, Holland chipping in 23 and Rye 20, while poor Johnson trudged back without so much as a nibble at his stumps. The story of the innings, though, was Ray Howard, who turned his arm over to devastating effect and finished with 5-61 — a spell that, rather splendidly, also carried him past Ernest Cruttenden and up to 13th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list. Terry Whittaker toiled admirably in support, two tidy maidens among his 2-60, with Stan Wratten chipping in a wicket for 21.

Set 187, we made distinctly harder work of it than the required rate suggested, stumbling to 190/7 in 40.3 overs. Stan Wratten was magnificent, his 87 the innings' backbone, with Tony Snook (31) and Pat Hardwick (20) offering sturdy company. Robert Wood, alas, departed for a golden duck, and poor Roy Clarke had an afternoon to forget behind the stumps, 23 byes leaking through. I shall confine myself to one boast: it was pleasing to see my own season's run tally edge further clear at the top of the 1st XI charts, with Terry now handily placed third among the wicket-takers too.

Three wickets to spare, two points banked, and a great deal of grey hair earned along the way — but a win, thoroughly and stubbornly, is a win.

🗓️ 19 Sep – 25 Sep 1959 · 1959 — 2 matches

Sun 20 Sep 1959 🧢 1st XIv TS Tilling Steven · HomeformLDDLL

I called correctly at the toss and there was never any hesitation in my mind — we would bat first. Pat Hardwick set about the Tilling Steven bowling with real purpose, a fine 71 the platform for everything that followed, and Robert Murray played the anchor role beautifully for an unbeaten 47. We closed on 157 for 6 from our 38 overs, though the scorebook was not without its blemishes — Eddie Lawrence departed for nought, and I fared no better myself, my own duck the sort of innings one prefers not to dwell on.

Defending the total, we need not have worried. Tilling Steven's reply folded to 45 all out in just 12.3 overs, three of their number — Baker, Webb and Seager — following my own sorry example into the pavilion for ducks. The bowling honours belonged unarguably to John Ames, whose 8 for 25 was a five-for and then some, while Ray Howard chipped in with a tidy 2 for 20.

It was a day for the record books too. Robert Murray brought up 1,000 career runs for the club and, with his 47, passed William Blight into 20th on our all-time run charts. Ray Howard's brace saw him move past Don Hartridge into 14th among the club's wicket-takers. For the season, Pat Hardwick sits second in the 1st XI's 1959 run charts with 551, while John Ames leads the wicket-takers with 52.

A comfortable win by 112 runs, then, and a fine afternoon's work all round — my own contribution notwithstanding. Onwards.

🗓️ 12 Sep – 18 Sep 1959 · 1959 — 2 matches

Sun 13 Sep 1959 🧢 1st XIv TC Tunbridge Wells CC · HomeformWWLDD

The toss went against us, as it so often does when the sky looks kind and the outfield looks quick, and Tunbridge Wells duly padded up. Roger Phillips made hay to the tune of a superb, unbeaten 105, well supported by Peter Lloyd's tidy fifty, and between them they steered the visitors to 202 for 4 from their forty-three overs. I can't claim we let it happen without a fight — I picked up 2 for 17 with the ball, which I'm reliably informed nudges me past Herbert Rayner into 9th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list, and Pat Hardwick and John Ames chipped in for a wicket apiece, toiling manfully for little reward on an unresponsive pitch.

Chasing 203 was always going to want application, and we mustered rather less of it than the situation demanded, bowled out for 118 in the fortieth over. Frank Brattle played the innings of the day, a well-made 63 that quietly moves him past Len Conway into 22nd on the club's all-time run charts, with Trevor Fermor's 22 the only other real resistance. The rest of us, it must be said, rather queued up to get out — John Ames, Robert Wood and Roy Clarke all walked back for nought, a triple duck that will not go unmentioned in the bar.

Down by 84 runs, then, and no way of dressing that up. Heads up, chins out, and back to the nets — Tunbridge Wells had the better of us fair and square.

Sat 12 Sep 1959 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformLWWLD

I'll allow myself one line here: a personal 70 was the innings' backbone, and reaching my own fifty along the way was every bit as satisfying as it sounds. Pat Hardwick's 38 and Frank Brattle's 23 gave it proper support, though poor S Lusher, having bowled his heart out, trudged off for a duck — cricket's little cruelty.

Amid the runs and near-misses, a small piece of club history: Maurice Fenner's 9 nudged him past John Ames into 7th on our all-time run-charts, a name now etched a little higher on the honours board. In the end, honours were shared — a draw, neither side quite able to land the decisive blow, which on this showing felt about right.
