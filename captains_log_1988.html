🗓️ 01 Oct – 07 Oct 1988 · 1988 — 1 match

Sat 01 Oct 1988 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformWWDLL

Lost the toss again — I do wonder some seasons whether the coin itself has taken against me — and Hastings & St Leonards Priory duly sent us out into the field. It began nervously, a wicket down with just 6 on the board, but a solid 51-run stand for the fourth wicket steadied matters and let Keith Turk (40), I Phillips (29) and Ian Gillespie (28) build them up to a competitive 196/6 off 45.2 overs. Matt Large was the pick of the attack, miserly with four maidens and finishing with 2-46, well backed by Simon Hammond (2-62) and David Castle (1-41). Their last pair then had the cheek to bolt on 50 further runs we could all have done without.

Chasing 197, we never quite found our rhythm. Tim Thirkell battled to 31 and I was glad to contribute 37 of my own, the two of us putting on 83 for the third wicket to give the innings some shape. But once that partnership broke, the middle and lower order went from 117-6 to 129-9 in a rush we'd rather forget, with poor Paul Sendles and Stewart Morrison both undone for ducks. Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps too, shipping 8 byes for his troubles. All out for 129 in 40.4 overs, we fell 67 runs short.

There was, at least, one thing to raise a glass to: Paul Gibson brought up 4,000 career runs for the club, a landmark worth savouring even in defeat. The season charts remain kind to us — I lead the run-scoring with 1,135, and Matt Large tops the wicket-takers with 68 — but on the day we were second best. We'll be better for it next week.

🗓️ 24 Sep – 30 Sep 1988 · 1988 — 3 matches

Wed 28 Sep 1988 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformLLDWD

Captain's Log — Wednesday 28 September 1988. Off we trooped to Penshurst Park, and I won the toss with all the swagger of a man who'd rather be doing anything other than fielding on a Wednesday, so naturally I put us in.

It wasn't our finest hour with the willow. We were bowled out for 143 in 39.4 overs, rescued from ignominy by Nigel Thirkell's fine 57 and Tim Thirkell's supporting 29. Elsewhere in the order things were less romantic — Peter Whyte and I both trudged back for ducks, and I can tell you the walk feels no shorter at my age than it did at twenty. Say no more about it.

In the field we scrapped hard but couldn't quite land the killer blow. Penshurst reached 144 for 5 in 35.3 overs, built on half-centuries from P Grayland (52) and John Hyde (50), whose third-wicket stand of 90 did the real damage. Simon Hammond bowled beautifully for 2-27, Ian Brattle strangled the scoring with two maidens, and Nigel Thirkell and I chipped in with a wicket apiece.

So it was Penshurst by five wickets, and Nigel Thirkell continues to lord it over the rest of us atop both the 1988 run and wicket charts — showing off, frankly. As for me, a pair of failures with bat and a solitary wicket with ball is a fair summary of a mixed afternoon. We'll regroup, we'll remember how to hold a bat properly, and we'll be better for it next week.

Sun 25 Sep 1988 🧢 1st XIv LX Lord Cornwallis XI · HomeformWWWDL

Sunday last, we welcomed the Lord Cornwallis XI to Linton Park in gentle late-September sunshine, and won the toss with my customary flourish of confidence — I stuck us in, on the theory that runs on the board settle nerves better than any team talk of mine ever could.

We were duly bowled out for 140 in 47.4 overs, which on reflection says rather more about our lower order than our top. Paul Gibson was the innings' backbone with a fine 46, and a stand of 59 for the third wicket gave the total some much-needed dignity. Not a score to frighten anyone, but not one to be embarrassed by either.

With ball in hand we made a promising start, sending Turner back without so much as a run to his name — the sort of duck that makes a captain's afternoon. But Lord Cornwallis steadied and, led by Rudd's polished half-century and useful support from Oldridge (29) and Allen (24), ground their way to 141 for 6 off 35.3 overs. Matt Large was magnificent, 4 for 46 with two miserly maidens thrown in, and duly leapfrogged Ron Hickman into 13th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — a landmark worth raising a glass to. I chipped in with 2 for 20, which I mention only the once before modesty reasserts itself.

Four wickets the wrong side of victory, then, and a long walk back to the pavilion. Not our day — heads up, back to the nets, and on to the next one.

Sat 24 Sep 1988 🧢 1st XIv MO Malta Maniacs - Other · HomeformWWWWD

Five wickets down, then, on a summer in which I've otherwise been rather pleased to top the 1st XI's run charts with 1096. Not our day. Heads up, lads — back to the nets.

Sat 10 Sep 1988 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · AwayformWDWWW

Saturday 10 September took us down to Southborough, and for once the coin obliged: heads, and I had no hesitation in sending us in first on what looked a decent strip to bat on.

Brian Piper treated the toss as merely the prelude to a proper carve-up, cracking a magnificent 102, while I hung about at the other end for 97 of my own — close enough to three figures to taste it, not quite close enough to claim it, which is rather the story of my batting career. Together we put on 215 for the first wicket before I finally departed, and Paul Gibson strolled in to finish things off in style with an unbeaten 52. With 25 extras thrown in for good measure, 280 for 2 off our 46 overs left Southborough a fair mountain to climb.

They gave it a decent go. Peter Young top-scored with a punchy 77, and a fourth-wicket stand of 91 between Simon Fisher and Steve Dann (29 apiece) threatened briefly to make a match of it, but Ian Brattle had other ideas, scything through for 3 for 37, well backed by Alan Boyns (2-11) and Gibson, who added a wicket to his earlier runs. Matt Large bowled two admirably tidy maidens, though poor Jim Hall endured a torrid afternoon behind the stumps, 12 byes slipping past him. Ray Grayland and Barry Hart trudged off without troubling the scorers.

Southborough closed on 167 for 7 — 113 runs shy. A grand day all round: Gibson notched his hundredth career wicket, and Piper's ton eased him past Jim Hall into 10th on the club's all-time run list. Season honours, meanwhile, keep ticking along nicely, with David Castle second in the 1988 run charts on 831 and Matt Large leading the wickets with 59. A good day for the club, and no mistake.

Skipper's Log, Friday 2nd September 1988 — away at Beacon CC, Cornwall

Beaten by two wickets, then, in a match that had chances for us right until the death. Tim leads our season's run charts, I lead the wickets — small consolations. Heads up, and back to the nets.
