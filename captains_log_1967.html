🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1967 · 1967 — 1 match

Sat 30 Sep 1967 🧢 1st XIv Headcorn CC · AwayformWLLDD

Sunday, 27 September was one of those away days at Headcorn where honest cricket met stubborn resistance, and honours finished shared. We lost the toss — no great surprise, my luck with the coin has been patchy all summer — and were sent into the field on a pitch that gave the bowlers precious little for their trouble.

Headcorn made us work for every run, building a platform through a watchful 99-run second-wicket stand before declaring on 145 for 5. R. Excell's well-crafted fifty and C. Smith's equally polished 58 did the bulk of the damage, but credit where it's due: Terry Donovan bowled beautifully for 3 for 26, five of his overs going for nothing at all, and shuffled himself past Ray Howard into 14th on the club's all-time wicket-taking list in the process — a lovely landmark on an otherwise stingy afternoon. Terry Whittaker chipped in with a useful 2 for 45 to keep the home side honest.

Chasing 146, we never quite found the fluency to threaten it, closing on 113 for 5 from our 35 overs. I managed 39 myself, which at least kept the scoreboard ticking, while Basil Sears (23) and an unbeaten 23 from Alan Cridland provided the support without ever cutting loose. Not our most convincing batting display, but not a collapse either — just tea-time cricket of the honest, unspectacular variety.

A draw, then — the fair result when neither side could land the knockout blow. Frustrating in the moment, but with Donovan's wickets column and my own run tally both ticking along nicely this season, there's plenty to be cheerful about on the drive home.

🗓️ 23 Sep – 29 Sep 1967 · 1967 — 1 match

Sat 23 Sep 1967 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformLWLLD

There are worse ways to spend the last Saturday of September than winning the toss and inviting West Farleigh to have a bowl at us, and for a while it looked a shrewd move. We stumbled early, seven on the board and already hunting for excuses, before our second-wicket pair steadied the ship with a priceless stand of 75. Terry Whittaker made a fine 56, calm as you like, with Paul Bowles chipping in a tidy 27 and I added 22 down the order. Basil Sears and Malcolm Pay both fell for ducks, proof if it were needed that cricket remains a game of fine margins and blank scorecards. We closed on 146 for 7 from our 48 overs — a total that felt more useful than imposing.

Defending it, we made West Farleigh work. Their openers laid a solid platform of 65 before the breakthrough came, and Micky Back's 44 with Joe Fenner's 37 threatened to see them home in comfort. But from 96 for 3 they subsided to 102 for 6, Brian Relf the chief tormentor with 3 for 21, well supported by Jon Harris's 2 for 13 and my own 1 for 17. They battled to 118 for 6 from 31 overs before honours were shared.

A word for Terry Whittaker, whose 200th appearance in Linton colours also carried him past Frank Taylor into 14th on the all-time run-scorers' list — a landmark innings in every sense. A draw, then, and a fair one: nobody quite deserved to lose, and nobody quite managed to win.

🗓️ 16 Sep – 22 Sep 1967 · 1967 — 1 match

Sun 17 Sep 1967 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLLWLL

Sunday 17th September, and the sun did us the courtesy of showing up for Anchorians CC. I won the toss and had no hesitation batting first — bowler's decks have a habit of turning benevolent by mid-afternoon, and I fancied ours would be no different.

We settled to 162 for 5 off our 47, which felt about right for the surface. The openers gave us the sort of platform captains dream about and rarely get, 64 without loss before the first wicket fell, and the fourth-wicket pair added a hearty 69 to give the innings its spine. Paul Bowles finished unbeaten on a composed 44, John Ames chipped in a fine 42, and I was glad to get among the runs myself with 37 — not that anyone will remember my contribution once Bowles's name is mentioned twice at the bar.

In the field we set about defending it, and for a while it looked a formality — Brooker and Batchelor both departed without so much as a run to their names, and the top order never quite got moving. Alan Wilkes was in irresistible form, ripping through with 5 for 39 off nine miserly maidens, well backed up by Terry Whittaker's 2 for 18. But Watkins (32) and Phelan (20) dug in gamely enough to see Anchorians home to 86 for 7 and a draw.

Honours shared, then — and on this evidence, with Wilkes doing his usual demolition work and the runs continuing to flow my way atop the charts, we shan't complain about where the season stands.

🗓️ 09 Sep – 15 Sep 1967 · 1967 — 2 matches

Sun 10 Sep 1967 🧢 1st XIv HC Harrietsham CC · HomeformWLLWL

Sunday the 10th of September brought Harrietsham CC to Linton Park, and for once the coin behaved — I called correctly and sent us in to bat, fancying our chances on a decent surface. Paul Bowles anchored things beautifully for 48 not out, Roger Craddock chipped in a handsome 39 and Robert Chance 34, while our openers gave us the platform every captain dreams of and prays for in equal measure, putting on 72 before the first wicket fell. I managed a modest 17 myself, more application than artistry, as we closed on 165 for 4 from our 46 overs — respectable, if perhaps a touch shy of matchwinning.

Defending it proved another matter entirely. Anthony Moss played the innings of the day, 74 that had us chasing our own tails round the boundary, well supported by Keith Chaney's 30 and J Wisden's 25, and a stubborn 53-run stand for the third wicket did the real damage. Dick Langridge toiled manfully for 3 for 54 and Roger Craddock backed him up with 3 for 58 and two admirably tight maidens, but Harrietsham edged home at 166 for 7 in 39.1 overs, three wickets to spare and precious little to choose between the sides.

A defeat, then, but not a disgrace — and with 583 runs to my name I still head the run charts for the summer, Dick sitting fourth among the wicket-takers with 24. Tea was, as ever, the true victory. We'll dust ourselves down and come back stronger next week.

A six-wicket defeat, then, and not our finest afternoon. Heads up, chins out, and back to the nets.

Sun 03 Sep 1967 🧢 1st XIv FC Frindsbury CC · HomeformWWWLL

John Thirkell's Match Report — Sunday 3rd September 1967

There is nothing quite like winning the toss on a fine September morning and telling the opposition, ever so politely, that they can field first — and so I did, and I still maintain it was the right call. Frindsbury CC were the visitors, and after a wobble at 1 for 1 that had me reaching for the smelling salts, Stan Wratten and his partner steadied the ship with a superb 62 for the second wicket. Stan went on to a chanceless 60 not out, Paul Bowles chipped in a handy 31, and I managed a tidy 22 of my own to help us on to a respectable 141 for 5 from our thirty overs. Special mention, as tradition demands, for John Ames, who contributed a duck to proceedings and can add his name to the club's ever-growing ledger of shame — the walk back to the pavilion done, one imagines, at a brisk pace.

Ames, however, is a man who believes in redemption, and what a way to find it. With the ball in hand he was a different beast entirely, ripping through Frindsbury with 6 for 37 — a five-for and then some, richly deserved. Dick Langridge lent superb support with 3 for 39, and though B Day's 37 and L Dudgeon's 28 built a promising 52 opening stand, the middle order crumbled from 81 for 3 to 91 for 6 under the pressure we applied. They finished on 120 for 9, and we held on for a 21-run victory.

A thoroughly good day for the club — Frindsbury will not thank Mr Ames, but we certainly do.

Nine wickets the margin, and no arguing with it. Back to the nets, gentlemen — there's plenty to work on before we're troubling anyone again.
