🦆 Duck News — 29 September 1990

0 ducks
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1990
A quiet one for the record books, ducklings. One match played on the green this day and, remarkably, all eleven walked back having troubled the scorers at least once. The zero club stood at the gate all afternoon, checked its ledger, sharpened its quill… and went home with nothing to write. Every single Linton bat found a way to avoid the dreaded nought — the art of not scoring, defeated for once by the art of actually scoring. Even Ron Kemp, who sits on a frankly heroic 50% strike rate for ducks this season, seems to have kept his powder dry. A rare day of restraint from a man built for ignominy. So no golden ducks, no king pairs, no father-and-son double acts for the Honours Board this time — just eleven batsmen quietly denying the club its favourite pastime. Frankly, a bit rude. 0️⃣ Duck Hunting Season 1990 — the chase continues The season standings remain untouched and no less magnificent for it: 🥇 Fred Cheesman — 7 ducks in 26 innings (26.92%) 🥈 Matt Large — 6 ducks in 20 innings (30.00%) 🥉 Jon Harris — 5 ducks in 22 innings (22.73%) 4. Steve Cheesman — 4 ducks in 17 innings (23.53%) 5. Ron Kemp — 3 ducks in 6 innings (50.00%) — still the most efficient collector in the field, technically speaking Matt Large's 30% strike rate deserves a nod of its own — nearly one in three trips to the crease ending in the walk of shame. Keep it up, Matt, the club needs role models. 🏏 Onwards to the next fixture, then — the ducks evaded capture this time, but the season's still young and Ron Kemp is still out there somewhere, bat in hand, statistically overdue. Get your entries in, gentlemen. The zero club never sleeps. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — 22–26 September 1990 🦆

0 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1990 – 26 Sep 1990
A quiet week down at Linton Park, and not in a good way for connoisseurs of the naught. Three matches came and went across the period and, remarkably, not a single soul troubled the Duck Club's admissions desk. Every bat that walked to the middle found its way off the mark, every scorer's "0" column stayed stubbornly empty, and the exclusive zero club processed not one new application. Whether this was superb batting, generous bowling, or simple bad luck for those of us who enjoy the art of not scoring, we cannot say. What we can say is that somewhere out there, three matches' worth of batsmen collectively decided this was not their week to audition for cricketing ignominy. Frankly, a touch selfish of them. Fear not — with the season drawing towards its business end, normal service is surely just around the corner. The ducks are out there. They are merely, for now, evading capture. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🏏 🥇 Fred Cheesman — 7 ducks from 26 innings (26.92%) 🥈 Matt Large — 6 ducks from 20 innings (30.00%) 🥉 Jon Harris — 5 ducks from 22 innings (22.73%) 4️⃣ Steve Cheesman — 4 ducks from 17 innings (23.53%) 5️⃣ Ron Kemp — 3 ducks from 6 innings — a startling 50% strike rate, the shortest route to the honours board this season Fred Cheesman leads the chase for Duck of the Year, but with Ron Kemp converting half his innings into noughts, nobody's crown is safe yet. Roll on the next fixture, where surely someone will do their duty and restock the cabinet. The zero club never sleeps — it just occasionally has a quiet week. 🍩0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — 15 to 19 September 1990

4 ducks
Week 21 · 15 Sep 1990 – 19 Sep 1990
Three fixtures, four ducks, and the exclusive zero club opens its doors once more before the season winds down. Let's get into it. Saturday 15th — 1st XI away at Southborough CC Dave Charlton departed for a two-ball nought, bowled by Steven Thomas at number four. His first duck of the season, and the 5th of a career now standing at 40 innings — 115th on the all-time list of 1142. On this very day back in 1982, Trevor Hales made a near-identical departure against The Mote CC, a man who'd go on to join the Retirement Duck club himself. Sean Charlton, Dave's sibling, already carries 4 career ducks of his own — clearly a family with an eye for the art of not scoring. Sunday 16th — 1st XI host Northend Sunday, a two-for A rare home double at Bearsted Green. Keith Hatcher weathered 14 balls before falling to N Wood, his 2nd career duck from 85 innings — 244th all-time. Then Piers McDonald followed at six, lbw to K Stapley off just 3 balls, his 2nd duck of the season and 6th career, good enough for 109th all-time and a paid-up member of both the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. Fittingly, both men share the very same historical echo — Richard Ashby, who on this day in 1962 also ducked against Staplehurst CC en route to 28 career noughts and a place in the Ducks in a Row (5 consecutive) hall of infamy. Northend Sunday clearly bring out something in this fixture list. Wednesday 19th — Midweek XI host Kent Invicta CC Ron Kemp lasted just 2 balls before falling to Griffiths, his 3rd duck of the season — and with only 6 innings played this year, that's a rate that would make anyone blush. 5 career ducks from 21 innings puts him 122nd all-time, and he already carries the Retirement Duck honour from a past farewell. On this day in 1982, David Iliffe made a duck of his own against Anchorians CC, one of two Iliffes to grace the honours board that year courtesy of the Quacked by Kin category. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from three matches — Northend Sunday supplied half of them in one sitting, and Ron Kemp's midweek effort keeps him on an eye-watering pace for the run-in. Consistency of a very particular kind continues to flourish at Linton Park. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Fred Cheesman — 7 ducks, 25 innings, 28.0% - Matt Large — 6 ducks, 20 innings, 30.0% - Jon Harris — 5 ducks, 22 innings, 22.73% - Steve Cheesman — 4 ducks, 17 innings, 23.53% - Ron Kemp — 3 ducks, 6 innings, 50.0% Ron Kemp's strike rate of failure is the one to watch — at 50% he's threatening to run away with a category nobody wants to win but everyone secretly wants to talk about. 🦆 Bats up next weekend, and the zero club never sleeps. Who's queuing up for the walk of shame this time? We watch, we wait, we celebrate.

🦆 Duck News — 8-12 September 1990 🦆

2 ducks
Week 20 · 8 Sep 1990 – 12 Sep 1990
A quiet week in the Duck Hunting fields — three matches played, but the noughts confined themselves to a single innings. Quality over quantity, evidently. Saturday 8th September 1st XI v Sandwich Town CC (home) — a rare twin strike in the same innings for the home faithful. Peter Whyte departed at first drop for nought, caught Kevin Beaumont bowled R Wickham after seeing off a defiant 9 balls before the aerial route finally did for him. It's his 2nd duck of the season and the 9th of a steadily swelling career collection, now sat 78th of 1142 in the all-time roll call. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1981, Richard Ashby was doing much the same against Paul CC en route to 28 career ducks and a berth in the Tourist Duck top 10 — Peter keeps fine, if scoreless, company. A note for the family album: sibling Tom Whyte already carries a career duck of his own, so the trait is evidently hereditary. Six places back, Fred Cheesman made it two for the afternoon, caught S Smith bowled Andy Jnr Smith after just 4 deliveries. This is Fred's 7th duck of the season and 17th of a genuinely illustrious career, moving him to 42nd of 1142 all-time — no small feat for the reigning 1990 Duck of the Year, who already holds the exceptionally rare Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11), Only Two Quacks status alongside Adam Pooley, and a seat in the Tourist Duck top 10. Curiously, Fred shares his on-this-day companion with Peter above — the very same Richard Ashby, felled by Paul CC on this date in 1981. And the Cheesman name runs deep in these parts too, with Steve (4 career ducks) and Stuart (1) both paid-up contributors to the family tally. Week in summary Three matches, two ducks, both courtesy of the 1st XI's trip against Sandwich Town — a proper matching pair, though not quite a Duck Club "pair" in the technical sense since it was two different batsmen, not one man twice. No family doubles this week, but the Cheesman honours board is doing more than enough heavy lifting on its own. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇🍩 1. Fred Cheesman — 7 ducks in 23 innings (30.43%) 2. Mike Bains — 2 ducks in 3 innings (66.67%) Fred sits proudly atop his own leaderboard, defending the 1990 crown in some style. The scorer's pencil is freshly sharpened for the next fixture — who'll be queuing up to join the exclusive zero club next time? We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck Club Despatches — West Country Tour, 2–3 September 1990

2 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1990 – 3 Sep 1990
The touring party rolled into Cornwall this weekend and, true to form, the exclusive zero club found two fresh recruits at St Austell CC on the Sunday. Saturday's fixture, mercifully for the tourists' averages, passed off duck-free. 🏏 Sunday 3rd September — Tour XI at St Austell CC (away) Down at the tail, Stuart Watson did what tourists dread most — he found the same trick twice. Matt Large, walking out at nine, departed bowled Watson for his 6th duck of the summer and 28th of an illustrious career spanning 108 innings — good enough for 23rd on the all-time chart of 1,142 hopefuls. A three-time Duck of the Year in his pomp (1987, '88 and '89), Matt remains a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his account with nought all those years ago, and is a fixture on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens — a man who has quite literally toured the country in search of failure. He remains cruelly stuck as the Full House nearly-man, having ducked at ten of the eleven positions and presumably eyeing number 11 with grim intent. Fittingly, on this very day in 1972, Benny Bell also strolled back for nought, against Staplehurst CC, en route to 30 career ducks in a run from 1966 to 1975. Two balls — and two batsmen — later, last man Jon Harris fell to the same Watson trap, chalking up his 5th duck of the season and a staggering 68th for the club. That places Jon 3rd on the all-time leaderboard of 1,142, trailing only the true legends of the game, and cements a résumé that already boasts three Duck of the Year titles (1976, 1980, 1981), the rare and complete Full House — a duck at every position 1 to 11 — and entries on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck, Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, plus shared Quacking Together honours with Jim Hall and Trevor Back. He also carries the family gene, kin to fellow club member Richard Harris, who has 36 career ducks of his own. Jon, too, shares his day with history — on this same 3rd of September in 1972, Benny Bell was going through precisely the same ordeal at Staplehurst. A watching purist might call it a matching pair for Watson, tail-end specialist — nine and eleven both back in the hutch without troubling the scorers, a fine advertisement for the art of not scoring. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches toured, one delivered the goods: two ducks, both at St Austell, both to the same bowler, both from the bottom of the order. The Duck of the Year race for 1990 tightens accordingly — Matt Large's sixth of the season nudges him level at the top of the current standings. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 - Matt Large — 6 ducks in 19 innings (31.58%) - Fred Cheesman — 6 ducks in 21 innings (28.57%) - Jon Harris — 5 ducks in 20 innings (25.00%) - Steve Cheesman — 4 ducks in 16 innings (25.00%) - Mike Bains — 2 ducks in 3 innings (66.67%) — a poultry rate to fear 🦆 The tour continues, and with it the hunt. Who fancies adding their name to the honours board next fixture? We watch. We wait. We celebrate.

Nice symmetry too — his sibling Dave Charlton sits on exactly 4 career ducks himself, so the Charlton household is now level on noughts. And, as with Trevor, the calendar throws up Mike Iliffe's 1970 duck against Gravesend CC as the day's echo from the past. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both from the same Goatacre CC fixture at the foot of the NVKC order — the middle and lower order took the hit while the top of the innings stayed disciplined. With "Duck of the Year 1990" already sitting on his honours board, Fred looks the man to catch in this year's race, and he's a paid-up member of the Full House club having now ducked at every position from 1 to 11 — a completionist's nightmare, a Duck Club romantic's dream. Keep an eye on the family too — son Steve was waiting in the wings for his own moment four days later. Sunday 5th August — 1st XI v Staplehurst CC (h) Brian Piper edged Escott through to Ian Henson without troubling the umpire's finger for long — another first-baller, his maiden duck of the summer and 17th of a career now spanning 299 innings, 41st all-time. His brother David sits on 7 career ducks of his own, so the poultry runs in the family. Back on this day in 1895, Ernest Cruttenden — himself Duck of the Year that season — made an identical trudge back against Rochester CC, one of 10 across a career running 1881 to 1912. Wednesday 8th August — Midweek XI v Bexley CC (h) Steve Cheesman kept the family tradition alive, trapped lbw by Michael Shelvey without scoring. His 4th duck of the season and, remarkably, his 4th in career from just 14 innings — an alarming early strike rate for a lad whose father Fred was doing much the same thing on Saturday, and whose brother Stuart carries a duck of his own. On this day in 1979, Paul Brattle also departed for nought, against Maidstone Police, en route to 25 career ducks between 1969 and 1990. Roll on next weekend, and may the zeros keep coming. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 21st–26th July 1990

5 ducks
Week 13 · 21 Jul 1990 – 26 Jul 1990
