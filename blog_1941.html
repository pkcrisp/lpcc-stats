The Duck Club Gazette — Linton Park CC, 22nd to 24th June 1941

3 ducks
Week 6 · 22 Jun 1941 – 24 Jun 1941
A quiet week for the exclusive zero club, but not an idle one. Of the two fixtures played, one innings against the Royal Army Ordnance Corps on the 22nd proved a positively fertile hunting ground, yielding all three ducks of the period — each to the same bowler. A triple strike of that sort does not come along often, and the Duck Club salutes R- Brierley as an honorary patron of the art of not scoring. 1st XI — v Royal Army Ordnance Corps, 22nd June (home) - Richard Harris, opening the innings, was bowled by R- Brierley without troubling the scorers — his first duck's egg of the season and the second of a modest career now standing at two in five innings, a plucky if fruitless knock that leaves him 71st of 502 in the club's dubious all-time reckoning. Curiously, this very day in 1935 saw Percy Brattle likewise stroll back for nought, albeit in different colours against Bearsted CC — history, it seems, keeps a calendar. - J Wenham, sent in at six, fell lbw to the same tireless Brierley — his second duck of the summer, and his second for the career too, from nineteen innings, lodging him 60th of 502 on the honours ledger. Like his captain in misfortune, he shares the curious distinction of a duck falling on this same date back in 1935, when Percy Brattle made his own contribution to the club's archives. - Don Hartridge, at number eight, completed Brierley's morning's work, also bowled for nought — his second duck of the season and the fourth of a career that has now brought him level with several distinguished names, and a lofty 16th of 502 on the all-time list, a place of dubious distinction indeed. The Brattle parallel holds for him too, the 22nd of June evidently being an auspicious date for the art of the duck. The second fixture of the period passed without a single addition to the ledger — a rare instance of the ducks evading capture entirely, though the eleven will no doubt make amends before long. Three ducks, one bowler, one thoroughly sticky wicket for the home side's middle and lower order — a week that will be remembered less for what was scored than for what conspicuously was not. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100% - Arthur Ash — 5 innings, 2 ducks, 40% - Don Hartridge — 9 innings, 2 ducks, 22.22% - Frank Sutton — 12 innings, 2 ducks, 16.67% - J Wenham — 12 innings, 2 ducks, 16.67% The season's leading exponents of the straight bat that met a straighter ball. Members are warmly invited to the next fixture, where the eleven will doubtless offer fresh candidates for the honours board — the ducks, as ever, are only ever one delivery away.

THE LPCC DUCK CLUB GAZETTE — 14th to 20th June, 1941

8 ducks
Week 5 · 14 Jun 1941 – 20 Jun 1941
A week that has swelled the exclusive zero club by eight new — and repeat — members, all drawn from the 1st Eleven, who between three fixtures contrived to bat as though the very sight of a cricket ball were an affront to good manners. 1st Eleven Against Charles Arkcoll on the 14th, the top of the order fell in ominous procession. J Wenham opened proceedings by departing bowled G Wood for his 1st season duck, and his 1st career duck in 17 innings — a debut entry into the book, and a tidy 143rd of 501 on the all-time roll. On this very day in 1882, so the honours board reminds us, C Gibbs made a duck against the self-same 1st Innings Boxley CC — history, it seems, keeps a straight bat of its own. Arthur Ash followed him back almost at once, also bowled G Wood, his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from 14 innings, sitting 40th of 501 — company that includes the newly-minted 1941 Duck of the Year holder among its ranks, though Ash himself must be content with the numbers alone this week. Then, at number ten, Dennis Smith fell the same way to the same bowler — his 1st duck of the season but 2nd of his career in 19 innings, 46th of 501. Smith is inscribed upon the Retirement Duck board, that most poignant of honours reserved for those presumed to have bowed out on a nought — though nothing in this week's play suggests any such farewell; he merely joins a long and distinguished list of gentlemen sharing his exact career tally of two. Four days later, against the Royal Army Service Corps, Frank Sutton was bowled by Tom Henley without troubling the scorers — his 1st duck of this season, but a rather more seasoned 6th of his career from 27 innings, a lofty 5th of 501 on the club's roll of dishonour, and a paid-up name upon the 1940 Duck of the Year board. On this same date in 1904, Tom Peach Jnr suffered identically against Leeds Castle CC — the calendar has long memory for these things. The 20th, against Maidstone Borough Police, proved a positive migration to the pavilion. Joe Fenner departed lbw to A Martin for his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of his career in 20 innings (33rd of 501), keeping fine company with A Tapsfield on the same tally. Percy Brattle went next, bowled G Baglee, likewise his 1st duck of the season and 3rd career (22 innings, 34th of 501) — level pegging with Fenner and half the honours board besides. Then came Frank Sutton once more, bowled MacNaughton, his 2nd duck of the week and 7th of his career — and with it, a grim little footnote to the season: a pair, two ducks within the period, first against the Service Corps and now against the Police. Not the ignominy of a king pair — he did trouble the bowlers for at least a ball each time — but a pair all the same, and one that draws him level on seven with A Startup, that veteran of the 1868, 1870 and 1882 Duck of the Year boards and holder of "Ducks in a Row." Finally, and most tenderly, R Lee made his very first appearance in senior colours and marked it with a duck first ball off G Baglee — a true and proper debut, his 1st duck in his 1st innings, 161st of 501. A soft landing into the club's fondest tradition, and he joins A E Leatham and a long roll of gentlemen on precisely one career duck. Week in Summary Eight ducks, three matches, and the bowling pair of G Wood and G- Baglee between them accounting for five of the fallen. Frank Sutton's pair was the week's standout feat of consistency, of a very particular kind. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks (100%) - Arthur Ash — 5 innings, 2 ducks (40%) - Frank Sutton — 10 innings, 2 ducks (20%) - Percy Brattle — 1 innings, 1 duck (100%) - R Lee — 1 innings, 1 duck (100%) The eleven take the field again next week, and the Duck Club's scribes stand ready with pen sharpened, confident that the art of not scoring shall find fresh practitioners yet.

The Duck Club Gazette — 7th and 8th of June, 1941

0 ducks
Week 4 · 7 Jun 1941 – 8 Jun 1941
A quiet fortnight's beginning for the Duck Club, and, it must be recorded with a certain wistfulness, an entirely uneventful one. Two fixtures were played across these two days, the willow was wielded with evident care by all who came to the crease, and not a single batsman was required to make that lonely walk back to the pavilion for a duck's egg. The exclusive zero club, so accustomed to welcoming new members, finds its visitors' book unsigned this week. Whether this speaks of a plucky eleven playing the straighter bat, or merely of fortune smiling where it usually frowns, the committee cannot say. Such things have a habit of correcting themselves, and correct themselves sharply, before the season is much older. A Week Without Incident Two matches, two results, and not one duck's egg laid between them. A curious sort of triumph, this — for once the art of not scoring found no practitioners, and the honours board rests undisturbed for another week. The old campaigners atop the season's tally may reflect, with quiet relief, that their standing goes unchallenged a little longer. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100 per cent, a season of really quite total dedication to the cause - R Dann — 3 innings, 1 duck, 33.33 per cent - Arthur Ash — 4 innings, 1 duck, 25 per cent - Don Hartridge — 5 innings, 1 duck, 20 per cent - Herbert Rayner — 6 innings, 1 duck, 16.67 per cent The committee trusts this dry spell will not persist. Members are warmly invited to the next fixture, where surely some plucky soul will oblige the ledger with a fresh entry, and the noble tradition of the duck's egg will resume its accustomed place in proceedings.

🦆 The Duck Club Gazette — 1st & 2nd June, 1941 🦆

1 duck
Week 3 · 1 Jun 1941 – 2 Jun 1941
A brief entry this week, but the honours board never rests, and one gentleman kept faith with tradition against the Home Guard. 1st XI — v Home Guard (Bungs Mob), home Arthur Ash, at number four, essayed an lbw to Gordon Fenner and trudged back with his wicket unscored — the first duck's egg of the season for him, his career total now standing at a single egg from thirteen innings, tucked in at 85th of 497 in the club's long and honourable roll of failure. He is not alone at that particular table — a whole company of gentlemen down the years, from A Barker of 1885 to C Crundwell of 1934, have sat on precisely one career duck apiece, and Mr Ash now takes his modest place among them. And by a pleasant coincidence of the calendar, on this very day in 1903 the Reverend W A Carr also strolled back for nought, against 1st Innings Maidstone CC no less — proof, if proof were needed, that the art of not scoring respects no era. The Week in Brief Two matches played, and but a single duck laid between them — the eleven, on the whole, kept the bowlers rather more honest than usual. A quiet week for the club's less celebrated members, though quiet weeks have a way of not lasting, as any student of the honours board will tell you. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks — 100.0% - R Dann — 3 innings, 1 duck — 33.33% - Arthur Ash — 4 innings, 1 duck — 25.0% - Don Hartridge — 5 innings, 1 duck — 20.0% - Herbert Rayner — 5 innings, 1 duck — 20.0% B- Vince continues to lead the season with a perfect record of failure, unmolested this week but surely not for long. The next fixture awaits, and with it the ever-present chance for a fresh name to join the roll. The eleven go out to bat in hope; the Duck Club, as ever, in quiet expectation.

The Duck Club Gazette — 24th to 30th May, 1941

0 ducks
Week 2 · 24 May 1941 – 30 May 1941
🦆 A quiet week at Linton Park, friends, and no mistake about it. Three fixtures were played in the period under review, and for once the eleven kept their heads down and their bats straight — not a single duck's egg to report from any innings, on any ground, by any hand. The zero club's ledger stands untroubled this week, its doors closed for want of a candidate. Whether this speaks to a sudden outbreak of application at the crease, or simply that the bowlers had an off week of their own, this correspondent cannot say. Suffice it that the exclusive fellowship of the nought gained no new members, and the old hands were spared the long walk back to the pavilion. Long may such abstinence prove the exception rather than the rule — the Gazette rather depends on it being the latter. Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks, a chastening 100% - R Dann — 3 innings, 1 duck, 33.33% - Don Hartridge — 4 innings, 1 duck, 25.0% - Herbert Rayner — 4 innings, 1 duck, 25.0% Do mark that B- Vince sits atop the table on a perfect record of imperfection — two innings, two ducks, a feat of consistency few would care to claim, yet claim it he does. Until the willow next fails a member at the vital moment, the Gazette bids you good health and steady batting. The season is young yet, and the bowlers of Kent will surely not let this drought persist. Same time next week, and may someone give us something to write about.

The LPCC Duck Gazette — the weekend of 17th and 18th May, 1941

5 ducks
Week 1 · 17 May 1941 – 18 May 1941
Two fixtures, two afternoons, and the eleven found the art of not scoring practised with rare devotion. Five ducks were laid down for the exclusive zero club this week — a place of dubious distinction, but never short of new members. 1st XI - Herbert Rayner — in at four against the Royal Army Ordnance Corps on the 17th, and undone by Brierley for his first duck's egg of the season. His second of a career now nineteen innings deep, and it lodges him at 51st of 493 in the club's long roll of the dismissed. He draws level on two career ducks with B W Haynes, a name from the club's Edwardian heyday who compiled his own identical pair somewhere between 1891 and 1910 — proof, if any were needed, that this particular failing spans the generations undiminished. - B- Vince — arrived at the crease for the very first time in claret and gold, at nine against the RAOC, and departed for nought before he had properly arrived at all, bowled Brierley. A debut duck, and with the season young he stands, for now, the leading claimant to the club's own Duck of the Year 1941. He joins A Barker, of the 1885 vintage, on a single career duck apiece. But the story did not end there — the very next day, at ten against the Home Guard's Bungs Mob, he was caught off A Stevens for nought once more, completing a grim pair inside forty-eight hours of his arrival. Two innings, two ducks, a full house already — a baptism as chastening as any this correspondent can recall. - R Dann — run out for nought at five against the Home Guard, his tale a touch confused (the striker's end remains, charitably, "unsure"). His first career duck in two innings, placing him 156th of 493. Curiously, on this very day in 1940, Joe Fenner also strolled back for a duck's egg, against Staplehurst CC — the calendar, it seems, has its own sense of humour. - Don Hartridge — stumped by P Richmond off the bowling of George Winter, also against the Home Guard, for his third career nought in fifteen innings, and 28th of 493 on the all-time list. He shares his duck's anniversary with the same Joe Fenner of 1940, who was evidently no stranger to a Saturday spent walking back early. The week in brief Two matches, five ducks, and the Home Guard's Bungs Mob proved especially fond of the eleven's lower order, claiming three of the five between run-out, stumping and catch. The standout performance, if that is the word, belongs to B- Vince, who turned up, introduced himself to the crease twice, and left it twice without troubling the scorer — a pair to mark a debut that will not soon be forgotten. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - B- Vince — 2 innings, 2 ducks, 100% - Herbert Rayner — 1 innings, 1 duck, 100% - Don Hartridge — 2 innings, 1 duck, 50% - R Dann — 2 innings, 1 duck, 50% The season is young and the wickets, by all accounts, still a touch sticky. Members are warmly invited to the next fixture, where no doubt further candidates for the honours board await — the eleven's tail has shown it can wag, but rarely for long.
