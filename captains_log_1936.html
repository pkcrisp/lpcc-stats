🗓️ 29 Aug – 04 Sep 1936 · 1936 — 1 match

Sat 29 Aug 1936 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformLDWLW

Charles Arkcoll played host on a fine Saturday, and having called correctly at the toss on 29 August, I didn't so much as pause for breath before opting to bat — a decision I shall claim full credit for regardless of how the day unfolded.

Arthur Ash led the way with a fine 39, well backed up by J Pocock's 22, and between them they gave our innings some much-needed backbone. It wasn't all glory, mind — E Cheeseman, N Tiley and Frank Sutton each took the long walk back for nought, and I dare say the tea urn heard a few choice words muttered on the way past. Still, runs enough were banked to give us something to bowl at.

And bowl we did. Hubble threatened to make a real fist of it for the opposition with 36, but he found precious little support, with P G Honnor shipped back without so much as a nibble at the scorebook. The day, however, belonged to N Judge, whose 5-0 was a five-for of the highest quality and thoroughly deserved — ably assisted by C Thomsett, who chipped in with figures of his own.

With Bert Buckoke now sitting second in our season's run charts and Judge leading the wickets column with nine to his name, the campaign is shaping up nicely. We came home 29 runs to the good — a thoroughly good day for the club, and one the tea and biscuits were richly earned for.

🗓️ 15 Aug – 21 Aug 1936 · 1936 — 1 match

Sat 15 Aug 1936 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · HomeformTLDWL

Saturday the 15th of August brought Bearsted CC to Linton Park, and what followed was the sort of match that keeps a captain's nails bitten to the quick. Batting first, we never built the platform I'd have liked, but N Tiley played the innings of the day with a fine 30, ably supported by Bert Buckoke's unbeaten 25. I'll own it plainly and only the once: both O Fryer and I contributed precisely nothing, back in the pavilion with matching golden ducks before we'd troubled the groundsman.

Out we went to field, rather grateful for the change of scenery, and Bearsted gave as good as they got. R J Hall and L F Wright both made watchful 28s, but W 'Bill' Tree, Thomas Benger and George Smith departed without so much as a single to their names. N Judge was magnificent, taking 4-25, Arthur Ash chipped away for 4-32, and even O Fryer found redemption with the ball, snaring a wicket without conceding a run.

A captain is meant to lead by example, and on this occasion I did — straight back to the pavilion, second ball, proving that whatever else deserted me that afternoon, my sense of theatre did not. N Tiley remains our leading run-scorer this season with 73, and Arthur Ash now heads the wicket charts with five. None of it mattered in the end, though, beside the only figure that counts: we got over the line by six runs. A scrappy, nerve-shredding, entirely typical village win — and a very good day for the club, whatever the state of its captain's average.

🗓️ 27 Jun – 03 Jul 1936 · 1936 — 1 match

Sat 27 Jun 1936 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · AwayformLTLDW

Bearsted away fixtures have a habit of humbling us, and Saturday was no exception, though not without its flashes of pride. Put in to bat, we found the runs harder to come by than the flask of tea left in the boot, and wickets tumbled around a rather fine effort from N Tiley, unbeaten on 43 and the innings' undoubted anchor. I chipped in with 37, glad to lend some support, while Bert Buckoke's 28 kept the scoreboard ticking over. Poor E Godden will want to forget his afternoon entirely, dismissed without troubling the scorers — a duck to file alongside the sandwiches as one of the day's less appetising offerings.

Defending what we had, Bearsted's Jack Tree decided he fancied a long stay at the crease, and sixty-eight not out later he was still there, as immovable as the clubhouse bar on a Sunday lunchtime. Thomas Benger (30) and Frank Harnett (26) gave him company enough. Buckoke, to his enormous credit, kept plugging away for figures of 4-64, with O Fryer (2-50) and Arthur Ash (1-16) chipping in, and we did at least have the satisfaction of seeing off W 'Bill' Tree for a duck.

But it wasn't to be — beaten by the narrowest of margins, a single wicket, the sort of defeat that lingers longer than the urn takes to boil. Tiley now tops our run charts for the season, Buckoke our wicket ones, which is scant consolation but consolation nonetheless. Heads up, lads — back to the nets.
