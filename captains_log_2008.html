🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 2008 · 2008 — 1 match

Sat 27 Sep 2008 🧢 1st XIv Chelsfield CC · HomeformLLWDD

I confess I entered Saturday's fixture against Chelsfield with quiet optimism, which the toss promptly dented — lost it, and we were sent out to bowl first on 27th September. Chelsfield made themselves thoroughly at home, racing to 227/5 from their 39 overs. C Martin played the innings of the day with a fine 85, well supported by U Hassan's equally fine 53, while X Stuary finished unbeaten on 49 to round things off nicely for the visitors. Credit where due: Sam Boyns led the attack superbly with 2-32, Chris Newman and Michael Beeney chipped in with a wicket apiece, and Richard Knowler bowled two maidens that gave nothing whatsoever away.

Chasing 227 was always going to ask questions of us, and in truth we never quite found the answers, closing on 147/8 from our 39. Tim Thirkell top-scored with a battling 38, ably backed up by Richard Piper's 27. As for my own contribution — I'm afraid I must report a golden duck, the sort of dismissal best filed under "least said, soonest mended," and I shall leave it there.

Elsewhere, the season charts continue to make pleasant reading regardless of my own failings: Richard Piper sits 5th in the 1st XI run charts with 181 for the year, and Michael Beeney 4th in the wicket charts with 13.

In the end, honours were shared — a draw, and on this occasion probably a fair reflection of an afternoon where neither side could quite land the killer blow.

🗓️ 20 Sep – 26 Sep 2008 · 2008 — 1 match

Sat 20 Sep 2008 🧢 1st XIv SC Sidcup CC · HomeformLLLWD

From the captain's chair — Saturday 20 September 2008, and Sidcup CC in town for what turned into a proper old-fashioned arm-wrestle of a draw.

No luck with the coin, and Sidcup wasted no time making us pay for it, rattling along to 248/2 from their 38 overs. David G Rolls was the chief tormentor, an imperious 102 not out, with Kim O'Mara's well-crafted 77 and Stephen Shanks' 42 doing plenty of damage either side of a 129-run stand for the second wicket that had us chasing our tails in the field. Tim Thirkell was the one bright spark with the ball, snaffling 1-11 off a probing spell, with Richard Knowler chipping in for 1-45.

Chasing 249, we made a proper fist of it. James Piper produced an innings of real substance — 105, no less, and along the way brought up 3,000 career runs for the club, which rather puts the rest of us to shame. Nigel Thirkell gave him fine company with a well-made 70, the pair adding 126 for the fourth wicket to drag us right back into it. But cricket, being cricket, could not resist a late twist: from 227-5 we stumbled to 228-8 in the blink of an eye, Matthew Carter enduring the walk of shame for a duck along the way.

So honours shared, neither side quite able to land the killer blow. Piper's ton keeps him second in the season's run charts, and with Knowler still leading the wickets column, there's plenty to be cheerful about — even in a draw that felt, frankly, like a win we let slip through our fingers.
