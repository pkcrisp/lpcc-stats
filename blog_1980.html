🦆 Duck News — 27 September 1980

1 duck
Week 23 · 27 Sep 1980
A quiet weekend for the Duck Hunting fraternity, but not one entirely without incident. Season 1980 drew towards its close with a single fixture on the books, and thankfully — for the sake of tradition — it did not pass without one gentleman adding his name to the ledger. 🏏 1st XI v Lordswood CC Martin Thompson, walking in at four, departed lbw to Henderson Carrington for his second duck of the season and his sixth for the club. Martin now sits 89th in Linton's all-time roll of dishonour from 43 innings — proof that even a steady career can be punctuated, more than once a summer, by the pure art of not scoring. He remains, and has long been, a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club, that most solemn of honours reserved for those who once bowed out for nought at a past Linton farewell — no danger of that repeating itself today, one trusts. On this very day back in 1964, Robert Murray — who turned out for Linton between 1958 and 1965 and finished with 17 career ducks, good enough for 35th on the all-time list — also trudged off for nought against Linton Park CC. Sixteen years on to the day, history quietly rhymes. 📊 Week in Summary One match, one duck. A restrained showing by Duck Club standards, but a showing nonetheless — the zero club never truly closes for business, even on the quietest of weekends. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 7 ducks / 31 innings (22.58%) 2. David Iliffe — 6 ducks / 19 innings (31.58%) 3. Ross Brice — 5 ducks / 15 innings (33.33%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks / 34 innings (14.71%) 5. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks / 21 innings (14.29%) Jon Harris still commands the summit, though David Iliffe's alarming one-in-three strike rate suggests this race is far from run. The rest of the club stands warned — the next fixture waits for no one, and the ducks are always circling. 🍩 Bring your bats, gentlemen. Or don't. It rather adds to the fun. 🦆

🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 20-21 September 1980

1 duck
Week 22 · 20 Sep 1980 – 21 Sep 1980
Two fixtures on the books this weekend, and the club's exclusive zero club welcomed just the one new entry — proof, if it were needed, that even a modest two-match slate can still produce a moment of pure, uncluttered failure. 1st XI — Anchorians CC (Sunday) David Iliffe strode out at number eight and departed for nought, caught by R. Durbridge off the bowling of Derek Underwood. It's the 6th duck of David's season and the 10th of his career from 35 innings, lodging him at a very respectable 53rd of 994 in the all-time standings — a place of genuine, dubious distinction. Curiously, on this very calendar day back in 1958, LPCC stalwart Stan Wratten also trudged off for a duck of his own against Tilling Steven, one of 35 he amassed across a career running 1943 to 1980 (good for 11th all-time, and a paid-up member of the "Quacking Together" honours board alongside John Ames). Some anniversaries write themselves. Worth a mention too that David isn't the only quack in the family tree — his kin Mike Iliffe carries 41 career ducks of his own, a fine bit of ornithological pedigree passed down. Elsewhere on the card, the other fixture passed off entirely fowl-free — all eleven batted, none troubled the Duck Club, the ledger untouched. Week in Summary One duck from two matches is a quiet weekend by Duck Club standards, but David's contribution keeps him firmly lodged in the season's top two — and with 19 innings already yielding 6 ducks, a strike rate north of 31% suggests there's plenty more sport to come before the season's out. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 7 ducks in 30 innings (23.33%) 2. David Iliffe — 6 ducks in 19 innings (31.58%) 3. Ross Brice — 5 ducks in 14 innings (35.71%) 4. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks in 33 innings (15.15%) 5. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks in 21 innings (14.29%) Jon Harris holds a narrow lead at the summit, but David Iliffe's alarming conversion rate means nobody in the dressing room should feel safe. The season draws on — who joins the roll of dishonour next? Same time next fixture, ducks permitting. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 13-17 September 1980

5 ducks
Week 21 · 13 Sep 1980 – 17 Sep 1980
A tidy little haul this week: five ducks in three matches, and the season table has suddenly gone very interesting indeed. Saturday 13th - 1st XI away at Southborough CC David Iliffe walked in at nine and departed first ball, lbw to Martin Thomas — a proper golden duck, no messing about. It's his 9th for the club from 33 innings, tucking him in at 59th on the all-time list. Neat symmetry on the fixture card too: on this very day in 1975, club record-holder John Thirkell (90 ducks, still top of the pile) also fell for nought against Southborough. And keep an eye on the surname — Iliffe joins the ledger alongside kinsman Mike Iliffe, who has quietly amassed 41 career ducks of his own. Sunday 14th - 1st XI at home to Northend Two for the price of one at home. Andy Woollett opened his account at number four, lbw for nought — his first career duck from 23 innings, and 257th on the all-time list with plenty of climbing room. Ross Brice went one further, caught by P Francis off B East for his 5th of the season and 11th career duck, keeping fine company as reigning Duck of the Year 1979. Both share a curious slice of calendar trivia: 22 years to the day earlier, in 1958, Stan Wratten (35 career ducks) made his own nought against Tunbridge Wells CC. Wednesday 17th - Midweek XI away at The Mote CC The Mote proved a happy hunting ground. David Carden edged behind for 0, his 2nd of the season and 4th career duck, 110th all-time. And Jim Hall, a name that barely needs introducing round these parts, was bowled by Terry Holloway for his 2nd of the season — but his 45th career duck, good enough for 6th on the all-time list. Hall's honours board reads like a tasting menu: two-time Duck of the Year 1970 and 1977, fixtures on the Lone Duck, Homebody Duck and Tourist Duck top tens, and a Full House nearly-man just one position shy of the complete set. Both Mote victims share history's calendar: on this same day in 1972, John Thirkell was dismissed for nought against Mersham Hatch — the great man simply will not leave this report alone. Week in summary Five ducks, three matches, one very busy Wednesday. The real story is the season table — David Iliffe and Ross Brice both moved up to 5 season ducks this week, drawing level with Tim Thirkell and setting up a proper three-way scrap for the runners-up spot behind Jon Harris. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 7 ducks, 29 innings, 24.14% 2. Ross Brice — 5 ducks, 12 innings, 41.67% 2. David Iliffe — 5 ducks, 17 innings, 29.41% 2. Tim Thirkell — 5 ducks, 32 innings, 15.63% 5. Allan Cooper — 3 ducks, 20 innings, 15.0% Next fixture, more victims for the exclusive zero club. See you all out there 🍩0️⃣🏏

🦆 Duck News — 30 August to 5 September 1980

5 ducks
Week 19 · 30 Aug 1980 – 5 Sep 1980
Five ducks hit the water across three fixtures as the season heads into its final stretch, headlined by a spectacular top-and-tail collapse on tour in Cornwall. Let's get into it. Saturday 30 August — 1st XI v British Rail Ashford (h) David Carden departed at first drop, caught A Owen off J Jenkins, without scoring. It's his 1st duck of the season and 3rd of a career now 53 innings deep, good (or bad) enough for 134th on the all-time list. On this very day back in 1965, Frank Brattle — 38 ducks across a career spanning 1937 to 1971, 9th all-time — was performing the identical trick against Bearsted CC. Some Linton Park traditions are handed down through the generations. Sunday 31 August — 1st XI v Staplehurst CC (a) Allan Cooper edged behind at four, caught John Hughes off John Munn, for his 3rd duck of the season and 25th of a thoroughly decorated LPCC career — 21st all-time from 144 innings. Cooper's name is already inked twice into the honours board for shared quacking, alongside both Jim Hall and Jon Harris — fitting, since both were about to catch him up (see below). On this day in 1974, Mike Iliffe — 41 ducks, 1966 to 1980, 7th all-time — fell exactly the same way against Whitstable. Friday 5 September — Tour XI v Cornwall Crusaders (a) The end-of-season tour to Cornwall was rather less restful than advertised for the top and tail of the Tour XI order. Openers Paul Bowles and Jim Hall fell in matching fashion, both bowled by H James without scoring — a new-ball double strike guaranteed to delight the Crusaders and unsettle everyone else. For Bowles, a 1st duck of the season and a hefty 33rd career nought, 14th all-time from 428 innings, adding to a cabinet already containing Duck of the Year honours from 1966 and 1968. For Hall, also his 1st of the season, it's a mighty 44th career duck — 6th all-time — and a name already sat on the Duck of the Year 1980 board among a wall of others, including one for "nearly" completing the Full House at 10 of 11 positions. Then, as the innings wound down, last man Jon Harris was run out — "unsurely", says the book — for his 7th duck of the season, stretching his lead at the top of the season table. It's his 48th career duck, 3rd on the all-time roll, and with position 11 now ticked off, it completes his own Full House — a duck recorded at every batting position from 1 to 11. A chip off an unlucky block, too: his relative Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks of his own in Linton Park colours. Remarkably, all three Tour XI victims share the same historical echo: David T Lowen, who made his own duck on this exact calendar date in 1970, against A. Trembeths. Three ducks, one innings, one ghost from a decade earlier watching on approvingly. Week in summary Five ducks from three matches, three of them arriving in a single unfortunate Tour XI innings. The 1st XI contributed one apiece across their weekend fixtures. A tidy week's work for the exclusive zero club. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Jon Harris — 24 innings, 7 ducks, 29.17% - Tim Thirkell — 28 innings, 5 ducks, 17.86% - Ross Brice — 9 innings, 4 ducks, 44.44% - David Iliffe — 16 innings, 4 ducks, 25.0% - Allan Cooper — 17 innings, 3 ducks, 17.65% Jon Harris holds a commanding lead at the top, but with the season still to run there's plenty of time for a late surge from the chasing pack. 🏏 See you all at the next fixture — the ducks, as ever, will be waiting. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 LPCC Duck Club Report — 23-27 August 1980 🦆

4 ducks
Week 18 · 23 Aug 1980 – 27 Aug 1980
Four matches on the fixture list this week and the Duck Club's attentions fell squarely on the 1st XI, who contrived not one but two separate top-order collapses to keep the honours board busy. A quiet week for the rest of the club — but quality over quantity, as ever, in this exclusive zero club. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — v Downham & Bellingham CC Graham Cove departed for nought, caught Mick Goddard bowled Geoff Goddard at first wicket down — his 1st duck of the season and 2nd of a career now standing at 27 innings, good enough for 192nd on the all-time list. Graham is a card-carrying member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, so no danger of him actually walking off into the sunset today. Spookily, on this very day in 1952, D Simmons was doing precisely the same thing against Charles Arkcoll — 28 years apart, same script. Not to be left out, John Bloomfield followed at number 7, caught R Clarke bowled Adam Cannon, his 2nd of the season and 9th career duck from 42 innings — up to 60th all-time. He too finds himself in the company of D Simmons, who marked this exact date in 1952 the same unproductive way. And rounding off a torrid innings, last man Ross Brice was bowled by Stewart Barnes for his 4th duck of the season — a hefty tally that already has him 3rd in the current Duck Hunting Season standings — and 10th of a career now on 24 innings, 55th all-time. Ross remains a former Duck of the Year from 1979, a title he'll be eyeing again if this form continues. Fittingly, D Simmons rounds out the trio of 1952 company for a third time today. 🏏 Sunday 24th — v Old Anchorians CC The headline act: Tim Thirkell, bowled by B Heather, recorded his 5th duck of the season and a colossal 46th of an career spanning 374 innings — good enough for 5th on the all-time leaderboard, a genuine club legend of the art of not scoring. Tim's honours cabinet groans under the weight of it: Duck of the Year 1972, Full House (a duck at every position 1 to 11 — true consistency of a very particular kind), plus top-ten standing in both the Homebody and Lone Duck categories, and a shared Quacking Together entry with Jim Hall. A proper family affair too — his father John Thirkell sits on a mighty 90 career ducks, with siblings Nigel (28) and Simon (1) also paid-up members, so ducking is clearly hereditary round the Thirkell household. On this same day back in 1968, Benny Bell was also dismissed for nought, against Aylesford Paper Mills CC, en route to 30 career ducks of his own. 📊 Week in Summary Four ducks from four fixtures, every single one landing in the 1st XI camp — the top and tail of the order both took a hit, with Graham, John and Ross clearing out the top order on Saturday before Tim kept the tradition alive on Sunday. The 2nds, 3rds and 4th XIs, meanwhile, enjoyed a rare and suspicious spell of scoring actual runs. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Jon Harris — 22 innings, 6 ducks, 27.27% - Tim Thirkell — 27 innings, 5 ducks, 18.52% - Ross Brice — 8 innings, 4 ducks, 50.0% - David Iliffe — 15 innings, 4 ducks, 26.67% - Malcolm Pay — 24 innings, 3 ducks, 12.5% With Ross Brice's 50% strike rate and Tim Thirkell closing the gap on Jon Harris, the race for Duck of the Year is very much alive. Next fixture brings fresh opportunity for glory through adversity — get your bats out, gentlemen, the Club is watching. 🦆🍩

🦆 DUCK REPORT — 16 to 20 August 1980

5 ducks
Week 17 · 16 Aug 1980 – 20 Aug 1980
Five new members were inducted into cricket's most exclusive zero club this week, spread across three fixtures. The art of not scoring was alive and well at Linton Park. 🏏 Saturday 16th — St Luke's (H) Stan Wratten got the ball rolling, caught Underhill bowled Hoare, for his 1st duck of the season and 35th of a long and storied career — good enough for 11th on the all-time list of 992. Stan already shares Quacking Together honours with the legendary John Ames, and fittingly it was on this very day back in 1953 that Ames himself ducked against Mote A, en route to 49 career noughts and 2nd place all-time. Moments later Ross Brice fell to the same bowler, bowled Hoare, for his 3rd duck of the season and 9th of his career — reigning Duck of the Year for 1979, and still adding to the trophy cabinet. Ross, too, walks in Ames' footsteps from that same 1953 afternoon. 🏏 Sunday 17th — Whitstable CC (A) David Iliffe, caught Robertson bowled Drinkwater, brought up his 4th duck of the season and 8th of his career, 67th all-time. David's father Mike carries 41 career ducks of his own — the family clearly has an eye for line and length, just not the bat to match. On this day in 1969, Percy Brattle also perished against Mote A, going on to be crowned that year's Duck of the Year with 16 to his name. 🌙 Wednesday 20th — Gravesend CC (A), Midweek XI A rare midweek brace. Gareth Thomas, caught Myers bowled Bowen, opened his account with duck number 1 of both season and career — he's already a standing member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, so no pressure on this one. Back in 1944, W Bunting made his own duck against KCC Maidstone on this exact date. Simon Thirkell followed, caught by a fielder the books never named, bowled Bowen, also his 1st season and career duck. Simon arrives with pedigree hard to match — father John sits on 90 career ducks, brothers Tim and Nigel on 45 and 28 respectively, and Simon himself already features on the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top ten boards. The Thirkell name is never far from the honours list. Like Gareth, his duck landed on the very date W Bunting made his, back in 1944. 📊 Week in Summary Three matches, five ducks, and not a golden one among them — everybody at least troubled the scorer's pencil before departing for nought. Hoare and Bowen each claimed a brace with the ball, and both the Iliffe and Thirkell duck dynasties had representatives on parade. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Jon Harris — 6 ducks / 20 innings (30.0%) 2. David Iliffe — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.77%) 3. Tim Thirkell — 4 ducks / 25 innings (16.0%) 4. Ross Brice — 3 ducks / 7 innings (42.86%) 5. Malcolm Pay — 3 ducks / 23 innings (13.04%) Ross Brice's 42.86% strike rate for nought keeps him dangerously golden-duck-adjacent, while Jon Harris holds the overall lead as the season enters its final stretch. 🦆 Same time next week, no doubt more names for the board 🍩. Until then — mind that first ball.

🦆 Duck News — 21-23 July 1980

1 duck
Week 13 · 21 Jul 1980 – 23 Jul 1980
A quiet weekend for the exclusive zero club, ladies and gentlemen. Two matches played, and the ducks mostly kept their heads down — but one soul did answer the call. 🏏 1st XI v West Farleigh CC (Home, Monday 21st July) Coming in at number 8, Martin Thompson found Eric Mitchell in irresistible form and departed without troubling the scorers — his first duck of the 1980 season and the 5th of his career from 36 innings. Two fixtures on the books this weekend, and only one proved fertile hunting ground — but quality over quantity, as ever, is the club's watchword. 1st XI v Chatham Nomads CC (Home) Coming in at number 8, David Iliffe departed for nought, bowled by D Barton — a tidy, uncomplicated dismissal, the kind that asks no questions and gets none in return. It's his 1st duck of the season and the 5th of a career now running to 17 innings, which lodges him at a very respectable 91st of 980 in the all-time standings. The Iliffe name is no stranger to these pages either, with a fellow member of the family carrying 40 career ducks of his own — proof that a taste for the zero can run in the blood. Neatly enough, on this very calendar day back in 1968, Cliff Anscombe also strolled back for a duck, against West Farleigh CC of all sides. Cliff played from 1957 to 1975, finished with 25 career ducks and sits 22nd all-time — a Debutante Duck to boot, having opened his account with one, and Duck of the Year in 1957. History, it seems, likes an anniversary. Elsewhere across the weekend's second fixture, the opposition bowlers came away empty-handed — no further additions to report, much to the collective relief of whoever was padded up next. Week in Summary One duck from two matches is a quiet return by the club's usual standards — a single, dignified entry rather than a stampede. Still, the season is younger than a fresh pair of whites, and there's a long summer of dubious batting ahead in which to make up the numbers. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - David Iliffe — 1 innings, 1 duck, 100% Early days, and the field is wide open. With fixtures rolling on, the honours board waits for its next volunteer — bring your bat, bring your nerve, and may the bowlers find your edge. 🦆🏏0️⃣
