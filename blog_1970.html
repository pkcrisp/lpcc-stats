🦆 DUCK NEWS — 26 September 1970 🦆

0 ducks
Week 22 · 26 Sep 1970
A single fixture out on the green this day, and — brace yourselves — not a nought to be found anywhere in it. The Duck Club's traps were baited, the honours board stood ready with a fresh page turned, and the entire membership went home having actually scored. A shocking, near-scandalous outbreak of competence. We shan't pretend this isn't a disappointment. The exclusive zero club thrives on adversity, and adversity took the day off. Still, every empty week is merely a pause between triumphs — the art of not scoring is a patient art, and Linton's finest will be back at the crease soon enough, bats in hand, ready to fail spectacularly in the club's proud tradition. So: nothing to report, no new entrants, no milestones tumbled. A rare and quiet Saturday in the history of the game's most dubious distinction. Let it be logged as such and let us move swiftly on to the only numbers that matter this season. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇🍩 1. Jim Hall — 5 ducks from 14 innings (35.71%) — the pace-setter, showing a consistency of a very particular kind 2. Tim Thirkell — 4 ducks from 12 innings (33.33%) — hot on Hall's heels and closing fast 3. Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks from 15 innings (26.67%) — quietly accumulating 4. David Piper — 4 ducks from 15 innings (26.67%) — level with Cradduck to the innings, sharing the podium in every sense 5. Benny Bell — 4 ducks from 19 innings (21.05%) — proof that patience, or at least persistence, pays off eventually A tight scrap at the top, with Hall's strike rate for failure currently unmatched in the division. Thirkell will fancy his chances of closing that gap given a bad enough afternoon. Rest assured, dear members, the ducks are only resting. Next weekend's fixtures offer fresh opportunity for glorious ignominy, and this correspondent will be watching every dropped shoulder and leaden footwork with great interest. Bats up, standards low — see you at the crease. 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 19th & 20th September 1970

5 ducks
Week 21 · 19 Sep 1970 – 20 Sep 1970
A full house of noughts across the weekend, five in total, and the 1st XI supplied every single one of them across two home fixtures. The exclusive zero club welcomes some very familiar faces. 🏏 Saturday, West Farleigh CC (h) Three down for nothing in the same innings, and Duck Club records will show the calendar has a long memory. Paul Bowles went first, bowled Trevor Back at number two, his 2nd of the season and 21st of a career that now sits 15th on the all-time list. A two-time Duck of the Year (1966 and 1968), a fixture on the Ducks on the Road top ten, and forever twinned with Adam Pooley in the Only Two Quacks ledger — Paul barely needs the introduction anymore. Eleven years to the very day, 19 September 1959, G Martin also departed for nought, against Bearsted CC no less, his 3rd for the club. History repeats itself with unsettling precision. Tim Thirkell followed, caught Colin Clark bowled Micky Back at seven, his 4th of the season and 10th career duck from just 33 innings, a rattling 30% strike rate for failure. He shares today's page in the 1959 ledger with G Martin too, and remains a proud member of the Full House board — a duck at every position, one through eleven — plus the Lone Duck and Homebody Duck top tens. His father John Thirkell, no less, sits on 67 career ducks of his own, so the family knows the drill. Then Jim Hall, bowled Ray Ford at nine, his 5th of the season and 8th career duck from 39 innings. Jim and Tim, already bound together on the Quacking Together honours board, renewed their acquaintance in the very same innings — a proper reunion of old quacking partners. With the season only just past its halfway mark for ducks, 1970 is shaping up nicely as Jim's own Duck of the Year vintage. He remains a nearly-man on Full House with 10 of 11 positions covered, and rides high on the Tourist and Homebody Duck top tens besides. 🌱 Sunday, Harrietsham CC (h) Brian Humphries opened the innings and opened his duck account in the same breath, bowled Edward Smith for his maiden club nought — his 1st of the season and 1st of his career from 17 innings. On this same date in 1959, Eddie Lawrence made a duck against Tilling Steven; welcome to the club, Brian, the anniversary is apparently compulsory reading. Jon Harris then fell at seven, bowled Michael Lidstone, his 3rd of the season and 9th career duck from 31 innings, a healthy 29% strike rate. Jon shares today's date with Eddie Lawrence too, and already carries a heavy honours load of his own — Full House completed in full, Ducks on the Road, Homebody and Lone Duck top tens, and an Only Two Quacks entry with Allan Cooper. His father Richard Harris carries 36 career ducks, so the art of not scoring plainly runs in the blood. 📋 The Week in Brief Five ducks, two matches, both at home, and the 1st XI shouldering the lot. Saturday's West Farleigh collapse alone accounted for three of them — a genuine cluster, the kind selectors pretend not to notice. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Jim Hall — 5 ducks in 14 innings (35.71%) - Tim Thirkell — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) - Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) - David Piper — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) - Benny Bell — 4 ducks in 19 innings (21.05%) Fittingly, the top two spots both fell at West Farleigh this very weekend. The chase for Duck of the Year 1970 is well and truly on. Next fixture beckons — pads on, bats ready, and the zero club stands by for new arrivals. 🦆0️⃣🏏

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 13 September 1970

0 ducks
Week 20 · 13 Sep 1970
A rare and faintly suspicious weekend, ducklings. One match played on Linton soil this day in 1970, and not a single batsman could be persuaded to nick off for nought. The zero club convened, checked its membership rolls, and found nobody new queuing at the door. Make no mistake — this is not good news for the connoisseur. Somewhere out there, eleven batsmen walked out, faced the music, and simply refused to cooperate with tradition. A hollow, duckless afternoon. We can only assume the bowlers were off their game, the pitch too kind, or the fielders too busy admiring the September sunshine to hold a catch. Whatever the cause, the art of not scoring took a rest day, and the honours board gathers dust for one more week. Still, the club's grand tradition of dubious distinction does not pause for a quiet Sunday — the season standings roll on regardless, and there is late-season drama enough to keep us occupied while we wait for the ducks to return. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - 🥇 Jim Hall — 4 ducks from 13 innings, a startling 30.77% strike rate for the art of not scoring - 🥈 David Piper — 4 ducks from 14 innings, 28.57%, breathing down Hall's neck - 🥉 Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks from 15 innings, 26.67%, keeping the top three honest - Benny Bell — 4 ducks from 19 innings, 21.05%, the patient accumulator's route to the podium - Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks from 29 innings, 13.79%, proof that consistency of a very particular kind can be achieved the long way round Five men, level on four ducks apiece, separated only by how efficiently they got there. Hall leads on percentage alone — the purest measure of triumph through adversity — but with the season still open, Piper and Cradduck need only one soft dismissal each to leapfrog him. Iliffe, meanwhile, is playing the long game, quietly accumulating his noughts across nearly thirty innings like a man in no hurry at all. 📅 Looking ahead A blank week for the record books, but never fear — Linton's batsmen are not known for sustained good behaviour, and normal service will surely resume at the next fixture. Until then, keep the willow loose, the footwork loose, and the concentration looser still. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — 5th & 6th September 1970 🦆

1 duck
Week 19 · 5 Sep 1970 – 6 Sep 1970
A quiet weekend on the nought front, but the exclusive zero club never fully closes its doors, and one gentleman was duly admitted this week. 🏏 1st XI Saturday's home fixture against A.Trembeths saw David T Lowen walk in at first wicket down and depart bowled by B- Lewis without troubling the scorers — his 2nd duck of the 1970 season and, fittingly, his 2nd career duck from 17 innings. He takes his place at 151st in the all-time roll of honour, a solid, unspectacular entry in the club's grand tapestry of not-scoring. Neatly enough, on this very day back in 1954, John Larking — a Linton man of the 1944–1963 vintage — also strolled back for a duck against Gillingham CC, Kent, on his way to the same tally of 2. Sixteen years apart, same opposition county, same ignominious arithmetic. The Duck Club calendar has a long memory. 📊 Week in Summary Two matches, one duck — a distinctly lean harvest by the club's usual standards. Whether this reflects improved application at the crease or simply bowlers having an off week is, as ever, a matter for the members' bar rather than this report. Either way, the ducks evaded the net more successfully than David T Lowen evaded B- Lewis. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Jim Hall — 4 ducks in 13 innings (30.77%) 2. David Piper — 4 ducks in 14 innings (28.57%) 3. Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 4. Benny Bell — 4 ducks in 19 innings (21.05%) 5. Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks in 29 innings (13.79%) A tightly bunched top five all locked on four apiece — the percentages are doing the sorting this season, and there's precious little in it. Plenty of cricket left for someone to break clear. 🍩 Until the next fixture, the season presses on and the club's bowlers remain, as always, cricket's most reliable talent scouts for new membership. See you at the crease — or, more likely, on the honours board. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — 29–31 August 1970

1 duck
Week 18 · 29 Aug 1970 – 31 Aug 1970
A remarkably quiet weekend for the Duck Club — ledger-keepers might say too quiet. Three matches went into the book across Saturday, Sunday and the Bank Holiday Monday, and the exclusive zero club admitted precisely one new entry. Consistency, it seems, took the weekend off. 🏏 Monday – Bearsted CC (away) The 1st XI's trip to Bearsted CC produced the weekend's solitary casualty. In at number 9, Nigel O'Toole departed for nought, stumped by Peter Johnson off the bowling of Bryan Elswood. It's O'Toole's 3rd duck of the season and, rather alarmingly, his 3rd in just 5 career innings — a strike rate for nought that places him 127th of 857 in the all-time standings, and marks him out as a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, his very first LPCC innings having ended the same way some seasons back. The apple, it seems, has not fallen far from the tree. Neatly, on this very day back in 1952, club immortal John Thirkell also trudged back for a duck, against A Levick XI. Thirkell, who graced Linton Park from 1943 to 1970, sits proudly at #1 of 857 all-time with a staggering 67 career ducks and no fewer than four Duck of the Year titles to his name (1952, 1954, 1955, 1967) — proof, if proof were needed, that even the very greatest of failures can become a career. 📊 Week in Summary One duck from three matches is a lean return by Duck Club standards — the other two fixtures passed by disappointingly fowl-free. Still, quality over quantity: O'Toole keeps the season ticking over nicely. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard — Top 5 1. David Piper — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.77%) 2. Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.77%) 3. Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks / 14 innings (28.57%) 4. Benny Bell — 4 ducks / 18 innings (22.22%) 5. Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks / 28 innings (14.29%) A tight scrap at the summit, four players locked together on four ducks apiece — plenty of season left for someone to break clear 🍩. 🦆 Roll up for the next fixture, LPCC — the zero club always has room for one more.

🦆 Duck Report — 22 to 26 August 1970 🍩

7 ducks
Week 17 · 22 Aug 1970 – 26 Aug 1970
Three fixtures, seven noughts, and the exclusive zero club welcomes its usual mix of old hands and one very fresh face. On with the ceremony. Saturday 22 August — v Aylesford Paper Mills CC Tim Thirkell fell caught Sprigmore bowled Peters for his 3rd duck of the season and 9th of a well-decorated career (30 innings, 45th of 857 all-time) — a fully paid-up member of the Full House, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, and one half of Quacking Together with none other than season pacesetter Jim Hall. On this very day in 1953, J Barclay (1953–54) also strolled back for nought against Charles Arkcoll — some anniversaries just won't leave a man alone. Sunday 23 August — v Mersham Hatch (away) Four for the price of one this week. Robert Chance departed caught Smith bowled Jeanes, his 1st of the season and 5th career (92 innings, 78th all-time). Then John Ames — 3rd on the all-time leaderboard with a mighty 332 innings behind him — was run out for his 1st of the season and a staggering 43rd career duck, adding another line to an already crowded honours cabinet (Ducks in a Row, Homebody top 10, Only Two Quacks twice over). Nigel O'Toole, lbw to F.H. Ames, took his 2nd of the season and career alike (a veteran of the Debutante Duck club from an earlier innings), while Tony Gauna rounded things off caught Jeanes bowled Holbrook for his own 2nd of the season and career. Splendidly, all three of Chance, O'Toole and Gauna share a parallel — D Simmons also ducked on this exact date back in 1952, so whatever was in the water at Mersham Hatch clearly has form. Wednesday 26 August — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC Paul Bowles was bowled by Mukul Lall for his 1st of the season and 20th career duck (133 innings, 16th all-time), a two-time Duck of the Year (1966, 1968) and, fittingly, his on-this-day parallel is 1962's J Ames — the very same John Ames who was busy adding to his own tally elsewhere this week. Then Steve Gower marked his senior debut in the grandest fashion available, caught Sparling bowled Lall for a genuine golden entry into the record books — first innings, first duck, an instant and unqualified Debutante Duck. Welcome to the club. Week in brief Seven ducks, three matches, Mersham Hatch doing the heaviest lifting with four in a single innings. Debuts don't come more efficient than Steve Gower's. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. David Piper — 4 ducks/12 inns (33.3%) 2. Jim Hall — 4 ducks/12 inns (33.3%) 3. Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks/14 inns (28.6%) 4. Benny Bell — 4 ducks/17 inns (23.5%) 5. Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks/26 inns (15.4%) A tight top of the table as we head towards the business end of the season. Next fixture, more names, more noughts — the Duck Club never sleeps. 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 15 August 1970

2 ducks
Week 16 · 15 Aug 1970 – 16 Aug 1970
A lean weekend for the exclusive zero club, but what it lacked in volume it made up for in pedigree — both of this week's noughts came from the same unhappy trip to Bowaters CC, and both carry some proper history behind them. 1st XI — away at Bowaters CC Mike Iliffe, promoted to number 2, was bowled by Graham Piesley without scoring — his 4th duck of the season and 16th of a career now running to 101 innings, good enough for 24th on the all-time list of 856. Splendidly, on this very calendar date in 1964, club immortal John Thirkell also strolled back for nought against these same opponents — Thirkell (1943–1970) sits top of the all-time charts on 67 ducks and a small library of Duck of the Year titles, so Mike keeps distinguished company on the Bowaters cursed-fixture list. At 7, Tim Thirkell was run out by Nick Nicholls for his 2nd of the season and 8th career duck, 54th of 856 all-time and a proud member of the Full House board — a duck at every position 1 to 11, no small feat of versatility in failure. Tim's own on-this-day parallel is, delightfully, his father: John Thirkell made his identical duck against Bowaters on this same date back in 1964, six years before his son repeated the family trick almost to the day. A chip off the old block in the most literal sense — the Thirkell name already carries real weight in these records, with sibling Nigel also on 2 career ducks and cousin Stuart on 1. Tim also carries Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top-10 honours, plus Quacking Together with Jim Hall for company. Week in summary Two matches, two ducks, both wrapped into one grim trip to Bowaters Green — the rest of the club's batting order kept its nerve elsewhere. Consistency of a very particular kind from the Thirkell family this week, generations apart but united in the art of not scoring. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - David Piper — 4 ducks / 11 innings (36.4%) - Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 12 innings (33.3%) - Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks / 13 innings (30.8%) - Benny Bell — 4 ducks / 16 innings (25.0%) - Mike Iliffe — 4 ducks / 24 innings (16.7%) Mike's weekend duck keeps him ticking over nicely in 5th, though the top of the table remains a fierce four-way scrap. 🥇🍩 Next fixture awaits, and the zero club never sleeps for long — see you at the crease, or rather, on the way back from it. 🦆🏏0️⃣

On this same date in 1940, J Short was making the identical walk back against Boughton Monchelsea CC. Alan Cradduck came and went at seven, bowled for his 4th duck of the season — which happens to also be his 4th career duck, in just 12 innings, 82nd all-time. A card-carrying Debutante Duck, Ducks in a Row member, and one half of the Quacking Together honour with Ron Hickman. J Short, 1940, keeps him company down the years too. Percy Brattle rounded things off at nine, also undone by the same bowler — his 2nd duck of the season, 15th of his career from 73 innings, 25th all-time, and a past Duck of the Year (1969) dining out on old glories. Something of a family trade, this — kin Frank Brattle carries 37 career ducks and Paul Brattle another 3. J Short once more for company. Three LPCC wickets, three ducks, nought runs conceded in doing it — the Paddock Wood opener can consider himself an honorary Duck Club patron this week. Week in summary 4 ducks from 2 matches — a quiet one by Linton standards, but concentrated with menace: three of the four fell in the very same innings to the very same bowler. The art of not scoring remains alive and well. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - David Piper — 4 ducks / 10 inns (40.0%) - Jim Hall — 4 ducks / 10 inns (40.0%) - Alan Cradduck — 4 ducks / 12 inns (33.33%) - Benny Bell — 4 ducks / 15 inns (26.67%) - Richard Ashby — 3 ducks / 9 inns (33.33%) A four-way scrap at the top with the season entering its final stretch. Next fixture awaits — bring your bats, if only for the Duck Club's sake. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 18-19 July 1970

1 duck
Week 12 · 18 Jul 1970 – 19 Jul 1970
A quiet weekend for the Hunting Season, but not entirely feather-free... 1st XI — Away at Tilling Steven (Saturday 18th) David Piper, promoted to number six, walked back for nought — stumped by T Mitchell off the bowling of D Carcary. It's Piper's 3rd duck of the season and his 3rd career duck from 9 innings, keeping him at a steady 112th in Linton's all-time roll of 854 hopefuls. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1964, club great Frank Brattle also fell for a duck, against Rumwood CC no less — Brattle, who turned out for Linton from 1937 to 1970, sits 5th on the all-time list with a mighty 37 to his name.
