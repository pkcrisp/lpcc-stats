🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1972 · 1972 — 1 match

Sat 30 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv BC Bexhill CC · HomeformDLDLW

Saturday saw Bexhill CC roll into Linton Park for what turned out to be a proper old-fashioned battle of attrition, and I have to say winning the toss was the easy part of my afternoon — deciding to bat was, thankfully, a decision that needed no second thought.

The lads made hay in the sunshine. John Ames played the innings of the day, striding well past his fifty to finish on 84, with Cliff Anscombe chipping in handsomely with 45. I found a bit of form myself, seeing us home unbeaten on 43 as we closed on a healthy 204 for 3 from our 34 overs — the sort of total that lets a captain enjoy his tea rather than gnaw through it.

Out in the field, it was Bexhill's turn to graft, and graft they did. G Elliott's departure for a duck set the tone early, and though Martin Grimes (22) and Michael Dawes (20) did their level best to steady things, wickets kept tumbling around them. I managed to get among them myself with figures of 2 for 14, while Brian Fuller bowled with real discipline — 2 for 28 and six maidens to show for his patience — and Jon Harris weighed in with 2 for 40. It all added up to Bexhill closing on 114 for 8, well short but not quite short enough for us to force the issue in the overs remaining.

So honours shared, and a draw it was — no crime in that, with the season's charts still shaping up nicely (I'm told I sit third in the run-scoring table, with Brian's 30 wickets keeping him well clear at the top). A fair result, fairly earned, and a thoroughly pleasant way to spend a Saturday.

🗓️ 23 Sep – 29 Sep 1972 · 1972 — 2 matches

Sun 24 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDDLDL

We lost the toss on Sunday and were sent in the field, which on a warm September afternoon is rarely the invitation one hopes for. Anchorians settled in and made themselves quite at home, closing on 141/5 from their 46 overs, with T Aitchson unbeaten on 35 and useful support from R Durbridge (29) and G Morris (27). Credit where due — Dick Langridge bowled beautifully for 2-16, Allan Cooper chipped in with 2-35 and five miserly maidens besides, and Nigel Thirkell took 1-21. Poor Cliff Anscombe, meanwhile, had one of those afternoons behind the stumps that goalkeepers dream about forgetting, with 21 byes going begging.

Chasing 142, we made distinctly hard work of it, stumbling to 142/9 off 32.3 overs before getting over the line. John Ames was the mainstay with a fine 46, well supported by Allan Cooper's 24, while Colin Dicker's golden duck rather summed up the nervous energy in the middle order. Still, a win's a win, and by one wicket it doesn't get much tighter than that.

A nod, too, to the record books: Allan Cooper's brace nudged him past Brian Fuller and into 25th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list, while in this season's charts Tim Thirkell sits third with 226 runs and Cooper himself third with 21 wickets. Two points, thoroughly earned, and a night for the lads to sleep soundly — even if Cliff's gloves may need a stern talking-to before next Sunday.

Sat 23 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · AwayformWDDLD

The road to West Farleigh on Saturday brought us about as agonising a defeat as this fixture list has served up in years. I lost the toss, we were asked to chase, and out we trooped into the field with nothing but hope and a new ball. Trevor Back and Micky Back put bat to leather with 49 and 35 respectively, and 160 all out looked a decent total off 36.3 overs. But Brian Fuller was magnificent, ripping through their order for 5-19 — a thoroughly deserved five-for — while Ron Hickman, miserly as ever with three maidens, chipped in with 2-58 and Paul Bowles took 1-13. Don Fermor, I. Edwards and John P Day all departed without troubling the scorers, which kept us in the hunt.

Chasing 160, Paul Bowles again showed the way with a well-crafted fifty, 56 in all, and Paul Brattle added a useful 28. I managed 32 myself, though rather too many of us — Tim Thirkell and Terry Whittaker among them — went the way of the duck, and 49.3 overs later we were all out for 158.

Two runs. Two wretched runs separated triumph from the long drive home in silence. Ron Hickman's afternoon wasn't entirely without cheer, mind — those two wickets nudged him past Ray Howard into 16th on the club's all-time list. Small consolations aside, this was a match we should have won and didn't. We'll be better for it next week.

🗓️ 16 Sep – 22 Sep 1972 · 1972 — 2 matches

Sun 17 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv MH Mersham Hatch · HomeformDWDDL

John Ames anchored the innings with a fine 48, and with Allan Cooper's 39 and Dick Langridge's unbeaten 26 either side of him, we crept to a respectable 189/7 from our 45 overs — helped along, it must be said, by a generous 26 extras from a Mersham Hatch attack in no mood for discipline. I can't claim any credit for the runs myself, mind: I strode out, had a look, and was back in the pavilion for a duck before my pads had warmed up. We shall speak no more of it.

In the field the story was rather different. Ron Hickman was superb, prising out 3-32 with three tight maidens thrown in, and in doing so quietly nudged his way past Herbert Rayner into 17th on the club's all-time wicket-takers list — a nice landmark on an otherwise scrappy afternoon. Allan Cooper (1-20) and Tony Gauna (1-32) chipped in dutifully, and between them they kept Mersham Hatch to 95/5 from 21.4 overs, K. Batchelor's 25 and W. Jeanes' 24 the main resistance. Eight byes on our card, though, suggest poor Cliff Anscombe had a torrid time of it behind the stumps.

So it finished all square, honours shared, neither side able to force the issue. A draw, then — the great leveller of village cricket, and about right on the balance of a day where my own personal highlight was, alas, the tea urn.

Sat 16 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformLDWDD

Saturday the 16th of September found us playing host to Charles Arkcoll, and my luck deserted me before a ball was bowled — lost the toss, and duly found myself leading the lads into the field rather than pads-on in the pavilion. Still, there are worse fates on a fine September afternoon than an hour or two of honest toil in the outfield.

And honest toil it was, rewarded handsomely. Arkcoll were shot out for a modest 86 in 29.3 overs, Brian Aiston (25) and Keith Filmer (20) offering the only real resistance while John Mighall and Vic Wellard departed without troubling the scorers. The bowling was a thing of beauty: Terry Donovan produced a wonderful 5-16, the sort of spell that has opposition batsmen muttering into their tea, Tony Gauna toiled for 3-34 with four miserly maidens, and Nigel Thirkell chipped in with a tidy 2-2.

Eighty-six should have been a formality. It was not. We subsided to 67 all out in 34.2 overs, Mike Iliffe's blob rather setting the tone, and somehow contrived to lose by 19 runs — the sort of batting collapse that makes a captain reach for something stronger than tea.

A five-for from Donovan deserved better support from the willow than it got. Small consolation that the season ticks on regardless: Tim Thirkell sits third in the 1st XI run charts with 212 to his name, and Tony Gauna's efforts here move him to fourth in the wicket charts with 18. We'll dust ourselves down and be better for it next week — cricket, like the English weather, has a habit of humbling you just when you think you've got its measure.

🗓️ 09 Sep – 15 Sep 1972 · 1972 — 1 match

Sun 10 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv BC Bexhill CC · AwayformLLDWD

Sunday 10 September saw us motor over to Bexhill CC, coin duly won and battle plan set: bat first and see what the square would give us.

It gave us 150 for 4 off our 49 overs, most of it courtesy of Paul Bowles, who middled his way to 90 not out and, en route, brought up 4,000 career runs for the club — a landmark worth more than one round at the bar. Robert Chance will not want reminded of his contribution, back in the pavilion for a duck before he'd troubled the scorers, but these things happen to the best of us.

In the field we squeezed Bexhill to 147 for 8 from 36.2 overs, J Wisden's 40 the main resistance, with Neil Harding (29) and X Stephens (25) chipping in round him. Tony Gauna was the pick with 3 for 34, Paul Bowles backed up his batting with 3 for 49, and Ron Hickman took 2 for 38, while Allan Cooper strangled the scoring with four maidens and Martyn Cowley, A Hopkins and Robert Saxby all departed for ducks of their own. I managed a modest 19 with the bat, more supporting cast than leading man.

In the end honours were shared, neither side able to force it home. A fair result, and with Bowles now sitting proudly atop both our run and wicket charts for the season, a very good day to be his captain.

🗓️ 02 Sep – 08 Sep 1972 · 1972 — 2 matches

Sun 03 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv Staplehurst CC · AwayformDLLDW

Lost the toss again — I'm beginning to suspect the Staplehurst coin has a grudge against me personally — and duly found ourselves in the field on a Sunday that promised more graft than glory. They made hay of the opportunity, racking up 201 for 8 off their 49.4 overs, built chiefly around C Rogers's fine 89, with A West unbeaten on 30 and P Holland chipping in a watchful 29. It was the sort of total that makes a captain's tea taste slightly bitter.

Credit where it's due, though: Paul Bowles was in unplayable form, spinning his way to 5 for 72 and doing most of the damage himself, while Ron Hickman weighed in with 2 for 62. Tony Gauna bowled with the miserly discipline of a man guarding his allotment, three maidens and not a run given away lightly. J Munn and B Pudney both departed without troubling the scorers — small mercies gratefully accepted.

Chasing 202, we made a decent fist of it without quite getting there, closing on 166 for 8 from 43 overs. Bowles, not content with his bowling heroics, top-scored with a superb 68, well supported by Allan Cooper's 31 and my own patient 27. Poor Benny Bell fell for a duck, joining the club's growing collection of them this weekend.

Worth noting: those five wickets nudged Paul past Dick Langridge into 7th on the club's all-time list — quietly historic stuff amid the Sunday scones. In the end, honours were shared, neither side able to force the issue. A draw, then — fair reflection of a match where effort outstripped result, and nobody's pride was too badly dented.

Sat 02 Sep 1972 🧢 1st XIv Whitstable CC · AwayformDDLLD

Whitstable, 2 September 1972 — and never let it be said we do things the easy way.

Lost the toss, put out to field, and after Whitstable had crawled to 87 all out in 32.5 overs I confess a certain smugness set in. Rightly so, perhaps: Graham Mountford bowled like a man possessed, 6 for 42 and a five-for thoroughly earned, ably supported by Tony Gauna (2-19) and the ever-parsimonious Allan Cooper, whose 3 maidens on the way to 1 for 23 told their own story. A- Collinson's 26 was the only real resistance the home side could muster.

Then, as is traditional at Linton Park, we set about making a straightforward chase look like open-heart surgery. Wakeford, Tritton and Nutten all departed without troubling the scorers, and poor Jon Harris contributed the golden variety of nothing, but Allan Cooper — not content with his heroics in the field — dug in for 38 to drag us, wheezing and stumbling, over the line by two wickets with 88 for 8 from 36.5 overs. A committee-room finish if ever there was one.

Small mercies and larger footnotes: Cooper's solitary wicket nudged him past Ernest Cruttenden into 25th on the club's all-time list, and he now sits third in this season's bowling table with 18 — Andrew Moss, still imperious with 363 runs, remains out on his own at the top. All told, a scrappy, nervy, thoroughly satisfying afternoon. We'll take it.

🗓️ 26 Aug – 01 Sep 1972 · 1972 — 2 matches

Wed 30 Aug 1972 🧢 Midweek XIv Gravesend CC · HomeformLDDDL

From the captain's chair — Wednesday 30 August 1972, Gravesend CC at home.

Lost the toss again, and they had no hesitation putting us out in the field — I'm beginning to suspect my coin-calling is the weakest part of my captaincy. Still, the lads made light work of it. Gravesend were bowled out for 113 in 42.2 overs, with only P Rue, past his fifty on 52, giving us any real bother. Paul Bowles was simply unplayable, running through them with 6 for 36 off seven miserly maidens, ably backed up by Dick Langridge's 3 for 15 and Nigel Thirkell's 1 for 19. Three of theirs departed without so much as a whisker on the scoreboard, which felt almost unfair to watch.

Chasing 113 should have been a formality, and in the end it was, though we made rather more drama of it than necessary, stumbling to 117 for 5 in 25.5 overs. Andrew Moss led the reply with a fine 41, well supported by Alan Crowest's 33, while poor Ray Grayland's duck will not trouble him for long given the result. Twenty-one byes tells you all you need to know about Steve Gower's afternoon behind the stumps — a testing day indeed.

Two notable landmarks amid the runs: Moss's 41 lifts him past George Champion into 14th on the club's all-time run charts, and Langridge's three wickets edge him past Bowles into 7th among the all-time wicket-takers.

A five-wicket win, two points, and thoroughly deserved.

Sun 27 Aug 1972 🧢 Sunday NVKCv TC Troon CC · AwayformWWWWW

John Thirkell here, filing from the Troon road trip of Sunday 27th August — and what a rollercoaster it turned out to be.

Winning the toss, I had few doubts: bat first, set a target, let the lads enjoy the sunshine before Troon got their turn. And for a good while it looked the right call. Paul Bowles played beautifully for 48, Cliff Anscombe chipped in a handsome 45, and Andrew Moss's 25 gave the innings some proper backbone. But cricket has a way of humbling a captain, and our lower order rather fell off a cliff — myself, Graham Mountford and Ron Hickman all trooping back for ducks, a sorry little procession that the scorebook will remember long after the rest of us have forgotten it. Still, 167/9 from our forty overs felt like something to bowl at.

Sadly, "something to bowl at" is not the same as "enough." Troon's openers simply refused to be parted, J Spry motoring to 88 not out and B Carter matching him stride for stride with 74 not out, and by the 35th over they'd strolled home by nine wickets. Graham Mountford was our pick of the attack with 1-39, and Allan Cooper bowled tidily with two maidens, but there was little any of us could do against batting of that class. My own contribution — a duck, quickly forgotten, we hope — did nothing to stem the tide.

Not our day, then, and a chastening one at that. Heads up, back to the nets, and onward to the next fixture.

Wed 23 Aug 1972 🧢 Midweek XIv Gravesend CC · AwayformWLDDD

Mike Iliffe's Match Report — Gravesend CC, Wednesday 23 August 1972

We lost the toss, which these days feels less like bad luck and more like Gravesend's home comfort blanket, and were sent out to field under a sky that promised more than it delivered. P Rue's 43 did the bulk of the damage, with W Carter (27) and Gaynor Kingston (20) chipping in to nudge the total to 139 all out. Credit where it's due, though — John Lambert bowled beautifully for 4-34, four maidens and all, well backed up by Ron Hickman's 4-36 and Nigel Thirkell's 1-25. And M Youseman departed without so much as troubling the scorers, which always cheers a fielding side up no end.

Chasing 140 should have been a formality on paper, and paper, as ever, proved a poor substitute for application. I got us going with 23, R- Philips matched it unbeaten, and Steve Gower's 20 kept hope alive, but wickets tumbled around us and we were bowled out for 138 in 41.5 overs — with Nigel Thirkell and John Lambert both suffering the walk of shame for ducks. Even our extras, a generous 29, couldn't quite close the gap.

One run. One solitary, agonising run short. It's the kind of defeat that makes you replay every misjudged single on the drive home. Not our day, then — heads up, chins out, and back to the nets.

Sat 19 Aug 1972 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · AwayformWDDDL

1972 saw us hosting no tie – we travelled to Bowaters, and having won the toss on a Saturday that felt made for batting, I had no hesitation whatsoever in sending us in. Wisdom, for once, was rewarded, more or less: 150 for 8 off 44.3 overs is not a total to frighten anyone, but it had a certain honest, workmanlike charm to it. Eight wickets and a good deal of pride the worse for wear, we trudged from the field knowing full well we'd been outplayed rather than unlucky. Still, Basil Sears sitting third in the season's run charts and Terry Donovan fifth among the wicket-takers offer some comfort that the summer isn't entirely lost. We'll be better for it next week — we rather have to be.

🗓️ 12 Aug – 18 Aug 1972 · 1972 — 2 matches

Sun 13 Aug 1972 🧢 1st XIv Sibton Park CC · HomeformLWDDD

I elected to bat when we won the toss on Sunday against Sibton Park, and with hindsight it's fair to say the pitch had rather different ideas to mine. Twenty all out in 11.4 overs is not a total you build an innings report around so much as a total you apologise for, and when three of us — Tim Thirkell, Paul Brattle and, yes, your captain — all trooped back for ducks, it began to feel less like a cricket match and more like a synchronised swimming display. I include myself in that unhappy trio; twenty balls faced between us and not a run to show for it, which is the sort of statistic that makes a man take a long, hard look at his guard.

Twenty to defend was always going to ask a lot of the bowling, and Sibton Park duly strolled home to 21 without loss in under seven overs. No fightback, no hint of a twist — just a chase completed with the minimum of fuss, which stung rather more than a close finish would have.

Elsewhere in the season, Tim Thirkell continues his solid form and sits fourth in the 1972 run charts on 142, while Allan Cooper's 17 wickets have him second in the bowling table — small consolations on an otherwise grim afternoon.

Twenty all out, beaten without loss: not our finest hour by any measure. Heads up, and back to the nets.

Sat 12 Aug 1972 🧢 1st XIv Headcorn CC · AwayformDLWDD

Saturday 12th August 1972, and off we trundled to Headcorn with the sun doing its best village-green impression. I won the toss and had no hesitation in taking first knock — the pitch looked one for batting early and thinking later, and I still maintain it was the right call.

Basil Sears, though, was a picture of composure throughout, ending unbeaten on 22 and shouldering the lion's share of the scoring almost single-handed while the rest of us admired from the other end.

Out we went to defend it, braced for a long afternoon in the field — and somehow got one. Headcorn were bowled out for nought, a scorecard entry that still raises an eyebrow whenever I glance back at it, and between the two efforts honours were shared: neither side could quite land the knockout blow, and a draw it was.

Basil continues to be the man in form, now sitting third in our 1972 first-XI run charts with 170 for the season — a fine return, and one the rest of us might do well to study. As for the match itself: not a classic for the purists, but a scrap, a stalemate, and, all things considered, a fair result honestly earned.

🗓️ 05 Aug – 11 Aug 1972 · 1972 — 3 matches

Wed 09 Aug 1972 🧢 Midweek XIv SC Sennocke CC · AwayformLWLDD

Wednesday 9th August, and off we trundled to Sennocke, the sun (for once) obliging.

Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in taking first knock — a decision I shall now claim full credit for. C. Reader played the innings of the day, 85 crisp and correct runs that put Sennocke's attack thoroughly out of sorts, with Cliff Anscombe chipping in a handy 32 and Dick Langridge seeing us safely home unbeaten on 22. Declaring, or rather simply running out of overs, at 184/4 from our 45.3, we felt we had enough on the board, though not so much that we could put our feet up in the pavilion.

And so it proved. Sennocke set about the chase with R. Cheeseman the chief architect of their reply, 51 well-made runs, backed up by J. Wharton's 38 and M. Robinson's 31. Paul Bowles bowled beautifully for his 2-50 and leads our wicket charts nicely as a result, while Alf Thorne was a model of miserly restraint — two maidens and 1-19 — and Allan Cooper picked up 1-39. Spare a thought for poor Peter Tapper, sent back for a duck without so much as troubling the scorers. My own afternoon behind the stumps was rather less distinguished, nine byes escaping my gloves like naughty schoolboys.

In the end Sennocke closed on 180/5, five runs shy and one wicket to spare — honours, quite fairly, shared. A draw that satisfied nobody and disappointed nobody, which is, I suppose, cricket's way of telling us both sides deserved better.

Sun 06 Aug 1972 🧢 Sunday NVKCv BC Blunham CC · HomeformWWWWW

Won the toss on a warm Sunday against Blunham, and elected to bat — the right call, as it turned out, though the lads made rather harder work of it than the scorebook perhaps suggests. Forty overs brought us 143 for 5, built patiently around John Ames's fine 39, with Cliff Anscombe chipping in 30 and myself adding 29 down the order. Poor Andrew Moss will want to forget his afternoon entirely, back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers — these things happen to the best of us, and no doubt he'll have his revenge before the season's out.

In the field we were rather more clinical. Blunham never quite got hold of the total, subsiding to 121 all out in 35.4 overs despite a spirited 41 from P Fordham and useful support from P Ranson's 21. Three of their number — Ashwell, Lawson and Watson — departed without scoring, victims of an attack in irresistible mood. Allan Cooper was the chief tormentor, a beautifully mean 4 for 18 including three maidens, with Tony Gauna (3-24) and Graham Mountford (1-20) mopping up the rest.
