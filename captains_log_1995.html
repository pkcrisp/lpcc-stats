🗓️ 30 Sep – 06 Oct 1995 · 1995 — 1 match

Sat 30 Sep 1995 🧢 2nd XIv YC Yalding CC · HomeformWWWWL

Won the toss, and for once I trusted my own judgement rather than the weather forecast — elected to bat, and it was the right call, even if I say so myself. David Castle and the openers put on 152 before anyone troubled the scorebook, Castle purring to 81, and by the time Mike Caddick had cracked a fine 70 and Mike Elworthy steered us home unbeaten on 29, we'd posted a handsome 226 for 4 off our 42. A total to make Yalding's coach feel slightly unwell over his tea.

And so it proved. They lost a wicket before they'd troubled the umpires for guard — 1 for 1 — and never really recovered their poise, three of our number sending batsmen back without so much as a single to their name. Only Iain Cardew, with a gutsy 58 not out, offered any real resistance. Paul Bowles was superb with the ball, 3 for 28, Jon Harris metronomic with 2 for 12 and five maidens, and I chipped in with 2 for 0 — the sort of figures I shall be quietly reminding people of well into the winter. Poor Roly did leak 11 byes behind the stumps, though nobody's perfect.

Beyond the scoreline, a day of real landmarks: Castle's 12,000th run for the club, Elworthy's 200th appearance, and Caddick climbing past Mike Iliffe into 25th on the all-time list. Two points, 119 runs to the good, and thoroughly, deservedly earned.

🗓️ 23 Sep – 29 Sep 1995 · 1995 — 1 match

Sat 23 Sep 1995 🧢 2nd XIv HC Higham & Mockbeggar CC · HomeformWWWWW

Saturday last, and Higham & Mockbeggar came calling to Linton Park with the sun still holding and the square looking a picture. I called correctly at the toss and never once considered bowling first — some habits die harder than others, and putting the ball down before us on a good pitch has never sat easily with me.

We laboured to 130 all out in 37.3 overs, which tells its own story, though Mike Elworthy batted like a man who knew exactly why he was there, cruising past his fifty to 65 and dragging the total to something respectable almost single-handed. The rest of us, it must be said, offered rather less resistance.

In the field we started brightly enough — Jon Harris bowling five miserly maidens and Dave Todd striking first with 1-22 — but Colin Robson and P Davis put on 81 for the first wicket before we so much as sniffed a breakthrough, and by the time N Scudder finished unbeaten on 27 the game had long slipped from our grasp. Beaten by seven wickets, no arguing with that.

Still, a day with real cause for pride: Mike Elworthy brought up his 200th appearance in Linton colours, a landmark few of us will match, and sits second in the 2nd XI run charts with 354 to his name, with Dave Todd third among the bowlers on 14 wickets. The result stung, but the club goes on. We'll be better for it next week.

Sun 10 Sep 1995 🧢 1st XIv CC Crowborough CC · HomeformLWDDD

Sunday 10 September saw Crowborough CC roll into Linton Park, and having called correctly at the toss, I didn't need long to make up my mind — on a day like that, you bat first and ask questions later.

We made a real fist of it, cruising to 73 for the first wicket before the breakthrough finally came, and settled on 155 for 5 from our 43 overs. I was delighted to lead from the front with a fifty of my own, well supported by Mike Elworthy's 42 and Daniel Thirkell's unbeaten 29 to give the total some proper backbone. Not every contribution can be a heroic one, mind — spare a thought for Richard Ellis, who departed for a golden duck and will no doubt be reminded of it over the winter nets.

With the ball we set about defending it, and Crowborough, to their credit, refused to be shifted either way. Neither side could force the win, and honours were shared in the end — the sort of draw that leaves both dressing rooms mildly satisfied and mildly frustrated in equal measure.

As for the bigger picture, it's pleasing to see the runs adding up — I now sit third in the 1995 first XI charts with 873 for the season, though I'd trade every one of them for a result on a day like this.

Verdict: a fair draw, honestly arrived at — we shall take the point and the fifty, and leave Richard to take the ribbing.

Alas, Larry Martin's 37 and a composed 23 not out from Simon Tripconey saw the Cornishmen home by two wickets.

Not our day, then, but no disgrace either. Heads up, and back to the nets.

Fifteen byes did rather give the game away about poor Clive Newman's afternoon behind the stumps, but we finished on 203 for 6, agonisingly short.

A draw, then — honours shared, nobody quite deserving to lose, and a fine watch for anyone lucky enough to be at Linton Park on a Monday.
