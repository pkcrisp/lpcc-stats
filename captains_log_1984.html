🗓️ 29 Sep – 05 Oct 1984 · 1984 — 1 match

Sat 29 Sep 1984 🧢 1st XIv HC Hastings & St Leonards Priory CC · HomeformWWWLD

Lost the toss on a fine late-September afternoon and were asked to field — hardly the omen I'd hoped for against Hastings & St Leonards Priory, but the lads had other ideas. Alan Booth-Jones was back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers with just 7 on the board, and from there the visitors never quite settled. I found some rhythm with the new ball and kept finding it, finishing with 5-37 including three maidens, while Paul Bowles (2-15) and Ian Brattle (2-25) tidied up around me. Only C Watson, with a battling 27, gave their innings any real shape as they were bowled out for 108 in 43.2 overs. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid time behind the stumps — 8 byes is not a line he'll want read out too loudly at the AGM — but nobody begrudges a keeper an off day on a treacherous one.

With 109 to win, Bob Lines simply strolled to it, cruising to an unbeaten 70 and barely breaking a sweat, ably supported by Brian Piper's composed 28 not out, and we cantered home at 112/1 in 32.5 overs. Nine wickets to the good and two points safely banked — about as tidy an afternoon as club cricket offers. Bob's knock keeps him third in our 1984 run charts with 549, and I'll confess to a quiet satisfaction at leading the wicket column with 90 for the season. A thoroughly deserved win, and a fine one to bank before the autumn draws in.

🗓️ 22 Sep – 28 Sep 1984 · 1984 — 2 matches

Wed 26 Sep 1984 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWWLWD

Wednesday last saw us head to Penshurst Park, and for once the toss fell kindly — I called it right and had no hesitation in taking first knock. Trevor Back set the tone with a well-made fifty, and when we wobbled alarmingly from 12 for 1 to 21 for 4, it was the calm restoration act of Paul Bowles, unbeaten on 53, alongside Jim Hall's watchful 30, that steadied nerves; their fifth-wicket stand of 80 was the innings' backbone. Poor Tim Theobald will want to forget his duck, but a defiant last-wicket flourish of 48 gave our 177 for 7 from 48 overs a rather more respectable look than the middle order had threatened.

Defending it, we never quite let Penshurst settle, an early wicket falling with the board still reading nought. Jim Littlechild, Mark Jenner and John Boyden all departed without troubling the scorers, though P Grayland's fifty-plus 72 and A Robertson's 28 kept us honest for a while. The day, though, belonged to Paul Bowles, whose 6 for 39 was a five-for richly deserved and thoroughly earned. Ian Brattle chipped in with 3 for 37, and I did my own bit too — 1 for 40 with seven miserly maidens to show for my efforts. Jim Hall had a trickier afternoon behind the stumps, 9 byes going begging.

Trevor Back's fifty also nudged him past Gordon Fenner into 25th on the club's all-time run-scorers list, and he now leads our 1984 Midweek XI run charts with 143, with Brattle top of the wicket pile on 15. A fine win by 48 runs — onwards indeed.

Sun 23 Sep 1984 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLWWWL

Jon Harris here, filing this month's despatch from Linton Park on what turned out to be a fine Sunday for cricket, if not quite a decisive one.

Winning the toss, I had no hesitation in taking first knock — and for a while it looked a shrewd call, notwithstanding the alarming sight of 2 for 1 before some of us had properly finished our tea. Andy Woollett, however, was in no mood to let early jitters spoil his afternoon, compiling a superb unbeaten 76 to drag us to a defendable 128 for 9 off our 35 overs. Less gloriously, David Iliffe, Jim Hall and Sean Cook all trudged back for ducks, and the Duck Club ledger groaned under the weight of new entries 🦆 — a hazard of the game we accept with good grace, if gritted teeth.

With the ball, we set about defending our total with real discipline. I managed to find some rhythm, picking up 3 for 8 and chipping in with 18 with the bat for good measure, while Allan Cooper bowled two miserly maidens to choke the runs. Anchorians never quite got away from us, finishing on 47 for 4 off their 16 overs as the game petered into a draw honours shared.

There was a quiet personal footnote too — those 18 runs nudged me up past Cliff Anscombe into 13th on the club's all-time run list, which is either a triumph or simply proof of longevity. A draw, then: no losers, but plenty for both sides to mull over on the drive home.

Wed 19 Sep 1984 🧢 Midweek XIv HC Hawkenbury CC · HomeformLWWLW

I first won the toss and had no hesitation in asking Hawkenbury CC to have a bowl at us — the right call, I felt, even if our top order didn't entirely agree, with the first wicket down for just one run and both Andy Woollett and Mike Elworthy back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. It took the steady application of Bob Lines, 31 well-worked runs, with good support from my own bat — I chipped in a useful 21 and picked up 2-23 with the ball later on — to drag us to a defendable-looking 95/9 from our 47 overs. Not a total to frighten anyone, but on a slow Wednesday evening pitch, enough to work with.

And work with it we did. Hawkenbury lost their first wicket cheaply too, 3-1, and when we got among them properly it turned into a procession — 3-1 became 16-4 before they'd had time to settle, with T Theobald, T Cooper and X Boakes all departing without scoring. Ian Brattle was quite magnificent, 4-11 with seven miserly maidens, and found fine support from Trevor Back's 3-24. Only A Chapman, with a defiant 25, stood between us and victory, dragging them to 82/9 from 41 overs before the close.

For the record books, Trevor Back now sits second in our 1984 run charts and Ian Brattle tops the wicket pile with a dozen scalps — form the club can be proud of.

In the end neither side could quite land the final blow, and honours were shared. A draw, then — but one that felt, in the fading September light, rather like a shared victory for good, honest cricket.

Sun 16 Sep 1984 🧢 1st XIv NS Northend Sunday · HomeformWLWWW

I'm the captain (Jon Harris) writing the club gazette match report — no tools needed, just prose.

The bar was set at 113 all out from 46 overs on Sunday, and if I'm honest it felt about forty runs light before we'd even taken the field. I chipped in with 33 to top-score, for which small mercies were duly given, though quite what the rest of the order were thinking during that mid-innings wobble — 35 for 3 collapsing to 49 for 6 quicker than you could pour a tea — is a question best left for the AGM.

Defending a total like that against Northend Sunday always felt like asking a deckchair to stop a freight train, and so it proved. T Pearce made merry to the tune of 58 not out, ably assisted by M Smith's unbeaten 32 and a brisk 20 from B Fairall, and between them they walked it home at 114 for 1 in 35.3 overs without ever looking remotely troubled. If there was a crumb of comfort in the wreckage, it was Allan Cooper, miserly as ever with four maidens and the solitary wicket to show for a genuinely tidy spell — the sort of bowling that deserved a far kinder scoreline around it.

Small personal footnote amid the gloom: my 33 nudges me past Malcolm Pay into 14th on the club's all-time run charts, which I shall enjoy for approximately as long as it takes to forget the result. Paul Bowles, meanwhile, continues his imperious form atop the 1984 season charts with 1405 runs — some of us are playing a different sport entirely.

Six wickets the margin, and a fair reflection of the afternoon. Not our day. Heads up, back to the nets, and on to the next one.
