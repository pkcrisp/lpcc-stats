🗓️ 22 Sep – 28 Sep 1962 · 1962 — 3 matches

Sun 23 Sep 1962 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformLWWDD

Sunday 23rd September brought Linton Park CC to our ground, and having called correctly at the toss I did what any captain raised on the primacy of runs on the board would do — I stuck us in to bat. Confidence, I confess, was riding rather higher than it had any right to.

What followed will not trouble the history books. Ten for three from two overs is not so much a batting collapse as a batting implosion, the sort of scoreline that has you double-checking the scorebook and wondering whether the umpire miscounted the overs. Three wickets down before the openers had so much as loosened their shoulders properly — one struggles to recall a briefer, more chastening spell at the crease. I shall take my share of the blame for the decision at the toss, though in fairness no amount of captaincy wisdom accounts for what unfolded once bat met ball.

And yet cricket, bless it, has a way of levelling things out. For all our early troubles, honours finished shared — neither side able to force home the advantage, and a draw was the fair and honest result of an afternoon that promised drama and rather delivered farce.

So: a match to file under "character-building" rather than "vintage". We batted like strangers to the game, but we held our nerve to share the spoils, and for that alone the lads deserve a pint at the bar.

Sun 23 Sep 1962 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformDLWWD

As it stands, we shan't be writing the pavilion clock into folklore just yet, but 23 September did give us a match worth savouring. Batting first against Linton Park, we crawled and occasionally cantered to 185/9 off our 41, and I'm rather pleased to say I top-scored with 40 — though I say that with the caveat that John Crellin's 36 and Robert Murray's 33 did the real graft in the engine room. Poor Terry Whittaker, meanwhile, will be quietly hoping the scorebook develops a smudge where his golden duck is recorded; these things happen to the best of us, and I dare say the tea urn heard some choice words on his return to the pavilion.

What lifted the afternoon beyond the ordinary was the tail, who wagged with genuine mischief — a defiant last-wicket stand of 35 that had the Linton Park fielders muttering into their sunhats and wondering when exactly this innings was supposed to end. And amid all that, two proper landmarks: Ray Howard brought up his hundredth appearance in our colours, a milestone that deserves rather more than a line in a gazette, and Roy Clarke quietly ticked over 1,000 career runs for the club — the sort of achievement built one unglamorous single at a time.

In the end neither side could force the issue, and honours were shared. A draw, then — not the stuff of legend, but a thoroughly decent afternoon's cricket, and with Howard's cap and Clarke's runs to toast, nobody left the ground short-changed.

Sat 22 Sep 1962 🧢 1st XIv MC Maidstone YMCA CC · HomeformWWDWW

I called correctly at the toss on a fine September Saturday and, with Maidstone YMCA the visitors, had no hesitation in batting first — a decision I stand by, even if 150/7 from our 42 overs was more workmanlike than thrilling. Ron Aiston played the innings of the day, a really fine 58, and found a willing partner in John Carter, whose 31 helped the pair add a crucial 72 for the fourth wicket and give our total some backbone. Elsewhere the order rather queued up to get back in the pavilion, and poor Jack Knowler's afternoon began and ended with a duck — the walk of shame, as we call it.

Defending 150 was always going to want discipline, and for a long while we had them rattled, an early wicket at 0-1 setting just the tone we wanted. Terry Whittaker bowled tidily for his two maidens, I chipped in with the bat earlier and enjoyed my part in the total, and the wickets were shared nicely between John Carter (2-32), Gordon Fenner (2-45) and J Nolan (1-27) — Carter's the pick of the lot. But P. Johnston played the anchor role beautifully for an unbeaten 41, well supported by Ossie Fryer's 33 and N. Phillips' 28, and just when we thought we had them, their last pair put on an infuriating 57 we could well have done without, though at least L. Smith departed for a second-innings duck of his own. Fourteen byes past Roy Clarke didn't help our cause either.

Beaten by four wickets, then, and no disgrace in it — but chances went begging that shouldn't have. We'll be better for it next week.

A word too for Alan Tucker, who with those four wickets has climbed past Dennis Smith into 21st on the club's all-time list. Two points, well and truly earned: victory by 89 runs, and Staplehurst sent home to reflect on where it all went wrong.

Sat 15 Sep 1962 🧢 1st XIv BC Bowaters CC · HomeformLLWWD

John Thirkell, Captain — Linton Park CC

Well, the coin let us down again on Saturday — I'm beginning to suspect it's rigged — and Bowaters CC took first use of what looked a fine batting deck. True to form they duly cashed in, openers putting on 87 before we so much as glimpsed a breakthrough, and John Woollett cruised to 63 while Harry Jeffery (39) and Roy Cole (35) filled their boots either side of him. Our reply began disastrously — 0 for 1 before some of us had finished doing our buttons up — and Bob Bourne and William Baker departed without scoring, which is never the ideal way to settle the nerves. But cricket has a habit of rewarding patience, and I found some form to carry my bat for an unbeaten 102, with Ron Aiston a splendid ally in a stand worth 87. The tail, God bless them, wagged merrily to add a defiant 49 and get us home by two wickets — Frank Brattle and Roy Clarke fell without scoring, but nobody remembers the sinners when the side wins.

A cracking, nerve-jangling victory, and Terry's seven-for has apparently nudged him up the club's all-time wicket-takers list. Onwards, gentlemen — same again next week, please, minus the ducks.
