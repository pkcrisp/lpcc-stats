🦆 Duck News — Married v Singles Special, 22 September 1963 🦆

6 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1963
The season closer at Linton Park delivered exactly what tradition demands: the Married men and the Singles going toe to toe, and six fresh entries for the exclusive zero club before anyone could reach for their tea. Spookily, this very date in 1951 also claimed a duck against Hunton Wanderers CC courtesy of John --'s Taylor (15 career, 16th all time) — the ghost of '51 was evidently watching over all six victims today. 🏏 Married Bob Arnold, in at 5, was trapped lbw by Len Conway for his 6th duck of the season and 45th of an extraordinary career — good enough for 2nd all time out of 783 names. He already wears the Duck of the Year 1963 crown and now extends his Ducks in a Row honour to four on the bounce. Consistency of a very particular kind. Cliff Anscombe fell at 7, bowled by Alan Tucker, for his first duck of the season and 13th career nought from 43 innings, ranking 20th all time. A paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club and a former (1957) Duck of the Year, proving the family tradition of failure has deep roots. 🏏 Singles Norman Mattingley, at 4, was stumped — by Married's own Cliff Anscombe, no less, off Gordon Fenner — for his 2nd duck of the season and career alike, just three innings into his LPCC journey. Rank 151 of 783 and climbing fast. Barry Langridge, at 9, suffered the ultimate indignity: bowled by his own kin, Dick Langridge, earning the exact "Quacked by Kin (1963)" honour the board records. His 5th duck of the season and career, from 14 innings, sits him at 53rd all time. Being undone by family truly is a triumph through adversity. C- Haylor, at 10, was bowled by Terry Donovan for his 3rd of the season and 14th career duck in just 30 innings, ranking a lofty 18th all time. This confirms his Runaway Duck (1963) status — a whole season without troubling the scorers — while his Retirement Duck club membership remains a standing honour from a past farewell, nothing more. Michael Chambers, at 11, became Terry Donovan's second victim in a row, also bowled, for his 5th duck of the season and 8th career, from 27 innings, ranking 36th all time. Donovan can add "brace of ducks" to his afternoon's work. 📊 Week in Summary One match, six ducks, and Terry Donovan doing his bit for the honours board with a tidy two-for. The art of not scoring was alive and well at both ends of the wicket today. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Bob Arnold — 12 innings, 6 ducks, 50.0% - Dick Langridge — 18 innings, 6 ducks, 33.33% - John Thirkell — 32 innings, 6 ducks, 18.75% - Michael Chambers — 10 innings, 5 ducks, 50.0% - Barry Langridge — 14 innings, 5 ducks, 35.71% That's the season wrapped for now — but the club never truly closes for business. Next time bat and ball meet at Linton Park, the honours board awaits its next volunteers. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 14-15 September 1963

2 ducks
Week 21 · 14 Sep 1963 – 15 Sep 1963
A quiet weekend by the Club's usual standards of catastrophe, but Staplehurst CC still managed to prise two names loose for the honours board. 1st XI v Staplehurst CC (away) Norman Mattingley, at four, was trapped lbw by C. Smith and departs with his very first senior duck — 1 for the season, 1 for the career, and only his second innings in whites. He joins the exclusive zero club at 299th of 783 in the all-time standings, a debut of the least distinguished kind. Curiously, on this very day back in 1962, Frank Brattle (1937-1963, 19 career ducks, 10th of 783 all-time) was also walking back for nought against Bowaters CC — history, it seems, enjoys a bit of symmetry. Down at seven, Dick Langridge was stumped by Jack Eastland off the bowling of W. Tipples, the wicketkeeper doing the tidying-up honours. It's his 6th duck of the season and, rather more alarmingly, his 6th career duck from just 22 innings — a rate that puts him firmly among the season's frontrunners. He sits 45th of 783 all-time, and — fittingly — the same calendar date in 1962 also claimed a duck from Frank Brattle against Bowaters, making this something of an anniversary fixture for nought-scoring. Dick, incidentally, is not carrying the family torch alone — his relative Barry Langridge has 4 career ducks of his own, so the art of not scoring appears to run in the blood. Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, both surrendered in the same Staplehurst encounter — a modest haul, but every entry on the honours board started somewhere. Consistency of a very particular kind continues at Linton Park. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Dick Langridge — 17 innings, 6 ducks, 35.3% - John Thirkell — 31 innings, 6 ducks, 19.4% - Bob Arnold — 11 innings, 5 ducks, 45.5% - David Gale — 20 innings, 5 ducks, 25.0% - Roy Clarke — 31 innings, 5 ducks, 16.1% Dick Langridge leads the pack outright, but Bob Arnold's 45.5% strike rate for nought is the kind of "efficiency" nobody should be proud of and everybody secretly is. 🍩 The season rolls on, and Linton Park's batsmen will get further chances to add to this illustrious ledger at the next fixture. The Duck Club never sleeps — we watch, we wait, we celebrate. 🦆

🦆 Duck Report — 7th–8th September 1963

2 ducks
Week 20 · 7 Sep 1963 – 8 Sep 1963
Two matches, two ducks, and the exclusive zero club welcomed its usual suspects down at Maidstone this weekend. Modest numbers, but never let it be said the Duck Club deals in quantity over quality. 🏏 1st XI — St Lukes Maidstone CC (Home) Opening the innings and, evidently, opening the account for the weekend, Frank Brattle was bowled by W. Summerfield without scoring — his 2nd duck of the season and a hefty 19th of an already storied career, good enough for 10th on the all-time list of 783. A fine display of the art of not scoring, and one that clearly runs in the family: his kin Percy Brattle already carries 5 career ducks of his own. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1958 the great Cliff Anscombe (1957–1963, Debutante Duck, Duck of the Year 1957) also trudged off for nought, against Anchorians CC — some anniversaries just keep quacking. At number 4, Terry Donovan fell to George Winter without troubling the scorer, his 2nd duck of the season and 2nd of his career from 24 innings, sitting a modest 162nd of 783 all time. Terry remains the club's most industrious "Full House nearly-man" — having ducked from 10 of the 11 possible batting positions, he sits agonisingly one spot short of a complete set, a feat requiring patience, misfortune, and remarkable positional versatility. Like Frank, he shares his moment with history: this too is the anniversary of Cliff Anscombe's 1958 duck against Anchorians CC — the Duck Club calendar has a habit of repeating itself. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks from two fixtures, both credited to the 1st XI against St Lukes Maidstone CC — a tidy, if unspectacular, contribution to the 1963 tally. No pairs, no family affairs, no golden ducks this week. Sometimes the art of not scoring is simply a quiet, dignified business. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 - John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 29 innings (20.69%) - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks, 11 innings (45.45%) - Dick Langridge — 5 ducks, 16 innings (31.25%) - David Gale — 5 ducks, 19 innings (26.32%) - Roy Clarke — 5 ducks, 29 innings (17.24%) Bob Arnold's near-50% strike rate for nought remains the standard by which all aspiring duckers should measure their ambition. 🍩 The season rolls on — who'll be next to make that long walk back? Same time next week, ducks permitting. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 DUCK NEWS — 31 August to 1 September 1963

0 ducks
Week 19 · 31 Aug 1963 – 1 Sep 1963
Two fixtures, two full scorecards, and not a single nought to show for it. The exclusive zero club held no induction ceremony this week — every batsman who walked to the crease at Linton Park had the discourtesy to actually trouble the scorers. 🏏 A quiet weekend for quackery Frankly, it happens. Even the most dedicated collectors of dubious distinction need a rest, and this weekend the ducks evaded capture entirely. No golden ducks, no pairs, no father-and-son ignominy, no family affairs for the honours board — just eleven men a side getting on with the rather less interesting business of scoring runs. The Committee notes this is not necessarily cause for celebration; consistency of a very particular kind has been temporarily, and disappointingly, interrupted. Rest assured, normal service — the art of not scoring — will resume. It always does. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard The chase for 1963 Duck of the Year rumbles on undisturbed by this weekend's clean sheet: - John Thirkell — 6 ducks from 28 innings (21.43%) — the volume leader, grinding out noughts with the patience of a man who knows quality takes time - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks from just 11 innings (45.45%) — a quite staggering strike rate, nearly one duck every other knock, the sort of efficiency the rest of the club can only dream of - Dick Langridge — 5 ducks from 16 innings (31.25%) — firmly in the hunt, no signs of slowing - David Gale — 5 ducks from 19 innings (26.32%) — quietly keeping pace - Roy Clarke — 5 ducks from 28 innings (17.86%) — the most economical route to 5, taking the scenic path Bob Arnold's percentage is the standout figure here — at that rate he's practically obliged to add another before the season's out. 📅 Looking ahead The ducks may have evaded the net this week, but the pond is far from empty. With Thirkell out in front and Arnold snapping at his heels on sheer conversion rate, next weekend's fixtures offer every opportunity to put things right. Get out there, gentlemen — the honours board doesn't fill itself. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🦆🍩0️⃣

🦆 LPCC Duck Club — 24 August 1963

2 ducks
Week 18 · 24 Aug 1963
A quiet weekend in the paddy fields, but the ranks against Aylesford Paper Mills CC still swelled by two, and both from the home side's lower order. 🏏 1st XI — Aylesford Paper Mills CC (home) Coming in at number 8, H Mannering edged behind off C- Holden for nought — his 2nd of the season and 2nd of a career now standing at just 4 innings, a startling early strike rate for the zero column. He slots in at 137th of 781 on the all-time list, level for career ducks with a long queue of names including Bob Brigden (1955–1958) and Don Clayton (1961–1962), proving good company is never hard to find down here. Fittingly, on this very calendar day back in 1901, J H Coles was also trudging off for a duck against Yalding CC — some things never change on 24 August. Then at number 11, last man Barry Langridge was trapped lbw by R- David without scoring — his 4th duck of the season and 4th of his career from 13 innings. Barry now sits 63rd of 781 all-time, drawing level on career noughts with the likes of Charles Wycherley (1904–1912) and F E Wallis (1887–1908). There's a family flavour to this one too — Barry joins the club's Quacked by Kin board, dismissed this very season by his own kinsman Dick Langridge, who carries 5 career ducks of his own. And once again, the calendar obliges: J H Coles made his Yalding duck on this same date in 1901, keeping Barry company across the decades. 📋 Week in Summary One match, two ducks, both from the home 1st XI's tail — positions 8 and 11 doing their patriotic duty for the exclusive zero club. No pairs, no golden ducks, no family doubles this week — just honest, unspectacular application of the art of not scoring. Dick Langridge will no doubt be dining out on the Langridge household's shared duck heritage for weeks to come. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard — Top 5 1. 🦆 Duck Report — 17-18 August 1963

6 ducks
Week 17 · 17 Aug 1963 – 18 Aug 1963
Step back in time with us this week as the ledger reopens for the weekend of 17-18 August 1963. Six ducks, two matches, one long-suffering 1st XI doing all the honourable damage. The exclusive zero club was thriving even then. 🏏 Saturday 17th — away at Bowaters CC Three exiles for the price of one on the road. Roy Clarke went first, bowled Roy Ecclestone, his 5th duck of the season and 30th career nought — 4th on the all-time list of 781. A Duck of the Year champion from 1959 and a "Quacking Together" veteran alongside John Ames, Roy has form in the blood too, with brother H Clarke sitting on 3 career ducks of his own. Dick Langridge followed, caught Eustace bowled Chick, also his 5th of the season — a tidy 5 from just 19 innings, 53rd on the all-time chart. His relative Barry Langridge already carries 3 career noughts, so the surname is well represented in these pages. H Mannering rounded off the trio, caught Stanton bowled Chick — his 1st career duck, from only 3 innings, 252nd on the all-time list. Welcome to the club. Curiously, all three fell on the very date, in 1957, that club legend John Thirkell also succumbed to Bowaters CC. Some anniversaries are best left uncelebrated. 🌱 Sunday 18th — home to The Mote CC If Saturday's ducks summoned him, Sunday delivered him. John Thirkell — the club's undisputed King of Noughts on 52 career ducks from 442 innings, three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955) and fixture of the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens — walked out and departed bowled J. Webb without scoring. His 6th of the season sends him clear at the top of this year's race, a crown he wears rather well. Stan Wratten went next, caught Milsted bowled Webb, his 3rd of the season and 20th career duck — 9th on the all-time chart and, like Thirkell, a "Quacking Together" honoree alongside John Ames. Fittingly, it was on this very date in 1956 that Thirkell himself last ducked against Bowaters — the great man's shadow looms large this week. Robert Murray completed the rout, caught Waters bowled Briant, his 2nd of the season and 15th career duck, 17th on the all-time list — falling, like Wratten, on Thirkell's own 1956 anniversary. 📊 Week in Summary Six ducks, two matches, one 1st XI shouldering the lot. Bowaters CC and The Mote CC share the spoils. And after a week of being everyone else's historical comparison, John Thirkell went and reminded the club exactly why he sits top of the pile. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - John Thirkell — 6 ducks, 27 innings (22.2%) - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks, 9 innings (55.6%) - Dick Langridge — 5 ducks, 14 innings (35.7%) - David Gale — 5 ducks, 16 innings (31.3%) - Roy Clarke — 5 ducks, 28 innings (17.9%) Thirkell stands alone at the summit, but with Langridge and Clarke lurking on 5, the chase is well and truly on. Next fixture awaits — who fancies joining the ledger? 🦆🍩

A quiet weekend on the duck-hunting front, but quality over quantity, as ever, and today's solitary entry is a proper heavyweight. 🏏 1st XI — away to Tilling Steven Roy Clarke, promoted to first drop, departed for nought, bowled by B Telfer. No histrionics, no near miss to speak of — just Roy back in the pavilion to add his name to the log once more. This is Roy's 4th duck of the 1963 season and his 29th in a career now spanning 181 innings — figures that plant him at a genuinely rarefied 4th in the entire 780-strong all-time Linton Park standings. That is about as close to the summit of the exclusive zero club as a Duck Club member can climb without actually taking the crown outright. A former Duck of the Year in 1959 and a paid-up member of the "Quacking Together" honours board alongside John Ames, Roy remains one of the great custodians of the art of not scoring — consistency, as they say, of a very particular kind. Fittingly, he's not doing it alone in the family sense either: brother H Clarke carries 3 career ducks of his own, so the Clarke household knows a thing or two about the walk back. And in a pleasing quirk of the calendar — on this day in 1895, J Lampard also made a duck against Maidstone CC, the solitary blemish of his brief 1892–1895 career and good enough for 263rd on the all-time list. Sixty-eight years apart, same date, same fate. 📊 Week in Summary One match, one duck — a modest haul, but Roy's contribution alone was worth the price of admission. No pairs, no family affairs, and nobody troubling the debutante board this week. Sometimes triumph through adversity looks like exactly one man, quietly making history at number four on the all-time list. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Dick Langridge — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) Bob Arnold sets the pace with a fearsome 62.5% strike rate — though John Thirkell's sheer weight of innings shows there's more than one way to build a season's tally. 🍩 That's the lot for this week, but the season presses on and the ducks never sleep for long. 🦆 Duck News — 3rd to 5th August 1963

3 ducks
Week 15 · 3 Aug 1963 – 5 Aug 1963
Two fixtures dotted the first weekend of August, but it was the away trip to Bearsted CC on the 5th that did the real damage — three ducks in a single innings, top, middle and tail all paying tribute to the art of not scoring. 🏏 1st XI at Bearsted CC Opening the innings, John Thirkell departed early, caught Colin Robbins bowled David Andrews, for his 5th duck of the summer and — brace yourselves — his 51st in senior colours. That is not merely a fine tally, it is THE tally: Thirkell now sits alone at the summit of the all-time Linton roll of dishonour, 1 of 779, a throne he's held comfortably since sweeping Duck of the Year in 1952, 1954 and 1955. A fixture of both the Ducks on the Road and Homebody Duck top tens, he clearly discriminates against no ground and no postcode. His Quacking Together honour with Bob Arnold feels timely too, given Arnold currently sits jointly top of this very season's charts. Fittingly, on this exact date back in 1895, one Ernest Cruttenden was busy doing much the same thing against Rochester CC — 0️⃣ knows no era. At number seven, Ossie Fryer became the second man back for nought, bowled by Colin Warnett — his 3rd duck of the season and 4th of an already eventful career, in just 10 innings. That's 74th on the all-time list at a canter, and rather a promising strike-rate for someone so early in his membership application. And at eleven, Michael Chambers completed the set, trapped lbw by the same Colin Warnett, for his 4th duck of the season and 7th for the club from 26 innings — good enough for 41st all time. Three Linton batsmen, one long afternoon, one thoroughly satisfied Bearsted attack — and all three, curiously, walked off on the precise anniversary of Ernest Cruttenden's own nought against Rochester CC in 1895. The zero club welcomes company across the centuries. 📅 Week in Summary Three ducks from two fixtures, every one of them concentrated into that unfortunate Bearsted innings, with Colin Warnett doing brisk trade among the wickets. A rare weekend of restraint everywhere else — 🍩 stocks briefly ran low. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks in 8 innings (62.5%) - David Gale — 5 ducks in 16 innings (31.25%) - John Thirkell — 5 ducks in 24 innings (20.83%) - Michael Chambers — 4 ducks in 9 innings (44.44%) - Dick Langridge — 4 ducks in 11 innings (36.36%) With Thirkell extending his all-time lead and Chambers closing in behind, the race for Duck of the Year 1963 remains wide open. Roll on the next fixture — the zero club always has room for one more. 🦆🏏

🦆 Duck Report — 27th to 29th July 1963 🦆

7 ducks
Week 14 · 27 Jul 1963 – 29 Jul 1963
Three days, three fixtures, seven ducks, and a family occasion the club will be dining out on for weeks. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees below. Saturday 27th July — 1st XI v Tovil A torrid session for the middle-and-lower order at Tovil, where three Linton batsmen shuffled back to the pavilion without scoring — all off the bowling of C- Banes. Dick Langridge went first at six, bowled, his 3rd duck of the season and career from 15 innings, 67th on the all-time list. David Gale followed at eight, caught by M- Taylor, his 5th duck of both season and career from 27 innings, 52nd all-time. And at nine, Barry Langridge, also bowled by Banes, his 3rd of season and career from a mere 8 innings. All three, curiously, share the same historical companion in misfortune: on this day in 1952, Basil Sears likewise departed for nought against Folkestone CC, en route to 17 career ducks across a sixteen-year span from 1947 to 1963. The Langridge pairing is the real story, though — father Dick and son Barry combining for a Duck and Ducklings, the rarest ceremony the club offers, Dick moving to 4 career ducks and Barry to 3 by weekend's end. Barry's contribution also earns him the 1963 "Quacked by Kin" honour, credited to his father. Sunday 28th July — 1st XI v Folkestone CC (away) Dick Langridge wasn't done. Bowled by Derek Waddup, he fell again for his 4th duck of the season and career, completing a grim pair for the weekend — though with bat on ball both times, he stops agonisingly short of King Pair immortality. On this day in 1957, D- Jordan also ducked against Folkestone CC, one of just two in a brief single-season career. Monday 29th July — Midweek XI v Birkenhead Grasshoppers CC The Grasshoppers hopped away with three Linton wickets for nought apiece. Gordon Fenner, trapped lbw by K Acton, took his 2nd duck of the season and 20th of his career from 158 innings — 8th on the all-time list, a family trait shared by brothers Joe Fenner (5 career ducks) and Maurice Fenner (1). Ossie Fryer, stumped off R Stears, made his 2nd of the season, 3rd of his career from 9 innings. And Alan Wilkes, bowled by Stears, brought up his 1st of the season and 17th of his career from 70 innings — 12th all-time, and a paid-up double member of the "Only Two Quacks" club, alongside both Bob Arnold and John Ames. All three Grasshoppers victims share their on-this-day companion too: in 1896, Ernest Cruttenden made a duck against Headcorn CC on his way to 10 career noughts across three decades, 1881 to 1912. Week in Summary Seven ducks, three matches, one glorious father-son double at Tovil, and a clean sweep of Birkenhead's afternoon at the Grasshoppers' expense. Dick Langridge's weekend pair keeps him firmly in the season's title race. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Bob Arnold — 5 ducks in 7 innings (71.43%) - David Gale — 5 ducks in 15 innings (33.33%) - Dick Langridge — 4 ducks in 10 innings (40.0%) - John Thirkell — 4 ducks in 22 innings (18.18%) - Barry Langridge — 3 ducks in 8 innings (37.5%) Bob Arnold sits imperious atop the table on a positively alarming strike rate. Who dares challenge him before the season's out? Same time next week — the crease awaits its next volunteer. 🦆🍩

🦆 Duck News — 20 to 23 July 1963 🦆

6 ducks
Week 13 · 20 Jul 1963 – 23 Jul 1963
🏏 Saturday, 1st XI v Rumwood CC (h) A vintage afternoon for the exclusive zero club, as four Linton batsmen queued up to demonstrate the art of not scoring. Basil Sears led the way, caught Mike Iliffe bowled B- Hayes — his 2nd duck of the season and 17th of an increasingly decorated career, good enough for 12th of 777 in the all-time roll. Splendidly, on this very day back in 1906, R W Mitchell was doing precisely the same thing against the Royal West Kent Regiment CC Officers' XI. Some habits span the centuries. David Gale, bowled John Carter, banked his 3rd of the season and 3rd of his career alike — level pegging, as career and season are still one and the same for our newest addition. He too keeps Mitchell's 1906 company today, though as we'll see, Rumwood-week wasn't done with him yet. Barry Langridge fell bowled B- Buckland for his 2nd of the season and career, joining Mitchell's 1906 club for a third time this innings. A paid-up member of this year's Quacked by Kin honours besides, having watched father Dick Langridge notch his own duck earlier in 1963 — the family evidently believes in shared hobbies. And then, majestically, Bob Arnold — bowled John Carter for his 5th duck of the season, taking a career tally of 44 that stands 2nd of 777 all-time. Arnold sits proudly atop the Duck of the Year 1963 table, with a Ducks in a Row honour and a Quacking Together nod alongside John Thirkell already banked. A genuine great of the format, and Mitchell's fourth companion of the day. 🏏 Wednesday, Midweek XI v Senacre Staff XI (h) Gordon Fenner was undone by Cridland for his 1st duck of the season, his 19th in 157 innings and 8th of 777 all-time — a family trade, with siblings Joe (5 career ducks) and Maurice (1) also enrolled. He shares his date with Brian Relf, who ducked against Tovil on this same day back in 1960. And returning for an unwelcome encore, David Gale fell bowled Tucker for his 4th of the season and career — a proper pair for the week, Rumwood to Senacre without so much as troubling the scoreboard in between.
