🗓️ 21 Sep – 27 Sep 1968 · 1968 — 1 match

Sat 21 Sep 1968 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · HomeformWLLDW

Saturday last, West Farleigh rolled up the drive and I won the toss and, with that unfailing instinct for making my own life difficult, stuck us in. Twenty-one runs down before the tea urn had properly boiled, we subsided to 121 all out in 41.5 overs, rescued from utter ignominy by Mike Iliffe's watchful 24. The less said about Frank Brattle and Derry Evans the better — both walked back for ducks, and the pond behind the pavilion has never looked so inviting.

Defending a total that a stiff breeze could have blown over, we set about it with more hope than method. West Farleigh wobbled early at 2 for 1 and I allowed myself a quiet smile, but Colin Clark had other ideas, farming the strike to an unbeaten 44, well supported by Ron Kemp's 33 not out and Don Fermor's 20. Paul Bowles toiled manfully for 3 for 39, and Terry Whittaker — three maidens and 2 for 50, mean as a Kent hedgerow — kept the scoring honest without reward enough. I chipped in with 15 of my own, which at least kept the average ticking over; I now lead the 1st XI's run charts this season with 622, and Whittaker sits second among the wicket-takers with 46. But their last pair found 80 runs we simply couldn't afford them, and we went down by four wickets.

A chastening afternoon, all told — but character-building, if the scorebook is to be believed. Onward to next week, wiser and no less optimistic.

🗓️ 14 Sep – 20 Sep 1968 · 1968 — 1 match

Sat 14 Sep 1968 🧢 1st XIv SC St Lukes Maidstone CC · HomeformDWLLD

Terry Whittaker here, and what a Saturday it turned out to be against St Lukes Maidstone. Losing the toss and being sent out to field is never how I'd choose to start a Saturday, but the lads had other ideas about how the day should go. St Lukes battled to 126 all out from 47.4 overs, M. Eastman doing the bulk of the graft with 39, but our bowling had them in all sorts from 13-1 to 22-4 before they'd barely settled. The story of the innings, though, was Terry Donovan, who bowled with real fire and control to finish with a thoroughly deserved 7-52 — a five-for and then some. Jon Harris chipped in with 1-18, and I was pleased enough to nab 1-27 myself, along with six maidens for good measure, though the headlines belonged entirely to Donovan.

Chasing 127, we got there for the loss of five wickets, 129 all told from 41.2 overs, and it was Paul Bowles who anchored things beautifully with an unbeaten 49, well supported by Fred Greenfield's 23 and Jim Hall's 21. Special mention too for Bowles, whose innings nudged him past Tom Osborn and up to 24th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — no small feat, that. He now sits second in this year's first-team run charts with 532, while Donovan, unsurprisingly after today, leads the wicket-takers with 55.

A five-wicket win, then, built on a fine bowling display and a calm, composed chase. Onwards we go.

🗓️ 07 Sep – 13 Sep 1968 · 1968 — 2 matches

Sun 08 Sep 1968 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformWDWLL

Sunday 8th September saw Anchorians CC visit us at Linton Park, and winning the toss I had no hesitation in taking first strike on a pitch that looked, to my admittedly optimistic eye, like it owed us a few runs.

Roger Craddock led the way with a fine 36, and he found a willing partner in Frank Brattle (34) as the pair put on 54 for the first wicket to give us the platform every captain dreams of at breakfast and rarely gets by lunch. Robert Chance chipped in with a useful 23, but from a position of some comfort at 123 for 5 we contrived, with the enthusiasm of men queuing for the tea urn, to stumble to 130 for 8 — poor Terry Donovan a spectator throughout his own duck. We limped home to 155 for 9 off our 53, a total that flattered to deceive but would, I felt, prove defendable.

And so it did, largely. Anchorians never got away from us, A. Watkins's 27 the only real resistance as they closed on 98 for 9 from their 40 overs. Paul Bowles was quite magnificent, four wickets for 27 with five overs given away for nothing, ably backed by Dick Langridge's three for 33 and a wicket apiece for the ever-reliable Craddock.

In the end honours were shared, and perhaps rightly so — a match of fine margins that neither side quite deserved to lose. A draw, then, and a fair one at that.

Sat 07 Sep 1968 🧢 1st XIv A A.Trembeths · HomeformWWDWL

From the captain's chair — Saturday 7th September 1968, and we played host to A. Trembeths on what I like to think was a very reasonable strip of turf.

Jim Hall's 26 was the innings' backbone, Paul Bowles chipping in gamely with 21, but too many of us trooped back for nought — myself, Basil Sears and Mike Iliffe all contributing to what the ledger cruelly records as a hat-trick of ducks. Fell (38 not out) simply battened down and saw them home at 83 for 3. Beaten by seven wickets, then, and my own duck sits rather sourly alongside it. Bowles continues to build a fine season with the bat, and I'll take what comfort I can from the wickets column. We'll be better for it next week.

Mon 02 Sep 1968 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · AwayformWWWDW

A case, then, of a total that was a good 20 runs light of matchwinning, and a Bearsted side who made us pay for those early jitters. We'll dust ourselves down and be better for it next week.

Sat 24 Aug 1968 🧢 1st XIv AC Aylesford Paper Mills CC · HomeformLLLDW

Skipper's Log — Saturday 24th August, and a rare treat: sunshine, a full square, and Aylesford Paper Mills CC come to visit. Paul Bowles backed him up superbly with 4 for 41, and between them Aylesford's innings simply came apart, 68 for 4 collapsing to 75 for 7 in the blink of an eye, Geoff Hughes among the ducks. Poor Jim Hall had a torrid afternoon behind the stumps — 11 byes tells its own story — but it mattered not a jot.

A 31-run victory, two points banked, and thoroughly deserved. Some days the scorebook flatters you; this one, for once, told the truth.
