🗓️ 27 Sep – 03 Oct 1958 · 1958 — 2 matches

Sun 28 Sep 1958 🧢 Marriedv Linton Park CC · HomeformDDWDL

Here's the match report:

Sunday last saw us host Linton Park CC, and winning the toss I did what any captain fancying a long afternoon in the sun does — I put us in to bat. It proved a fine call. We compiled a solid 156 for 9 from our 47 overs, the innings anchored by a really well-made fifty from Clive Smith, unbeaten on 76 at the close. Elsewhere the returns were more mixed, not least for poor Tony Levick, whose duck will be entered into the ledger with the usual ceremony and lack of sympathy from the rest of the dressing room.

There was, though, a quieter note worth striking amid the afternoon's larger dramas. Tom Osborn's five runs, modest as they read on the scorecard, were enough to ease him past Don Hartridge and into 11th on the club's all-time run-scorers list — the sort of landmark that means rather more to those of us who keep half an eye on such things than it does in the moment. I chipped in with the ball and bat as needed, glad to do my bit for the cause.

The season charts continue to make pleasant reading too, with Stan Wratten holding 4th in the 1958 1st XI list on 394, Clive Smith leading the Married table, and Terry Whittaker out in front among the Singles.

We defended our total comfortably, running out winners by 94 runs. A thoroughly good day for the club.

Sat 27 Sep 1958 🧢 1st XIv A A.Trembeths · HomeformWDLLW

Skipper's log, and not one I'll be rushing to forget. Winning the toss on a bright Saturday, I did what any self-respecting captain does with sun on his back and vanity in his heart — I stuck us in. Ninety-two all out in 44.3 overs was hardly a declaration of intent, more a nervous shuffle to the crease and back, with Terry Whittaker and Tony Snell both departing for ducks before they'd troubled the groundsman. Not our finest hour with the willow, and I'll say no more about it than that.

Happily, cricket has a habit of rewarding those who bowl straight and field like their tea depends on it. A.Trembeths folded for 67 in 24.1 overs, with only H- Clancy showing any real fight for 27, and three of theirs — a- Trembeth, K- McDowall and J- Mortlock — walking back for ducks of their own. The afternoon belonged, unarguably, to M- Harden, whose 7-18 with five maidens thrown in was bowling of the highest order, well supported by Ray Howard's tidy 3-29. Howard had a day to remember off the field too, climbing past Bob Arnold and Rev Thomas Burra in the club's all-time lists in the same afternoon.

For my own part, I'm proud to sit atop the 1958 1st XI run charts with 660 to my name. We got home by 25 runs — a scruffy total defended beautifully, and a very good day for the club indeed.

Sun 21 Sep 1958 🧢 1st XIv TS Tilling Steven · HomeformWWDLL

Sunday 21 September, and Tilling Steven were the visitors. I called correctly at the toss and, with barely a glance at the sky, decided we should bat first - a decision I spent much of the afternoon quietly questioning.

We were bowled out for 79 in 30.4 overs, a scoreline that flattered nobody. Frank Brattle held things together with a battling 31, but the rest of the card made for grim reading, with Stan Wratten, John Ames and Roy Clarke all back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. Seventy-nine felt like a total built more on hope than conviction as we took the field.

Tilling Steven set off briskly through F. Baker's 29, but the innings never found its feet. G. Winters, R. Bailey and T. Maskell all fell for ducks, and John Ames turned from batting to bowling with a vengeance, spinning through them for 5-26. Alan Wilkes was scarcely less miserly, claiming 5-33 with two maidens to strangle any hope of a chase. Tilling Steven were dismissed for 59 in 20.2 overs, and we had got over the line by 20 runs.

A funny old game, cricket - a total that looked like defeat at tea somehow carried us to victory by the close. I currently lead our run charts for the season with 655, though this was not the day for it, while John Ames tops the wicket charts with 59 and showed exactly why. A fine team performance, and a thoroughly good day for the club.

Sat 20 Sep 1958 🧢 1st XIv Bearsted CC · AwayformLWWDL

Saturday 20th September saw us head over to Bearsted, and after losing the toss I knew we were in for a long afternoon in the field — asked to bowl first on a day when the away dressing room already smelled of quiet resignation.

To our credit, we scrapped hard. Bearsted eased to 96/8 from their 43 overs, Ian Lambert's 44 the backbone of the innings alongside a watchful third-wicket stand of 50 that threatened to bat us out of the game entirely. But cricket has a habit of turning, and turn it did — 82/4 became 87/7 in a blur, with F Bayley and Ray Grainger both back in the pavilion without troubling the scorers. Bob Brigden was superb, 3-13, ably backed by G Martin (2-16) and J Donovan (2-26), while Tony Snell bowled four maidens of the kind that makes batsmen question their life choices.

Chasing 97 should have been routine. It was not. We stumbled to 2-1 almost before anyone had found their guard, and from there the innings never quite found its feet — Robert Murray's battling 21 the only real resistance, while poor Tony Snell followed his miserly bowling with a golden duck. I chipped in with the bat, but 69 all out from 30.4 overs was well short of the mark. There was cheer amid the gloom: Bob Arnold's four runs eased him past Ray Howard to 14th on the club's all-time run charts, and Brigden's three wickets did the same for him at 21st among the wicket-takers.

Beaten by 27 runs, then — a day where the bowlers did their job and the batsmen forgot theirs. We'll be better for it next week.

Sun 14 Sep 1958 🧢 1st XIv TC Tunbridge Wells CC · AwayformWLWWD

Well, we lost the toss again — I'm beginning to think the coin has a grudge against me — and Tunbridge Wells duly stuck us in the field on a fine Sunday for the trip down. Stan Wratten, Cliff Anscombe and I all contributed golden ducks apiece to a batting card that makes for grim reading, and we were bowled out for a paltry 77 in 28.2 overs. Still top of the runs charts for the season on 650, for what little comfort that offers this week.

Beaten by 98 runs, then, and no arguments about it — Tunbridge Wells were the better side from the first ball to the last. We'll dust ourselves down, have a stern word about application with the bat, and go again next Sunday.

John Ames elects to bat first against Anchorians CC — the right call, if I do say so myself, though the scorecard didn't entirely agree with me by the time we'd been shot out for 99 in 37.4 overs. Murray, meanwhile, sits second in this season's run charts, and Wilkes leads the wickets column with 53 — the numbers rather speak for themselves.

A scrappy total, but a famous 61-run win. We'll take it, and happily.

Sun 31 Aug 1958 🧢 1st XIv HH Halfway House · HomeformDWDWW

Halfway House were the visitors on Sunday last, 31 August, and for once the coin fell my way — I sent them into the field, a decision I rather came to regret.

Batting first should set the tone for the day; ours set the tone for a horror film. We were bowled out for a threadbare 61 in 32.1 overs, rescued from utter ignominy only by Cliff Anscombe's gritty 21. Behind him, the wheels came off in style: E- Childs, Roy Clarke and I all made our way back to the pavilion without troubling the scorers, my own effort a golden blob so brief I barely had time to admire the bowling that did for me.

With so little to defend, it fell to the ball to do the rescuing, and for a good while it very nearly did. Alan Wilkes was magnificent, scything through the Halfway House order for a thoroughly deserved 7-42, while I did my bit at the other end with 1-20 and five maidens — more containment than conquest, but useful all the same. Don Capeling and Cyril Dyson both departed for ducks of their own, and for an hour or so we dared to believe. In the end Halfway House got home at 65/8 from 29.1 overs, sneaking it by two wickets.

Not our day, then, though Wilkes marches on at the top of the 1958 wicket charts with 49 to his name — some crumb of comfort amid the wreckage. Heads up, chins out, and back to the nets.

Sat 23 Aug 1958 🧢 1st XIv CA Charles Arkcoll · HomeformWLWDW
