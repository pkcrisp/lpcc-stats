Wed 28 Sep 1983 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformWDDDL

The road took us down to Penshurst Park CC on Wednesday 28 September 1983, and for once the toss fell my way — I called correctly and, with barely a flicker of my usual indecision, chose to bat first.

We batted with real purpose to post 211 for 3 off our 44 overs. I managed 78, which I'll mention only the once and then never again, ably supported by Andy Woollett's fine 58 and Bob Lines's unbeaten 36 to see us to a total that felt, even to my nervous captain's eye, like a proper score.

In the field we kept them honest. Richard Watters made a well-crafted 54 and N Fowler chipped in with 46, Brian Milns finishing unbeaten on 27, but poor Jim Littlechild trudged back without troubling the scorers. I found some rhythm with the ball, spinning my way to 5 for 60 — passing 900 career wickets along the way, which is either a proud landmark or a sign I should have taken up gardening years ago. Trevor Back (2-45) and Malcolm Pay (1-14) chipped in usefully, while Michael Chambers bowled five miserly maidens to keep the shackles on. They finished on 180 for 9 from their 45 overs.

With the season charts now showing 344 runs and 22 wickets to my name, I really ought to be embarrassed — and I am, slightly. Neither side, in the end, could force it home: honours shared, and a fair reflection of an afternoon well spent.

Sun 25 Sep 1983 🧢 1st XIv Linton Park CC · HomeformDDDWW

Skipper's Log — Sunday 25 September 1983

I'll confess I fancied our chances the moment that coin landed my way, and, with the pitch looking a shade too kind to leave out, I had few qualms about batting first. Malcolm Pay set about proving me right with a fine 45, Brian Piper stood firm for an unbeaten 43, and Bob Lines chipped in with a tidy 35, the three of them dragging us to a very respectable 202 all out from our 45 overs — though I use "respectable" loosely, given poor Jim Hall's afternoon began and ended without troubling the scorers. Even the extras got in on the act with a generous 29, rather more than some of the specialist batsmen managed, and I added a workmanlike 20 of my own, more grit than grace.

There was a proper occasion to mark, too: Mike Iliffe brought up his 300th appearance in Linton colours, a milestone that rather put the rest of us whippersnappers in our place and deserved a good deal more than the ripple of applause it got between overs.

As for the season at large, Paul Bowles continues to purr along in 2nd spot in the 1st XI charts with 996 runs to his name, while over in the 2nds Tim Thirkell leads the way with 51.

In the end it came down to the finest of margins — beaten by just 2 runs, a result that will sting rather more for its closeness than any thumping ever could. We'll be better for it next week.

🗓️ 17 Sep – 23 Sep 1983 · 1983 — 1 match

Sat 17 Sep 1983 🧢 1st XIv West Farleigh CC · AwayformDDDDW

We won the toss at West Farleigh and I had no hesitation in electing to bat — on a day like that, you don't need asking twice. The lads set about building something substantial, and by the end of our thirty-nine overs we'd racked up a handsome 201 for 3. Jim Hall played beautifully for his 73, a proper well-made fifty that gave the innings its backbone, and I was fortunate enough to have one of those days where everything comes off the middle of the bat, ending up with 107 to my name. Not bad for an old man's knees.

Defending that total, we knew West Farleigh would have to play out of their skins, and to their credit they gave it a go — Peter Cooper and Tony Hyde both chipped in with 22, Micky Back with 21 — but wickets kept tumbling at the other end. Fred Baker and Don Fermor will want to forget the whole afternoon, back in the pavilion without so much as a single to their names. The real story, though, was with the ball: Paul Bowles was magnificent, tearing through with 5 for 27, well backed up by Jon Harris's 5 for 40, while Mike Elworthy strangled the life out of the middle overs with three maidens. All out for 122 in 38.1 overs — game, set and match.

A 79-run win, and a proper one at that. With the season charts now showing me handily out in front with 1123 runs and Paul Bowles leading the wickets column with 72, it's fair to say Linton Park are having rather a good year. Onwards indeed.

It also leaves me sitting atop the Tour XI run charts for the season, with Paul Bowles doing likewise among the bowlers on seven wickets.

A thoroughly satisfying five-wicket win, then, and one to file under "days when everything simply clicked." Onwards indeed.

Sun 28 Aug 1983 🧢 1st XIv SC Staplehurst CC · AwayformLLDDD

Skipper's Log — Sunday 28 August 1983

The road to Staplehurst was, as ever, filled with hope over experience, and having called correctly at the toss I fancied our chances enough to bat first. It proved the right call. Nigel Thirkell played the innings of the summer, three figures no less, 114 of the finest, and while I'd love to claim my own contribution turned the tide, it was Brian Piper's brisk 40 and Tim Thirkell's 29 that gave the innings its shape. We closed on 220/5 from our 46 overs, a total that felt, over tea, distinctly defendable.

And so it very nearly proved. Staplehurst chased hard, Knope top-scoring with 43, well supported by Wavell's 32 and Bull's 30, but our attack kept chipping away. Allan Cooper was the pick of the bowlers, 3-28 and three maidens of pure miserliness, with Brian Piper weighing in at 3-29 — a fine afternoon for a man who, we later discovered, had that day passed 100 career wickets, a landmark worthy of the mantelpiece if ever there was one. I did my bit with 2-45, and poor B- Penney departed without troubling the scorers. Jim Hall had a torrid time behind the stumps, 21 byes a reminder that even the best gloves have off days.

In the end neither side could force it, and honours were shared. A draw, then — the great English compromise, and on this evidence, a fair one.
