🦆 Duck News — 29–30 September 1956

8 ducks
Week 23 · 29 Sep 1956 – 30 Sep 1956
The season winds down but the Duck Club never clocks off. Two fixtures this weekend, eight ducks harvested, and the ancient rite of Married v Singles delivering its usual bumper crop. 🏏 Saturday — 1st XI v A. Snell's XI Frank Brattle, in at 3, was bowled by S Capon for his 3rd duck of the season and 4th career, from 20 innings — rank 49 of 683. He joins C Amies, who played 1867–1870, on exactly 4 career ducks. A family trait, too — kinsman Percy Brattle also sits on 4 for his career, so the naught clearly runs in the blood. S Capon then struck again lower down, accounting for Tony Tipples at 5 — his 2nd of the season, 5th career from 33 innings, rank 29 of 683. He joins Alan Wilkes (1953–1956), himself a veteran of the "Only Two Quacks" honours with both Bob Arnold and John Ames. Tony wasn't finished for the weekend, as we'll see. ⚭ Sunday — the annual Married v Singles duel, Linton Park The old fixture did what it always does, contributing six of the weekend's eight ducks, three of them to one bowler. For the Married side, Basil Sears opened and fell lbw to John Ames — his 1st duck of the season but 8th of a long career spanning 128 innings, rank 17 of 683. On this very day in 1951, Don Hartridge (1940–1952, 10 ducks, rank 11) also made a duck against Linton Park CC — history repeating five years to the date. Peter Weedon, at 7, became Ames's second victim, bowled for his 1st season duck and 11th career nought from just 56 innings — rank 10 of 683 and closing in on the elite. A paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, though today he's very much still in the fight. Monty Deakes at 10 fell caught Wright bowled Howard, his 1st of the season and 3rd career in only 11 innings, rank 76. A Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack alumnus of old — a short career, but never a dull one. Completing Ames's hat-trick of scalps, Tony Snell was stumped down the leg side for his 2nd season duck, 6th career from 29 innings, rank 28. A proud member of the "Ducks in a Row" board for four on the spin — he clearly knows a rhythm when he finds one. For the Singles, Tony Tipples returned to complete a grim weekend double — out Saturday for the 1s, out again bowled here for his 3rd season duck and 6th career, from 33 innings, rank 29. A proper weekend tour of Linton's duck-getting grounds. C- Haylor fell lbw for his 3rd of the season and 5th career from just 16 innings, rank 34 — another Retirement Duck board member from years gone by. All four Married/Singles duckers can toast a shared moment — on this day in 1951, Don Hartridge also walked back for nought against Linton Park CC. Some traditions run deeper than the fixture list. 📊 Week in Summary Eight ducks, two matches, one bowler (John Ames) doing the lion's share of the damage with three scalps in an afternoon. Tony Tipples's weekend pair is the standout feat — turning out for two different sides in two days and finding the same fate in both. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Michael Morton — 13 inns, 4 ducks (30.8%) 2. Bob Arnold — 16 inns, 4 ducks (25.0%) 3. Richard Harris — 16 inns, 4 ducks (25.0%) 4. Tony Tipples — 7 inns, 3 ducks (42.9%) 5. C- Haylor — 10 inns, 3 ducks (30.0%) 🥇 The race for Duck of the Year is well and truly on. Next fixture, fresh victims — the Club watches, waits, and celebrates. 0️⃣🍩

🦆 Duck News — 22–23 September 1956

2 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1956 – 23 Sep 1956
Two matches on the fixture list this week, and the zero club kept it tidy — a single innings did all the damage, with both ducks arriving back to back at the death of the same Bearsted CC innings. Consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. 🏏 Saturday 22nd September — 1st XI v Bearsted CC The tail folded in matching fashion, both men undone by the same hand — A "Tony" Snook claiming numbers nine and ten in successive deliveries, bowled all ends up. First to go was Bob Arnold, at nine, bowled without scoring for his 4th duck of the season and 19th of an increasingly storied career — a career now sitting at a mighty 3rd on the all-time list of 683. Bob is a fully paid-up honours-board resident, having once strung together "Ducks in a Row" with four on the spin, and he shares standing entries in "Only Two Quacks" with Alan Wilkes and "Quacking Together" with John Thirkell — proof that misery, much like runs, is best shared. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1951, club stalwart John --'s Taylor also trudged off for nought, against Hunton Wanderers CC no less — a fine bit of calendar symmetry, 705 days and one opposition removed. In at ten came C Sandford, who fared no better, chalking up his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd for his career from just 18 innings — climbing to 65th on the all-time chart. Sandford, too, finds himself on the same 22nd of September page as history, joining the same 1951 vintage as Arnold — evidently a date the Duck Club has long had circled in red. 🌱 Sunday 23rd September The second fixture of the weekend passed off without incident for the collectors — every LPCC bat found the middle, or at least avoided the meeting entirely. A rare afternoon of restraint. Don't get used to it. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks, two matches, one bowler doing the damage twice in a row — Bearsted's Snook can consider his weekend's work well and truly done. The rest of the club, for one day at least, kept its nose clean. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Michael Morton — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) 2. Bob Arnold — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 3. Richard Harris — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 4. R Vincent — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) 5. John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks in 16 innings (18.75%) Michael Morton clings to top spot, but with Arnold and Harris breathing down his neck on the same tally from fewer innings, this one's far from settled. 🦆 That's the lot for this week — the ducks had a quiet Sunday, but the season's young yet. See you next weekend for whatever fresh indignities the middle order has in store.

🦆 Duck News — Monday 2nd September 1956

2 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1956
A single fixture on the books this week, but the exclusive zero club never takes a night off. Even a quiet Monday at home to Halfway House was enough to add two fresh names to the ledger of the gloriously scoreless. 🏏 1st XI v Halfway House (home) In at four, Stan Wratten was trapped lbw by Denis Fowle without scoring — his 10th career duck from 128 innings, lifting him to 14th in the all-time standings of 683 hopefuls. Stan is also a long-standing member of the Quacking Together club, having once shared a nought with the legendary John Ames, proof that misery, like batting, is better with company. Eagle-eyed club historians will note that on this very day in 1945, D Clark also wandered back for nought against 1st Innings MCPC — some traditions simply refuse to die. Six batsmen later, at number seven, Gordon Fenner joined him in the shed, bowled by Jack Whibley for a duck of his own. It's Gordon's 14th career nought from 95 innings, good enough for 5th on the all-time leaderboard, daylight between him and all but a handful of legends. A touch of family form too: his sibling Joe Fenner already carries five career ducks, so the appetite for zero clearly runs in the blood. And by pleasant coincidence, Gordon shares Stan's own on-this-day omen, with D Clark's 1945 blob against 1st Innings MCPC doing double duty as the historical footnote for both dismissals. 📊 Week in Summary Just the one match, but it delivered handsomely: two ducks, both at Halfway House, both landing their victims inside the all-time top fifteen. A quiet week for quantity, not for pedigree. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Michael Morton — 4 ducks / 11 innings (36.36%) - Richard Harris — 4 ducks / 15 innings (26.67%) - R Vincent — 3 ducks / 12 innings (25.00%) - Bob Arnold — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) - John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks / 15 innings (20.00%) Michael Morton clings to top spot, though with over a third of his innings ending in nought he's hardly playing it safe up there. 📅 Looking Ahead The season rolls on, and with Morton still haunting the summit, the next round of fixtures surely has more zeroes queued up. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🍩0️⃣

🦆 DUCK REPORT — Saturday 25th to Sunday 26th August 1956

4 ducks
Week 18 · 25 Aug 1956 – 26 Aug 1956
Four for four at Charles Arkcoll on Saturday, and fittingly the entire collapse fell on the very date that old stalwart T Woollett also strolled back for nought, back in 1904 — the calendar clearly has a taste for anniversaries. Away at Charles Arkcoll (Saturday) Openers first: John Basten fell early, bowled by Gordon Fenner, his maiden LPCC duck and a modest 231st on the all-time roll of dishonour from just 8 career innings — a debut entry in the club's least wanted list. Ernie Chiesman followed, caught Fenner bowled John Ames, his 2nd career duck from 16 innings and 96th on the all-time table. Ernie remains a paid-up member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell — no suggestion today's nought is anything of the sort, merely a man revisiting familiar ground. R Vincent went lbw to John Ames for his 3rd of the season and 3rd of his career in just 12 innings, 75th all-time. Vincent already holds his Debutante Duck honours from way back — proof that some habits, once formed, are cherished for life. And propping up the innings, Richard Harris — caught Jack Knowler bowled Reg Waters — brought up his 4th duck of the season, and a frankly staggering 26th of his career across 131 innings. That leaves him 2nd on the all-time Linton list, a whisker from the summit and a man who has made not scoring something of an art form. Sunday's fixture, by contrast, was a strictly vegan affair — not a single duck to be found. Week in Summary Two matches, one rogue's gallery, four ducks — all crammed into a single Saturday innings at Charles Arkcoll. A fine demonstration that when the Duck Club decides to convene, it does so all at once. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Michael Morton — 4 ducks / 11 innings (36.4%) - Richard Harris — 4 ducks / 15 innings (26.7%) - R Vincent — 3 ducks / 12 innings (25.0%) - Bob Arnold — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.1%) - John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks / 15 innings (20.0%) Richard Harris breathing down Michael Morton's neck at the top — one more nought either way could settle this season's crown. Roll on next weekend, and may the zeros keep coming. 🦆🏏0️⃣

As the reigning all-time duck monarch — rank 1 of 683, a throne nobody is troubling any time soon — Thirkell is a three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955) and a permanent fixture on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. Another brick, then, in an already formidable monument to the art of not scoring. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1951, Peter Weedon (1945–1956) also strolled back for nought, against F.R. Sutton XI, on his way to 10 career ducks and a place in the Retirement Duck club. The calendar, it seems, has a taste for August anniversaries. 🏏 Sunday 19th — Home to Mote A Coming in at six, John --'s Taylor edged behind off a-Milsted for his 3rd duck of the season and 12th of his career from 115 innings, good enough for 8th in the all-time standings. A fine — or rather dreadful — achievement, entirely his own this week. And wouldn't you know it, on this same date in 1944 Herbert Rayner (1939–1948) also departed for a duck, against R.A. Training Wing Wrotham, en route to 6 career ducks of his own. History repeats, at least where failure is concerned. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one team responsible for the lot. The 1st XI carried the club's honour — such as it is — while everyone else kept their heads down and, mercifully or suspiciously, their bats occupied. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Five ducks recorded, all courtesy of the 1st XI — a vintage weekend for the art of not scoring. Saturday — 1st XI v Headcorn CC Fiennes Cornwallis got things underway at five, trapped lbw by J Kemp for his maiden LPCC duck — first of the season, and first in six innings, installing him at 194th in the all-time list. On this very day in 1906, C L Pemberton was doing much the same against Boxley CC. R Vincent followed at eight, caught behind off L Murrell, his second duck of the season and second of a career now standing at 121st all-time from 11 innings — a paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club from his very first LPCC knock. Like Cornwallis, he shares his moment in history with Pemberton's 1906 effort. And L Murrell wasn't finished — Frank Wright fell at eleven, bowled for his second of the season and fourth career duck (18 innings, 48th all-time), handing Murrell a tidy brace of noughts for his afternoon's work. Wright is a veteran of the Quack-to-Quack club, having opened his LPCC account with a duck on debut some years back — 1906's Pemberton keeps him company again. Sunday — 1st XI v Harrietsham CC John Larking, at two, was bowled by F Moody for his first duck of the season and second of his career (11 innings, 109th all-time). On this day in 1867, G a K Simpson was also falling for nought, against Leeds Park CC — good company across the centuries. Clive Smith joined the queue at seven, lbw to Ray Tong, also his first of the season and second career duck (12 innings, 92nd all-time) — level with Larking on the career count, if nothing else. 🦆 Duck News — Weekend of 21–22 July 1956

4 ducks
Week 13 · 21 Jul 1956 – 22 Jul 1956
Two fixtures, one venue, four ducks — Linton's 1st XI treated the home faithful to a proper double helping of nought this weekend. 🏏 Saturday 21st — v Shipbourne CC (home) The afternoon's real story was the right arm of Fairweather, who helped himself to three Linton wickets for three Linton ducks in the same innings — not quite a documented hat-trick, but close enough to have the scorebook blushing. First to go, at four, was R- Waters — bowled Fairweather for his 2nd duck of the season and 4th of a career now standing at 19 innings, good for 51st on the all-time list. Waters is also a long-standing member of the Retirement Duck club from a past farewell, and keeps company with H G Smith, who — on this very date in 1897 — also trudged off for nought against Maidstone CC. In at five, William Blight fared no better, also bowled Fairweather, for his 1st duck of the season but a rather more seasoned 8th of his career from 54 innings — an eye-catching 18th on the all-time chart. Blight shares the H G Smith 1897 bill for company on this historic date.
