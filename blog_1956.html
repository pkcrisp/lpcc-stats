Week 23 · 29 Sep 1956 – 30 Sep 1956
29–30 September 1956 The exclusive zero club took delivery of eight fresh members this week, across two fixtures — and one repeat customer who very nearly booked a rarer honour altogether. Saturday — 1st XI v A. Snell XI (home) - Frank Brattle — bowled by S Capon at first drop. His 3rd of the season and 4th of a 20-innings career (49th of 679 all-time), drawing level with C Amies, who carried the same tally through 9 innings back in 1867–70. Keeping it in the family, too — kin Percy Brattle sits on an identical career quartet. - Tony Tipples — also undone by Capon. Season duck #2, 5th career (33 innings, 29th all-time), matching Alan Wilkes (1953–56, 27 innings), himself a two-time honoree on the Only Two Quacks board. Tipples, as it turns out, wasn't finished for the weekend. Sunday — the Married v Singles season closer (home) Married innings first, and John Ames turned into a one-man cull: - Basil Sears — lbw, pos. 1. Season #1, a hefty 8th career nought from 128 innings (17th all-time). Eerily, on this very date in 1951 Don Hartridge also strolled back for a duck against Linton Park CC — the ground clearly has a long memory. - Peter Weedon — bowled Ames. Season #1, 11th career (56 innings, 10th all-time) — a paid-up Retirement Duck club member from a past farewell, though today was merely business as usual. Same spooky 1951 echo applies. - Monty Deakes — caught Wright, bowled Howard. Season #1, 3rd career (11 innings, 76th all-time), and a veteran of the rare Debutante Duck / Quack-to-Quack / Retirement Duck trifecta from his own long-ago bow and bow-out. Echo intact. - Tony Snell — stumped off Ames, completing a Ames treble. Season #2, 6th career (29 innings, 28th all-time), a Ducks in a Row (4 consecutive) alumnus. Echo intact. Singles reply, undone twice by John "-'s" Taylor: - Tony Tipples — bowled again, completing a grim pair across Saturday's A. Snell XI game and today's Singles innings. Season duck #3, and his career tally ticks to 6 (33 innings, 29th all-time) — one bad ball on Saturday shy of true immortality, he'll have to settle for merely doubling up. - Colin Haylor — lbw. Season #3, 5th career (16 innings, 34th all-time), another standing Retirement Duck honouree. The 1951 echo rounds off a full house of coincidence. Week in summary Eight ducks, two matches, three separate bowlers claiming braces or better (Capon, Ames with a clean treble, Taylor with a pair) — and every single Sunday dismissal falling on the exact anniversary of Don Hartridge's 1951 vintage. Tony Tipples' weekend pair takes top billing: consistency of a very particular kind. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Michael Morton — 4 ducks / 13 inns (30.77%) 2. Bob Arnold — 4 ducks / 16 inns (25.00%) 3. Richard Harris — 4 ducks / 16 inns (25.00%) 4. Tony Tipples — 3 ducks / 7 inns (42.86%) 5. Colin Haylor — 3 ducks / 10 inns (30.00%) The season may be winding down, but the zero club never quite closes its books. See you at the next crease. 🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 22–23 September 1956

2 ducks
Week 22 · 22 Sep 1956 – 23 Sep 1956
Two matches on the fixture list this week, and the zero club kept it tidy — a single innings did all the damage, with both ducks arriving back to back at the death of the same Bearsted CC innings. Consistency, as ever, of a very particular kind. 🏏 Saturday 22nd September — 1st XI v Bearsted CC The tail folded in matching fashion, both men undone by the same hand — A "Tony" Snook claiming numbers nine and ten in successive deliveries, bowled all ends up. First to go was Bob Arnold, at nine, bowled without scoring for his 4th duck of the season and 19th of an increasingly storied career — a career now sitting at a mighty 3rd on the all-time list of 683. Bob is a fully paid-up honours-board resident, having once strung together "Ducks in a Row" with four on the spin, and he shares standing entries in "Only Two Quacks" with Alan Wilkes and "Quacking Together" with John Thirkell — proof that misery, much like runs, is best shared. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1951, club stalwart John --'s Taylor also trudged off for nought, against Hunton Wanderers CC no less — a fine bit of calendar symmetry, 705 days and one opposition removed. In at ten came C Sandford, who fared no better, chalking up his 2nd duck of the season and 3rd for his career from just 18 innings — climbing to 65th on the all-time chart. Sandford, too, finds himself on the same 22nd of September page as history, joining the same 1951 vintage as Arnold — evidently a date the Duck Club has long had circled in red. 🌱 Sunday 23rd September The second fixture of the weekend passed off without incident for the collectors — every LPCC bat found the middle, or at least avoided the meeting entirely. A rare afternoon of restraint. Don't get used to it. 📊 Week in Summary Two ducks, two matches, one bowler doing the damage twice in a row — Bearsted's Snook can consider his weekend's work well and truly done. The rest of the club, for one day at least, kept its nose clean. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Michael Morton — 4 ducks in 12 innings (33.33%) 2. Bob Arnold — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 3. Richard Harris — 4 ducks in 15 innings (26.67%) 4. R Vincent — 3 ducks in 13 innings (23.08%) 5. John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks in 16 innings (18.75%) Michael Morton clings to top spot, but with Arnold and Harris breathing down his neck on the same tally from fewer innings, this one's far from settled. 🦆 That's the lot for this week — the ducks had a quiet Sunday, but the season's young yet. See you next weekend for whatever fresh indignities the middle order has in store.

🦆 Duck News — Monday 2nd September 1956

2 ducks
Week 19 · 2 Sep 1956
A single fixture on the books this week, but the exclusive zero club never takes a night off. Even a quiet Monday at home to Halfway House was enough to add two fresh names to the ledger of the gloriously scoreless. 🏏 1st XI v Halfway House (home) In at four, Stan Wratten was trapped lbw by Denis Fowle without scoring — his 10th career duck from 128 innings, lifting him to 14th in the all-time standings of 683 hopefuls. Stan is also a long-standing member of the Quacking Together club, having once shared a nought with the legendary John Ames, proof that misery, like batting, is better with company. Eagle-eyed club historians will note that on this very day in 1945, D Clark also wandered back for nought against 1st Innings MCPC — some traditions simply refuse to die. Six batsmen later, at number seven, Gordon Fenner joined him in the shed, bowled by Jack Whibley for a duck of his own. It's Gordon's 14th career nought from 95 innings, good enough for 5th on the all-time leaderboard, daylight between him and all but a handful of legends. A touch of family form too: his sibling Joe Fenner already carries five career ducks, so the appetite for zero clearly runs in the blood. And by pleasant coincidence, Gordon shares Stan's own on-this-day omen, with D Clark's 1945 blob against 1st Innings MCPC doing double duty as the historical footnote for both dismissals. 📊 Week in Summary Just the one match, but it delivered handsomely: two ducks, both at Halfway House, both landing their victims inside the all-time top fifteen. A quiet week for quantity, not for pedigree. 🏆 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard - Michael Morton — 4 ducks / 11 innings (36.36%) - Richard Harris — 4 ducks / 15 innings (26.67%) - R Vincent — 3 ducks / 12 innings (25.00%) - Bob Arnold — 3 ducks / 13 innings (23.08%) - John --'s Taylor — 3 ducks / 15 innings (20.00%) Michael Morton clings to top spot, though with over a third of his innings ending in nought he's hardly playing it safe up there. 📅 Looking Ahead The season rolls on, and with Morton still haunting the summit, the next round of fixtures surely has more zeroes queued up. We watch. We wait. We celebrate. 🍩0️⃣

As the reigning all-time duck monarch — rank 1 of 683, a throne nobody is troubling any time soon — Thirkell is a three-time Duck of the Year (1952, 1954, 1955) and a permanent fixture on the Ducks on the Road, Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens. Another brick, then, in an already formidable monument to the art of not scoring. Fittingly, on this very day back in 1951, Peter Weedon (1945–1956) also strolled back for nought, against F.R. Sutton XI, on his way to 10 career ducks and a place in the Retirement Duck club. The calendar, it seems, has a taste for August anniversaries. 🏏 Sunday 19th — Home to Mote A Coming in at six, John --'s Taylor edged behind off a-Milsted for his 3rd duck of the season and 12th of his career from 115 innings, good enough for 8th in the all-time standings. A fine — or rather dreadful — achievement, entirely his own this week. And wouldn't you know it, on this same date in 1944 Herbert Rayner (1939–1948) also departed for a duck, against R.A. Training Wing Wrotham, en route to 6 career ducks of his own. History repeats, at least where failure is concerned. 📋 Week in Summary Two matches, two ducks, one team responsible for the lot.
