Sun 23 Sep 2007 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv ? · HomeformDDWDL

Here's the match report:

The coin fell my way and I had no hesitation putting us in — a decision that looked rather shrewd once the lads got going. We racked up a healthy 234 for 7 from our 45 overs, built on the sort of innings that makes a captain's job easy. Richard Piper led from the front with a well-made 91, and just when it looked like the innings might stall, Jim Hall came in to play a lovely unbeaten 53, with Nigel Thirkell chipping in 26. The two of them shared a magnificent 100-run stand for the seventh wicket that turned a decent total into a proper one.

Defending it, we made them work hard for every run. They stumbled early at 1 for 1 and never quite escaped the shadow of that start, C Hobbs battling to 39 with support from S Sims (23) and F Pearman (20) before the middle order simply fell apart — 127 for 6 became 129 for 9 in the blink of an eye, with C Young, I Hart and — Page all departing without troubling the scorers. Will Thirkell was outstanding with the ball, taking 4 for 26, backed up nicely by Matthew Carter (2-19) and Chris Newman (1-27), while I was rather pleased to bowl seven maidens of my own. They finished on 140 for 9 from their 46, just short of denying us.

A word too for Jim Hall, who brought up his 500th appearance in Linton colours — a landmark few of us will ever match. In the end honours were shared, a draw that felt, on balance, like the fairest result cricket could offer.

Sat 22 Sep 2007 🧢 1st XIv SC Sidcup CC · HomeformLLWLW

Lost the toss again — I really must start practising that call — and Sidcup elected to bat, leaving us in the field under a fine late-September sky. They made merry early doors, though not before we nabbed one at just 1 on the board, a lovely nervy start that came to nothing. Tim Dawes played the innings of the day, 89 full of the shots we'd rather he hadn't played, with Paul Jenkins unbeaten on a composed 56 and Daniel Corbett chipping in with 47; their fourth-wicket pair put on a chastening 89 between them. I did what I could with the ball, taking 1-25 with a couple of maidens to at least keep a lid on things, and Tim Wright and Jack Jenner picked up a wicket apiece. Small mercy: Stephen Shanks and Mark Amphlett went for nought at our hands. Sidcup closed on 219/5.

Chasing 220 was always going to want something special, and Richard Piper very nearly provided it, a gutsy 81 that kept hope alive long after sensible men would have packed it in, with Kevin Presneill's 23 the only other score of note. Extras did their bit too, 29 of the things. But three ducks — Jack Jenner, Tim Thirkell and Carl Davis all walking back for nought — told their own sorry tale, and we folded for 179.

Beaten by 40 runs, then, fair and square. Not our finest afternoon, but with Craig Sands still purring at 435 runs for the season and my own wickets column ticking past 35, there's life in this old side yet. Onwards to next week.

Wed 19 Sep 2007 🧢 Midweek XIv Penshurst Park CC · AwayformLLDDD

Skipper's Log — Wednesday 19 September 2007. Away at Penshurst Park, and having lost the toss I was informed, in the traditional Penshurst manner, that we'd be doing the running about first. Their innings ticked along nicely without ever quite escaping us, closing on 226/7 from their forty overs. Rob Hunter was the chief architect at 76, with Ed Hill's well-crafted 71 doing plenty of the heavy lifting alongside him, and Peter Eames chipping in 29. Andy Eames and Michael Meade both fell for ducks, which I mention purely in the interests of completeness and not at all with relish. I had a happy afternoon with the ball, picking up 3-41, with Darren Styles miserly as ever — 2-30 and three maidens to show for his parsimony — and Stuart Cheesman weighing in with 1-16.

Chasing 227 is never a stroll, but Mike Elworthy made it look like one, striking a magnificent 120 that carried the innings almost single-handed, well supported by Trevor Back's 34. Chris Newman's contribution, sadly, rhymed with nothing. We scrambled home on 227/8 from 38.5 overs, winning by two wickets — the kind of finish that ages a captain rather more than the scorebook admits.

A note for the club archives: Elworthy's century pushes him past Clive Newman into 10th on the all-time run charts, a thoroughly deserved bit of history. In the 2007 table, Craig Sands leads the run-scoring with 332, while I find myself, not unhappily, out in front of the wicket charts on 9. A fine win, tightly earned. Onwards.

Sun 16 Sep 2007 🧢 Sunday 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformDDDWD

Sunday 16 September, and Anchorians came to Linton Park with the sun out and, as it turned out, their eye thoroughly in. I lost the toss – no great surprise this season – and we were put into the field. James Hockley made us pay for it in style, cashing in with a majestic 195, while J Thompson (84 not out) and D Thompson (41) tucked in around him. The openers put on 132 before we so much as glimpsed a breakthrough, and a third-wicket stand of 142 buried us further. I did what I could with the ball, picking up 1-38, with Carl Davis (1-67) and Michael Beeney (1-85) chipping away without ever really checking the flow. 348/3 declared-in-all-but-name off 34.4 overs told its own story.

Chasing 349 was always a tall order, and so it proved, though not without a flicker of defiance. James Piper played beautifully for 71, with Chris Newman a composed 36 not out, and our openers laid a fine platform of 76. But from 99-2 we somehow contrived 107-5, and the middle order rather lost its nerve. As for the captain – a golden blob, first ball, out. Leadership by example is a fine principle; on this occasion I led only by the wrong sort of example, joining Richard Piper in the Duck Club for the week. All out 186 in 28.2 overs.

A 162-run defeat, then, no way of dressing that up. But James Piper's form (now 2nd in the club's Sunday run charts) and Beeney's growing wicket tally are real positives to bank. We'll regroup, dust down the pads, and come back sturdier next week.

Sat 15 Sep 2007 🧢 2nd XIv Marden CC, Kent · HomeformDWWWL

Here's the match report:

Not much luck at the toss on Saturday – Marden called correctly and stuck us in the field – but I can't say I was too disheartened, and so it proved. They ground their way to 189/3 from 45 overs, with Rupert Humphrey the chief architect of our discomfort, an unbeaten 79 that never looked remotely troubled, well supported by Jim Hall's 38 and, for what it's worth, 23 of my own. Chasing 190 is never a formality on a club afternoon, but Graham Walker set about it with a fine 75, and Richard Piper played the anchor role to perfection with an unbeaten 70 — the sort of innings that wins matches and, we later discovered, rather more than that. Clive Newman chipped in with 23, and if there was a blot on the card it was poor Christopher Thirkell's duck, though I daresay he'll dine out on the ignominy for a week or two yet.

We got home by five wickets with the 42nd over still breathing, which is about as good as club cricket gets. And there was a footnote worth savouring: Piper's 70 eased him past Paul Brattle into 18th on the club's all-time run-charts, while his season's tally now leads the 2nd XI for 2007. A thoroughly good day for Linton Park — the sort you cycle home from with the bat still smelling of linseed and a smile you can't quite shift.

I managed to prise out one for 40 myself, which I shall count as a contribution rather than a footnote.
