🦆 DUCK NEWS — 24th to 28th September 1994 🦆

0 ducks
Week 23 · 24 Sep 1994 – 28 Sep 1994
A rare and troubling week for the exclusive zero club: three fixtures played, and not a single soul obliged us with a nought. The Duck Club convenes this week not to celebrate but to lament — the art of not scoring was, apparently, put entirely on hold. Somewhere across three separate scorecards, batsmen up and down the order simply insisted on scoring runs. Selfish stuff. Whether this was defiance, incompetence at the crease avoided by sheer luck, or a temporary conspiracy of good form, the Duck Club cannot say. What we can say is that the honours board gathers dust this week, the pen stays capped, and the club's most dedicated servants will simply have to try harder next time out. Let it be known: a blank week is not a dead week. The ducks are merely regrouping. 🍩 📊 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard Even in a week of drought, the season standings remain a monument to consistency of a very particular kind: 1. Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks, 22 innings, 31.82% 2. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 33 innings, 18.18% 3. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks, 10 innings, a startling 40.00% 4. Matt Large — 4 ducks, 25 innings, 16.00% 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 10 innings, 30.00% Stuart Cheesman continues to lead the race for Duck of the Year with an admirable strike rate of failure, though Ian Brattle's 40% duck rate remains the week's most alarming statistic — four dismissals in just ten trips to the crease is triumph through adversity of the highest order. Piers McDonald, meanwhile, plays the long game, quietly accumulating his six across a much broader innings count. 🏏 Looking ahead With the season drawing towards its close, there is still time and opportunity for the leaderboard to shuffle. History shows the ducks are never far away — they were simply on their own fixture this week, elsewhere, minding their business. Bats up, guards taken, standards (low) upheld. We watch. We wait. We celebrate — next week, hopefully, with actual ducks to report. 🦆 0️⃣

🦆 Duck Report — Saturday 17 September 1994

3 ducks
Week 22 · 17 Sep 1994
Two fixtures, three fallen batsmen, and the 1994 season edging towards its close. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees. 1st XI v West Farleigh CC Clive Newman opened the innings and opened the club's account for the day too, caught Ed DeLucy off Glen Aukett for nought. It's his 1st duck of the campaign and 3rd of a career now 81 innings deep, sliding him to 168th of 1201 in the all-time roll of dishonour. Neat historical footnote: on this very date in 1972, club great John Thirkell also went for a duck, against Mersham Hatch — Thirkell finished his 1943–1977 career with a barely comprehensible 90 ducks, still the outright all-time No.1. Good company to be keeping. 2nd XI v Rainham (Kent) CC Paul Leandro didn't last long either, stumped by Steve Cooper off Paul Loader — his 1st duck of the season and, in fact, the 1st of his career, from only 3 innings (491st all-time). Fittingly, he too shares his date with Thirkell's 1972 effort against Mersham Hatch — some anniversaries are best not celebrated twice. Further down the order, No.9 R Waite made his senior debut and marked it in the only way the club truly respects: caught Stewart Macfarlane off Mick Pay, first innings, nought to his name. That's an instant and entirely involuntary induction onto the Debutante Duck board — a duck on debut is rare enough to be worth a proper nod, even as baptisms of fire go. He too arrives on the same 1972 date that saw Thirkell add to his ledger — quite the calendar to share a page with. Week in summary Three ducks from two matches — a modest haul by Linton's exacting standards, but the art of not scoring never truly rests. A debutant claimed straight off the bat is always the headline when it happens. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 🥇 1. Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks, 21 innings (33.33%) 2. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 31 innings (19.35%) 3. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks, 10 innings (40.0%) 4. Matt Large — 4 ducks, 24 innings (16.67%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30.0%) Cheesman sits comfortably at the summit, though Brattle's 40% failure rate remains the most terrifying number in the club this week. Plenty of season left, plenty of batsmen yet to make their uninvited contribution. Same time next match — bring your bat, and perhaps leave your footwork at home. 🦆🏏0️⃣

🦆 Duck News — 10–11 September 1994

1 duck
Week 21 · 10 Sep 1994 – 11 Sep 1994
A quiet weekend for the hunting fraternity — two fixtures on the go, but the ducks mostly kept their heads down. Still, one name stepped up to keep the honours board ticking over. Saturday 10th September 1st XI away at High Halstow CC — Stewart Morrison opened the innings and departed early, caught Tim Munday, bowled Chris Dockwray. It's his 2nd duck of the season and his 14th for the club overall, nestling him in at 55th of 1199 in the all-time standings from 117 innings — a rate that keeps him firmly inside the exclusive zero club rather than merely visiting it. Stewart is, of course, a long-standing paid-up member of the Debutante Duck club, having opened his Linton account with a nought many summers ago — proof that some traditions are for life, not just for Christmas. He also lurks in the Full House nearly-man registry, having now ducked from 10 of the 11 batting positions (somewhere there is one lonely, untroubled slot still waiting its turn), and sits comfortably inside the Lone Duck top 10 for good measure. A chip off the old block, too — brother Phillip Morrison carries 2 career ducks of his own, so the surname is not unfamiliar to this particular honours board. Splendidly, on this very day back in 1955, John --'s Taylor also strolled back for nought, against Old Roffensians no less — the fixture's changed, the calendar hasn't. Taylor went on to finish his 1949–1963 career with 15 ducks, good for 51st all-time, so Stewart is in decent historical company on the walk back to the pavilion. Sunday 11th September The second fixture of the weekend passed off without incident — every batsman got off the mark, a rare and faintly suspicious outbreak of competence. Week in Summary One duck from two matches — not the club's most productive weekend on record, but the art of not scoring never truly rests. Consistency of a very particular kind continues elsewhere in the top of the table. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard - Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks, 20 innings (35%) - Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 31 innings (19.35%) - Ian Brattle — 4 ducks, 10 innings (40%) - Matt Large — 4 ducks, 24 innings (16.67%) - Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 10 innings (30%) Ian Brattle's 40% remains the season's most alarming statistic, and Stuart Cheesman still leads the chase for glory — or ignominy, depending on your view. Next round of fixtures awaits, and with them, no doubt, fresh additions to the register. The ducks evaded the net this week, but they never stay hidden for long. 🍩🏏

🦆 Duck Club Weekly Bulletin — 27 August to 2 September 1994

7 ducks
Week 19 · 27 Aug 1994 – 2 Sep 1994
Seven fresh entries to the ledger of shame this week, spread across six fixtures against Southfleet, Bearsted, Sheldwich and the touring party's adventures in Cornwall. The exclusive zero club welcomes its usual mix of the seasoned and the merely unlucky. 🏏 1st XI At home to Southfleet, Stewart Morrison fell for nought at first drop, bowled by I Hayes, his 1st of the season and 13th career duck — a place of dubious distinction he shares with a proper Full House nearly-man board entry to his name, having ducked at ten of the eleven positions going. On this very day in 1993, Piers McDonald was doing exactly the same thing against Sheldwich, a coincidence the calendar seems rather fond of. Adam Pooley followed him back not long after, caught behind for his 2nd career duck in just 7 innings — a Duck Club apprentice still finding his feet, or rather, not finding the middle of the bat. Away at Bearsted, the top order took a proper hammering. Mike Caddick edged behind at number two for his 3rd duck of the season and 3rd of his career — three from forty, and off the mark for the summer's own private hat-trick of noughts. And in walked Piers McDonald again, this time for real, caught at slip for his 6th duck of the season and 25th of his career, comfortably installed in the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. Both men shared the honour of matching Stan Wratten, who managed the same feat against D Hambrook XI on this day in 1954 — a name from four decades back nodding in grim solidarity. 🔥 2nd XI Away at Sheldwich, Chris Tymms was bowled by Tony Marsh at number nine, his 2nd of the season and 5th career duck — level, by pure coincidence of arithmetic, with Adrian Simmonds, a Debutante Duck and Quack-to-Quack club member from 1985–1990. On this day in 1983, Mike Elworthy was likewise dismissed against British Rail Ashford, proving the fixture list has always had it in for somebody. ✈️ Tour XI — Cornwall Campaign The touring party did not escape unscathed. At Falmouth, Alan Boyns was caught off P Thomas for his 2nd duck of the season and 15th career duck, a Diamond Duck club member since 1990 (out without facing a ball, no less) and now level on 15 with both John --'s Taylor (1949–1960) and Martin Mears. Tim Thirkell, the club's own all-time leader, was doing precisely the same thing against Whitstable on this day back in 1973. Then at Mullion, Steve Cheesman was caught and bowled by James Roue for his 2nd of the season and 12th career duck — joining a rather crowded table of Jack Fryer, Len Conway, Martin Larkin and Michael Chambers, all sitting on exactly a dozen. A chip off two old blocks, too: his father Fred owns 29 career ducks and brother Stuart 11, so the Cheesman name is thoroughly paid up at the club. Fittingly, the great Paul Bowles — the club's own 50-duck man — made his own duck against Bearsted on this same 2 September back in 1968. 📊 Week in Summary Seven ducks, six matches, one touring side determined to prove that ignominy travels well. No golden ducks and no pairs this week — everyone at least survived a ball or two before departing, which by Duck Club standards counts as restraint. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks, 19 innings, 36.84% 2. Piers McDonald — 6 ducks, 30 innings, 20.0% 3. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks, 9 innings, 44.44% 4. Matt Large — 4 ducks, 23 innings, 17.39% 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 9 innings, 33.33% Ian Brattle's 44% strike rate remains the number to beat, though Stuart Cheesman's volume keeps him top of the table for now. 🍩 The season presses on, and with it the eternal question — who fancies adding their name to the honours board next weekend? The nets, and the bowlers, await. 0️⃣🦆

🦆 Duck News — Week of 20-24 August 1994

2 ducks
Week 18 · 20 Aug 1994 – 24 Aug 1994
Four matches played across the week and the exclusive zero club kept its books admirably lean — just two new entries, though both from thoroughly seasoned campaigners. 🏏 Sunday, 1st XI v Old Anchorians CC Matt Large departed for nought at four in the order, bowled by Lycett. It's the 4th duck of Matt's season and the 44th of an extraordinary Linton career spanning 200 innings — enough to hold 9th place in the all-time rankings, a spot earned through the art of not scoring practised at the very highest level. Matt's honours cabinet is already groaning: five separate Duck of the Year titles (1987, 1988, 1989, 1992 and 1993), permanent residence in both the Homebody Duck and Lone Duck top tens, a fixture of the Ducks on the Road list, and a nearly-man entry for having ducked in ten of the eleven batting positions — position four keeps him agonisingly short of Full House immortality. On this very date back in 1955, John Ames — 49 ducks across a quarter-century at the club (1952–1978) — was also trudging back to the pavilion, against Halfway House. Some traditions run deep. 🔥 Wednesday, Midweek XI v Coleshill CC, Warks Ian Brattle edged behind at number eight, caught Brian Piper, bowled M Hoefield, for his 4th duck of the campaign and 32nd of his career overall — good enough for 20th on the all-time list from a mere 97 innings, consistency of a very particular kind. Ian is a paid-up member of the 50 Before a 50 club and once strung together seven ducks in a row, a run of triumph through adversity if ever there was one. His brother Paul isn't far behind either, quietly assembling 26 career noughts of his own — evidently the trait runs in the blood. Back in 1968, Benny Bell made his own duck on this same calendar date against Aylesford Paper Mills CC, part of a career tally of 30 that still holds him at 22nd overall. 📊 The Week in Brief Just two ducks from four matches — a decidedly restrained showing by the standards of this column, though notably both fell to grizzled veterans padding out already formidable tallies rather than any fresh blood. The honours boards did the moving this week, not the debutantes' column. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season Leaderboard 1. Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks from 16 innings (43.75%) 2. Piers McDonald — 5 ducks from 27 innings (18.52%) 3. Ian Brattle — 4 ducks from 9 innings (44.44%) 4. Matt Large — 4 ducks from 20 innings (20.00%) 5. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks from 7 innings (42.86%) Stuart Cheesman sits well clear at the summit, but with the season heading into its final stretch there's still time for the chasing pack to make their move. Same invitation stands for the next fixture — pads on, bat first, ask questions later. 🦆0️⃣

5 ducks
Week 17 · 13 Aug 1994 – 17 Aug 1994
Five ducks hatched across two fixtures this week, with the Midweek XI doing the heavy lifting on a blustery Wednesday at Gravesend. 🏏 Saturday 13 August — 1st XI v Harrietsham CC (home) Paul Gibson departed for nought, caught Brooks bowled Fittall at number two — his 2nd duck of the campaign and 22nd of an increasingly distinguished career, good enough for 39th on the all-time list of 1197. Paul remains a paid-up member of the Platinum Duck club from his golden effort back in '91, though this one took its time arriving by comparison. Fittingly, on this very day in 1960, one Pat Hardwick was also trudging back against Bowaters CC, en route to 8 career ducks and a Duck of the Year gong that season — Paul's tally has long since sailed past him. Keith Hatcher followed not long after, stumped Southern bowled Chaney at six — his first duck of the season and only his 3rd in 142 innings, evidently not a man who makes a habit of it. Keith shares his moment in history with the same Pat Hardwick, 34 years to the day earlier against Bowaters. 🌱 Wednesday 17 August — Midweek XI v Gravesend CC (away) Three ducks for the price of one at Gravesend. Piers McDonald opened and closed almost as quickly, bowled Glazebrook without scoring — his 5th of the season and 24th of a career now 217 innings deep, comfortably inside the Lone Duck and Tourist Duck top tens. On this day in 1969, Percy Brattle was suffering identically against Mote A, en route to a Duck of the Year title and 16 career ducks of his own. Stuart Cheesman kept his extraordinary season rolling, stumped Mann bowled Hasler for his 7th duck in just 15 innings — a barely believable 46.67% strike rate that keeps him clear atop the current Duck Hunting Season standings, and squarely justifies his Duck of the Year 1994 silverware to date. A Full House man and Debutante Duck alumnus besides, Stuart comes from proper duck stock — brother Steve carries 11 career ducks, father Fred a formidable 29. Like his teammates, he shared his moment with Percy Brattle, 25 years to the day earlier. Ian Brattle rounded things off, bowled Elliott for his 3rd of the season and 31st for the club, keeping him at a lofty 21st on the all-time chart. A veteran of the Ducks in a Row and 50 Before a 50 clubs, Ian too found company with Percy Brattle's 1969 effort against Mote A — no relation, but a fine name to share a duck with regardless. 📊 Week in Summary Five ducks from four fixtures — a quiet one for the 2s, 3s and 4s, who escaped unscathed, but the Midweek boys made sure the tally kept climbing. Gravesend alone accounted for three of the five, a rate that will not have gone unnoticed in the away dressing room. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Stuart Cheesman — 7 ducks, 15 innings (46.67%) 2. Piers McDonald — 5 ducks, 24 innings (20.83%) 3. Simon Thirkell — 3 ducks, 7 innings (42.86%) 4. Ian Brattle — 3 ducks, 8 innings (37.5%) 5. Trevor Back — 3 ducks, 13 innings (23.08%) Stuart Cheesman sits well clear at the top, but with Simon Thirkell's rate close behind, the race for Duck of the Year 1994 is far from settled. Next fixture, someone new earns the chance to join the club — see you at the crease. 🦆0️⃣

A brisk, tidy weekend's work — nothing showy, just the art of not scoring, executed to a high standard. 🏏 1st XI at Headcorn CC Piers McDonald came in at four and departed via the increasingly fashionable route of c Simon Phipps b Graham Mackman — his 4th duck of the season and 23rd of a distinguished career, good enough for 36th on the all-time list of 1,197. On this very day in 1989, Dave Charlton was doing much the same against Chadwell Thors Oak CC — of which more shortly. Same 1989-vintage parallel: Dave Charlton, out on this day against Chadwell Thors Oak CC. And bringing up the rear at eleven, Ian Brattle went b Peter Smith for his 2nd of the season and 30th of his career, 95 innings in, rank 22 of 1,197 — a proper club stalwart. Ian is paid-up in the 50 Before a 50 club (51 ducks, high score 33), a graduate of Ducks in a Row (7 consecutive), a card-carrying member of the Debutante Duck club from way back, and shares Only Two Quacks honours with Michael Beeney. His sibling Paul isn't far behind on 26 career ducks — clearly a family gift for the nought. Same day, same parallel: Dave Charlton, 1989. 🏏 2nd XI vs Bearsted CC Home advantage counted for nothing as Dave Charlton — yes, him, the very man three Headcorn ducks were just measured against — fell b Robin Cross for his own 1st duck of the season, and 16th of his career (113 innings, rank 47 of 1,197). A tidy bit of symmetry: on this same day in 1989, Peter Whyte was doing the same against Chadwell Thors Oak CC. Dave's sibling Sean carries 4 career ducks of his own — the family colours well and truly flying. 📋 Week in Summary Four ducks from two of the weekend's three fixtures — the third match kept its powder dry and let everyone off the hook. Headcorn did the early damage with three, Bearsted's home comforts accounted for one more. A quietly effective weekend for the collectors. 🥇 Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Adrian Caddick — 3 ducks, 15 innings, 20.0% Stuart Cheesman sits imperious atop the table with an almost artistic 46% conversion rate. Can anyone catch him before season's end? 🍩 Next fixture awaits — same bats, same bowlers, same beautifully empty scorebooks. See you there.

🦆 Duck News — 23 to 29 July 1994

4 ducks
Week 14 · 23 Jul 1994 – 29 Jul 1994
A tidy little haul this week — four noughts across three fixtures, with the 1st XI doing most of the damage and Midweek XI chipping in for good measure. The exclusive zero club welcomes its latest inductees below. 🏏 Saturday 23rd — Hollingbourne CC (h) Paul Gibson, in at four, was snapped up c Kevin Phillips b Mike Donald — his first duck of the summer, and his 21st for the club in 287 innings, good enough for 39th on the all-time list. Paul is already a fixture on the Platinum Duck board from 1991 (out first ball of the game), proof that dismissal-by-first-delivery is very much the family trade. On this very day in 1960, Brian Relf was walking back for nought against Tovil too — he'd go on to 25 career ducks across a two-decade stint from 1950 to 1971. At the other end of the order, number 11 Ian Brattle was bowled by Alan Ovenden, also his season-opening duck, and his 29th overall from just 93 innings — 26th in the club's history of futility. He now sits shoulder to shoulder with David Iliffe, who plied his trade from 1976 to 1988 and also settled on 31 (his 136th innings brought it up), one place above Simon at 19th. Different decades, identical arithmetic. Simon arrives with pedigree in this parish too — a fully paid-up member of the 50 Before a 50 club (59 ducks against a highest score of a distinctly modest 48), a Duck of the Year laureate from 1983, and a fixture in both the Ducks on the Road and Tourist Duck top tens. Not bad for a man who, on this evidence, still can't middle one at Hollingbourne. And it runs in the blood. Father John Thirkell (90 career ducks) and brothers Tim (66) and Nigel (49) all keep the family name well represented on the honours board, with cousin Stuart (1) presumably just getting started. A proper duck dynasty, this one. Week in Summary One match, one duck, one Thirkell doing the honours. A quiet opening exchange by Duck Club standards, but the season is young and the club's finest have barely left the pavilion. Duck Hunting Season leaderboard 1. Simon Thirkell — 1 innings, 1 duck, 100% Early days, but Simon sets the pace by default — nowhere to go but level or better from here. That's the lot for this round, ducklings.
