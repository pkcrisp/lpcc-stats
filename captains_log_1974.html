🗓️ 21 Sep – 27 Sep 1974 · 1974 — 1 match

Sun 22 Sep 1974 🧢 1st XIv AC Anchorians CC · HomeformLLDDD

Sunday last saw Anchorians come to Linton Park, and for once the coin let me down before a ball was bowled — they won the toss and had no hesitation in batting first. It looked, for a good while, like being the sort of afternoon where the tea urn gets more of a workout than the scoreboard, as Ward (36) and Durbridge (35) put together the kind of watchful partnership that tests a captain's patience and his field placings in equal measure. Croker chipped in with 20, and between them they nudged Anchorians to 124 for 8 off their 44 overs — respectable, but not, I felt, insurmountable.

Credit where it's due: Tony Gauna was the pick of the attack with 2 for 23, Ron Hickman weighed in with 2 for 48, and my brother Nigel picked up a wicket too. Allan Cooper, meanwhile, bowled with the miserly discipline of a man who begrudges every run as if it were coming out of his own pocket — five maidens, not a boundary in sight.

With the target in view, Cliff Anscombe played the anchor role beautifully for 48, and Nigel rounded things off in style with an unbeaten 40 as we cruised home at 128 for 3 with overs to spare — I chipped in modestly along the way, happy enough just to see us over the line. A word too for Tony, whose two wickets nudged him up to 17th on the club's all-time list — quietly, deservedly earned.

All told: a solid team performance, seven wickets in hand, and a very pleasant walk back to the pavilion. A good day for the club.

🗓️ 14 Sep – 20 Sep 1974 · 1974 — 2 matches

Sun 15 Sep 1974 🧢 1st XIv MH Mersham Hatch · HomeformWLLDD

Sunday 15 September 1974, and Dummy Site were our visitors for what turned into one of those honourably indecisive afternoons club cricket does so well. I lost the toss — no shock there, my coin-calling remains a weakness rivalled only by my off-side driving — and we were put into the field.

They ground their way to 128 for 8 off 39 overs, built chiefly around A. Gosling's unbeaten 45 and useful support from F-H. Ames with 37. Nigel Thirkell was magnificent with the ball, 3 for 32 including five maidens that must have driven their middle order to distraction, while Paul Bowles chipped in with 3 for 44 and Terry Donovan took 2 for 4. I. Cloke and D. Bromley both departed without troubling the scorers.

With 128 to chase, we settled for 60 for 2 off our 23 overs, Paul Bowles top-scoring with 23 and Cliff Anscombe unbeaten on 21 — no great urgency in it, but no alarms either.

Two milestones worth raising a glass to: Cliff Anscombe brought up his 200th appearance in Linton colours, a proper landmark of loyal service, and Terry Donovan's brace nudged him past Dick Langridge into 8th on the club's all-time wicket-taking list. Meanwhile Paul Brattle continues to lead our 1974 run charts on 612, with Nigel Thirkell now second among the season's wicket-takers on 66.

A draw, then — honours shared, and honestly, about right.

Sat 14 Sep 1974 🧢 1st XIv SC Southborough CC · AwayformDWLLD

Saturday 14th September saw us head to Southborough with the sun, remarkably, still cooperating. I won the toss and, against all instinct for a captain who usually can't call heads from tails, opted to bat first.

It proved the right call, up to a point. Gordon Dent played the innings of the day, a thoroughly well-made 68 that gave the rest of us something to hide behind, and I chipped in with 39 to keep the scoreboard honest, with Paul Brattle's 21 rounding things off nicely. We closed on 175/6 from our fifty overs — not a total to frighten anyone, but enough, we hoped, to make Southborough work for their tea.

And work they did, largely thanks to Gordon Baldwin, whose 82 was the innings of the match and rather more than we'd bargained for, ably assisted by Peter Young's 25. Ron Hickman was magnificent with the ball, four wickets for 37 and nine — nine! — maidens, bowling with the kind of miserly discipline that makes batsmen question their life choices, while Brian Fuller and Nigel Thirkell chipped in either side of him. Ron Kingsmill, mercifully for him, was gone before he could trouble the scorers.

In the end Southborough fell short at 140/7 from 44, and honours were shared. A draw, then — neither side quite able to land the killer blow, which felt about right for an afternoon of good cricket and better company.

Sat 31 Aug 1974 🧢 1st XIv Whitstable CC · AwayformDDWLL

Saturday 31st August 1974 saw us take the road to Whitstable, and if you're after a tale of glorious conquest, best turn the page now — this one's a story of honest toil and a hard-earned handshake.

Tritton (48) and Mike Tritton (31), the three of them steering the innings to 204 for 7 off their 51 overs. Small mercies came our way when Jim Long and Andy Parker both departed without troubling the scorers. Chasing 205 was always going to be a stretch, and so it proved as we crawled to 120 for 6 from our 48 overs. Thirkell topped the order with 35, well supported by Robert Chance's 27, though poor Mike Iliffe's duck was a blot nobody needed on the card.

So, honours shared — a draw when neither side could force the issue. Not a classic for the scrapbook, but with Thirkell now second in our season's run charts and Harris leading the wickets, the engine room is clearly purring nicely. Onwards.

Wed 28 Aug 1974 🧢 Midweek XIv Gravesend CC · HomeformDWWDD

As I stepped out for the toss on a fine Wednesday against Gravesend, fortune smiled and I had no hesitation in asking us to bat first — a decision that, for once, didn't come back to haunt me.

We laboured a touch for our 134 all out in 38 overs, though John Ames played the innings of the day with a handsome 54, ably supported by Cliff Anscombe's brisk 32. There was, it must be said, a rather unfortunate brace of ducks from Roland Parr and Jim Hall, the sort of thing best filed under "these things happen" and never spoken of again.

Whatever anxieties lingered over tea were swiftly dispelled once we took the field. Gravesend simply had no answer, subsiding to 59 all out in 38.3 overs, with M David, Mukul Lall and S Knott all shown the door without so much as a run to their names. Paul Bowles was quite magnificent, tearing through the order with 7 for 29, while Ron Hickman offered superb support, 3 for 20 built on nine miserly maidens — not a boundary given away lightly. Cliff Anscombe had a trickier time of it behind the stumps, ten byes going astray, though nobody was much minded to dwell on it given the scoreline. For my own part, I chipped in enough with bat and ball to keep the runs in the family, now sitting second in our Midweek XI charts, with Bowles rightly out in front with the ball.

A thumping 75-run win, then, and a thoroughly satisfying afternoon's work. Onwards we go.

Wednesday, 21 August 1974, and the road took us to Gravesend. Winning the toss and electing to bat felt right at the time, and by the end of the day I'd stand by it — though the scorebook has a couple of blemishes I'd sooner not dwell on.

Rather more than a match, in the end. Ron Hickman brought up 200 career wickets and leapfrogged George Champion into 14th on the club's all-time list — no small thing on a Wednesday in Kent. Jon Harris, meanwhile, continues to top both our run and wicket charts for the season, and doesn't look inclined to relinquish either.

A draw, then — but with Ron's milestone in the bag, I'd call it a highly satisfactory afternoon's work.
